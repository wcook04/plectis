Introduction

Throughout, z+=max⁡(z,0)z_+=\max(z,0). Statements about a tail permit deletion of a finite prefix, but the index in a specified higher-order rate is retained. In particular, 1+3/n+o(n−3)1+3/n+o(n^{-3}) must not be silently replaced by the same formula in a shifted index. Sylvester’s sequence 2,3,7,43,1807,…2,3,7,43,1807,\ldots is generated by an=an−12−an−1+1a_{n}=a_{n-1}^{2}-a_{n-1}+1 and has reciprocal sum 11; the recurrence is equivalent to the telescoping identity 1an−1−1=1an−1+1an−1.\frac1{a_{n-1}-1}=\frac1{a_{n-1}}+\frac1{a_n-1}. Consequently the tail beginning with 1/an−11/a_{n-1} equals 1/(an−1−1)1/(a_{n-1}-1). Erdős Problem #243 asserts that this is the only way a sequence of that growth can have a rational reciprocal sum:

Below we index the same recurrence as an+1=an2−an+1a_{n+1}=a_n^{2}-a_n+1, which is the shift the formal sources use; the two forms are the same statement. The hypothesis is asymptotic and the conclusion is exact. Clearing a rational tail as Cn/DnC_n/D_n exposes that distinction in the integer En=Dn−(an−1)CnE_n=D_n-(a_n-1)C_n: a Sylvester tail has En=0E_n=0, while quadratic growth only gives En/Cn→0E_n/C_n\to0. Integrality alone cannot turn this relative estimate into En=0E_n=0 when CnC_n grows. The exact dynamics retain extra information: old divisors persist, and they constrain which numerator heights the orbit can visit. This is the arithmetic information used by the bounded-negative and record-crossing arguments.

Section  carries out that conversion at one prescribed rate and settles the assertion of Problem  on the resulting class of sequences. Section  places the theorem against the published sufficient conditions, states the evidence class of every result here, and guides the rest of the note.

Irrationality at the cubic rate

The short note treats the cubic-rate theorem as a further consequence. This section supplies its full polynomial and number-field argument. The canonical integer state is constructed first; the general error coordinates are developed in Section .

The irrationality theorem needs only exclusion of eventual equality. Theorem  proves more: the exceptional set has strictly positive lower density. Equivalently, no subsequence of prefix lengths can have an exceptional proportion tending to zero. The density constant may depend on the orbit and on the proposed cubic. The two restrictions that meet on the same cubic are integrality, which supplies the rising-factorial shape and the scale, and the denominator transport, which imposes an arithmetic square condition; the scale calculation and the two residue words that close the argument can be checked on the page.

Prior work, and the status of the note

Classical sufficient conditions and the pseudo-greedy formulation.

The rational-tail method predates these coordinates. Erdős and Straus [15] use eventual integer remainders under a small-numerator hypothesis. For polynomial Cantor series, Hančl and Tijdeman decompose the numerator into finitely many shifted products; rationality is characterised by the vanishing of the resulting polynomial sum [16]. We use that polynomial theorem as methodological context, not as an unrestricted theorem about arrays of signed integer coefficients. The finite-to-infinite rearrangement requires boundary control, which is available in the polynomial setting. Neither cited result supplies the bounded-negative first-crossing argument proved below.

A factorial residue reduction for forced orbits

In the case m=c=1m=c=1 of Section , where the shape equation  reads Dn+1=(an−1)(n+1)D_n+1=(a_n-1)(n+1), each multiplier is determined by its predecessor; we call such an orbit . At index nn the numerator of the next multiplier is num⁡(n,a)=(n+1)a2−(n+2)a+(n+3),\operatorname{num}(n,a)=(n+1)a^{2}-(n+2)a+(n+3), the forced numerator, and the divisor is n+2n+2. The orbit survives a step when that division is exact, giving a survival predicate, the survival predicate. Example  is the forced orbit from a=3a=3 read this way: num⁡(0,3)=6\operatorname{num}(0,3)=6 is divisible by 22 and gives a1=3a_1=3, while num⁡(1,3)=13\operatorname{num}(1,3)=13 is not divisible by 33, so the orbit stops there. Deciding survival by iteration is expensive because the orbit grows doubly exponentially, and it is unnecessary: survival over a finite horizon depends on the initial value only through a factorial residue. Computing a pseudo-greedy orbit through residues modulo a shrinking product modulus is Koizumi’s method ; for the forced numerator that product is a factorial.

Theorem B.1 (factorial residue reduction). For all hh and all integers a≡b(mod⁡(h+1)!)a\equiv b \pmod{(h+1)!}, the orbit from aa survives hh forced updates exactly when the orbit from bb does.

Proof. Let M(0,i)=1M(0,i)=1 and M(h+1,i)=(i+2)M(h,i+1)M(h+1,i)=(i+2)M(h,i+1), an ascending factorial with M(h,0)=(h+1)!M(h,0)=(h+1)!. Formalised as the factorial residue reduction, over the shrinking-modulus transport, the polynomial congruence, and the exact-division cancellation; the modulus identifications are the ascending-factorial form and the factorial value at the initial index.

At h=1h=1 the modulus is 2!=22!=2, and surviving one update means 2∣a2−2a+32\mid a^{2}-2a+3, which holds exactly for odd aa: for instance num⁡(0,3)=6\operatorname{num}(0,3)=6 but num⁡(0,4)=11\operatorname{num}(0,4)=11. So one step of survival is decided by the parity of aa alone, which is Theorem  at its smallest nontrivial horizon.

Result map and proof dependencies

The following map is for mathematical navigation, not a catalogue of every registered declaration. A checked component does not confer that status on an assembled ordinary theorem.

Bounded defect.

Canonical rational tail and normalised vanishing →\longrightarrow eventual absorption; cofinally bounded negative values →\longrightarrow stable gcd; reduced tail plus bounded rise →\longrightarrow CRT contradiction. The state endpoint and the identified canonical R7 transfer have supplied main-repository build evidence. The extra bounded-defect assumption is not deduced from the problem.

Finite negative mass.

Cn+1≤Cn(1+(−En)+/Cn)C_{n+1}\le C_n(1+(-E_n)_+/C_n) and summability →\longrightarrow bounded integer height →\longrightarrow finitely many rises and eventual constancy. This scalar result needs no normalised vanishing. Its scalar and exact-orbit endpoints are among the cited main-repository declarations; the separately linked R8 canonical formulation is outside that receipt.

Weighted records and protected epochs.

LCM records give weighted first crossings; primitive prime-power persistence gives protected epochs. The canonical odd-power supply and the assembled energy equivalence are proved ordinarily in this revision. Separate-release integer lemmas and main-repository conditional record declarations retain their own, narrower evidence boundaries.

Cubic rate.

The quantitative tail comparison and Gamma-ratio estimate →\longrightarrow integer cubic extraction; transport congruences and Chebotarëv →\longrightarrow a square in the cubic field; trace classification →\longrightarrow two scales, each obstructed modulo seven. The displayed assembly is an ordinary proof. Finite checks and component sources are supporting evidence, not a formalised cubic-rate endpoint.

Static barriers.

Sparse primes disprove a scale-free small-rise extension. At the specified double-exponential modulus scale, prefix density and CRT give the exact whole-modulus maximal-gap coefficient. Both are ordinary auxiliary results, without a canonical reciprocal-tail realisation.

What remains.

The unrestricted problem would require an additional global estimate, such as the stated mass or record budget, or a contradiction to the full nonterminal profile. None of the static examples or local identities supplies one. The more detailed source registry and inherited declaration catalogue remain in the research package rather than being repeated here.
