Introduction

For a finite prime set PP, let 𝒮P\mathcal S_P consist of the positive integers whose prime factors lie in PP, including 11. We use ℕ={0,1,2,…}\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\} for exponent indices. For x≥1x\ge1, put L⁡(x)=lcm⁡{u∈𝒮P:u≤x},ℛP=∑u∈𝒮PL⁡(u)−1.\operatorname{L}(x)=\operatorname{lcm}\{u\in\mathcal S_P:u\le x\},\qquad \mathcal R_P=\sum_{u\in\mathcal S_P}\operatorname{L}(u)^{-1}. Erdős asked whether ℛP\mathcal R_P is irrational when |P|≥2|P|\ge2 [1]. For P={p,q,r}P=\{p,q,r\}, write H⁡(x)=p⌊log⁡px⌋q⌊log⁡qx⌋r⌊log⁡rx⌋,K⁡(i,j,k)=H⁡(piqjrk)−1.\operatorname{H}(x)=p^{\lfloor\log_p x\rfloor}q^{\lfloor\log_q x\rfloor} r^{\lfloor\log_r x\rfloor},\qquad \operatorname{K}(i,j,k)=\operatorname{H}(p^iq^jr^k)^{-1}. Prime-power divisibility gives L⁡=H\operatorname{L}=\operatorname{H} (Proposition 4.1). Fan used this identity to separate the two-prime kernel [7]. With three primes, even a finite sum of separated products is impossible. This is a statement about the kernel, not the arithmetic nature of its sum.

Exact rank is not an approximation obstruction.

Truncating the original kernel to i<Ni<N gives a sum of NN separated terms. The bound H⁡(x)>x3/(pqr)\operatorname{H}(x)>x^3/(pqr) makes the truncation error tend to zero both uniformly and in ℓ1(ℕ3)\ell^1(\mathbb N^3). By contrast, the rescaled matrix in the rank proof has uniform distance (1−1/r)/2(1-1/r)/2 from the matrices of finite rank. Rescaling preserves exact rank but removes the decay responsible for the first approximation. The companion proves both assertions, including the distinction between finite restrictions and the full infinite matrix, in Section 4.1.

The two-prime comparison

Fan’s post of 26 June 2026 gives the repeated two-prime factorisation, its quadratic expression in one Hecke–Mahler value and its transcendence conclusion [7]. Counting the contributions at powers of each prime also gives the affine formula for the distinct-height sum below. The calculation fixes the normalisations and distinguishes the two sums.

Two steps of the recurrence already show the finite data we need: Xℓ+2=bℓ+1bℓXℓ−(bℓ+1mℓ+mℓ+1).X_{\ell+2}=b_{\ell+1}b_\ell X_\ell -(b_{\ell+1}m_\ell+m_{\ell+1}). If BXℓBX_\ell is an integer, this identity determines the residue of BXℓ+2BX_{\ell+2} modulo the product of the two bases. We will compare the least positive representative of that residue with the upper bound for the endpoint tail.

For a window starting at ℓ≥0\ell\ge0 and having h≥0h\ge0 steps, define its product of bases and accumulated numerator from [eq:actual-digit] by Wℓ,0=1,Fℓ,0=0,Wℓ,h+1=bℓ+hWℓ,h,Fℓ,h+1=bℓ+hFℓ,h+mℓ+h.W_{\ell,0}=1,\quad F_{\ell,0}=0,\qquad W_{\ell,h+1}=b_{\ell+h}W_{\ell,h},\quad F_{\ell,h+1}=b_{\ell+h}F_{\ell,h}+m_{\ell+h}. Induction on hh gives Xℓ+h=Wℓ,hXℓ−Fℓ,h.\begin{equation} \label{eq:actual-tail} X_{\ell+h}=W_{\ell,h}X_\ell-F_{\ell,h}. \end{equation} For integers W≥1W\ge1 and tt, define lpr⁡W(t)=1+((t−1)mod⁡W)\operatorname{lpr}_W(t)=1+((t-1)\operatorname{mod} W), using a remainder in {0,…,W−1}\{0,\ldots,W-1\}. Thus a zero residue is represented by WW, not 00. Throughout this section use the upper bound K(B,a)=90B(a+1)2.\begin{equation} \label{eq:actual-bound} K(B,a)=90B(a+1)^2. \end{equation}

Lemma 5.1 (least positive residues). If dd is a positive integer with d≤Kd\le K and d≡−BF(mod⁡W)d\equiv -BF\pmod W, where W≥1W\ge1, then lpr⁡W(−BF)≤K\operatorname{lpr}_W(-BF)\le K.

Lean: paper finite endpoint obstruction.

Proof. Every positive representative of the residue is at least its least positive representative, so lpr⁡W(−BF)≤d≤K\operatorname{lpr}_W(-BF)\le d\le K. ◻

For example, the window starting at ℓ=1\ell=1 with length 66 has W1,6=648000W_{1,6}=648000 and F1,6=524431F_{1,6}=524431. At B=1B=1 its least positive residue is 123569123569, exceeding K(1,7)=5760K(1,7)=5760. This rules out an integral X1X_1, but not a rational denominator that would clear only at a later index. The theorem therefore asks for windows beyond every prescribed starting index, as well as for every eligible denominator.

Theorem 5.2 (a residue criterion for irrationality). The number SS is irrational if and only if for every B≥1 with gcd⁡(B,30)=1 and every a0≥1,there are ℓ≥a0,h≥1 such thatlpr⁡Wℓ,h(−BFℓ,h)>K(B,ℓ+h).\begin{equation} \label{eq:escape} \begin{gathered} \text{for every }B\ge1\text{ with }\gcd(B,30)=1 \text{ and every }a_0\ge1,\\ \text{there are }\ell\ge a_0,\ h\ge1\text{ such that} \operatorname{lpr}_{W_{\ell,h}}(-BF_{\ell,h})>K(B,\ell+h). \end{gathered} \end{equation}

Lean: short window equivalence.

Proof. Assume [eq:escape] and suppose SS is rational. Theorem 4.6 supplies a positive integral carry da=BXad_a=BX_a after its onset. Choose a window in [eq:escape] beyond that onset. Equation [eq:actual-tail] gives dℓ+h≡−BFℓ,h(mod⁡Wℓ,h)d_{\ell+h}\equiv-BF_{\ell,h}\pmod{W_{\ell,h}}, contradicting Lemma 5.1 and the upper bound.

Conversely, let SS be irrational and fix B,ℓ≥1B,\ell\ge1. Equation [eq:prefix-lattice] makes BXℓBX_\ell nonintegral. Put δ=⌈BXℓ⌉−BXℓ∈(0,1)\delta=\lceil BX_\ell\rceil-BX_\ell\in(0,1). Since Wℓ,h≥2hW_{\ell,h}\ge2^h and Xℓ+h=O((ℓ+h+1)2)X_{\ell+h}=O((\ell+h+1)^2), for all sufficiently large hh the number δ+BXℓ+h/Wℓ,h\delta+BX_{\ell+h}/W_{\ell,h} lies in (0,1)(0,1). The window identity shows that the integer ⌈BXℓ⌉Wℓ,h−BFℓ,h\lceil BX_\ell\rceil W_{\ell,h}-BF_{\ell,h} lies strictly between 00 and Wℓ,hW_{\ell,h} and is congruent to −BFℓ,h-BF_{\ell,h}. Hence lpr⁡Wℓ,h(−BFℓ,h)=δWℓ,h+BXℓ+h.\operatorname{lpr}_{W_{\ell,h}}(-BF_{\ell,h}) =\delta W_{\ell,h}+BX_{\ell+h}. Its exponentially growing first term eventually exceeds K(B,ℓ+h)K(B,\ell+h). This proves [eq:escape], in fact from each fixed start. ◻

The proved bound 90B(a+1)290B(a+1)^2 controls every positive integral BXaBX_a that rationality would force. Polynomial growth alone does not suffice: the zero bound would make the residue inequality automatic. The companion, Section 7, treats smaller and more general bounds. No finite search establishes the unbounded quantifiers in [eq:escape].

The remaining task has the following three equivalent formulations.

Problem 5.3 (the repeated three-prime target). Prove that S=ℛ{2,3,5}S=\mathcal R_{\{2,3,5\}} is irrational.

Problem 5.4 (no reduced tail is an integer). Prove that for every a≥1a\ge1 and every integer B≥1B\ge1 coprime to 3030, BXa∉ℤ.\begin{equation} \label{eq:tail-nonintegrality} BX_a\notin\mathbb Z. \end{equation}

Problem 5.5 (residue inequalities after every starting index). Prove [eq:escape].

Indeed, one integral scaled tail makes SS rational by [eq:prefix-lattice], whereas denominator clearing gives such tails from a rational SS. Theorem 5.2 supplies the third equivalence. These arithmetic assertions remain unproved. The companion’s finite certificate (Section 8) and comparisons with other approaches (Section 9) are supplementary, not premises of this argument.

Scope of the supplementary literature.

The companion develops the comparisons omitted from the main argument. Its Section 5 relates the bounded-ratio argument to Erdős–Taylor [10] and Fan [11]; Section 6 compares the exact carry restrictions and polynomial-radix assumptions of Hančl–Tijdeman [12]. The Isabelle development of Koutsoukou-Argyraki and Li [18] verifies classical criteria, not the present series.

Section 9 of the companion separates the finite-difference hypotheses of Luca–Ouaknine–Worrell [13] from quadratic growth alone, and checks the obstacles to using the echoing , digit-complexity [19] and multivariate Mahler [20] results. These comparisons do not establish the missing hypotheses for SS.

For broader background, the companion distinguishes work on fixed-prime semigroups [15][16] from other unit-fraction irrationality problems [17]. No estimate from those papers is used in this note’s counting argument.
