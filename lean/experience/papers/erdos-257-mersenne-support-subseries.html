Introduction

For a set AA of positive integers and a real number b>1b>1, write XA(b)=∑a∈A1ba−1.X_A(b)=\sum_{a\in A}\frac1{b^a-1}. This series converges by comparison with a geometric series, since (ba−1)−1≤b1−a/(b−1)(b^a-1)^{-1}\le b^{1-a}/(b-1). Erdős proved irrationality for full support at integer bases , and later proved the same conclusion for pairwise coprime supports of finite reciprocal sum [7]. On that page he stated that the coprimality assumption could be removed, without giving the details. The question for arbitrary infinite support in base two is Problem #257:

Problem 1.1 (Erdős #257). Is XA(2)X_A(2) irrational for every infinite A⊆ℕ>0A\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{>0}?

The conditions below are hereditary: if a set satisfies one of them, so does each of its infinite subsets. For the first condition, consider an exponent a=2kma=2^km with mm odd. We replace its reciprocal weight 1/(2km)1/(2^km) by 1/[m(b2k−1)]1/[m(b^{2^k}-1)], so that a large power of 22 in aa reduces its contribution to the sum. More generally, for a finite set PP of primes, let the PP-part of aa be h(a)=∏p∈Ppvp(a)h(a)=\prod_{p\in P}p^{v_p(a)}, where vp(a)v_p(a) is the exponent of pp in aa.

Theorem 1.2 (a weighted condition on the support). Let b≥2b\ge2 be an integer, let A⊆ℕ>0A\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{>0} be infinite, and let PP be a finite nonempty set of primes. Set h(a)=∏p∈Ppvp(a)h(a)=\prod_{p\in P}p^{v_p(a)}. If Wb,P(A):=∑a∈Ah(a)a(bh(a)−1)<∞,\begin{equation} \label{eq:weighted-fixed-base} W_{b,P}(A):=\sum_{a\in A} \frac{h(a)}{a(b^{h(a)}-1)}<\infty, \end{equation} then XA(b)X_A(b) is irrational. In particular, the base-two condition ∑a∈Ah(a)a(2h(a)−1)<∞\begin{equation*} \label{eq:weighted-return} \sum_{a\in A}\frac{h(a)}{a(2^{h(a)}-1)}<\infty \tag{W} \end{equation*} implies that XA(b)X_A(b) is irrational for every integer b≥2b\ge2. Both conclusions are hereditary under passage to infinite subsets.

Consider the support A⋆={2km:k≥1,m odd,m≤22k}.A_\star=\{2^km:k\ge1,\ m\text{ odd},\ m\le2^{2^k}\}. For a fixed kk, the sum of 1/m1/m over the odd cofactors is of order 2k2^k. Thus the corresponding terms contribute at least 1/41/4 to ∑a∈A⋆1/a\sum_{a\in A_\star}1/a, but at most 21−k2^{1-k} to [eq:weighted-return]. The calculation in Section 2 proves both bounds. Every infinite subset of A⋆A_\star consequently gives an irrational value at every integer base. Since h/(2h−1)≤1h/(2^h-1)\le1, the same theorem also includes reciprocal-summable supports. Section A gives a direct proof of Erdős’s stated extension.

The divisor-cover condition instead bounds fractional powers of finite divisor counts by positive sums over divisors (Theorem 3.1). We prove that the two conditions are incomparable and that their union gives a further irrationality criterion: if EE satisfies the weighted condition and VV has a summable divisor cover, every infinite subset of E∪VE\cup V has irrational sum (Theorem 4.1). The separating constructions in Proposition 5.1 then give a union outside both individual classes (Corollary 5.2). These are sufficient hypotheses for Problem 1.1; the arbitrary support in that problem need not satisfy them.

The proofs compare a multiple of XA(b)X_A(b) with an integer. For a positive integer NN, put Δb,A(N)=∑a∈AbNmod⁡a−1ba−1=(bN−1)XA(b)−Jb,A(N),Jb,A(N)=∑a∈Aa≤N∑j=1⌊N/a⌋bN−ja.\begin{equation} \Delta_{b,A}(N)=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{b^{N\operatorname{mod} a}-1}{b^a-1} =(b^N-1)X_A(b)-J_{b,A}(N),\qquad J_{b,A}(N)=\sum_{\substack{a\in A\\a\le N}} \sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor N/a\rfloor}b^{N-ja}. \label{eq:intro-displacement}\tag{D} \end{equation} The identity follows by division of NN by aa. If AA is infinite, then Δb,A(N)>0\Delta_{b,A}(N)>0, since some a∈Aa\in A exceeds NN. Suppose that b≥2b\ge2 is an integer and XA(b)=p/qX_A(b)=p/q, with p∈ℤp\in\mathbb Z and q≥1q\ge1 an integer. Then qΔb,A(N)q\Delta_{b,A}(N) is a positive integer, and hence Δb,A(N)≥1/q\Delta_{b,A}(N)\ge1/q. To obtain a contradiction, it therefore suffices to make Δb,A(N)\Delta_{b,A}(N) arbitrarily small. Erdős also proposed using fractional parts of powers times the series value [7].

We average over multiples of a modulus divisible by a finite subset of AA, whose terms in [eq:intro-displacement] then vanish. For each remaining exponent, the mean contribution over a complete residue period is small when its gcd with the modulus is large. The incomplete period causes the difficulty: its bound lacks the factor 1/a1/a needed to sum over the exponents. A second average, over dyadic lengths, restores this factor. Indeed, an exponent contributes only at lengths that reach it, and the reciprocals of those lengths have a geometrically decreasing sum. Keeping the dependence on the modulus explicit allows both criteria to use the same finite average.

Section 2 proves the weighted estimate. Section 3 introduces divisor covers through a finite example and proves their averaging estimate. Section 4 combines the criteria. We construct the separating supports in Section 5 and discuss the limits of the argument in Section 6. The main argument ends there. Appendix A gives the inherited reciprocal-summable proof. The other appendices collect finite-denominator results and conditional tests for rational membership, a separate question from the support criteria. Neither 1/21/2 nor 1/211/21 is decided by those tests.

Positive divisor majorants

The weighted criterion treats exponents individually. A divisor cover can take advantage of correlations between several exponents. The relevant quantity is how many members of a finite set divide the same integer.

We shall also use the divisor counts cA(n)=#{a∈A:a∣n},XA(b)=∑n≥1cA(n)b−n.\begin{equation} c_A(n)=\#\{a\in A:a\mid n\},\qquad X_A(b)=\sum_{n\ge1}c_A(n)b^{-n}. \label{eq:incidence} \end{equation} Expanding (ba−1)−1=∑j≥1b−aj(b^a-1)^{-1}=\sum_{j\ge1}b^{-aj} and interchanging nonnegative sums gives the identity. Although 𝟏A\mathbf1_A takes only the values zero and one, its divisor transform may take larger values, with 0≤cA(n)≤τ(n)0\le c_A(n)\le\tau(n).

Limits of the small-displacement criterion

The small-displacement condition does not cover even every support whose sum is already known to be irrational. For full support we have Δ2,ℕ>0(N)>(2N−1)∑a>N2−a=1−2−N≥12(N≥1).\Delta_{2,\mathbb{N}_{>0}}(N) >(2^N-1)\sum_{a>N}2^{-a}=1-2^{-N}\ge\frac12 \qquad(N\ge1). The full-support value is nevertheless irrational . Thus a proof for arbitrary support would have to use more than this particular family of small displacements. The example leaves other integer linear forms and averaging arguments available.

The logarithmic lower bound is not a converse: replacing a fractional majorant by a logarithmic one need not control averages along the multiples of a prescribed modulus. The counterexample and the bound that remains valid on ordinary initial intervals are proved in Section 13 of the long record; those proofs were first worked out in an AI-assisted note of 17 September 2026. These are statements about finite averages; they do not rule out an irrationality criterion based on other uses of logarithmic divisor counts.

For squarefree support, Duverney and Tachiya’s theorem already gives irrationality. It holds at every base 2j2^j, j≥1j\ge1, by Corollary 1.2 and Example 1.1 of Duverney and Tachiya . Their corollary covers the ss-free products of any pairwise coprime sequence of polynomial growth, and they present it as support for the conjecture of Erdős and Graham stated here as Problem 1.1. For the squarefree support with 11 removed, the divisor count is 2ω(n)−12^{\omega(n)}-1, which is odd for n≥2n\ge2. This excludes a first-block-divisibility condition at even bases. Adjoining 11 removes that parity obstruction while changing the sum by a rational number. The argument concerns that normalisation of the certificate, and gives no obstruction to irrationality of the sum. Details and the other known support families appear in the comparison with known support theorems in the long record.

A growth-only criterion gives a different class: if A={c1<c2<⋯}A=\{c_1<c_2<\cdots\} and limsup⁡ncn/2n=∞\limsup_n c_n/2^n=\infty, then XA(b)X_A(b) is irrational for every integer b≥2b\ge2 [8]. The same section of the long record proves the translation from denominator growth and gives examples showing that this class and reciprocal summability are incomparable. The interval-filling constructions in  and the freely chosen lacunary denominators of [16] are not constructions of subseries of these fixed Mersenne weights.

Prime-incidence arguments require additional care as well: multiplying all prime exponents by 22 creates positive correlations between their divisibility indicators. The identity and covariance calculation appear at the end of the long record’s discussion of mixed supports. Size conditions alone do not supply that independence.

Reciprocal-summable supports

Erdős stated the following extension of his pairwise-coprime theorem [7]. It follows from Theorem 1.2, since h/(2h−1)≤1h/(2^h-1)\le1, but the direct argument explains why reciprocal summability makes the averaging simpler.

Theorem A.1 (reciprocal-summable supports). Let A⊆ℕ>0A\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{>0} be infinite. If ∑a∈A1a<∞,\sum_{a\in A}\frac1a<\infty, then XA(b)X_A(b) is irrational for every integer b≥2b\ge2.

Erdős also discussed weaker conditions [7]. We do not identify our weighted hypothesis with the conditions he suggested.

Here we can take the observation length to infinity while holding the modulus fixed: reciprocal summability supplies a summable bound for all the remaining exponents. We then enlarge the modulus to make each fixed exponent divide it. No common period for the whole infinite support is required.
