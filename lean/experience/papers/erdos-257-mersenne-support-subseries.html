Rational values and scaled tails

The displacement identity gives a fixed arithmetic lattice under rationality. Its recurrence is zN+1=2zN−vcA(N+1),zN=v∑r≥1cA(N+r)2−r∈ℤz_{N+1}=2z_N-vc_A(N+1),\qquad z_N=v\sum_{r\ge1}c_A(N+r)2^{-r}\in\mathbb{Z} when XA(2)=p/vX_A(2)=p/v. The sequence cAc_A is determined by one Boolean selector.

Two elementary facts explain why size information alone has little force here. If a0=min⁡Aa_0=\min A, every a0a_0 consecutive integers contain a multiple of a0a_0, so a divisor-incidence zero window has length at most a0−1a_0-1. If AA is infinite, choose kk exponents and a common multiple LL; then cA(L)≥kc_A(L)\ge k and TL−1(2)≥k/2T_{L-1}^{(2)}\ge k/2. Both facts hold without rationality. The arithmetic information lies in the lattice and in the compatibility of all coefficients with the same selector.

Precisely, Dirichlet convolution gives μ*cA=𝟏A\mu*c_A=\mathbf1_A, where μ\mu is the Möbius function. A putative recurrence with integral forcing must therefore satisfy (μ*cA)(n)∈{0,1}(\mu*c_A)(n)\in\{0,1\} for all nn. The complete Boolean–Möbius scaled-tail correspondence and its finite example are retained in the long record, under (Theorem thm:bmc). The correspondence permits finite supports; infinitude remains a separate requirement for a counterexample to Problem .

Where the return mechanism stops

The preceding criteria establish irrationality through small positive returns. This mechanism already fails at the full support: Δ2,ℕ>0(N)>(2N−1)∑a>N2−a=1−2−N≥12(N≥1).\Delta_{2,\mathbb{N}_{>0}}(N) >(2^N-1)\sum_{a>N}2^{-a}=1-2^{-N}\ge\frac12 \qquad(N\ge1). The full-support value is nevertheless irrational . A universal proof must therefore control arithmetic behaviour beyond small returns.

The distinction also appears in the squarefree support. Its series is known to be irrational at powers-of-two bases by Duverney–Tachiya . The zero-window obstruction for one normalised certificate scheme concerns that scheme’s hypotheses. It gives no obstruction to irrationality of the value. The precise normalisation counterexamples are retained in the long record, together with the complete catalogue of known support families.

A related independence boundary concerns prime-incidence arguments. Writing $f_A(n)=\sum_{a\in A}{\bf1}_{a\mid n}$, the local identity $$f_A(an)-f_A(n)=1-{\bf1}_{a\mid n}\quad(n\ge1)$$ for a fixed a∈Aa\in A holds exactly when aa is coprime to every other member of AA. Already for the dilated prime support 2𝒫2\mathcal P, $$\operatorname{Cov}({\bf1}_{2p\mid n},{\bf1}_{2q\mid n}) =\frac1{4pq}\qquad(p\ne q\text{ odd primes}),$$ where covariance is taken over a common arithmetic period. Thus multiplicative size conditions alone do not supply the independence used by the prime argument. The accompanying proof is not in this checkout; it is not an independently assessable result of this release.

Further questions

The preceding arguments isolate two different tasks. For new irrational supports, one needs a summable or uniformly averaged control of the shifted divisor atoms, or an arithmetic functional that also detects supports whose positive displacements stay separated from zero. For a proposed rational target, one needs the actual-selector inequality ; synthetic recurrences satisfying only growth and integrality conditions do not provide it.

Theorem 7 (fatal-branch quotient refinement at 1/211/21). The following statements hold.

  1. 1/21∈𝒜1/21\in\mathcal A if and only if ℱ21\mathcal F_{21} does not hold.

  2. If there is an unbounded sequence of ranks RR with sR≤2Rs_R\le 2^R, then 1/21∈𝒜1/21\in\mathcal A.

  3. On ℱ21\mathcal F_{21}, eventually sR>2Rs_R>2^R, the boundary rank R+1R+1 belongs to DR+1D_{R+1}, and (DR,sR)(D_R,s_R) follows one exact affine recurrence.
