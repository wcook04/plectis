Introduction

For a set AA of positive integers and a real number b>1b>1, write XA(b)=∑a∈A1ba−1.X_A(b)=\sum_{a\in A}\frac1{b^a-1}. This series converges by comparison with a geometric series, since (ba−1)−1≤b1−a/(b−1)(b^a-1)^{-1}\le b^{1-a}/(b-1). Erdős proved irrationality for full support at integer bases [5], and later proved the same conclusion for pairwise coprime supports of finite reciprocal sum [7]. On that page he stated that the coprimality assumption could be removed, without giving the details. The question for arbitrary infinite support in base two is Problem #257:

Problem 1.1 (Erdős #257). Is XA(2)X_A(2) irrational for every infinite A⊆ℕ>0A\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{>0}?

We seek conditions inherited by every infinite subset of the support. The first replaces reciprocal summability by a divisibility-sensitive weight. For a=2kma=2^km with mm odd, it uses 1/[m(b2k−1)]1/[m(b^{2^k}-1)] in place of 1/(2km)1/(2^km): a large power of 22 can compensate for many odd cofactors. More generally, fix a finite set PP of primes and write h(a)=∏p∈Ppvp(a)h(a)=\prod_{p\in P}p^{v_p(a)} for the PP-part of aa, where vp(a)v_p(a) is the exponent of pp in aa. The prime powers may grow with aa, but the prime set is fixed.

Theorem 1.2 (a weighted condition on the support). Let b≥2b\ge2 be an integer, let A⊆ℕ>0A\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{>0} be infinite, and let PP be a finite nonempty set of primes. Set h(a)=∏p∈Ppvp(a)h(a)=\prod_{p\in P}p^{v_p(a)}. If Wb,P(A):=∑a∈Ah(a)a(bh(a)−1)<∞,\begin{equation} \label{eq:weighted-fixed-base} W_{b,P}(A):=\sum_{a\in A} \frac{h(a)}{a(b^{h(a)}-1)}<\infty, \end{equation} then XA(b)X_A(b) is irrational. In particular, the base-two condition ∑a∈Ah(a)a(2h(a)−1)<∞\begin{equation*} \label{eq:weighted-return} \sum_{a\in A}\frac{h(a)}{a(2^{h(a)}-1)}<\infty \tag{W} \end{equation*} implies that XA(b)X_A(b) is irrational for every integer b≥2b\ge2. Both conclusions are hereditary under passage to infinite subsets.

Consider the support A⋆={2km:k≥1,m odd,m≤22k}.A_\star=\{2^km:k\ge1,\ m\text{ odd},\ m\le2^{2^k}\}. For a fixed kk, the sum of 1/m1/m over the odd cofactors is of order 2k2^k. Thus the corresponding terms contribute at least 1/41/4 to ∑a∈A⋆1/a\sum_{a\in A_\star}1/a, but at most 21−k2^{1-k} to [eq:weighted-return]. The calculation in Section 2 proves both bounds. Every infinite subset of A⋆A_\star consequently gives an irrational value at every integer base. Since h/(2h−1)≤1h/(2^h-1)\le1, the same theorem also includes reciprocal-summable supports. Section A gives a direct proof of Erdős’s stated extension.

The second condition uses joint divisibility: it bounds fractional powers of divisor counts by positive divisor sums (Theorem 3.1). Neither condition implies the other, but they combine: every infinite subset of E∪VE\cup V has irrational sum when EE satisfies the base-two weighted condition and VV has a summable divisor cover (Theorem 4.1). Two irrational subseries could sum to a rational number, so we must control their errors at the same index. The separating constructions in Proposition 5.1 give such a union outside both individual classes (Corollary 5.2). These are sufficient conditions, not a characterisation of the supports in Problem 1.1.

The proofs compare a multiple of XA(b)X_A(b) with an integer. For a positive integer NN, put Δb,A(N)=∑a∈AbNmod⁡a−1ba−1=(bN−1)XA(b)−Jb,A(N),Jb,A(N)=∑a∈Aa≤N∑j=1⌊N/a⌋bN−ja.\begin{equation} \Delta_{b,A}(N)=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{b^{N\operatorname{mod} a}-1}{b^a-1} =(b^N-1)X_A(b)-J_{b,A}(N),\qquad J_{b,A}(N)=\sum_{\substack{a\in A\\a\le N}} \sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor N/a\rfloor}b^{N-ja}. \label{eq:intro-displacement}\tag{D} \end{equation} Division of NN by each aa gives the identity. At an integer base, Jb,A(N)J_{b,A}(N) is an integer; for infinite AA, the displacement is positive because some a∈Aa\in A exceeds NN. Thus, if XA(b)=p/qX_A(b)=p/q with p∈ℤp\in\mathbb Z and q≥1q\ge1 an integer, then qΔb,A(N)q\Delta_{b,A}(N) is a positive integer and Δb,A(N)≥1/q\Delta_{b,A}(N)\ge1/q. We seek displacements tending to zero; Δb,A(N)\Delta_{b,A}(N) itself is not a fractional part and may exceed 11. Erdős also proposed using fractional parts of powers times the series value [7].

Divisibility cancels finitely many exponents when NN is a multiple of their least common multiple. The remaining terms have small complete-period means when their gcd with the modulus is large. Incomplete-period bounds lack a factor 1/a1/a. Averaging over dyadic lengths restores it: an exponent enters the truncated error only when the sampled range reaches it, and the reciprocals of subsequent lengths form a geometric tail bounded by a multiple of 1/a1/a. Exponents beyond the sampled range are estimated separately. Explicit modulus dependence lets both criteria share this average.

Sections 2–4 prove the three criteria; Sections 5–6 separate their support classes and explain the method’s limits. The appendices give the direct reciprocal-summable proof, finite-denominator results and rational-membership tests. The last of these decide neither 1/21/2 nor 1/211/21.

An example beyond reciprocal summability

Positive divisor majorants

The weighted criterion treats exponents separately. A divisor cover instead uses their joint divisibility. To describe it, write the series in terms of its divisor counts: cA(n)=#{a∈A:a∣n},XA(b)=∑n≥1cA(n)b−n.\begin{equation} c_A(n)=\#\{a\in A:a\mid n\},\qquad X_A(b)=\sum_{n\ge1}c_A(n)b^{-n}. \label{eq:incidence} \end{equation} Expand (ba−1)−1=∑j≥1b−aj(b^a-1)^{-1}=\sum_{j\ge1}b^{-aj} and interchange the nonnegative sums. The selector 𝟏A\mathbf1_A is zero or one, whereas 0≤cA(n)≤τ(n)0\le c_A(n)\le\tau(n), with τ(n)\tau(n) the number of positive divisors of nn. These are power-series coefficients before any carrying; they need not be base-bb digits.

Kaneko–Suzuki–Tachiya [2] require sparsity of the nonzero power-series coefficients. Sparsity of the selected Lambert denominators does not suffice: for any nonempty AA, cA(n)c_A(n) is positive on every multiple of min⁡A\min A. Its nonzero positions therefore have positive lower density, violating those support-counting hypotheses. The criteria do not apply directly to [eq:incidence]. The density calculation and the distinction between their remote-tail average and our displacement appear in Section 1.2 of the companion paper.

Limits of the small-displacement criterion

Small displacement is a sufficient condition for irrationality, not a necessary one. Full support already shows the distinction: Δ2,ℕ>0(N)>(2N−1)∑a>N2−a=1−2−N≥12(N≥1).\Delta_{2,\mathbb{N}_{>0}}(N) >(2^N-1)\sum_{a>N}2^{-a}=1-2^{-N}\ge\frac12 \qquad(N\ge1). The full-support value is nevertheless irrational [5]. Thus a proof for arbitrary support cannot rely on making these displacements tend to zero in every case. Other uses of the displacement, integer linear forms and averaging arguments are not excluded.

Full prime support gives a second obstruction. Although every prime singleton is admitted by both support criteria, for the set of all primes 𝒫\mathcal P one has Δ2,𝒫(N)>1/3\Delta_{2,\mathcal P}(N)>1/3 for every N≥1N\ge1: indeed ∑r≥12−rω(N+r)≥1\sum_{r\ge1}2^{-r}\omega(N+r)\ge1, whereas X𝒫(2)≤∑a≥2(2a−1)−1<2/3X_{\mathcal P}(2)\le\sum_{a\ge2}(2^a-1)^{-1}<2/3. Here ω(n)\omega(n) is the number of distinct prime divisors of nn.

Yet Tao–Teräväinen prove the full-prime case at base 22 [3]. The paragraph following it states extensions to prime support at every integer base and to full prime-power support at base 22, leaving the modifications to the reader. It does not treat arbitrary infinite thinnings. The proposed thinning extension is not a premise of any theorem here.

The logarithmic lower bound is not a converse: replacing a fractional majorant by a logarithmic one need not control averages along the multiples of a prescribed modulus. The counterexample and the bound that remains valid on ordinary initial intervals are proved in Section 13 of the companion paper; those proofs were first worked out in an AI-assisted note of 17 September 2026. These are statements about finite averages; they do not rule out an irrationality criterion based on other uses of logarithmic divisor counts.

For squarefree support, Duverney and Tachiya’s theorem already gives irrationality. It holds at every base 2j2^j, j≥1j\ge1, by Corollary 1.2 and Example 1.1 of Duverney and Tachiya [1]. Their corollary covers the ss-free products of any pairwise coprime sequence of polynomial growth, and they present it as support for the conjecture of Erdős and Graham stated here as Problem 1.1. For the squarefree support with 11 removed, the divisor count is 2ω(n)−12^{\omega(n)}-1, which is odd for n≥2n\ge2. This excludes a first-block-divisibility condition at even bases. Adjoining 11 removes that parity obstruction while changing the sum by a rational number. The argument concerns that normalisation of the certificate, and gives no obstruction to irrationality of the sum. Details and the other known support families appear in the comparison with known support theorems in the companion paper.

A growth-only criterion gives a different class: if A={c1<c2<⋯}A=\{c_1<c_2<\cdots\} and limsup⁡ncn/2n=∞\limsup_n c_n/2^n=\infty, then XA(b)X_A(b) is irrational for every integer b≥2b\ge2 [8]. The same section of the companion paper proves the translation from denominator growth and gives examples showing that this class and reciprocal summability are incomparable. The interval-filling constructions in [9] and the freely chosen lacunary denominators of [16] are not constructions of subseries of these fixed Mersenne weights.

Prime-incidence arguments require additional care as well: multiplying all prime exponents by 22 creates positive correlations between their divisibility indicators. The identity and covariance calculation appear at the end of the companion paper’s discussion of mixed supports. Size conditions alone do not supply that independence.
