Introduction

Two points in the open unit disc are less than 22 apart. The extra condition in the following question is that the whole connecting curve stay where a given polynomial has modulus less than 11. For a monic polynomial ff, write Ωf={z:|f(z)|<1},Kt(f)={z:|f(z)|≤t}.\Omega_f=\{z:|f(z)|<1\},\qquad K_t(f)=\{z:|f(z)|\le t\}. Erdős, Herzog and Piranian [2] asked whether two zeros in the open unit disc could always be connected by a short curve in Ωf\Omega_f. Their question is listed as Problem 1041 in Bloom’s catalogue [1].

Problem 1.1 (Erdős #1041). Let f(z)=∏i=1n(z−zi)f(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-z_i) be monic with n≥2n\ge2 and all ziz_i in the open unit disc. Must two root occurrences be joined by a curve of length less than 22 inside {|f|<1}\{|f|<1\}?

A repeated root allows a constant curve between two occurrences, so we consider distinct zeros. Pendyala [4] proved the degree-four case using a close-pair chord or radial segments through the centre of a smallest enclosing disc. In degree seven the answer is negative. The example was constructed by the erdosproblems.com contributor ani.

Theorem 1.2 (ani’s degree-seven example). The monic polynomial ff in [eq:ani-f] has seven distinct zeros in the open unit disc. Every connected set K⊂ΩfK\subset\Omega_f containing two of its zeros satisfies ℋ1(K)>2\mathcal H^1(K)>2.

Here ℋ1\mathcal H^1 is one-dimensional Hausdorff measure. Applied to a path image, the theorem also rules out a rectifiable path of total variation less than 22.

Five components of Ωf\Omega_f contain one zero each, and the sixth contains two. Thus only the last component can contain a connected set joining two zeros. On this component, ff is a double cover of the value disc, and the sum of the distances from its critical point to its two zeros is slightly greater than 22. Distance estimates alone do not show that the roots nearest to that critical point lie in a common component; Section 6.1 supplies no such component-selection argument.

We cut the value disc from the critical value to the unit circle and confine the whole preimage of the slit to a small disc about the critical point. A connected set joining the zeros must approach this disc on both inverse sheets. Radial projection then gives a lower bound for its measure on each sheet. Adding these bounds subtracts twice the radius of the small disc from the sum of the two root distances; the remaining quantity is greater than 22.

We begin with a quadratic example in which the slit preimage is small but the two zeros can still be joined by a segment of length less than 22. The double-cover estimate identifies the additional inequality needed for the degree-seven example. In Section 3, the root equation for zn+azm+bz^n+az^m+b bounds the polynomial on every segment from the origin to a zero. A polynomial with four nonzero terms shows that this conclusion need not persist. Sections 4 and 5 concern small or isolated critical values. These analytic arguments are independent of the counterexample and the trinomial theorem, and remain unformalised. Full proofs are given in the companion.

For the analytic arguments we use the component-wise Riemann–Hurwitz calculation of Ebenfelt, Khavinson and Shapiro [8] together with Pólya’s area inequality in the forms given by Crane [7]. Inverse branches, slit domains and capacity also occur in Crane’s work on Smale’s mean-value conjecture [17], whose derivative normalisation differs from the monic normalisation here. The companion paper gives the rational tests for the degree-seven polynomial and the full arguments underlying Sections 6–7.

A component with a narrow bottleneck

A quadratic model

Consider p(z)=z2−r2p(z)=z^2-r^2 with 0<r<10<r<1. Its critical point is 00, its critical value is −r2-r^2, and the outward slit in the value disc is J=(−1,−r2]J=(-1,-r^2]. We can compute its preimage without choosing a branch: p−1(J)={iy:|y|<1−r2}.p^{-1}(J)=\{iy:|y|<\sqrt{1-r^2}\}. This vertical interval separates the two inverse sheets containing rr and −r-r. It shrinks to the critical point as r↑1r\uparrow1. Yet the horizontal segment [−r,r][-r,r] stays in {|p|<1}\{|p|<1\} and has length 2r<22r<2. A narrow neck alone therefore does not give the desired counterexample. For the radial-distance estimate below to exceed 22, the two root distances from the critical point must themselves have sum greater than 22.

Trinomials and radial segments

If f(z)=zn+bf(z)=z^n+b has a zero ζ\zeta in the open unit disc, then |b|=|ζ|n<1|b|=|\zeta|^n<1. The identity f(tζ)=b(1−tn)f(t\zeta)=b(1-t^n), 0≤t≤10\le t\le1, keeps the whole segment [0,ζ][0,\zeta] in the lemniscate. A middle monomial does not destroy this argument: the equation f(ζ)=0f(\zeta)=0 lets us eliminate its contribution before taking absolute values. Only bb and −ζn-\zeta^n remain, both of modulus less than one.

Theorem 3.1 (all-degree monic trinomials). Let 1≤m<n1\le m<n and f(z)=zn+azm+b,f(z)=z^n+az^m+b, with every zero in the open unit disc. For any zero ζ\zeta, the entire segment [0,ζ][0,\zeta] lies in {|f|<1}\{|f|<1\}. Distinct zeros ζ1,ζ2\zeta_1,\zeta_2 are therefore joined by the broken line ζ1→0→ζ2\zeta_1\to0\to\zeta_2 of length ∥ζ1∥+∥ζ2∥<2\|\zeta_1\|+\|\zeta_2\|<2 inside the open unit lemniscate.

Proof. Fix a zero ζ\zeta. Eliminating aζma\zeta^m with the root equation gives f(tζ)=b(1−tm)+ζn(tn−tm).f(t\zeta)=b(1-t^m)+\zeta^n(t^n-t^m). Vieta’s formula gives |b|<1|b|<1. For 0≤t<10\le t<1, both 1−tm1-t^m and tm−tnt^m-t^n are nonnegative, and hence |f(tζ)|≤|b|(1−tm)+|ζ|n(tm−tn)<1−tn≤1.|f(t\zeta)|\le |b|(1-t^m)+|\zeta|^n(t^m-t^n)<1-t^n\le1. At t=1t=1 the value is zero. We join any two distinct zeros by concatenating their segments through the origin. ◻

The same calculation has a useful partial-sum form. Write f(z)=∑k=0nakzkf(z)=\sum_{k=0}^n a_kz^k, take a zero ζ\zeta, and put Sj=∑k=0jakζkS_j=\sum_{k=0}^j a_k\zeta^k. Abel summation gives f(tζ)=∑j=0n−1(tj−tj+1)Sj.f(t\zeta)=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(t^j-t^{j+1})S_j. For 0≤t<10\le t<1, the weights are nonnegative and sum to 1−tn1-t^n. Thus f(tζ)/(1−tn)f(t\zeta)/(1-t^n) is a convex combination of the SjS_j. In the trinomial case these are bb and −ζn-\zeta^n. Additional coefficients introduce further partial sums, whose moduli need not be controlled by the root locations. The theorem controls segments ending at zeros; it makes no assertion that the whole lemniscate is star-shaped. A small least critical value

For a squarefree polynomial, put μ=min⁡f′(c)=0|f(c)|>0\mu=\min_{f'(c)=0}|f(c)|>0. We follow a component from its first merger at level μ\mu up to level 11. Suppose that no two distinct zeros can be joined by a curve of length less than 22. Conformal length estimates then give a lower bound for the hyperbolic distances between the zeros. A packing inequality bounds their number from below, and a boundary-length estimate converts this bound into a lower bound for the growth of the component’s area. When the interval of logarithmic levels, of length log⁡(1/μ)\log(1/\mu), is sufficiently large, this contradicts Pólya’s area bound.

The companion gives the ordinary analytic argument and its rational comparison. They yield the following unformalised criterion, with no restriction on the locations of the zeros.

Remark 4.1 (unformalised small-critical-value assertion). Let ff be squarefree and monic of degree n≥2n\ge2, and let μ\mu be its least critical-value modulus. If μ≤13/25\mu\le13/25, then two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1}\{|f|<1\} by a rectifiable curve of length strictly less than 22.

Remark 4.2 (scaling of the preceding assertion). The analytic argument

Suppose that no pair can be connected with length less than 22 in Ωf\Omega_f. At a regular level t∈(μ,1)t\in(\mu,1) we take the component CtC_t containing a chosen first merger. Let k≥2k\ge2 be its number of roots, and put a=Area⁡(Ct)/π≤1a=\operatorname{Area}(C_t)/\pi\le1 and x=log⁡(t/μ)x=\log(t/\mu). Further estimates

Root distances and component geometry

At a non-root critical point, the balance ∑j(c−zj)−1=0\sum_j(c-z_j)^{-1}=0 bounds the ratio of the two smallest root distances by n−1n-1. Comparing their sum with the geometric mean of all nn distances then gives a bound of 2r2r, where rn=∏j|c−zj|r^n=\prod_j|c-z_j|.

When all zeros lie in the open unit disc, a different estimate holds: at every non-root critical point the sum of the distances to the two nearest zeros is strictly less than 22. Both arguments and their precise statements are in the companion’s root-distance estimates. The nearest pair is chosen among all roots of ff. It need not be the pair in the two-root component of the degree-seven example, where containment restricts which pair can be joined.

These distance estimates alone do not imply containment. For the quintic (z−a)(z2+p2)(z2+902901pz+p2),p=9991000,a=901902p,(z-a)(z^2+p^2)\left(z^2+\frac{902}{901}pz+p^2\right), \qquad p=\frac{999}{1000},\quad a=\frac{901}{902}p, all roots lie in the disc and 00 is a non-root critical point, but its unique nearest-root segment leaves the unit lemniscate. Moreover, every root-pair midpoint of z3−(99/100)3z^3-(99/100)^3 lies outside it. This cubic still admits the two-segment connections of Theorem 3.1; it is the straight chords that fail. The exact counterexamples include the escape calculations.

The cubic z3+3z/100−3/4z^3+3z/100-3/4 illustrates the restrictions in our sufficient conditions: its least critical-value modulus exceeds 13/2513/25, while its two normalised critical values are too close for the isolation criterion.

A further cubic has two roots at its first merger but normalised component capacity one at level 2μ2\mu. By that level, all three roots lie in one component. Its capacity is therefore that of the whole filled lemniscate, whose normalisation uses the standard identity cap⁡(Kt(f))=t1/n\operatorname{cap}(K_t(f))=t^{1/n} [23].

Finally, z8−(3/2)zz^8-(3/2)z has a one-root unit-level component with perimeter larger than the proposed constant Γ(1/4)2/(2π)\Gamma(1/4)^2/(2\sqrt\pi). These examples, with full calculations, appear in the companion’s component examples. The last example imposes no restriction on root locations and disproves that specific perimeter constant. The existence of a uniform perimeter constant remains a separate question. Although [21] reports a Runge construction of unbounded one-root perimeters, its proof is unavailable and we do not use it.

Flow constructions and remaining questions

Inverse rays and perturbation

Along a Newton trajectory ż=−f(z)/f′(z)\dot z=-f(z)/f'(z) on a real interval, with f′(z(t))≠0f'(z(t))\ne0, the chain rule gives (f∘z)′=−f∘z(f\circ z)'=-f\circ z. Integrating this scalar equation yields f(z(t))=e−(t−t0)f(z(t0))f(z(t))=e^{-(t-t_0)}f(z(t_0)) for times in that interval. Thus the image in the value plane follows a fixed ray. This identity and the Newtonian graph of Shub, Tischler and Williams are used in the form given by Kozen and Stefánsson [6]. The companion also records local complex-parameter identities for (f∘z)′(f\circ z)' and (etf(z(t)))′(e^t f(z(t)))'. These are pointwise derivative statements; the integration above concerns an existing real-interval trajectory. The companion retains the value and finite-endpoint theorems.

For a finite interval [a,b][a,b], assume the Newton equation only for a<t<ba<t<b and continuity of f(z(t))f(z(t)) up to both endpoints. The interior identity then extends to f(z(b))=ea−bf(z(a))f(z(b))=e^{a-b}f(z(a)), without evaluating f/f′f/f' at an endpoint. Nonzero endpoint values therefore lie on the same positive ray. Critical values with distinct arguments exclude such a connection; distinct moduli are insufficient.

A generic constant perturbation separates rays when the original critical values are distinct, but cannot split equal values. The formal sources give the one-parameter collision locus and the finite affine-line avoidance argument. The companion records an unformalised argument that a linear perturbation can do so generically. Neither qualitative assertion supplies a numerical perturbation bound that preserves a near-extremal path inequality. The forbidden-ray locus calculation and the local logarithmic expansion, with remainder nqN+1/((N+1)(1−q))nq^{N+1}/((N+1)(1-q)) for q<1q<1, specify the available control. The expansion is inapplicable at the distance to the nearest zero.

Inverse-branch prerequisites.

For a nonconstant complex polynomial, each component of {|f|<1}\{|f|<1\} contains a root. The restriction to that component is onto the unit disc and has finite fibres. Over values avoiding the critical values attained in that component, it is a covering map. Choose finitely many nonzero slit starts that include every critical value attained in the component, and remove the outward rays beginning at those starts from the value disc. On the remaining domain, each root in the component determines a unique continuous inverse branch, taking 00 to that root. The archived Lean source states these prerequisites and supplies proof scripts; a kernel-check receipt for this module is not supplied here. The full conformal sheet decomposition, sheet count, monodromy and embedded-tree assertions remain ordinary arguments in the companion; the prerequisites give no length estimate.

The failed tree estimate

For the Cassini polynomial z2−(9/10)2z^2-(9/10)^2, any connected set containing both roots has length at least 9/59/5. The right side of Proposition 12 in the March manuscript [3], even with its proposed arbitrarily small error, is smaller than 41/2541/25 before that error. Thus the proposed spanning-tree estimate is false. At a simple saddle the local level set has four sectors, consistently with the lemniscate graph description in [13]. The Cassini calculation and corrected topological statement separate the metric failure from the valid sheet decomposition.

The full topological reconstruction in that discussion remains unformalised. Cutting outward from critical values to obtain inverse-sheet adjacency is classical; see [16] and [22]. Length control requires additional estimates on the actual pieces and their attachment. An uncut Newton orbit space can even fail to be Hausdorff; it cannot automatically be identified with a finite Reeb graph. The earlier numerical searches in degrees 5,6,8,105,6,8,10 found no counterexample on their tested grids. Such searches neither certify containment along unexamined edges nor prove a quantified statement about all polynomials.

Restricted extremal problems

The companion records the following ordinary compactness argument, which has not been formalised. In the closed lemniscate K1(f)K_1(f), a finite infimum of connecting path lengths is attained. At fixed degree, compactness of the closed root-disc class and lower semicontinuity of this infimum allow uniform upper bounds to pass to coefficient limits. These are statements about the closed lemniscate, as proved in the companion’s compactness argument. The binomial family approaches length 22. The assertion that the universal extremum is at most 22 is refuted by the degree-seven example; its earlier formulation is not a remaining open problem. Quantitative bounds for restricted classes and for varying degree still require further work.

The precise unresolved questions about cut-open flow strips, attachment lengths and quantitative perturbation are stated in the companion’s final questions. The origin-spoke failure for the near-regular-pentagon family has an ordinary, unformalised proof there. The stronger failure asserted in the research addendum [21] for every critical joining point has no available proof and is not used. Likewise the fixed degree-seven proof does not establish the whole small-parameter family.

Related results and verification

Eremenko and Hayman’s bound ℋ1{|p|=1}<9.173deg⁡p\mathcal H^1\{|p|=1\}<9.173\deg p [9] concerns the total boundary length. Their connectedness result, the derivative estimate of Eremenko and Lempert [11], and its equality analysis [12] optimise different quantities from an internal root-to-root path. Kuznetsova and Tkachev [19] study length functions on regular levels; their log-convexity does not imply a uniform one-root perimeter bound at a critical endpoint. Dubinin’s four-point distortion theorem [18] assumes a bound on all critical values and has a different normalisation. Pendyala’s work on Problem 1120 [5] asks for a path from 00 to the unit circle in the intersection of a lemniscate with the closed disc. Those endpoints and that additional region restriction differ from the present question.
