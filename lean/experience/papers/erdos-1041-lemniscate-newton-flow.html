Introduction

Two points in the open unit disc are less than 22 apart. The extra condition in the following question is that the whole connecting curve stay where a given polynomial has modulus less than 11. For a monic polynomial ff, write Ωf={z:|f(z)|<1},Kt(f)={z:|f(z)|≤t}.\Omega_f=\{z:|f(z)|<1\},\qquad K_t(f)=\{z:|f(z)|\le t\}. Erdős, Herzog and Piranian [2] asked whether two zeros in the open unit disc could always be connected by a short curve in Ωf\Omega_f. Their question is listed as Problem 1041 in Bloom’s catalogue [1].

Problem 1.1 (Erdős #1041). Let f(z)=∏i=1n(z−zi)f(z)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(z-z_i) be monic with n≥2n\ge2 and all ziz_i in the open unit disc. Must two root occurrences be joined by a curve of length less than 22 inside {|f|<1}\{|f|<1\}?

A repeated root allows a constant curve between two occurrences, so we consider distinct zeros. Pendyala [4] proved the degree-four case using a close-pair chord or radial segments through the centre of a smallest enclosing disc. In degree seven the answer is negative. The example was constructed by the erdosproblems.com contributor ani.

Theorem 1.2 (ani’s degree-seven example). The monic polynomial ff in [eq:ani-f] has seven distinct zeros in the open unit disc. Every connected set K⊂ΩfK\subset\Omega_f containing two of its zeros satisfies ℋ1(K)>2\mathcal H^1(K)>2.

Here ℋ1\mathcal H^1 is one-dimensional Hausdorff measure. A rectifiable path image has measure at most its total variation, so the theorem excludes short paths as well as more general connected sets.

Five components of Ωf\Omega_f contain one zero each; the sixth contains two and is the only possible place to join a pair. On this component, ff has degree two. Cut the value disc from its critical value to the unit circle. Inside the two-root component, the whole slit preimage lies in a small disc about the critical point. Connectedness forces a root-joining set to approach this disc on both inverse sheets. The two radial projections then give a lower bound: the sum of the root distances from the critical point, minus twice the small disc’s radius. For the chosen pair this bound exceeds 22.

Section 2 contrasts the quadratic with the counterexample; Section 3 treats trinomials and the obstruction introduced by a fourth term. Section 6.1 separates root proximity from containment. Sections 4 and 5 construct paths by component growth and continuation around an isolated critical value, respectively. These independent analytic arguments remain unformalised. The companion paper gives their proofs, the counterexample’s rational data, and the arguments behind the later estimates and questions.

A component with a narrow bottleneck

A quadratic model

Consider p(z)=z2−r2p(z)=z^2-r^2 with 0<r<10<r<1. Its critical point is 00, its critical value is −r2-r^2, and the outward slit in the value disc is J=(−1,−r2]J=(-1,-r^2]. A narrow neck alone therefore does not give the desired counterexample. For the radial-distance estimate below to exceed 22, the two root distances from the critical point must themselves have sum greater than 22.

Trinomials and radial segments

A small least critical value

A small critical value leaves a long interval of levels before the unit lemniscate is reached. For a squarefree polynomial, put μ=min⁡f′(c)=0|f(c)|>0\mu=\min_{f'(c)=0}|f(c)|>0 and follow a component from its first merger at level μ\mu to level 11. If no two roots admit a connection shorter than 22, conformal length estimates force them far apart in the component’s hyperbolic metric. Packing then bounds the root count from below; inverse-ray averaging turns that count into a lower bound for area growth. When log⁡(1/μ)\log(1/\mu) is large enough, this growth contradicts Pólya’s area bound.

The companion proves the analytic reduction and gives the rational comparison. The resulting criterion needs no restriction on root locations, but its analytic proof has not been formalised.

Remark 4.1 (unformalised small-critical-value assertion). Let ff be squarefree and monic of degree n≥2n\ge2, and let μ\mu be its least critical-value modulus. If μ≤13/25\mu\le13/25, then two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1}\{|f|<1\} by a rectifiable curve of length strictly less than 22.

Inverse-branch prerequisites.

For a nonconstant complex polynomial, each component of {|f|<1}\{|f|<1\} contains a root. The restriction to that component is onto the unit disc and has finite fibres. Over values avoiding the critical values attained in that component, it is a covering map. When 00 is not a critical value on the component, choose finitely many nonzero slit starts including its critical values, and remove their outward rays from the value disc. On the remaining domain, each root in the component determines a unique continuous inverse branch, taking 00 to that root. The archived Lean source states these prerequisites and supplies proof scripts; a kernel-check receipt for this module is not supplied here. The full conformal sheet decomposition, sheet count, monodromy and embedded-tree assertions remain ordinary arguments in the companion; the prerequisites give no length estimate.

The failed tree estimate

For z2−(9/10)2z^2-(9/10)^2, the distance between the roots already forces any connected set containing them to have length at least 9/59/5. Proposition 12 of the March manuscript [3] instead proposes a bound below 41/2541/25 plus an arbitrarily small error. Since 41/25<9/541/25<9/5, choosing the error smaller than this gap contradicts the lower bound. The spanning-tree estimate is false. At a simple saddle the local level set has four sectors, consistently with the lemniscate graph description in [13]. The Cassini calculation and corrected topological statement separate the metric failure from the valid sheet decomposition.

The full topological reconstruction in that discussion remains unformalised. Cutting outward from critical values to obtain inverse-sheet adjacency is classical; see [16] and [22]. Length control requires additional estimates on the actual pieces and their attachment. An uncut Newton orbit space can even fail to be Hausdorff, so it cannot be identified with a finite Reeb graph without further argument. Earlier grid searches in degrees 5,6,8,105,6,8,10 found no counterexample, but neither certified containment along unexamined edges nor established a statement for all polynomials.

Restricted extremal problems

The companion records the following ordinary compactness argument, which has not been formalised. In the closed lemniscate K1(f)K_1(f), a finite infimum of connecting path lengths is attained. At fixed degree, compactness of the closed root-disc class and lower semicontinuity of this infimum allow uniform upper bounds to pass to coefficient limits. These are statements about the closed lemniscate, as proved in the companion’s compactness argument. Closed containment survives such limits; strict containment need not. The binomial family approaches length 22, but the degree-seven example rules out 22 as a universal upper bound. That refuted assertion is not a remaining open problem. What remains is to determine quantitative bounds for restricted classes and as the degree varies.

The precise unresolved questions about cut-open flow strips, attachment lengths and quantitative perturbation are stated in the companion’s final questions. The origin-spoke failure for the near-regular-pentagon family has an ordinary, unformalised proof there. The stronger failure asserted in the research addendum [21] for every critical joining point has no available proof and is not used. Likewise the fixed degree-seven proof does not establish the whole small-parameter family.

Related results and verification

Several nearby results concern different geometric quantities. Inverse branches, slit domains and capacity also occur in Crane’s work on Smale’s mean-value conjecture [17], but its derivative normalisation differs from the monic normalisation used here. Eremenko and Hayman’s bound ℋ1{|p|=1}<9.173deg⁡p\mathcal H^1\{|p|=1\}<9.173\deg p [9] concerns total boundary length, not an internal root-to-root path. Their connectedness result, the derivative estimate of Eremenko and Lempert [11], and its equality analysis [12] likewise concern different extremal quantities. Kuznetsova and Tkachev [19] study length functions on regular levels; log-convexity there does not give a uniform one-root perimeter bound at a critical endpoint.

The hypotheses and endpoints also matter. Dubinin’s four-point distortion theorem [18] assumes a bound on all critical values, with a different normalisation. Pendyala’s Problem 1120 result [5] joins 00 to the unit circle inside a lemniscate intersected with the closed disc. Neither the endpoints nor that region restriction are those of Problem 1041.

Verification and reproducibility.

The supplied Lean sources prove the fixed counterexample for preconnected sets, the negation and answer(False) forms of the Formal Conjectures statement, and the total-variation formulation. They also prove the trinomial theorem and the other formally supported estimates identified in the companion’s verification notes. No fresh Lean build or independent human review is reported here, including review of the correspondence with the 1958 wording. The recorded full rational replay on 29 September 2026 reproduced the stopping time and 126126 dual certificates; those are the computational results cited here. The analytic assertions in Remarks 4.1–5.2, the fourth-power mean and the later topological and compactness arguments remain outside the formal conclusions. The evidence record retains the statement identifiers and their conditional dependencies.
