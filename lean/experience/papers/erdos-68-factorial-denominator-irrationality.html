Introduction

Erdős asked whether replacing n!n! by n!−1n!-1 preserves the irrationality of the reciprocal sum [1]. The question appears as Problem 68 in Bloom’s catalogue [2].

Problem 1.1 (Erdős Problem #68). Is S=∑n≥21n!−1S=\sum_{n\ge2}\frac1{n!-1} irrational?

The elementary proof that ee is irrational clears the partial sum by a factorial and bounds the remaining positive tail. Here the first step fails: for Hm=∑n=2m(n!−1)−1H_m=\sum_{n=2}^m(n!-1)^{-1}, already 3!H3=36/53!H_3=36/5. We instead construct forms MS+kMS+k, with M,k∈ℤM,k\in\mathbb Z, by cancelling initial weighted sums. If S=a/qS=a/q and q∣Mq\mid M, then MS+kMS+k is integral; proving it nonintegral excludes that denominator.

At depth four, the weight congruences force 115∣M115\mid M. Restricting the coefficient support to n≥2n\ge2 strengthens this to 1380∣M1380\mid M, and every multiple of 13801380 occurs. We determine the exact factor at every depth D≥2D\ge2 and construct a vector attaining the least positive multiplier.

First allow a coefficient at index one. Theorem 2.1 constructs an integral basis by correcting adjacent factorial differences at proper divisors to isolate each weighted sum. At fixed MM, cancellation determines the lower basis coefficients; removing index one leaves a single equation in the higher coefficients. Theorem 3.1 reduces its infinite gcd to a calculation below 2D22D^2. Bézout coefficients then construct an attaining vector. No assumption on the rationality of SS enters this classification.

Hančl and Tijdeman’s tail-integrality lemma treats factorial series with integer coefficients [4]. Their factorial-scaled partial sums are integers. Here the scaled partial sum need not be integral. Instead, the weight congruences give ℛ−MS∈ℤ\mathcal R-MS\in\mathbb Z [eq:integer-linear-form]; the assumption S=a/qS=a/q and q∣Mq\mid M then makes ℛ\mathcal R integral. Koepf and Schmersau’s factorial-digit criterion [3] is used in the companion’s digit argument to give an equivalent eventual-pattern condition for S−e+2S-e+2. Failure of that pattern at arbitrarily large indices also remains unproved.

Section 4 tests an attaining form; Section 5 gives the general nonintegrality test.

An integral basis

We need integer weights congruent to n!n! modulo d!−1d!-1, with nWd,n−1=Wd,nnW_{d,n-1}=W_{d,n} when n≥2n\ge2 and d∤nd\nmid n. Removing powers of d!d! preserves the residue; the floor exponent gives the required jumps. Put Wd,n=n!(d!)⌊n/d⌋(d≥2,n≥1).W_{d,n}=\frac{n!}{(d!)^{\lfloor n/d\rfloor}} \qquad(d\ge2,\ n\ge1). Writing n=kd+rn=kd+r, 0≤r<d0\le r<d, shows that Wd,nW_{d,n} is r!r! times the multinomial coefficient n!/((d!)kr!)n!/((d!)^k r!). Thus it is an integer, and n!=(d!)kWd,nn!=(d!)^kW_{d,n} gives Wd,n≡n!(mod⁡d!−1)W_{d,n}\equiv n!\pmod{d!-1}. For example, W3,4=4W_{3,4}=4 and (4!−W3,4)/(3!−1)=4(4!-W_{3,4})/(3!-1)=4.

For a prime p≥3p\ge3 the divisor sum is empty, and Up=pep−1−epU_p=pe_{p-1}-e_p. At a composite index the correction terms remove the extra weighted sums. Nonintegrality of the remainder

Fix a vector with V2=⋯=VD=0V_2=\cdots=V_D=0 and M>0M>0, and choose any cutoff N≥DN\ge D at or beyond its support. Separate the signed finite sum from the positive tail: AN=∑d=D+1NVd(λ)d!−1,ℛ(λ)=AN+M(S−HN).A_N=\sum_{d=D+1}^N\frac{V_d(\lambda)}{d!-1},\qquad \mathcal R(\lambda)=A_N+M(S-H_N). The remainder exceeds ANA_N. By [eq:series-tail-bound], it lies below the least integer strictly above ANA_N whenever 2M(N+1)!−1<⌊AN⌋+1−AN.\begin{equation} \frac{2M}{(N+1)!-1}<\lfloor A_N\rfloor+1-A_N. \label{eq:signed-block-gap} \end{equation} Then ⌊AN⌋<ℛ(λ)<⌊AN⌋+1\lfloor A_N\rfloor<\mathcal R(\lambda)<\lfloor A_N\rfloor+1, excluding every denominator dividing MM. The strict successor matters: at an integral ANA_N the gap is one, whereas just below an integer it can be arbitrarily small. Failure of the comparison is inconclusive.

At depth two, fix λ=−6e2+e4\lambda=-6e_2+e_4, with M=12M=12, and vary only the cutoff. At N=4N=4, its finite part A4=−239/115A_4=-239/115 lies just below −2-2, and its gap 9/1159/115 is smaller than the bound 24/11924/119. At N=5N=5, the finite part A5=−27061/13685A_5=-27061/13685 has crossed −2-2: the next integer is now −1-1, and the gap 13376/1368513376/13685 exceeds 24/71924/719. For any fixed vector with M>0M>0, ANA_N increases to ℛ\mathcal R. If ℛ\mathcal R is nonintegral, the gap tends to a positive number and the test eventually succeeds. If ℛ\mathcal R is integral, the eventual gap equals the omitted tail, so the strict upper-bound test fails. Changing the cutoff detects nonintegrality; it neither creates it nor changes MM. Coefficient changes at fixed MM preserve nonintegrality by [eq:residual-transparency].

To exclude every denominator, MM must vary. The companion’s progression construction adds size control: polynomial root conditions give primitive vectors (coefficient gcd one) with 0<M<N!0<M<N!, where NN is their largest supported index. Every fixed qq divides their multipliers for all sufficiently large NN. The tail at that endpoint is below 2/N2/N, but the next-integer gap may shrink too. For each q>0q>0, one still needs a vector with q∣Mq\mid M and a cutoff satisfying [eq:signed-block-gap]; the cutoff may exceed its support endpoint. No minimal-support assertion is made.

The companion treats the equivalent carry condition and the complementary-denominator tail inequality. It also records two larger finite computations: a non-unit carry at m=300000m=300000 excludes every q∣299999!q\mid299999!, and a continued-fraction enclosure excludes every q<239990q<2^{39990}. Both computations were performed outside Lean; their algorithms, exact outputs and scope are given there. The factorial-divisibility exclusion does not exclude all denominators with only small prime factors, whose exponents may be larger. Neither finite restriction establishes the required condition at arbitrarily large indices.
