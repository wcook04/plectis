Further questions

An irrationality argument based on these determinants would also require a bound for their coefficient denominators. After clearing them, the nonzero determinants must still tend to zero; positivity and the growth of the moment weights alone give no such bound. For comparison, the rational-value model in the companion paper has the same formal orders, leading coefficients and type of fixed-base power correction, although its target value is rational. The model and its denominator calculation are retained there with ordinary proofs as unformalised remarks. The companion paper also gives an ordinary argument for the ratio-limit extension under ak+1/ak→ρ∈(0,q−1)a_{k+1}/a_k\to\rho\in(0,q^{-1}), without the polynomial shift bound in Theorem 2.1. That extension remains unformalised.

A different question concerns the polynomial coefficients of the approximants, rather than their remainders. Van Assche’s little-qq-Legendre construction [12] and the multiple-orthogonality construction of Postelmans and Van Assche [13] make the normalisation important. In the 2016 family, the companion paper proves positivity of the first matrix of a coefficient pencil for sizes N≤8N\le8, with real, interlacing roots below F(p)F(p) for p>1p>1. The shifted minors and coefficientwise positivity are supported by the stated finite polynomial computations. The coefficient-moment section gives the definitions and the precise ranges. Positivity for every NN and convergence of the largest root remain open. The general moment criteria of Wang and Zhu [15] and Sokal and Walrad [17], Berg’s factorial-power example [16], and the quadrature construction of Golub and Welsch [18] provide the relevant comparisons. Using a recurrence from another family would require a proof that it holds for these coefficients. The root-of-unity method of Krattenthaler, Rochev, Väänänen and Zudilin [14], for example, concerns a separate construction.

Congruences and divided forms

At 3/23/2, a polynomial QQ of degree at most WW has integral homogenised value HW(Q)=2WQ(3/2)H_W(Q)=2^WQ(3/2). Congruences at powers of 22 and 33 give finite counting criteria for combinations of such values. The companion paper treats integer rescaling and endpoint congruences and signed sums. It also treats the rank-one case and proves the quantitative finite-fibre estimate used below. Equal residues give a useful irrationality form only when the divided remainder is also nonzero and small. Smith normal form describes the image of the evaluated lattice [6], but does not estimate these real remainders.

For example, suppose that MM integral rows (Aj,Bj)(A_j,B_j) have remainders ej=AjF(3/2)−Bje_j=A_jF(3/2)-B_j, and that the subset sums take QQ residue values modulo a positive integer DD. Put T=∑j|ej|T=\sum_j|e_j| and suppose that at most kk subsets give any fixed remainder within a residue class. The least and greatest subset remainders are ∑ej<0ejand∑ej>0ej;\sum_{e_j<0}e_j\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{e_j>0}e_j; their difference is TT. Thus TT bounds the width of the range in each residue class as well. For each positive integer nn, the inequality 2M>Qk(⌊nTD⌋+1)\begin{equation} \label{eq:quantitative-selector-budget} 2^M>Qk\left(\left\lfloor\frac{nT}{D}\right\rfloor+1\right) \end{equation} gives two subsets with the same residue vector whose remainder values differ by an amount strictly between 00 and D/nD/n in absolute value. Indeed, in each residue class divide the range of remainders into half-open intervals of width D/nD/n, starting at its least value. At most ⌊nT/D⌋+1\lfloor nT/D\rfloor+1 intervals are needed, including a last interval when the greatest value is an endpoint. If each interval contained only one remainder value, it would contain at most kk subsets, contrary to [eq:quantitative-selector-budget]. Thus some interval contains two different remainder values; the half-open convention makes their separation strictly less than D/nD/n. Subtracting the two subset sums and dividing their coefficients by DD gives an integral linear form of nonzero absolute value less than 1/n1/n. The quantitative lemma and its proof state the finite-set version without assumptions about Lambert series.

The elementary integer-base method provides a different comparison. Vandehey [10] and Duverney and Tachiya [11] use integer-base expansions. At a noninteger rational base the cleared tails satisfy a recurrence with a growing denominator contribution. The clearing and tail-recurrence calculations in the companion paper rule out one coordinatewise scheme at 3/23/2. They make no assertion about other approximation families. At the function level, Bell and Smertnig [9] exclude Mahler equations for the divisor generating series, while leaving the arithmetic of a single rational argument undecided.

A construction problem.

The unrestricted request for primitive polynomial rows and a signed combination whose divided remainder lies strictly between 00 and 1/n1/n is equivalent to the irrationality of the target. The companion paper retains the full statement and proves this equivalence in its section on divided linear forms. A more specific question must prescribe the approximation family. For the 2004 family one can instead ask whether a parameter direction improves θ*\theta^*. The all-base degree restriction proves an upper bound of 1/21/2 for the degree cutoff. Its hypotheses require one polynomial family, with common leading constants in its degree, coefficient-height and remainder estimates at every fixed real base x>1x>1; the error terms may depend on xx. Merely having estimates at 3/23/2 does not suffice.

Under those hypotheses, the nondecay corollary goes beyond the failure of a sufficient criterion: after denominator clearing, the undivided forms cannot tend to zero at any rational base a/ba/b with b<a<b2b<a<b^2, including 3/23/2, even without a quadratic degree limit. If that limit exists, their absolute values tend to infinity in this strict region. Division by additional base-dependent content and families outside these hypotheses remain unclassified. Equality in the rational-base cutoff is also unresolved by the estimates of Section 4.
