Introduction

For t>1t>1, expansion of each geometric series gives F(t)=∑n≥11tn−1=∑n≥1τ(n)tn,F(t)=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac1{t^n-1} =\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\tau(n)}{t^n}, where τ(n)\tau(n) is the number of positive divisors of nn. With q=1/tq=1/t, this is the qq-harmonic series ∑n≥1qn/(1−qn)\sum_{n\ge1}q^n/(1-q^n). The rearrangement is justified by nonnegativity, and the resulting series converges since τ(n)≤n\tau(n)\le n and ∑n≥1nt−n<∞\sum_{n\ge1}nt^{-n}<\infty.

Chowla conjectured that F(t)F(t) is irrational for every rational t>1t>1, as Erdős records [1]; this is Erdős Problem #1049. The conjecture remains open. We prove a sufficient condition on the numerator and denominator of tt, using Zudilin’s 2004 construction [4]. The condition includes 31/431/4 and every positive integral power of 31/431/4 (proved in Lean), and it excludes 3/23/2 (also proved in Lean).

The extra difficulty at a rational base is the denominator. If U,V∈ℤ[X]U,V\in\mathbb{Z}[X] have degree at most WW, then bWU(a/b)b^WU(a/b) and bWV(a/b)b^WV(a/b) are integers. To prove irrationality by these forms, their positive remainders U(a/b)F(a/b)−V(a/b)U(a/b)F(a/b)-V(a/b) must remain small after multiplication by bWb^W.

A small example shows why cancellation should come first. At 3/23/2, the pair (X+1)(X+2),(X+1)(X+3)(X+1)(X+2),(X+1)(X+3), cleared with degree bound 22, gives (35,45)(35,45). Cancelling X+1X+1 in the polynomial ring and then using degree bound 11 gives (7,9)(7,9). The example is not an approximation to FF; it illustrates how a common polynomial factor changes the required power of the denominator. For Zudilin’s polynomials the corresponding factors are cyclotomic, and their degrees determine the cutoff below.

For the cancelled polynomials Un,VnU_n,V_n used here, put Λn=UnF−Vn\Lambda_n=U_nF-V_n and Wn=deg⁡UnW_n=\deg U_n. We prove deg⁡Vn=Wn−1\deg V_n=W_n-1 and log⁡(bWnΛn(a/b))=(C1log⁡b−C0log⁡a)n2+o(n2),\log\!\bigl(b^{W_n}\Lambda_n(a/b)\bigr) =\bigl(C_1\log b-C_0\log a\bigr)n^2+o(n^2), where C0,C1C_0,C_1 are defined below. If F(a/b)F(a/b) were rational, multiplying by its fixed denominator would give positive integers tending to zero, a contradiction.

A collision must have a small nonzero real remainder

Integer-base methods.

Vandehey treats signed coefficients in the digit argument ; Duverney and Tachiya prove linear-independence refinements [11]. Van Assche gives a little qq-Legendre construction [12]. These integer-base methods are compared in the long record’s related-work section; they are not inputs to the rational specialisation in Section 2.

Coefficient moments.

The coefficient polynomials in [5] give a moment problem different from the remainder moments in Section 3. Long Section 3.3 treats it at fixed ranks only; the all-rank criterion of [15] is not addressed. Write ANA_N and BNB_N for the N×NN\times N Hankel matrices of the coefficients αm,βm\alpha_m,\beta_m in vm*=αmF(p)−βmv_m^*=\alpha_mF(p)-\beta_m, where p=1/qp=1/q. Lean proves the finite-pencil proposition: for real p>1p>1 and every rank N≤8N\le8, ANA_N is positive definite, all roots of det⁡(YAN−BN)\det(YA_N-B_N) are real and less than F(p)F(p), and roots at adjacent ranks interlace non-strictly. It also proves the positivity of the leading Hankel determinants behind the first conclusion, through rank eight at every real p≥1p\ge1. The other finite results are exact computations outside Lean, with their certificate lists and reproduction scripts in the long record: positivity of the shifted Hankel determinants through rank eight, complete polynomial contents through rank five and specified cyclotomic residue tests through rank eight. From these the long record proves a finite moment representation, and it discusses (m!)3(m!)^3 at the endpoint [16], formal continued fractions and finite quadrature [17][18]. None of these results asserts all-rank coefficient positivity or divisibility.

Functional equations.

Bell and Smertnig’s classification [9] excludes kk-Mahler equations for L(z)=∑n≥1τ(n)znL(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}\tau(n)z^n when k≥2k\ge2: there is no polynomial-coefficient relation among L(z),L(zk),…,L(zkd)L(z),L(z^k),\ldots,L(z^{k^d}) with a nonzero coefficient of L(z)L(z). The single-base exclusion is also a Lean theorem, in the form that for every k≥2k\ge2 no finite-dimensional ℚ(z)\mathbb{Q}(z)-subspace of ℚ((z))\mathbb{Q}((z)) containing LL is stable under z↦zkz\mapsto z^k. Rectangular exponent model.

Long Section 10.4 shows that within the rectangular two-function exponent model the threshold never exceeds the classical one-function margin, with equality only at the classical endpoint. Lean proves the bound for the rectangular exponent expression and the equality case for that bound. The bound concerns only that exponent model.
