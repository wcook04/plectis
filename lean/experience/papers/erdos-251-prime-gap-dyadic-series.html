Problem note

Sparse Congruence-Preserving Perturbations of Dyadic Series

Erdős #251

Introduction

Erdős Problem #251 asks whether the sum of the primes weighted by successive powers of 1/21/2 is irrational. Proposition  constructs an interval of dyadic sums while preserving the specified congruences and block statistics; Corollary  applies it to prime gaps. The interval contains both rational and irrational targets, so these properties do not determine rationality.

Finite integer changes add a dyadic rational and cannot change rationality. The construction therefore uses infinitely many corrections, separating their ordinary sum from their dyadic sum. At adjacent coordinates n,n+1n,n+1, the pairs (0,6),(2,4),(4,2),(6,0)(0,6),(2,4),(4,2),(6,0) all have ordinary sum 66, but their dyadic contributions are 6,8,10,126,8,10,12 divided by 2n+22^{n+2}. A third correction at n−1n-1 fixes the residue of the cumulative sum. Placing these triples farther apart leaves almost every fixed-length block unchanged. The choices within the triples then fill an interval of dyadic sums without changing the permitted indices or the congruence cutoffs.

Relation to prior work.

The fixed-denominator question is recorded in [1], [2] and [4]. Neither the variable-denominator counterexample in [18] nor the largest-prime-factor coefficient series in decides it.

The covering argument is established: Crmarić–Kovač treat finite choices; Fridy [6] and Kovač–Tao [7] give generalised-base and reciprocal-choice precedents. Van Doorn–Kovač combine finite subsum covering with divisibility at distinguished denominators [14]; their conclusion represents rational targets by finite subsums, rather than every real target by infinite choices. Van Doorn’s exchange [22] preserves a reciprocal sum; our pair preserves an ordinary sum and varies its dyadic contribution. The contribution here is the combination of covering with sparse nonnegative corrections and eventual congruences for both coefficients and cumulative sums.

A uniform Hardy–Littlewood hypothesis of the type in yields conditional irrationality in Land’s draft [9] and conditional normality in Ringer’s draft [19]. The uniform quantitative prime-pattern estimates in those arguments are not supplied by the present absolute o(1)o(1) block comparison. None of their hypotheses is assumed here. The companion, Section 3, compares the hypotheses and sampling conventions; Section  below retains the further literature references.

The remaining sections ask what information about the actual weighted tails would prove irrationality. Further equivalences, counterexamples and computations are in the companion, Prime-Gap Dyadic Series: Perturbations, Exact Criteria and Certificates.

Nonperiodic coefficients with a rational sum

Eventual periodicity characterises rational binary expansions with digits in {0,1}\{0,1\}, not arbitrary integer coefficients. For example, ∑i=0n−1i−12i+1=−n2n→0,\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{i-1}{2^{i+1}}=-\frac{n}{2^n}\longrightarrow0, although the coefficients are unbounded and hence not eventually periodic. The first coefficient is negative. Positive even coefficients give the same obstruction. Let cn=2(n2+4n+2)c_n=2(n^2+4n+2) and Un=2(n+4)2U_n=2(n+4)^2. Proposition 6.2 (prime gaps do not become periodic). For every positive hh, the actual consecutive-prime-gap sequence is not eventually periodic with period hh.

Lean: prime gaps not eventually periodic.

Proof. An eventually periodic sequence takes only finitely many values and is bounded. Prime gaps are unbounded: for each m≥2m\ge2, the m−1m-1 consecutive integers m!+2,…,m!+mm!+2,\ldots,m!+m are composite. The consecutive primes on either side of this interval therefore have a gap at least mm. ◻

Bounded gaps [15], clusters of each fixed size [12][28], and extreme large gaps [5] do not themselves supply a signed weighted-tail window, even with Polymath’s bound liminf⁡ngn≤246\liminf_n g_n\le246 [28]. The logarithmic depth chosen in the companion, Section 10, makes the explicit remainder tend to zero, but the finite sum still needs a larger margin from the relevant boundary.

For the actual prime tails, separation from the integers is still needed arbitrarily late for every positive shift; is a stronger sufficient condition. The preserved statistics do not supply this step, but primality or sharper rare-event estimates may still be used. The finite certificates do not prove irrationality.

Further comparisons.

The companion, Section 3, treats the broader subsum literature, which is not needed for the construction. Interval-containing multigeometric subsum sets [25] and Cantorvals with the overlap inequality holding on a density-zero set [24] show why sufficient covering conditions are not a classification. The terminology and classification are discussed in [23] and [29]; prescribed inequality patterns need not determine the presence of interior [30], while [31] gives a different sufficient condition requiring an infinite positivity procedure.

Schlage-Puchta’s result concerns each fixed polynomial in a fixed number of consecutive gaps [26]. The automatic-sequence density results of Adamczewski–Drmota–Müllner [21] likewise concern fixed automatic sequences, not growing families of automata or the unbounded gap sequence. They give logarithmic densities in general, and a sufficient condition for natural densities to exist and be rational; the logarithmic densities need not be rational.
