Introduction

Let p0=2,p1=3,…p_0=2,p_1=3,\ldots be the primes and put gn=pn+1−png_n=p_{n+1}-p_n. Erdős asked whether Π=∑n≥0pn2n+1\Pi=\sum_{n\ge0}\frac{p_n}{2^{n+1}} is irrational [1][2] [3]. Summation by parts gives Π=2+∑n≥0gn2−(n+1)\Pi=2+\sum_{n\ge0}g_n2^{-(n+1)}, with convergence justified in Section 4. We study which properties of the gaps survive when the latter sum is changed to a prescribed value.

We prove a perturbation theorem for arbitrary convergent dyadic series with nonnegative integer coefficients. All corrections are supported on one sparse set, chosen before the value of the sum. Their sizes may be eventually bounded by any prescribed function tending to infinity, while the sums still fill an interval. The construction fixes, before choosing the target, a cutoff for each modulus beyond which both the corrections and their partial sums are divisible by that modulus. For prime gaps, the original and altered empirical block distributions have total variation distance tending to zero for lengths o(log⁡log⁡X)o(\log\log X) sampled in [X,2X)[X,2X). The cumulative positions are asymptotic to nlog⁡nn\log n. The irrationality of the actual prime series remains a distinct question: these comparison sequences need not have prime cumulative positions.

We use a standard interval-covering argument for series with finite choices. Fridy’s generalised-base lemma [5] treats prescribed digit bounds and nonincreasing weights. Crmarić and Kovač [17] give the finite-choice form used here, and Kovač and Tao [6] use analogous intervals of reciprocal choices. We must arrange these choices on a sparse set while preserving cumulative congruences. To do so, we redistribute a fixed correction between two adjacent indices. The larger separations make the weighted contributions smaller, so we enlarge the range of each pair until the later contributions fill the gaps between consecutive present choices.

Proposition 1.1 (sparse changes preserving congruences). Let a:ℕ→ℕa:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N} satisfy A=∑n≥0an2−(n+1)<∞A=\sum_{n\ge0}a_n2^{-(n+1)}<\infty, let K∈ℕK\in\mathbb{N}, and let f:ℕ→ℝf:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R} tend to +∞+\infty. There exist a set S⊆[K,∞)S\subseteq[K,\infty) of upper Banach density zero and a nondegenerate interval I⊂(A,∞)I\subset(A,\infty) such that, for every r∈Ir\in I, there is an integer correction e:ℕ→ℕe:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N} satisfying supp⁡e⊆S,en≤f(n)eventually,∑n≥0(an+en)2−(n+1)=r.\operatorname{supp}e\subseteq S,\qquad e_n\le f(n)\ \text{eventually}, \qquad \sum_{n\ge0}(a_n+e_n)2^{-(n+1)}=r. For each integer q≥1q\ge1 there is a cutoff NqN_q, independent of rr, such that q∣enq\mid e_n and q∣∑i<neiq\mid\sum_{i<n}e_i for all n≥Nqn\ge N_q. For every ε>0\varepsilon>0, the choice f(n)=(log⁡(n+3))εf(n)=(\log(n+3))^\varepsilon can be made with |S∩[X,2X)|=Oε(Xlog⁡log⁡X),dTV(μa,X,m,μa+e,X,m)≤m|S∩[X,2X+m)|X.|S\cap[X,2X)|=O_\varepsilon\!\left(\frac{X}{\log\log X}\right), \qquad d_\mathrm{TV}(\mu_{a,X,m},\mu_{a+e,X,m}) \le\frac{m|S\cap[X,2X+m)|}{X}. In particular the distance tends to zero for every integer-valued m=m(X)≥1m=m(X)\ge1 with m(X)=o(log⁡log⁡X)m(X)=o(\log\log X), uniformly over target values.

The set SS, the interval II and every congruence cutoff are fixed before rr is chosen. The correction ee depends on rr; its support may occupy only part of SS. Polynomially growing nonnegative integer sequences satisfy the convergence hypothesis, whereas an=2na_n=2^n does not. The prescribed bound may grow as slowly as log⁡log⁡(n+3)\log\log(n+3). A bounded correction would have to vanish identically: a modulus exceeding the bound makes ee eventually zero, and its constant cumulative sum must then be divisible by every positive integer.

Section 2 gives the construction, and Section 3 applies it to prime gaps. We then identify the actual tails and characterise their rationality by integral shifts. This leads to finite separation tests and to a sufficient condition involving two consecutive small differences. The companion’s literature discussion contains the further comparisons, including finite subsums and other dyadic coefficient sequences.

Application to prime gaps

The elementary bound pn≤1250(n+1)4p_n\le1250(n+1)^4 suffices for convergence of the prime and gap series. We use the central-binomial argument of Erdős [19]; Appendix A of the companion derives this bound, including the prime-power estimate. This supplies the convergence hypothesis needed to apply Proposition 1.1 to the prime gaps.

The prime series and its actual tails

We return to the actual primes and write G=∑n≥0gn2−(n+1)G=\sum_{n\ge0}g_n2^{-(n+1)}. To use a recurrence for these sums, we must retain the endpoint in finite summation by parts and then show that it vanishes. The polynomial bound from Section 3 supplies this limit and convergence of both series.

Lemma 4.3 (the boundary condition for the tail recurrence). Let ∑j≥1|aj|2−j<∞\sum_{j\ge1}|a_j|2^{-j}<\infty and UN+1=2UN−aN+1U_{N+1}=2U_N-a_{N+1}. Then UN=∑j≥1aN+j2−jU_N=\sum_{j\ge1}a_{N+j}2^{-j} for every NN if and only if 2−NUN→02^{-N}U_N\to0.

Integral shifts

For the recurrence UN+1=2UN−aN+1U_{N+1}=2U_N-a_{N+1} with an∈ℤa_n\in\mathbb{Z}, write Dh(N)=UN+h−UND_h(N)=U_{N+h}-U_N. Modulo integers, each step is doubling: UNU_N has the fractional part of 2NU02^NU_0. An integral shift means that a fractional part has returned to an earlier value. Such a return makes 2N(2h−1)U02^N(2^h-1)U_0 an integer and forces rationality when h>0h>0. Conversely, doubling a rational number eventually gives periodic fractional parts. The next theorem records the denominator calculation and the exact quantifiers.

Theorem 4.4 (exact rationality classification). For a real integer-coefficient recurrence, the following are equivalent: U0∈ℚU_0\in\mathbb{Q}; Dh(N)∈ℤD_h(N)\in\mathbb{Z} for some h≥1,N≥0h\ge1,N\ge0; and, for some fixed h≥1h\ge1, Dh(N)∈ℤD_h(N)\in\mathbb{Z} at every sufficiently large NN. More precisely, if U0=u/(2sd)U_0=u/(2^sd) is in lowest terms, with dd odd, then den⁡(UN)=2max⁡(s−N,0)d,Dh(N)∈ℤ⇔N≥sandd∣2h−1(h≥1).\operatorname{den}(U_N)=2^{\max(s-N,0)}d,\qquad D_h(N)\in\mathbb{Z}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ N\ge s\ \text{and}\ d\mid2^h-1 \quad(h\ge1). Consequently U0U_0 is irrational exactly when every positive shift is nonintegral at every index, equivalently when for each fixed h≥1h\ge1 there are arbitrarily late nonintegral shifts.

Proof. Iteration gives UN−2NU0∈ℤ,Dh(N)−2N(2h−1)U0∈ℤ.U_N-2^NU_0\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad D_h(N)-2^N(2^h-1)U_0\in\mathbb{Z}. An integral shift with h≥1h\ge1 makes the nonzero integer multiple 2N(2h−1)U02^N(2^h-1)U_0 integral, and therefore forces U0∈ℚU_0\in\mathbb{Q}. Conversely, write U0=u/(2sd)U_0=u/(2^sd) in lowest terms. Multiplication by 2N2^N cancels exactly min⁡(N,s)\min(N,s) powers of two from the denominator. The remaining factor 2h−12^h-1 is odd, so the second relation is integral exactly when N≥sN\ge s and d∣2h−1d\mid2^h-1. For d>1d>1, Euler’s congruence provides such a shift h=φ(d)h=\varphi(d), and for d=1d=1 we take h=1h=1. This shift works for every N≥sN\ge s. Negating the pointwise and eventual criteria gives the two assertions about irrationality. ◻

The theorem applies to the recurrence itself, without the boundary condition of Lemma 4.3 or any assumption of primality. The eventual periodicity is that of fractional parts, and need not hold for the full tails. For instance, the positive even coefficients 2,4,2,4,…2,4,2,4,\ldots starting at n=1n=1 give tails 8/3,10/38/3,10/3 alternately. Every shift of length 11 is nonintegral and every shift of length 22 is zero. This explains why a single shift length is insufficient.

Small tail differences and finite tests

Fix h≥1h\ge1 and put DN=TN+h−TND_N=T_{N+h}-T_N and δN=gN+h+1−gN+1\delta_N=g_{N+h+1}-g_{N+1}. Then DN+1=2DN−δND_{N+1}=2D_N-\delta_N, where δN\delta_N is even. Two differences of absolute value less than one can both be integral only if they vanish, in which case δN=0\delta_N=0. More precisely, we have the following result.

Proposition 5.1 (two consecutive differences of absolute value less than one). Let D,D′∈ℝD,D'\in\mathbb{R}, δ∈2ℤ\delta\in2\mathbb{Z} and D′=2D−δD'=2D-\delta. The conditions |D|<1|D|<1, |D′|<1|D'|<1 and δ≠0\delta\ne0 hold exactly when, for some s∈{−1,1}s\in\{-1,1\}, δ=2s,12<sD<1.\delta=2s,\qquad \tfrac12<sD<1. In that case sD′∈(−1,0)sD'\in(-1,0) and both DD and D′D' are nonintegral.

Proof. The bounds on D,D′D,D' imply −3<δ<3-3<\delta<3, so a nonzero even δ\delta equals 2s2s with s∈{−1,1}s\in\{-1,1\}. Substituting in D′=2D−2sD'=2D-2s gives 1/2<sD<11/2<sD<1. Conversely this interval gives sD′=2sD−2∈(−1,0)sD'=2sD-2\in(-1,0). ◻

For δ=2\delta=2, the endpoints D=1/2,1D=1/2,1 give D′=−1,0D'=-1,0 respectively. The zero triple D=D′=δ=0D=D'=\delta=0 shows why the mismatch is required.

Problem 5.2 (two small tail differences at arbitrarily large indices). For every integer h≥1h\ge1 and every cutoff N0N_0, exhibit N≥N0N\ge N_0 with |TN+h−TN|<1,|TN+h+1−TN+1|<1,gN+h+1≠gN+1.\begin{equation} \label{eq:smallpair} |T_{N+h}-T_N|<1,\quad |T_{N+h+1}-T_{N+1}|<1,\quad g_{N+h+1}\ne g_{N+1}. \end{equation}

A solution would give a nonintegral shift arbitrarily late for every hh, and hence irrationality by Theorem 4.4. All three conditions must hold at the same index; separate infinite sets of small-difference and mismatch witnesses do not suffice.

By Proposition 5.1, such an index must have δN=±2\delta_N=\pm2. Schlage-Puchta’s lemma [18], applied to xh−x0±2x_h-x_0\pm2, shows that these eligible indices already have density zero. Thus the required witnesses concern a rare event whose relative frequency is not controlled by the absolute block comparison in Proposition 1.1.

Nonperiodic coefficients

The usual periodicity criterion for rational binary expansions assumes digits in {0,1}\{0,1\}. With unrestricted integer coefficients, the following telescoping identity already gives a different behaviour: ∑i=0n−1i−12i+1=−n2n→0.\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}\frac{i-1}{2^{i+1}}=-\frac{n}{2^n}\longrightarrow0. Its coefficients are unbounded and its first coefficient is −1-1. We obtain a positive even example by taking cn=2(n2+4n+2)c_n=2(n^2+4n+2) and Un=2(n+4)2U_n=2(n+4)^2. Direct substitution gives Un+1=2Un−cn+1U_{n+1}=2U_n-c_{n+1}, while the polynomial growth gives 2−nUn→02^{-n}U_n\to0. Lemma 4.3 therefore identifies UU as the complete tail and yields ∑j≥1cj2−j=32,∑n≥0cn2−(n+1)=18.\sum_{j\ge1}c_j2^{-j}=32,\qquad \sum_{n\ge0}c_n2^{-(n+1)}=18. The coefficients increase strictly and cn+1−cn=4n+10c_{n+1}-c_n=4n+10 is never ±2\pm2. The companion’s Section 8 gives further examples, including a bounded nonperiodic sequence in Appendix D. None of these examples constructs consecutive primes.

Proposition 6.1 (prime gaps do not become periodic). For every positive hh, the actual consecutive-prime-gap sequence is not eventually periodic with period hh.

Proof. If the gaps were eventually periodic, they would be bounded. For each m≥2m\ge2, however, the m−1m-1 integers m!+2,…,m!+mm!+2,\ldots,m!+m are composite. The consecutive primes on either side therefore have a gap of at least mm, contradicting boundedness. ◻

The results on bounded prime gaps [13], clusters of each fixed size [11][20], and extreme large gaps [4] do not give the simultaneous inequalities in [eq:smallpair]. This remains so with Polymath’s bound liminf⁡ngn≤246\liminf_n g_n\le246 [20]. For fixed hh and ε>0\varepsilon>0, taking L=⌈(4+ε)log⁡2(N+2)⌉L=\lceil(4+\varepsilon)\log_2(N+2)\rceil in the polynomial remainder gives an error Oh,ε(N−ε)O_{h,\varepsilon}(N^{-\varepsilon}). The finite sum must still be separated from the relevant boundary by more than this error. For the actual prime series, nonintegral shifts are needed arbitrarily late for every positive length. Problem 5.2 would supply them. The perturbation theorem concerns the insufficiency of the specified congruences and block statistics, and leaves possible arguments using primality or quantitative rare-event estimates untouched.
