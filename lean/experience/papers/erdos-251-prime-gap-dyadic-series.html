Sparse Congruence-Preserving Perturbations\\of Dyadic Series

Erdős #251

Introduction

Sparse corrections and local information.

Our main result concerns general summable nonnegative integer words, not only prime gaps. The support and target interval are chosen before the target value. A congruence buffer repairs the cumulative residue; a pair with fixed unweighted total then changes the dyadic value without undoing that repair. Sparsity ensures that almost every sufficiently short block meets no correction.

Relation to prior work.

Erdős’s fixed-denominator question appears in [1], [2] and [4]. Neither his factorial-denominator theorem nor the variable-denominator counterexample in decides it. The bounded prime-factor comparison digits of Erdős–Pomerance [3] are a different dyadic setting.

The closest mechanism for Proposition  is the finite-choice covering lemma of Crmarić–Kovač [20], which they use for product-denominator series. Fridy [6] and Kovač–Tao [7] provide generalised-base and reciprocal-menu antecedents. Van Doorn–Kovač combine interval filling with a chain absorbing every integer divisor [14]. Our factorial moduli use that absorption property. Van Doorn’s reciprocal-preserving exchange is a useful comparison: our pair instead preserves an ordinary sum while varying its dyadic weight. These are method comparisons, not claims that the present perturbation theorem is a special case of those arithmetic statements, or that interval filling is new.

Finite binary menus can have more complicated achievement sets. Central blocks of subsums can give intervals even when elementary term-versus-tail tests alternate [25]; Cantorvals may occur when the overlap inequality an≤∑i>naia_n\le\sum_{i>n}a_i holds only on a density-zero set of indices [24]. See [23] for background, and for Nitecki’s exposition of the Guthrie–Nymann classification. The repaired constructions in [30] show that even a prescribed pattern of strict term-versus-tail inequalities need not determine interior. Nowakowski’s different sufficient condition requires an infinite positivity procedure, not just mixed inequalities . Our proof avoids that classification issue by verifying a covering inequality for every finite menu.

There is a separate boundary concerning prime statistics. Schlage-Puchta’s fixed-block polynomial nonconcentration lemma is not a growing-block distribution theorem. Adamczewski–Drmota–Müllner give effective logarithmic densities for fixed automatic sequences along primes and a criterion for natural densities [21]; this does not describe the unbounded consecutive-gap word or a family of automata growing with XX. In particular, logarithmic densities in their general theorem need not be rational.

Under a uniform Hardy–Littlewood hypothesis of the type stated by Kuperberg [8], Land’s draft proves a conditional irrationality implication and Ringer’s draft a conditional normality implication [19]. The authors supply formalisation material; no independent rebuild of those external projects is claimed here. Ringer also separates weaker averaged comparison inputs from the stronger uniform conjecture. None of these conditional hypotheses is assumed in this note, and no unconditional irrationality claim is made.

What the countermodels exclude

The joint countermodel of Corollary  shows that the stated prefix, congruence, growth, nonconcentration and growing-block properties can hold together while the dyadic value is rational. The obstruction is to deductions from those properties alone, not to arguments using exact primality or more accurate rare-event estimates. The ordinary proof can also place its target interval inside (A,A+η)(A,A+\eta) for any η>0\eta>0: start beyond K′K' with (K′+1)2−K′<η(K'+1)2^{-K'}<\eta and use en≤ne_n\le n from the general schedule.

A simple comparison displays the carry freedom explicitly. Set cn=2(n2+4n+2)c_n=2(n^2+4n+2) and Un=2(n+4)2U_n=2(n+4)^2. Bounded gaps [15], bounded clusters of each fixed size [12][28], and extreme large gaps [5] concern different distributional data. In particular Polymath’s liminf⁡n(pn+1−pn)≤246\liminf_n(p_{n+1}-p_n)\le246 [28] improves the earlier bound 600600; no exact small-gap bound used here supplies a signed weighted-tail window. With the polynomial majorant, L=⌈(4+η)log⁡2(N+h+2)⌉L=\lceil(4+\eta)\log_2(N+h+2)\rceil for fixed hh makes the remainder small. It certifies a window only when the finite sum also has a larger margin from the boundary. A fixed positive margin is a convenient stronger sufficient condition, not a consequence of strict membership in an open window.

The remaining task is a quantitative prime-gap estimate producing the required separation or sparse signed-window witnesses. The construction, classification and finite certificates above do not supply it. The companion includes the free-pair alternative, affine-lattice obstructions, the complete countermodels and separate denominator exclusions. A lower bound on the denominator of a hypothetical rational value is neither a proof of irrationality nor an irrationality-exponent estimate.

Corollary 6.1 (exact irrationality reformulation). The prime-value dyadic series is irrational if and only if the prime-gap dyadic series is. The named equivalence still takes a summability hypothesis which this checkout discharges; neither side is proved irrational.

Proposition 6.2 (prime gaps do not become periodic). For every positive hh, the actual consecutive-prime-gap sequence is not eventually periodic with period hh.
