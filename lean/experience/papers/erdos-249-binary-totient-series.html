Bases and Integral Relations for the $k$-Kernel of Euler's Totient

Erdős #249

A basis and all its relations

Set φ(0)=0\varphi(0)=0. For k≥2k\ge2, the kk-kernel consists of the sequences n↦φ(kjn+r)n\mapsto\varphi(k^jn+r) with j≥0j\ge0 and 0≤r<kj0\le r<k^j. Coons proved that φ\varphi is not kk-regular for any k≥2k\ge2 , and Martin’s Theorem 1 implies that the functions n↦φ(ain+bi)n\mapsto\varphi(a_in+b_i) are linearly independent along pairwise nonproportional affine forms with integer coefficients and positive slopes [4]. The following theorem gives the exact rank, unique integral coordinates, and, in Corollary , all integral relations at every finite level. Section  gives a direct proof of the independence step, with the antecedent of its separation method credited there. No priority claim is made for affine independence.

The evaluation matrix

The two ingredients are scalar reduction and arithmetic independence. The reductions explain which sections must be dependent. The difficulty is to exclude every additional relation. We do this one coefficient at a time: an evaluation will annihilate all the other columns modulo a prime while leaving the chosen column nonzero. Thus no simultaneous primality assertion for several affine forms is needed. The local formula for φ\varphi gives the displayed reductions. For a prime p∣kp\mid k that does not divide uu, the argument kj−tn+uk^{j-t}n+u is a pp-adic unit, so multiplication by ktk^t contributes its power of pp and one factor 1−1/p1-1/p. When p∣up\mid u, the argument already contains pp, and only its power contributes. This gives Ck(t,u)C_k(t,u).

The separation method has an earlier source: Yazdani’s proof of Theorem 2, explicitly credited there to Shallit, uses the Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet’s theorem to distinguish kernel sections modulo a fixed modulus [10]. For a later account see Allouche, Shallit and Yassawi [11]. Here one separates a chosen column from every other column of a finite evaluation matrix. Nonsingularity, not merely distinctness of infinitely many sections, gives linear independence.

For independence, consider finitely many forms Li(n)=ain+biL_i(n)=a_in+b_i with positive slopes and aibj≠ajbia_ib_j\ne a_jb_i for i≠ji\ne j. A finite shift makes the intercepts positive. Write Li=giAiL_i=g_iA_i, where AiA_i is primitive. Choose an odd prime ℓ\ell avoiding the slopes, all giφ(gi)g_i\varphi(g_i), and the nonzero cross determinants. In row ii, choose distinct primes qij≡1(mod⁡ℓ)q_{ij}\equiv1\pmod\ell for j≠ij\ne i, outside the same finite exceptional set, and impose Lj(n)≡0(mod⁡qij),Ai(n)≡2(mod⁡ℓ).L_j(n)\equiv0\pmod{q_{ij}},\qquad A_i(n)\equiv2\pmod\ell. The module here is the one generated by the channels. It is not saturated in the group of all integer-valued sequences: at base 22 and e≥2e\ge2, φ(4n+3)/2\varphi(4n+3)/2 is integer-valued but has canonical coefficient 1/21/2.

The totient is not kk-regular, in the sense of Allouche and Shallit [1], for any k≥2k\ge2: Coons proved that the ℤ\mathbb{Z}-module generated by its kk-kernel is not finitely generated, by counting the poles of ζ(s−1)/ζ(s)\zeta(s-1)/\zeta(s) in the strip 0≤Re⁡s≤10\le\operatorname{Re}s\le1 . For integer-valued sequences this is equivalent to infinite dimension of the rational span: if the span has dimension dd, some dd evaluations map it injectively to ℚd\mathbb{Q}^d and the module into ℤd\mathbb{Z}^d (compare the evaluation argument in [3]). Theorem  therefore recovers Coons’s theorem by a different route and gives the dimension at every finite level.

The generating-function context is stronger than non-regularity alone. For multiplicative coefficients in characteristic zero, Bell and Smertnig prove that kk-Mahler implies kk-regular ; their totient example therefore rules out a kk-Mahler equation for Fφ(z)=∑n≥1φ(n)znF_\varphi(z)=\sum_{n\ge1}\varphi(n)z^n in every base k≥2k\ge2. Bell, Bruin and Coons give the corresponding algebraic and DD-finite classification framework [13]. These are restrictions on functions. Neither classification by itself determines the arithmetic nature of Fφ(1/2)F_\varphi(1/2).

For a kk-regular sequence the difference between a basis of the kernel span and a redundant spanning family is material: matrices associated to a basis compute the growth exponent through their joint spectral radius, whereas a spanning family gives only an upper bound [3]. That theorem does not apply to φ\varphi, but the section maps g(n)↦g(kn+i)g(n)\mapsto g(kn+i) still have an explicit form here. Each sends Fj,rF_{j,r} to Fj+1,kji+rF_{j+1,k^ji+r}, so it carries Vk,eV_{k,e} into Vk,e+1V_{k,e+1}, and no level is invariant under all of them because the dimensions increase. In the canonical bases, a retained section with j≥1j\ge1 goes to a retained section, while F0,0F_{0,0} and F1,0F_{1,0} go to F1,iF_{1,i} and to F2,kiF_{2,ki}, a positive integer multiple of F1,iF_{1,i}. Every element of ℬk,e\mathcal B_{k,e} is therefore sent to a positive integer multiple of an element of ℬk,e+1\mathcal B_{k,e+1}.

Bounded residues and rationality

There is an earlier coefficient-level obstruction: for each m≥3m\ge3, φ(n)mod⁡m\varphi(n)\operatorname{mod} m is not automatic in any integer base k≥2k\ge2 [10]; see also . This does not prove irrationality of the dyadic sum. When m>2m>2 the raw residues are not binary digits, and carrying changes the digit sequence. The residue theorem below instead forces neighbouring coefficients to vanish while retaining one central value, then uses a bounded-tail argument.   1. [exact reformulation] Actual LCM orbit frontier. Let Ha=lcm⁡(1,…,2a)H_a=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,2^a) and Ra=totientTail⁡(2Ha)−totientTail⁡(Ha)R_a=\operatorname{totientTail}(2H_a)-\operatorname{totientTail}(H_a).   2. [unconditional progress] Positive LCM corridor and top-edge boundary. For a≥8a\ge8 and J+(a+6)<2⋅2aJ+(a+6)<2\cdot2^a, the true translated orbit difference totientTail⁡(2Ha+J)−totientTail⁡(Ha+J)\operatorname{totientTail}(2H_a+J)-\operatorname{totientTail}(H_a+J) is strictly positive.   3. [unconditional progress] LCM factor-ideal shift-algebra no-go. For every t≥3t\ge3, with H=lcm⁡(1,…,t)H=\operatorname{lcm}(1,\ldots,t), there is a nonzero synthetic dyadic coboundary whose forcing letters reproduce the actual totient differences at t−2t-2 prescribed indices and lie in the ideal generated by φ(H)\varphi(H). A sparse-anchor variant also preserves every prescribed whole-ray anchor. Hence factor ideals, finite shift-algebra closure and these bounds cannot by themselves force a contradiction. The witness is synthetic: its letters are not claimed to be actual totient differences, nonlinear combinations are outside the theorem, and no cofinal family of certificates follows. finite shift-algebra no-go.

Antecedents and proof record

Allouche and Shallit introduced kk-regular sequences [1]. Coons proved that φ\varphi is not kk-regular ; the exact ranks recover this, as explained after Corollary . Martin’s Theorem 1 makes the ratios of totients on any prescribed finite list of pairwise nonproportional affine forms with integer coefficients and positive slopes arbitrarily separated; this implies their linear independence [4]. Section  gives a direct determinant proof tailored to the sections used here. The basis theorem gives the exact finite-level normal form. Section  treats the residue series at base two, where Wong’s matched-base answer is credited, and classifies dyadic observables. Erdős and Graham record the irrationality of SS as probably hopeless to prove at present [8].

The dyadic finite-level rank 2e+12^e+1, residue-series irrationality, full-depth amplification, canonical equivalence and the positive rank-one floor are in the public snapshot. The unconditional all-base exact-rank theorem is also kernel-checked: its CRT–Dirichlet independence proof does not assume Martin’s theorem. Rank from an independent all-base family remains the reusable conditional factorisation. The canonical gap is the remaining arithmetic endpoint. The historical source links retain 99f4bf47422a.
