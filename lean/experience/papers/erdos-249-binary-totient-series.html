Introduction

Splitting the totient sequence into its even and odd subsequences, then splitting once more, gives seven sequences: φ(n);φ(2n),φ(2n+1);φ(4n),φ(4n+1),φ(4n+2),φ(4n+3).\varphi(n);\qquad \varphi(2n),\ \varphi(2n+1);\qquad \varphi(4n),\ \varphi(4n+1),\ \varphi(4n+2),\ \varphi(4n+3). The identities φ(4n)=2φ(2n),φ(4n+2)=φ(2n+1)\varphi(4n)=2\varphi(2n),\qquad \varphi(4n+2)=\varphi(2n+1) leave five independent sequences. Thus every identity among the seven comes from these two substitutions. More precisely, an integral relation is a unique integral combination of the two displayed relations. We will prove the same statement at every depth and in every integer base.

For an integer k≥2k\ge2, the totient sections are the sequences n↦φ(kjn+r)n\mapsto\varphi(k^jn+r), where j≥0j\ge0 and 0≤r<kj0\le r<k^j. Throughout, nn ranges over ℕ={0,1,2,…}\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\} and φ(0)=0\varphi(0)=0. To describe relations we retain a separate index for each pair (j,r)(j,r), even when two pairs give the same sequence. For example, φ(4n+2)\varphi(4n+2) and φ(2n+1)\varphi(2n+1) are equal sequences but occupy different positions in the list above.

The sections form the kk-kernel of the totient sequence. A sequence is kk-regular when the ℤ\mathbb{Z}-module generated by its kk-kernel is finitely generated [1]. Coons proved that the totient sequence is not kk-regular for any k≥2k\ge2 [2]. Here we determine those modules at each finite level, including their integral relations. The theorem gives the basis and the substitutions; Corollary 3.1 explains how to recover every relation from them. Level zero contains only φ(n)\varphi(n) and has dimension one.

Theorem 1.1 (A basis through each finite level). Let k≥2k\ge2 and e≥1e\ge1 be integers, write Fj,r(k)(n)=φ(kjn+r)F^{(k)}_{j,r}(n)=\varphi(k^jn+r), and put Vk,e=span⁡ℚ{Fj,r(k):0≤j≤e,0≤r<kj}.V_{k,e}=\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Q}} \{\,F^{(k)}_{j,r}:0\le j\le e,\ 0\le r<k^j\,\}. The condition k∤rk\nmid r allows residues with gcd⁡(k,r)>1\gcd(k,r)>1. For instance, r=1r=1 and r=2r=2 are both retained in base six, and the reductions involving them are φ(36n+6)=2φ(6n+1),φ(36n+12)=4φ(6n+2).\varphi(36n+6)=2\varphi(6n+1),\qquad \varphi(36n+12)=4\varphi(6n+2). The factor 22 is already present in 6n+26n+2 but not in 6n+16n+1; only newly introduced prime divisors contribute factors 1−1/p1-1/p. Thus one removes whole factors of kk from the residue, not every common prime factor. Repeated prime factors of kk cause no exception.

Martin’s 2006 theorem on simultaneous totient inequalities already implies independence for every finite family of pairwise nonproportional affine forms with positive integral slopes [4]. Its conclusion is stronger than the independence used here. The point of the present description is the explicit reduction of the sections and the integral relation basis in Corollary 3.1. We also give a direct proof that constructs a nonsingular evaluation matrix. It uses the CRT–Dirichlet separation argument in Yazdani’s proof of Theorem 2, credited there to Shallit [12]. The step that needs care is to keep the progression for the selected affine form reduced while imposing prime divisors on all the others.

The proof in Section 2 separates scalar reduction from independence. Section 3 recovers the integral relations, starting in base six. Section 4 uses prime isolation for bounded functions of totient residues. The series S=∑n≥1φ(n)2nS=\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\varphi(n)}{2^n} has unbounded coefficients. Its irrationality, asked in Erdős Problem #249 [10], remains open. The coefficient basis supplies no irrationality conclusion for this value. Appendix A gives further coefficient consequences; Appendices B and C collect the tail identities, comparison sequences and still-unproved cancellation estimates relevant to SS. Their longer proofs are in The Binary Totient Series.

Proof of the basis theorem

Scalar reductions

The product formula proves spanning. For independence we first separate nonproportional affine arguments, then handle the two proportional arguments nn and knkn separately. The reductions depend only on which prime divisors of kk are already present. For a zero residue, knkn and kjnk^jn have the same prime divisors when n>0n>0. Hence φ(kjn)=kj−1φ(kn)\varphi(k^jn)=k^{j-1}\varphi(kn); both sides vanish at n=0n=0. For an omitted positive residue r=ktur=k^tu with t≥1t\ge1 and k∤uk\nmid u, we have t<jt<j because 0<r<kj0<r<k^j. Put m=kj−tn+um=k^{j-t}n+u. A prime p∣kp\mid k divides mm exactly when it divides uu. The product formula for φ(ktm)/φ(m)\varphi(k^tm)/\varphi(m) therefore contributes ktk^t and one factor 1−1/p1-1/p for each prime p∣kp\mid k absent from uu. The multiplier is an integer: its denominator is a product of distinct primes dividing kk, and therefore divides ktk^t. This gives Ck(t,u)C_k(t,u) and proves that the stated family spans Vk,eV_{k,e}.

For any ordering of pairwise nonproportional affine forms and any C>0C>0, it gives a set of positive lower density on which each successive totient ratio exceeds CC. The two zero-residue sections

The arguments nn and knkn are proportional, so they escape the preceding argument. Bounded functions of totient residues

The matrix argument separates one value from finitely many others. Now we separate one coefficient from all its neighbours in a long window. Boundedness turns that local separation into an irrationality proof: the right zero block forces a rational tail to vanish exactly; the left makes the isolated nonzero term too small to be rational with the same denominator. Residues in the first sum below are least nonnegative representatives, not necessarily binary digits.

Theorem 4.1 (Series of totient residues). For every m≥3m\ge3, ∑n≥1φ(n)mod⁡m2n∉ℚ.\sum_{n\ge1}\frac{\varphi(n)\operatorname{mod} m}{2^n}\notin\mathbb{Q}. Further consequences for the sections

Every finite set of dyadic sections lies in a single finite level. Theorem 1.1 therefore also determines the full kernel; “span” and “relation” here still mean finite linear combinations.

Corollary A.1 (Dyadic basis). The family {φ0,0,φ1,0}∪{φj,r:j≥1,0<r<2j,r odd},φj,r(n)=φ(2jn+r),\{\varphi_{0,0},\varphi_{1,0}\} \cup\{\varphi_{j,r}:j\ge1,\ 0<r<2^j,\ r\text{ odd}\}, \qquad \varphi_{j,r}(n)=\varphi(2^jn+r), is a basis for the rational span of the full dyadic kernel. Every rational relation is generated by the scalar reductions. The complete level-zero truncation has dimension one.

Proof. Each finite subset of this family is contained in some ℬ2,e\mathcal B_{2,e}. Theorem 1.1 gives independence, and its reductions give spanning. At level zero the sole section φ(n)\varphi(n) is nonzero, since φ(1)=1\varphi(1)=1. ◻

The affine argument also permits periodic coefficients by restriction to their common residue classes.

Indeed, φ(n)≤n\varphi(n)\le n gives logarithmic height at most log⁡n\log n, and the height theorem of Adamczewski, Bell and Smertnig would make such a series kk-regular [16]. Bell and Smertnig’s multiplicative classification gives the same conclusion [14]. Bell, Bruin and Coons treat related algebraic and DD-finite series (those satisfying polynomial-coefficient linear differential equations) [15]. These are statements about the function, not its value at 1/21/2.

The section operators Tig(n)=g(kn+i)T_i g(n)=g(kn+i) satisfy TiFj,r=Fj+1,kji+r,Vk,e+1=Vk,e+∑i=0k−1TiVk,e.T_iF_{j,r}=F_{j+1,k^ji+r},\qquad V_{k,e+1}=V_{k,e}+\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}T_iV_{k,e}. Each image is a positive integer multiple of a basis element, but rank growth prevents a finite invariant truncation. Consequently the growth formula using the joint spectral radius for regular sequences [3] does not apply.

The binary totient series

The proof of Theorem 4.1 uses boundedness where a long zero block makes the tail small. For SS, the estimate 0≤φ(n)≤n0\le\varphi(n)\le n proves convergence, but forcing 2L∣φ(N+L)2^L\mid\varphi(N+L) with N+L>1N+L>1 already requires 2L≤N+L−12^L\le N+L-1. The tail bound (N+L+2)2−L(N+L+2)2^{-L} then exceeds 11. This is a failure of the estimate, not a lower bound on the tail. The same divisibility that creates the gap can therefore move it too far out for this bound to be useful. We turn instead to a finite test for differences of scaled tails.

Put RN=∑j≥1φ(N+j)2−jR_N=\sum_{j\ge1}\varphi(N+j)2^{-j} and Δh(N)=RN+h−RN\Delta_h(N)=R_{N+h}-R_N. The tails are then periodic modulo integers, not necessarily equal. To disprove rationality it therefore suffices to find a nonintegral difference beyond every threshold for each positive hh.

For h,N,L∈ℕh,N,L\in\mathbb{N}, define the integer Dh,N,L=∑j=0L−1(φ(N+h+1+j)−φ(N+1+j))2L−1−j,B=N+h+L+2.D_{h,N,L}=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1} \bigl(\varphi(N+h+1+j)-\varphi(N+1+j)\bigr)2^{L-1-j}, \qquad B=N+h+L+2. Equivalent residue conditions

We write K(h,N,L)K(h,N,L) for [eq:r3-certificate].

Theorem B.1 (Propagation of one nonintegral tail difference). Fix d≥1d\ge1 and N≥0N\ge0. If Δd(N)∉ℤ\Delta_d(N)\notin\mathbb{Z}, then every sufficiently late pair {t,t+1}\{t,t+1\} contains an mm such that K(md,N,md)K(md,N,md) holds. Consequently ∃t≥1:K(td,N,td)⇔Δd(N)∉ℤ.\exists t\ge1:\ K(td,N,td) \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Delta_d(N)\notin\mathbb{Z}. Requiring this for every d≥1,N≥0d\ge1,N\ge0 is equivalent to S∉ℚS\notin\mathbb{Q}.

Completeness alone lets the depth grow while the shift stays fixed. Here the two must grow together. The recurrence Δ(t+1)d(N)≡2dΔtd(N)+Δd(N)(mod⁡ℤ)\Delta_{(t+1)d}(N)\equiv2^d\Delta_{td}(N)+\Delta_d(N)\pmod\mathbb{Z} provides the extra step: two consecutive failures would make the fixed nonintegral Δd(N)\Delta_d(N) arbitrarily close to an integer. The estimates are in Appendix A of the companion paper. A single pair d,Nd,N need not imply irrationality; the final assertion requires every positive dd and every N≥0N\ge0.

For a hypothetical denominator 2cv2^cv, a second test uses the modulus M=(2H−1)/vM=(2^H-1)/v, where HH is a positive multiple of φ(v)\varphi(v). Write BH,c=∑j=0H−1φ(c+1+j)2H−1−jB_{H,c}=\sum_{j=0}^{H-1}\varphi(c+1+j)2^{H-1-j}.

Equivalent formulation B.2 (A residue test modulo a divisor of 2H−12^H-1). The series SS is irrational if and only if, for every c≥0c\ge0 and odd v≥1v\ge1, some positive multiple HH of φ(v)\varphi(v) satisfies c+H+1<(−BH,c)mod⁡M<M−(c+H+1).\begin{equation} \label{eq:canonical-gap} c+H+1<(-B_{H,c})\operatorname{mod} M<M-(c+H+1). \end{equation}

The identity BH,c=M(vRc)−ΔH(c)B_{H,c}=M(vR_c)-\Delta_H(c) explains the modulus. Under rationality, vRc∈ℤvR_c\in\mathbb{Z}, so only the small tail difference remains modulo MM. The proof and residue recurrence make this comparison explicit. The required residues have not been shown to exist for every choice of the parameters in either test. Comparison sequences

To turn section rank into irrationality, one would need an upper bound on carry rank under rationality. The following comparison shows why rationality and linear growth alone cannot give it. There is an integer sequence cc with 0≤c(n)≤n0\le c(n)\le n, unchanged odd totients and |c(n)−φ(n)|≤2|c(n)-\varphi(n)|\le2 at even indices, for which ∑c(n)2−n=5/4\sum c(n)2^{-n}=5/4. Its carry is the integer sequence uN=4∑j≥1c(N+j)2−j=5⋅2N−4∑n=1Nc(n)2N−n.u_N=4\sum_{j\ge1}c(N+j)2^{-j} =5\cdot2^N-4\sum_{n=1}^{N}c(n)2^{N-n}. Its recurrence is 2uN−1−uN=4c(N)2u_{N-1}-u_N=4c(N) for N≥1N\ge1. For j≥1j\ge1 and odd 0<r<2j0<r<2^j, this gives 4φ(2jn+r)=2u2jn+r−1−u2jn+r.4\varphi(2^jn+r)=2u_{2^jn+r-1}-u_{2^jn+r}. Thus the 2e−12^e-1 independent odd-residue totient sections lie in the span of the carry sections through level ee. This explains how the rank lower bound survives even though the sum is rational. The construction and its limitations explain why it fails the exact prime-dilation laws of φ\varphi. The same section retains the cofinal LCM-diagonal criterion, its endpoint sign conditions and the limits of finite integer combinations of shifts.

The signed interpolation argument tests whether preserving specified analytic data fixes the value. For each integer β≥2\beta\ge2, sparse even-index corrections realise every value in an interval about ∑φ(n)β−n\sum\varphi(n)\beta^{-n}, with a prescribed prefix and all odd coefficients fixed. Errors are bounded by any prescribed positive f(n)→∞f(n)\to\infty; their support has upper Banach density zero. On each fixed arithmetic progression the Dirichlet-series difference is entire. At roots of unity the generating-function difference extends smoothly radially, with any prescribed finite collection of radial derivatives zero. This preserves Dirichlet singularities, not regular parts or zeros; a finite jet is not the full radial germ. The proof is not formalised in Lean and estimates no cancellation for the unchanged totient sequence.

Rational approximation imposes a different requirement. The identity Θ2:=∑d≥1μ(d)(2d−1)2=S−12\Theta_2:=\sum_{d\ge1}\frac{\mu(d)}{(2^d-1)^2}=S-\frac12 was posted by Amiram Eldar, together with its coprimality interpretation, on 15 March 2026 [8], and by Steve Fan on 16 May 2026 [9]. An exponential-sum question

For every h≥1h\ge1 and every X0X_0, suppose that some integers X≥max⁡(X0,1)X\ge\max(X_0,1) and L≥hL\ge h satisfy 16(2X+h+L+2)≤2L,Re⁡∑N=X2X−1exp⁡(2πiDh,N,L/2L)≤910X.\begin{equation} \label{eq:r3-harmonic} 16(2X+h+L+2)\le2^L,\qquad \operatorname{Re}\sum_{N=X}^{2X-1} \exp(2\pi iD_{h,N,L}/2^L)\le\frac9{10}X. \end{equation} Only a saving in the real part is needed: one must show that the phases cannot all cluster near 11. If every residue failed [eq:r3-certificate], each Dh,N,L/2LD_{h,N,L}/2^L would be within 1/161/16 of an integer. Each exponential would then have real part at least cos⁡(π/8)>9/10\cos(\pi/8)>9/10. Some NN in the block must therefore satisfy [eq:r3-certificate]. For a hypothetical rational value, we choose its period and then a sufficiently late block, contradicting [eq:tail-phase].

To separate the counting estimates from the cancellation estimate, we single out one totient in each window. Fix h,s≥1h,s\ge1, L≥s+hL\ge s+h, X≥1X\ge1 and 0<η<10<\eta<1, and put t=L−s+1t=L-s+1 and EN=exp⁡(2πiDh,N,L/2L)E_N=\exp(2\pi iD_{h,N,L}/2^L). For X≤N<2XX\le N<2X, let pNp_N be the largest prime factor of N+tN+t and mN=(N+t)/pNm_N=(N+t)/p_N. Define 𝒜={N∈[X,2X):mN≤⌊X/2⌋,pN>2⌊X⌋},𝒢={N∈𝒜:φ(mN)≥ηmN}.\begin{aligned} \mathcal A&=\{N\in[X,2X):m_N\le\lfloor\sqrt X/2\rfloor, \ p_N>2\lfloor\sqrt X\rfloor\},\\ \mathcal G&=\{N\in\mathcal A:\varphi(m_N)\ge\eta m_N\}. \end{aligned} All indices are integers. Problem C.1 (Four estimates for the first-harmonic sum). For each h≥1h\ge1, do there exist s≥1s\ge1 and 0<η<10<\eta<1 such that, for every X0X_0, there are X,LX,L with max⁡(X0,1)≤X,h≤L−s,16(2X+h+L+2)≤2L,\max(X_0,1)\le X,\qquad h\le L-s,\qquad 16(2X+h+L+2)\le2^L, for which those four bounds hold?

Fix s,ηs,\eta after hh but before X0X_0; all four estimates must hold on the same block. Take the least L≥s+hL\ge s+h satisfying [eq:r3-harmonic]’s size inequality. The smooth-number and small-cofactor estimates give the last two bounds for all sufficiently large XX when 0<η<1/(300log⁡4)0<\eta<1/(300\log4). The elementary excluded-cofactor estimate is (3ηlog⁡4+o(1))X(3\eta\log4+o(1))X; it needs no prime number theorem. With the prime number theorem as input, the group-mean estimate gives the second bound for s=26s=26, η=1/1000\eta=1/1000 at this depth and all large XX; the mean-subtracted bound remains open. At these fixed parameters it would suffice to prove that bound for arbitrarily large XX: the other three already hold beyond fixed thresholds and hence on those same sufficiently late blocks.

There is also a weaker sufficient condition, without a group-mean input. Since |𝒢|>67X/100|\mathcal G|>67X/100 for large XX, the open bound Re⁡∑N∈𝒢EN≤603X/1000\operatorname{Re}\sum_{N\in\mathcal G}E_N\le603X/1000 would put the average on 𝒢\mathcal G below 9/109/10: 603/1000=(9/10)(67/100)603/1000=(9/10)(67/100). The ordinary comparison argument gives larger first harmonics at all sufficiently large admissible scales, distinguishing its rational sequences from the cancellation required of φ\varphi. Its proof is included with the interpolation construction.

Balasubramanian, Giri and Srivastav give correlation estimates applicable to φ(n)/n\varphi(n)/n, uniformly for |h|≤x/2|h|\le x/2 [11]. Their correlation is a product of two normalised totient values. Here the modulus grows with LL, and wNw_N is an exponential of the other, overlapping totient contributions. Uniformity in the shift of that two-point product does not supply the required weighted exponential estimate.
