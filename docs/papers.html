Erdős #68 Short paper, long record ↓

Integer Linear Forms for a Factorial Reciprocal Series

Which integer coefficient vectors cancel initial weighted sums in factorial-denominator linear forms?

Read online PDF, 9 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

Abstract and reading guide

For S=∑n≥2(n!−1)−1, the paper classifies finitely supported integer vectors that cancel prescribed initial weighted sums in forms MS+k. An integral basis, one Diophantine equation and a finite gcd determine the possible moments M. At cancellation depth four, the least positive moment is 1380. Irrationality by these forms still requires a nonintegral form with q dividing M for every positive q.

Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence

Why does the cubic rate an²/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) force an irrational reciprocal sum?

For strictly increasing positive integers with an²/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3), the reciprocal sum is irrational. Erdős #249 Short paper, long record ↓

Integral Relations among Totient Sections

What explicit basis and integral relations does the totient k-kernel have at every integer base, and why does that not decide Erdős #249?

Read online PDF, 16 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

Abstract and reading guide

For integers k≥2 and e≥1, the totient sections n↦φ(k^j n+r) with 0≤j≤e and 0≤r<kj have an explicit rational basis of dimension ke+1. Every section is an explicit integer multiple of a retained one, and these reductions generate all integral relations, with unique coefficients. The proof combines the local formula for Euler's totient with a nonsingular evaluation matrix obtained from the Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet's theorem. The basis theorem does not decide the irrationality of S=∑ φ(n)2−n.

Explains
the exact finite-level totient-kernel basis, independence proof and integral relations; the long paper develops denominator exclusions and other approaches to the binary series.

Erdős #251 Short paper, long record ↓

Sparse Congruence-Preserving Perturbations of Dyadic Series

Which sparse congruence-preserving corrections can rationalise a dyadic series, and why does that not prove Erdős #251 irrational?

Read online PDF, 15 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

Abstract and reading guide

Sparse nonnegative integer corrections to a convergent dyadic series can attain every target in an interval. One set of upper Banach density zero supports the corrections, which satisfy any prescribed bound tending to infinity and eventually preserve each fixed coefficient and partial-sum congruence. For prime gaps with logarithmic corrections, block distributions remain close for lengths o(log log X), and cumulative positions remain asymptotic to n log n. Those positions need not be prime; the original irrationality question remains open.

the sparse correction construction and its prime-gap application; the long paper develops tail recurrences, local certificates and the limitations of other irrationality methods.

Erdős #257 Short paper, long record ↓

Irrationality criteria for Lambert subseries

Which supports are proved to give irrational Lambert subseries, and what still blocks the arbitrary infinite support that Erdős #257 asks about?

Read online PDF, 27 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

Abstract and reading guide

The series ∑a∈A(ba−1)−1 is irrational for every integer b≥2 and infinite A contained in E∪V when E satisfies a finite-prime weighted summability condition and V has a summable positive divisor cover. The two classes are incomparable, and their union reaches supports in neither class. A common average over dyadic lengths produces one index with both displacements small. Arbitrary infinite supports at base two remain unresolved.

the weighted, divisor-cover and mixed support criteria with complete proofs and method limits; the long paper treats finite denominators, the achievement set and rational membership.

Erdős #269 Short paper, long record ↓

Distinct running least common multiples

Why is the sum over distinct running LCMs of the 5-smooth integers irrational?

Read online PDF, 17 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

Abstract and reading guide

A five-map argument proves irrationality of the reciprocal sum over distinct running least common multiples of the 5-smooth integers. The spacing of powers of five separates the tail images, so rationality would force a periodic jump sequence. Counting running LCMs with multiplicity gives a different number, whose irrationality remains open here.

Erdős #1041 Short paper, long record ↓

Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

How does the degree-seven counterexample obstruct short lemniscate connections, and why do trinomials admit them?

Read online PDF, 19 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

Abstract and reading guide

The degree-seven polynomial constructed by ani has all its zeros in the open unit disc, but every connected subset of {|f|<1} containing two zeros has Hausdorff measure greater than two. The proof confines a slit preimage near a critical point and applies radial projection on its two inverse sheets. For monic trinomials with zeros in the open unit disc, the root equation instead keeps every root-to-origin segment inside the lemniscate.

the degree-seven counterexample and the trinomial connection theorem; the long paper develops critical-value estimates, other subclasses and subsidiary formal checks.

Factorial Linear Forms and Denominators: Detailed Proofs and Rationality Criteria

What growth, valuation, and finite-denominator facts are proved for ∑n≥2(n!−1)−1, and which tail inequalities remain?

Read online PDF, 51 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

The record proves liminf (log LN)/(N3/2 log N) ≥ 2√2/3 for L_N=lcm(2!−1,…,N!−1), by comparing a terminal block of denominators with pairwise gcds rather than an external multiplicity theorem. Specialising a Louwsma–Martino valuation, it exhibits primes 139 and 2593 absent from all later reduced denominators. Two exact calculations give q∤299999! and q≥239990>1012038. These exclusions do not prove irrationality.

Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Which growth hypotheses make a reciprocal sum irrational, and which sufficient conditions force the Sylvester recurrence without settling Erdős #243?

Read online PDF, 61 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. Erdős #251 Reasoning record, short paper ↑

Prime-Gap Dyadic Series: Perturbations, Exact Criteria and Certificates

What exact tail criteria, sparse countermodels, and finite denominator bounds are proved for the prime-gap dyadic series, and why is irrationality still open?

Read online PDF, 47 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

Abstract and reading guide

A convergent dyadic series with nonnegative integer coefficients can be made rational by sparse nonnegative corrections of upper Banach density zero, eventually bounded by any f(n)→∞, while preserving every fixed modulus and, for a logarithmic allowance, short-block empirical distributions. For prime gaps the cumulative positions remain asymptotic to n log n; no primality conclusion is asserted. Summation by parts reduces the prime series to the gap series, and the tails TN=∑j≥1 gN+j 2−j satisfy TN+1=2TN−gN+1. That recurrence characterises rationality and yields finite tests with explicit remainder bounds. An exact finite certificate shows that a rational value of the prime series would have denominator at least 239998>1012040. The bound does not establish irrationality.

Erdős #257 Reasoning record, short paper ↑

Reciprocal Mersenne Subseries

What weighted and cover criteria prove hereditary Mersenne irrationality for Erdős #257, and which targets remain open?

Read online PDF, 153 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

Abstract and reading guide

A weighted summability criterion proves irrationality of reciprocal Mersenne subseries: if h is the part of a supported on a finite nonempty prime set P, every infinite A with ∑ h(a)/(a(bh(a)−1)) finite has irrational ∑ (ba−1)−1. The base-two condition gives the conclusion at every integer base for every infinite subset, and permits an example with divergent reciprocal sum, while excluding full support and all odd exponents. The proof averages over a finite part of the support and over dyadic observation lengths. Erdős's weaker reciprocal-summable theorem is proved in full, and a common window extends both arguments to unions of weighted and positive-cover supports. Finite-denominator and achievement-set calculations formulate tests for the targets 1/2 and 1/21; those tests do not decide either target or the universal question. Prime support at base 2 and squarefree support at power-of-two bases are attributed as existing work.

a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #257: selected checked premises, finite computations, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

Running least common multiples: distinct values and multiplicities

What infinite-rank and tail-arithmetic facts are proved for the three-prime running LCM, and which residue windows remain open?

Read online PDF, 73 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

Abstract and reading guide

The reciprocal three-prime running-LCM kernel has nonsingular minors of every order: row and column rescaling leaves a two-valued matrix, and density supplies the required threshold pattern. The record computes the rank of every finite restriction and the least uniform approximation error over all matrices of finite separated rank. It also gives the full two-prime comparison, following Fan's earlier Hecke–Mahler reduction, and identifies the boundary terms that survive a proposed finite-difference cancellation.

Erdős #1041 Reasoning record, short paper ↑

Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

What path-length, critical-value, and coefficient-family results are proved for polynomial lemniscates, and what remains of the unrestricted length-2 problem?

Read online PDF, 86 pages LaTeX source plectis-erdos on GitHub

Abstract and reading guide

For a monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc, any two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1} by a path of length less than 2. For any squarefree monic polynomial of degree n≥2, an area-growth argument gives such a path when the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/25; scaling gives length less than (5/2)μ1/n inside {|f|<(25/13)μ}. The record develops this argument, an inverse-ray averaging estimate, and a construction using an isolated simple critical value, together with results for collinear roots and several coefficient families. A weighted Poisson identity gives a sharp mean bound on critical values; examples distinguish that bound from path-length control. The final sections examine inverse-sheet topology and refute an earlier spanning-tree estimate. These results do not prove the unrestricted length-2 assertion.

From a Cold Clone to a Proof Receipt

How can a reasoning agent comprehend a large Lean corpus before compiling, then cross into replayable proof authority and incremental validation?

Superseded, and kept here so an existing citation still resolves.

PDF on GitHub, 12 pages LaTeX source in plectis-erdos

Abstract and reading guide

A large formal library can be mechanically exact and still be practically unreadable to the next reader or reasoning agent. At the audited revision a cold clone exposes a bounded six-line tour over 1,019 Lean modules and 153,238 declarations, a map of ten programmes, 101 curated claims, and an elaborated reference graph, none of which requires a build. 503 of those modules and 8,171 of those declarations are marked machine-generated certificate shards, counted as source and never as claims, and that marking is a classification floor rather than the true generated share, since earlier emitted families predate the markers. Navigation receives no proof authority: probe verdicts come from the pinned Lean process and cannot be authored by the agent, and a claim must cite an accepted probe that replay reruns from stored bytes. The audit was author-operated; no external contributor had completed the tour or documented return path by 31 August 2026.

Explains
the agent-navigation architecture: layered corpus projections, bounded intent routing, kernel-authored session receipts, focused builds, and exact dependency-index cache receipts.
Not authority for
