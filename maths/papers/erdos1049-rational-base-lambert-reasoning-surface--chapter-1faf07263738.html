Geometric Moments and Rational Lambert Values: Proofs and Further Results

Erdős #1049 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 81 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. Specialising Zudilin's 2004 linear forms shows F(a/b) irrational for coprime 𝑎 >𝑏 ≥1a>b≥1 when θ=log b / log a is less than θ^*=0.4056830213840605…, with irrationality-exponent bound (1−θ)/(θ*−θ) for every positive integral power. In particular the bound is less than 301 for every positive integral power of 31/431/4. The constants are Zudilin's; the proof cancels common cyclotomic factors before rational specialisation. For the 2016 Hankel construction with x=z=1, the first nonzero term of the normalised determinant is computed at every rank. A separate positive-measure argument proves positivity and a size estimate for each fixed real 0<q<1. The final sections explain the limits of the stated clearing and congruence arguments at 3/23/2: no small nonzero sequence of divided remainders is constructed there, and the full rational-base conjecture remains open.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #1049, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #1049, which remains open.

Related work and comparison of constructions

The relevant comparisons require agreement of both target and construction. A theorem about the Lambert function need not decide a special value; a theorem at integer bases need not survive rational specialisation; and a shared orthogonal-polynomial kernel need not give the same recurrence on different diagonals. The following examples distinguish these three questions.

Functional nonrationality is not value irrationality.

At the level of functions, Rivin proves that if a sequence 𝛾γ and its divisor-sum sequence are both eventually linearly recurrent, then 𝛾γ is finitely supported [24]. His periodic-coefficient corollary therefore shows that

∑𝑛≥1𝑧𝑛1−𝑧𝑛∑n≥1zn1−zn

is not a rational function [24]. This is an exact structural statement about the function underlying Problem #1049, but it gives no irrationality statement for a special value at 𝑧 =1/𝑡z=1/t: a nonrational function may take rational values at particular rational points.

Padé and orthogonal-polynomial constructions.

In 1991 Borwein proved the irrationality of shifted series ∑𝑛≥1(𝑡𝑛 +𝑤)−1∑n≥1(tn+w)−1 at integer bases 𝑡 ≥2t≥2, for every nonzero rational 𝑤w with 𝑤 ≠ −𝑡𝑚w≠−tm for all 𝑚 ≥1m≥1, by Padé approximation rather than by digit clearing; his estimates also show that these values are not Liouville numbers [3]. In 2001 Van Assche recovered the integer-base irrationality and the bound 𝜇irr(𝐹(𝑝)) ≤2𝜋2/(𝜋2 −2) =2.50828…μirr(F(p))≤2π2/(π2−2)=2.50828… using little 𝑞q-Legendre Padé approximants  [18]. His more general Theorem 3 proves irrationality of ∑𝑘≥1(𝑐𝑝𝑘 −1)−1∑k≥1(cpk−1)−1 for an integer 𝑝 >1p>1 and a fixed nonzero rational 𝑐c with 𝑐𝑝𝑘 ≠1cpk≠1 for every 𝑘 ≥1k≥1  [18]; it does not cover a rational noninteger base 𝑡t in 𝐹(𝑡)F(t), because the multiplier needed to write 𝑡𝑘tk over an integer base varies with 𝑘k.

Related series and their different recurrences.

The same Lambert value was already the target of Amdeberhan and Zeilberger’s 𝑞q-WZ construction [5]. Here 𝑝 >1p>1 is the integer-base parameter, 𝑞 =𝑝−1q=p−1, and 𝑃𝑛(𝑥 ∣𝑞)Pn(x∣q) denotes the little 𝑞q-Legendre polynomial used by these authors, normalised by 𝑃𝑛(0 ∣𝑞) =1Pn(0∣q)=1. The two constructions share that bivariate little-𝑞q-Legendre Padé kernel, but Van Assche’s diagonal does not satisfy the Amdeberhan–Zeilberger scalar recurrence. Their Theorems 1 and 2 do prove, respectively, the irrationality of the non-alternating ℎ𝑝(1)hp(1) and alternating 𝑞q-logarithm values for their stated integer parameters, with reported irrationality measure 4.804.80; those are external integer-parameter results and do not transfer to the rational noninteger base 3/23/2 studied here.

Amdeberhan–Zeilberger use the moving diagonal 𝑃𝑛(𝑝𝑛+1 ∣𝑝−1)Pn(pn+1∣p−1), whereas Van Assche uses 𝑃𝑛(𝑝𝑛 ∣𝑝−1)Pn(pn∣p−1). Van Assche also records, citing Borwein’s 1992 Lemma 2, the neighbouring evaluation 𝑃𝑛−1(𝑐𝑝𝑛+1 ∣𝑝−1)Pn−1(cpn+1∣p−1) [18]; see [4]. The shared kernel therefore does not license transfer of recurrence, endpoint, lattice, or valuation claims between the diagonals. Indeed, if 𝐴𝑛(𝑝) =𝑃𝑛(𝑝𝑛 ∣𝑝−1)An(p)=Pn(pn∣p−1), exact substitution at 𝑛 =0n=0 into the Amdeberhan–Zeilberger operator [5] leaves

−𝑝(𝑝−1)2(𝑝+1)(𝑝5+2𝑝4+2𝑝3+2𝑝2+2),−p(p−1)2(p+1)(p5+2p4+2p3+2p2+2),

which is nonzero for every real 𝑝 >1p>1. One nonzero residual is decisive for non-transfer of that recurrence.

Lean checks the exact residual factorisation and its nonvanishing for 𝑝 >1p>1. These are finite statements at 𝑛 =0n=0; they do not supply a recurrence for either diagonal. No recurrence, endpoint, lattice, or valuation statement for one diagonal is used for the other; the displayed exact residual is the sole claim made here about their incompatibility.

Coussement and Smet use little 𝑞q-Jacobi polynomials for further Lambert series whose base parameter is the reciprocal of an integer [19], and Koizumi and Yokoi identify one of their three-parameter Apéry-type approximations with the Coussement–Smet Padé approximants [20]. That identification includes the source’s moving evaluation point 𝑧𝑛 =𝑝𝑛+12zn=p2n+1 and its normalization; it is not an identification of all little-𝑞q-Jacobi diagonals or their scalar recurrences.

At a noninteger rational base, convergence of the rational approximants is not enough. If 𝑃𝑛/𝑄𝑛 →𝜉Pn/Qn→ξ, the cleared error is 𝑄𝑛𝜉 −𝑃𝑛 =𝑄𝑛(𝜉 −𝑃𝑛/𝑄𝑛)Qnξ−Pn=Qn(ξ−Pn/Qn), whose size also depends on 𝑄𝑛Qn. For example, 1/𝑛 →01/n→0, but the corresponding cleared error is always −1−1. Thus convergence alone does not give the small integer linear forms required for irrationality.

In 2013 Vandehey proved that ∑𝑛≥1𝑑(𝑛)𝑎𝑛/𝑏𝑛∑n≥1d(n)an/bn is irrational whenever 𝑏 >1b>1 is an integer and (𝑎𝑛)(an) ranges in a finite integer alphabet excluding zero; taking 𝑎𝑛 =( −1)𝑛an=(−1)n completes the elementary digit method for integer bases 𝑏 ≤ −2b≤−2 [21]. His companion theorem permits a finite alphabet of nonnegative integers containing zero, provided the coefficient sequence is not eventually zero [21]. All of these statements require an integer base. Luca and Tachiya’s periodic-coefficient theorem likewise proves irrationality at every integer base of absolute value greater than one; their full-support example strengthens this to joint linear independence for finite families of iterated divisor functions [22]. Duverney and Tachiya refine the Chowla–Erdős method to linear independence results for Lambert series at an integer base 𝑞q with |𝑞| >1|q|>1 [23]; their Corollary 1.1 generalises Vandehey’s Theorem 1.2 [23]. Each of these results assumes an integer base. For the underlying small-integral-tail criterion, see also Duverney [35]. We use that elementary criterion only as methodological context, not his separate square-index linear-independence argument.

In 2004 Zudilin obtained the uniform bound 𝜇irr(𝐹(𝑝)) ≤2.46497868…μirr(F(p))≤2.46497868… for every integer 𝑝 ≥2p≥2, by way of Heine’s basic transform and a permutation group  [8]. His source theorem also covers negative integer inverse bases; our notation 𝐹(𝑡)F(t) and positive-remainder arguments are restricted to 𝑡 >1t>1. The ordinary-hypergeometric antecedent is Rhin and Viola’s 𝑆5S5 action and twelve-coset denominator reduction for rational forms in 𝜁(2)ζ(2) [17].

These sources suggest looking for common polynomial factors before specialisation. The congruence lemmas in Section 5 describe what the first and last coefficients can imply at 3/23/2. Their hypotheses have not been proved there for either cited coefficient family.

The neighbouring problem of Lambert subseries ∑𝑛∈𝐴(𝑡𝑛 −1)−1∑n∈A(tn−1)−1 over a restricted index set 𝐴A is treated by Kovač and Tao [25].

The rational non-integer progress relevant here is instead the height criterion of Bundschuh and Väänänen [6], which gives the strongest explicit numerical threshold for this value among the previously published sources compared here; Theorem 2.2 enlarges the region that criterion covers. Zudilin’s later Padé-and-Hankel treatment of the generalized 𝑞q-logarithm also comments on non-integral rational bases: it remarks that its results can be given for non-integer 𝑝 =𝑟/𝑠p=r/s with |𝑝| >1|p|>1, under an assumption log⁡|𝑟| >𝑐log⁡|𝑠|log⁡|r|>clog⁡|s| for some computable 𝑐 >0c>0 [9]. No value of 𝑐c is computed there. That remark announces the shape of a rational-base extension for the generalized 𝑞q-logarithm of that paper. Section 2 proves an explicit extension for 𝐹F using the different, 2004 construction. Its polynomial cancellation and denominator calculation show that 𝑐 =𝜇 =2.464978683574975…c=μ=2.464978683574975…, the 2004 bound quoted above, is admissible, and gives the resulting region and its application to 31/431/4.

A third published rational-base region for this same series is Duverney’s Théorème 2, whose hypothesis is log⁡|𝑠|/log⁡|𝑟| <13(1 −3/𝜋2) =0.2320…log⁡|s|/log⁡|r|<13(1−3/π2)=0.2320… [14]; his Théorème 1 for general numerators is weaker still. Both cutoffs lie strictly inside the Bundschuh and Väänänen region. Matala-aho, Väänänen and Zudilin’s combined treatment of 𝑞q-logarithms keeps the integer hypothesis 𝑝 =1/𝑞 ∈ℤ\{0, ±1}p=1/q∈Z\{0,±1} throughout, and states in print that its methods do not sharpen the 𝑞q-harmonic case of [8] [15]. So none of the printed regions for 𝐹F compared here reaches log⁡4/log⁡31log⁡4/log⁡31.
