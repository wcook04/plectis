Short paper

Irrationality criteria for Lambert subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 26 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The series ∑𝑎∈𝐴(𝑏𝑎 −1)−1∑a∈A(ba−1)−1 is irrational for every integer 𝑏 ≥2b≥2 and infinite A contained in E∪V when E satisfies a finite-prime weighted summability condition and V has a summable positive divisor cover. The two classes are incomparable, and their union reaches supports in neither class. A common average over dyadic lengths produces one index with both displacements small. Arbitrary infinite supports at base two remain unresolved.

This paper owns the weighted, divisor-cover and mixed support criteria with complete proofs and method limits; the long paper treats finite denominators, the achievement set and rational membership.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, which remains open.

Rational membership

The preceding results start from a support and prove irrationality. We now fix a target and ask whether any support represents it. The first recurrence is a necessary condition on a rational value; the greedy recurrence later gives an exact membership criterion. Neither supplies the missing arithmetic information for 1/21/2 or 1/211/21.

Rational values and scaled tails

Suppose 𝑋𝐴(2) =𝑝/𝑣XA(2)=p/v, where 𝑝 ∈ℤp∈Z and 𝑣 ≥1v≥1 is an integer. Multiplying the coefficient-series identity by 𝑣2𝑁v2N gives

Separating the first term of the tail then gives 𝑧𝑁+1 =2𝑧𝑁 −𝑣𝑐𝐴(𝑁 +1)zN+1=2zN−vcA(N+1). All the coefficients 𝑐𝐴(𝑛)cA(n) are divisor counts of the same set 𝐴A.

For nonempty 𝐴A, every 𝑎0 =min𝐴a0=minA consecutive integers contain a multiple of 𝑎0a0, so zero runs of 𝑐𝐴cA have length at most 𝑎0 −1a0−1. For infinite 𝐴A, choose 𝑘k exponents and a common multiple 𝐿L. Then 𝑐𝐴(𝐿) ≥𝑘cA(L)≥k, and the scaled tail satisfies ∑𝑟≥1𝑐𝐴(𝐿 −1 +𝑟)2−𝑟 ≥𝑘/2∑r≥1cA(L−1+r)2−r≥k/2. Thus scaled tails are unbounded.

The compatibility condition on the coefficients is expressed by Dirichlet convolution: 𝜇 ∗𝑐𝐴 =𝟏𝐴μ∗cA=1A, where 𝜇μ is the Möbius function. A putative recurrence with integral forcing must therefore satisfy (𝜇 ∗𝑐𝐴)(𝑛) ∈{0,1}(μ∗cA)(n)∈{0,1} for all 𝑛n. The telescoping proof of the integer-recurrence criterion and the Möbius inversion argument are given under Bounds for a general coefficient sequence in the companion paper. The example 𝐴 ={2}A={2} there has value 1/31/3. The correspondence permits finite supports; infinitude remains a separate requirement for a counterexample to Problem 1.1.

The achievement set

Let 𝑤𝑛 =(2𝑛 −1)−1wn=(2n−1)−1 and

A={∑𝑛≥1𝜀𝑛𝑤𝑛:𝜀𝑛∈{0,1}},𝑅𝑁=∑𝑛>𝑁𝑤𝑛.A={∑n≥1εnwn:εn∈{0,1}},RN=∑n>Nwn.

The estimate

2−𝑁<𝑅𝑁≤2−𝑁+234−𝑁<𝑤𝑁(𝑁≥1)2−N<RN≤2−N+234−N<wN(N≥1)

follows by writing 𝑤𝑛 =2−𝑛 +4−𝑛/(1 −2−𝑛)wn=2−n+4−n/(1−2−n) and using 𝑛 ≥𝑁 +1 ≥2n≥N+1≥2 for the middle bound. If two selectors first differ at 𝑛n, their sums differ by at least 𝑤𝑛 −𝑅𝑛 >0wn−Rn>0, proving uniqueness. Since every weight exceeds its tail, Hornich’s theorem [15], as proved by Nitecki [10], shows that the coding image is a Cantor set of measure lim𝑁2𝑁𝑅𝑁limN2NRN; the estimate above gives 2𝑁𝑅𝑁 →12NRN→1, so 𝜆(A) =1λ(A)=1. Concretely, fixing the first 𝑁N digits gives 2𝑁2N disjoint closed intervals of length 𝑅𝑁RN. These interval unions decrease to AA, and continuity of Lebesgue measure from above gives the same limit. Kovač–Tao record this strict-tail inequality and the Cantor conclusion in the fixed-base setting [4]. The geometry section of the companion paper also treats restricted supports. For any allowed set 𝐽J, removing exponents can only decrease the tail of each retained weight, so the same inequality gives injectivity. If 𝐽J omits exactly the finite set 𝐹F, its achievement set has measure 2−|𝐹|2−|F|; if 𝐽J omits infinitely many exponents, that measure is zero. The tail comparison, injectivity and measure dichotomy have separate source statements.

The greedy recurrence

For 𝑥 ≥0x≥0, the greedy rule starts with 𝑟0 =𝑥r0=x and, at rank 𝑛 ≥1n≥1, selects 𝑛n when 𝑟𝑛−1 ≥(2𝑛 −1)−1rn−1≥(2n−1)−1, subtracting that weight if selected. Let 𝐴𝑥Ax be the resulting support and 𝑐𝑥(𝑛) =#{𝑎 ∈𝐴𝑥 :𝑎 ∣𝑛}cx(n)=#{a∈Ax:a∣n}. Set

Theorem C.1 (membership and nonincreasing integer remainders). For every real 𝑥 ≥0x≥0, the following are equivalent:

𝑥∈A;∀𝐾≥0 ∃𝑁≥𝐾:𝑄𝑁+1≤𝑄𝑁;∀𝐾≥0 ∃𝑁∈[𝐾,𝐾+2⌊√𝐾⌋+12):𝑄𝑁+1≤𝑄𝑁.x∈A;∀K≥0 ∃N≥K:QN+1≤QN;∀K≥0 ∃N∈[K,K+2⌊K⌋+12):QN+1≤QN.

since 𝑐𝑥(𝑛) ≤𝜏(𝑛) ≤2√𝑛cx(n)≤τ(n)≤2n and √𝑁+𝑟 ≤√𝑁 +√𝑟 ≤√𝑁 +𝑟N+r≤N+r≤N+r. Put 𝑠 =⌊√𝐾⌋s=⌊K⌋ and 𝑇 =2𝑠 +12T=2s+12. Strict increase at all 𝑇T steps would imply 𝑄𝐾+𝑇 ≥𝑇QK+T≥T. But 𝐾 +𝑇 <(𝑠 +4)2K+T<(s+4)2 gives 𝑄𝐾+𝑇 <2𝑠 +12 =𝑇QK+T<2s+12=T. This proves the window condition and hence cofinal nonincreases.

Conversely, if 𝑥 ∉Ax∉A, then 𝛿 >0δ>0 and 𝑄𝑁 ≥2𝑁𝛿 −1QN≥2Nδ−1, which eventually exceeds 𝑐𝑥(𝑁 +1) ≤𝑁 +1cx(N+1)≤N+1. Equation (10) then makes 𝑄𝑁QN strictly increasing, contradicting cofinal nonincreases. ◻

The nonincreases must occur along the target’s own greedy sequence. For 𝑥 =0x=0, no exponent is selected and 𝑄𝑁 =0QN=0 at every rank. A finite subseries sum is likewise represented and satisfies the criterion. The target 𝑥 =3/4x=3/4 lies strictly between 𝑅1 <2/3R1<2/3 and 𝑤1 =1w1=1, so it is not represented and its integer remainders eventually increase strictly. For 1/21/2 and 1/211/21, the theorem does not establish which alternative occurs.
