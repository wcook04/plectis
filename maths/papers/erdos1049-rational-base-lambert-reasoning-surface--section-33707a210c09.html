Geometric Moments and Rational Lambert Values: Proofs and Further Results

Erdős #1049 By Will Cook September 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 79 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. Specialising Zudilin's 2004 linear forms shows F(a/b) irrational for coprime 𝑎 >𝑏 ≥1a>b≥1 when θ=log b / log a is less than θ^*=0.4056830213840605…, with irrationality-exponent bound (1−θ)/(θ*−θ) for every positive integral power. In particular the bound is less than 301 for every positive integral power of 31/431/4. The constants are Zudilin's; the proof cancels common cyclotomic factors before rational specialisation. For the 2016 Hankel construction with x=z=1, the first nonzero term of the normalised determinant is computed at every rank. A separate positive-measure argument proves positivity and a size estimate for each fixed real 0<q<1. The final sections explain the limits of the stated clearing and congruence arguments at 3/23/2: no small nonzero sequence of divided remainders is constructed there, and the full rational-base conjecture remains open.

A denominator-exponent model for Padé-type forms

For a sum of Padé-type terms, a common denominator must clear each summand. The two exponent expressions below are bounded by (3𝑛2 −𝑛)/2(3n2−n)/2, as their differences factor over ℤZ. We double all exponents to avoid fractions and write ˜𝐸𝑛 =3𝑛2 −𝑛E~n=3n2−n. These are algebraic comparisons only: no approximation family is specified, so integral coefficients and a nonzero remainder estimate would still be needed for an arithmetic application.

Proposition 9.1 (exponent model: summand bound and exact gap). Let ˜𝐸𝑛 =3𝑛2 −𝑛E~n=3n2−n and put

˜𝑃(𝑛,𝑘)=2(𝑘(𝑛−𝑘)+𝑛𝑘)+𝑘(𝑘−1),P~(n,k)=2(k(n−k)+nk)+k(k−1),
˜𝑄(𝑛,𝑚)=2(𝑛2−𝑛)+𝑗2+2𝑗𝑚+𝑗−𝑚2+3𝑚,𝑗=𝑛−𝑚−1.Q~(n,m)=2(n2−n)+j2+2jm+j−m2+3m,j=n−m−1.

Then, for integers 𝑛,𝑘,𝑚n,k,m:

  1. if 0 ≤𝑘 ≤𝑛0≤k≤n, then ˜𝑃(𝑛,𝑘) ≤˜𝐸𝑛P~(n,k)≤E~n, and the gap factors as ˜𝐸𝑛 −˜𝑃(𝑛,𝑘) =(𝑛 −𝑘)(3𝑛 −𝑘 −1)E~n−P~(n,k)=(n−k)(3n−k−1);

  2. ˜𝐸𝑛 −˜𝑄(𝑛,𝑚) =2(𝑛 +𝑚(𝑚 −1))E~n−Q~(n,m)=2(n+m(m−1)) identically;

  3. if 𝑛 ≥0n≥0 and 𝑚 ≥1m≥1, then ˜𝑄(𝑛,𝑚) ≤˜𝐸𝑛Q~(n,m)≤E~n.

Part (1) is the summand exponent bound, part (2) the exact gap identity, and part (3) the maximal exponent bound. The gap in (2) is an identity in ℤ[𝑛,𝑚]Z[n,m], so (3) follows from 𝑚(𝑚 −1) ≥0m(m−1)≥0 and 𝑛 ≥0n≥0. The range 𝑚 ≥1m≥1 is sufficient, not necessary for this polynomial inequality: for integer 𝑚m, one also has 𝑚(𝑚 −1) ≥0m(m−1)≥0 when 𝑚 ≤0m≤0. No claim about a summand of an unspecified approximation is needed.

Example 9.2. At 𝑛 =2n=2 the proposed doubled exponent is ˜𝐸2 =10E~2=10, and ˜𝑃(2,𝑘)P~(2,k) takes the values 0,6,100,6,10 at 𝑘 =0,1,2k=0,1,2. The three gaps are 10,4,010,4,0, matching the factorisation (2 −𝑘)(5 −𝑘)(2−k)(5−k) of part (1); the summand at 𝑘 =2k=2 is the one that saturates the proposed denominator. For part (2), at 𝑚 =1m=1 we have 𝑗 =0j=0 and ˜𝑄(2,1) =6Q~(2,1)=6, with gap 4 =2(2 +1 ⋅0)4=2(2+1⋅0).

These are inequalities between polynomials in the exponents. They establish that the proposed exponent ˜𝐸𝑛E~n dominates the two displayed summand exponent expressions, and nothing further. Positivity of the remainder, its rate of decay, and the comparison of that rate against the denominator height are the analytic obligations, and none of them is treated here, so nothing in this section is an irrationality measure.
