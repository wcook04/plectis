Short paper

Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 21 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For strictly increasing positive integers with 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3), the reciprocal sum is irrational. The argument extracts a cubic polynomial from an integer tail numerator, restricts its coefficients using a square in a cubic field, and excludes the remaining cases modulo seven. The unrestricted Erdős–Graham question remains open.

This paper owns the cubic-rate irrationality theorem and its proof; the long paper develops the separate Sylvester-tail criteria and broader recurrence questions.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

Sources and related work

The companion Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs supplies full proofs and fixed-revision links: Section 2 for regular rates and cubic disagreement; Sections 4–5 for integer tails; Section 12 for the gcd and bounded-increment arguments; Section 6 for LCM results; Section 13 for scalar summability; and Section 14 for open estimates and failed constructions. A final map records dependencies. The auxiliary constructions satisfy only their stated hypotheses, not necessarily those of a rational reciprocal series.

For the earlier integer-remainder method see Erdős–Straus [12]. The companion’s Section 3 distinguishes polynomial Cantor series [13] and the freely selectable series of Problem #270 [15] from the present setting. The Isabelle/HOL work [16] formalises older Erdős–Straus criteria, not Problem #243. General-rate extraction uses the Gamma-ratio asymptotics in [17]; the cubic case uses the explicit 𝐹𝑛Fn.

Indexing of the canonical tails.

Section 2 starts with 𝐶1 =𝑝C1=p, 𝐷1 =𝑞D1=q. Setting 𝑎0 =1a0=1, 𝐷0 =𝑞D0=q, 𝐶0 =𝑝 +𝑞C0=p+q extends the recurrences by one update, but gives neither 𝑎0 >1a0>1 nor strict increase at that step. The zero-based formal cubic theorem instead uses 𝑁N with 𝑎𝑁 >𝑁aN>N, 𝑏𝑛 =𝑛 +1bn=n+1 for 0 ≤𝑛 <𝑁0≤n<N and 𝑏𝑛 =𝑎𝑛bn=an thereafter. This sequence is strictly increasing and changes the reciprocal sum by a rational finite sum without shifting 𝑛n or 3/𝑛3/n on the tail. See the companion’s indexing subsection.
