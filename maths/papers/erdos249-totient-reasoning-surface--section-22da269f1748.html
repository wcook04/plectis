The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Equivalent quantified certificate conditions

Completeness identifies the base residue condition and the LCM-diagonal condition with irrationality. The following statements spell out the quantifiers and the implications between them. The existence of the required certificates beyond every threshold remains unproved.

Theorem 6.47 (The quantified certificate condition is equivalent to irrationality). (∀ℎ :ℕ, 0 <ℎ →∀𝑁0 :ℕ, ∃𝑁 ≥𝑁0, ∃𝐿, C(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿)) ↔𝑆 ∉ℚ(∀h:N, 0<h→∀N0:N, ∃N≥N0, ∃L, C(h,N,L))↔S∉Q. The quantified-condition side is exactly C(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿)C(h,N,L) quantified as ∀ℎ ≥1 ∀𝑁0 ≥0 ∃𝑁 ≥𝑁0 ∃𝐿∀h≥1 ∀N0≥0 ∃N≥N0 ∃L. The universally quantified assertion remains unproved. The equivalence identifies the finite witnesses that suffice; it does not establish their existence beyond every threshold.

Theorem 6.48 (It suffices to use multiples of a period). (∀ℎ0 >0, ∀𝑁0, ∃𝑚 >0, ∃𝑁 ≥𝑁0, ∃𝐿, C(𝑚 ⋅ℎ0,𝑁,𝐿)) →𝑆 ∉ℚ(∀h0>0, ∀N0, ∃m>0, ∃N≥N0, ∃L, C(m⋅h0,N,L))→S∉Q. The shift may be any positive multiple of a prescribed period. This enlarges the choice of finite witness: Theorem 6.47 gives the condition with 𝑚 =1m=1. Conversely, rationality would make all such tail differences integral after a fixed starting index. The displayed implication therefore makes this quantified condition equivalent to irrationality; its truth remains unproved.

Theorem 6.49 (The diagonal condition is equivalent to irrationality). (∀𝑡0 :ℕ, ∃𝑡 ≥𝑡0, ∃𝐿, C(𝐻(𝑡),𝐻(𝑡),𝐿)) ↔𝑆 ∉ℚ(∀t0:N, ∃t≥t0, ∃L, C(H(t),H(t),L))↔S∉Q. Indeed, a prime-power divisor of 𝑗j must exceed 𝑡t, and 𝑗 <2𝑡j<2t leaves no room for a cofactor larger than 11. This classifies the exceptional offsets in this short window; it does not bound their contribution to a weighted sum of totient differences.

Theorem 6.57 (A sufficient quantified finite-grid condition).
