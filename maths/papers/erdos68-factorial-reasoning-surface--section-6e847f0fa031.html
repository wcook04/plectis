Factorial Linear Forms and Denominators: Detailed Proofs and Rationality Criteria

Erdős #68 By Will Cook 1 September 2026; revised 7 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 51 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The record proves liminf (log LN)/(N3/2 log N) ≥2√2/3≥22/3 for L_N=lcm(2!−1,…,N!−1), by comparing a terminal block of denominators with pairwise gcds rather than an external multiplicity theorem. Specialising a Louwsma–Martino valuation, it exhibits primes 139 and 2593 absent from all later reduced denominators. Coefficient constructions give integer forms MS+k; at fixed moment the remainder changes only by an integer. Rationality criteria use factorial digits and the next integer above a scaled partial sum; the digit argument also recovers the irrationality of e. Two exact calculations give q∤299999! and 𝑞 ≥239990 >1012038q≥239990>1012038. These exclusions do not prove irrationality.

Support bounds and nonintegrality

There are three separate choices: the vector, its multiplier 𝑀M, and the cutoff used to estimate its remainder. The basis and support equations classify the first two. Once 𝑀M is fixed, R(𝑐) ≡𝑀(𝑐)𝑆(modℤ)R(c)≡M(c)S(modZ) fixes the fractional part: integer translation may put a representative near zero but cannot change its distance from an integer.

To exclude every rational denominator, each 𝑞 >0q>0 must divide the multiplier of some nonintegral form. Growth of the compulsory divisor 12𝐿𝐷12LD alone is insufficient: its 33-adic valuation is exactly one for every 𝐷D, so it does not even force 9 ∣𝑀9∣M. By contrast, extending the cutoff changes neither 𝑀M nor the remainder, only the finite enclosure used to test it.
