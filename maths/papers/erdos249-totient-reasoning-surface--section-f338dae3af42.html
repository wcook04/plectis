The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Prime divisors of Mersenne factors and finite exclusions

For 𝑋 −2X−2, multiplicative orders supply infinitely many prime divisors among the Mersenne factors. They therefore satisfy the abstract prime-support hypothesis below. This does not imply the required tail-residue inequality. The accompanying finite exclusions concern only their stated periods and basepoints.

Theorem 6.151 (Unbounded prime support in Mersenne factors). Every prime divisor 𝑝p of 2𝑞 −12q−1, for prime 𝑞q, satisfies 𝑞 ∣𝑝 −1q∣p−1 (the order of 22 mod 𝑝p is exactly 𝑞q, by Fermat/Lagrange in (ℤ/𝑝)×(Z/p)×); consequently the prime divisors appearing in the layers {2𝑛 −1}{2n−1} are unbounded, unconditionally, with no cyclotomic resultant hypothesis left open.

Theorem 6.152 (A prime satisfying the stated cyclotomic conditions). For every period ℎ >0h>0 and threshold 𝑁0N0, there exist a prime 𝑞q and a prime factor 𝑝p of |Φℎ𝑞(2)||Φhq(2)| (the binary cyclotomic layer) with 𝑝p coprime to ℎ𝑞hq, ℎ𝑞 ∣𝑝 −1hq∣p−1, and 𝑝 −1 ≥𝑁0p−1≥N0. The characteristic-prime exceptional case in the cyclotomic order decomposition is eliminated directly, by choosing 𝑞 >2ℎq>2h (rules out 𝑝 =𝑞p=q) and 𝑞 >ℎq>h (rules out 𝑝 ∣ℎp∣h).

Observation 6.153. The first theorem uses the multiplicative order of 22 modulo a prime. For the second, choose a prime 𝑞 >max(ℎ,𝑁0,2ℎ)q>max(h,N0,2h) and then a prime divisor of |Φℎ𝑞(2)| >1|Φhq(2)|>1. The cyclotomic order formula, together with the stated inequalities on 𝑞q, excludes prime divisors of ℎ𝑞hq and gives ord𝑝⁡(2) =ℎ𝑞ordp⁡(2)=hq. Existence of arbitrarily large primes suffices here; no prime-in-progression theorem is needed for this construction.

These results supply prime divisors with the required orders. They do not supply the tail-residue inequality at the resulting periods. The all-prime and eventual forms of the order hypothesis must also be distinguished, as explained in §6.6.12.
