Short paper

Irrationality criteria for Lambert subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 26 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The series ∑𝑎∈𝐴(𝑏𝑎 −1)−1∑a∈A(ba−1)−1 is irrational for every integer 𝑏 ≥2b≥2 and infinite A contained in E∪V when E satisfies a finite-prime weighted summability condition and V has a summable positive divisor cover. The two classes are incomparable, and their union reaches supports in neither class. A common average over dyadic lengths produces one index with both displacements small. Arbitrary infinite supports at base two remain unresolved.

This paper owns the weighted, divisor-cover and mixed support criteria with complete proofs and method limits; the long paper treats finite denominators, the achievement set and rational membership.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, which remains open.

A strict enlargement and finite unions

The mixed theorem now applies to a support outside both individual classes.

Corollary 5.2 (a support requiring the mixed criterion). There is an infinite positive support 𝑈U with 𝑈 ∉CU∉C and 𝑈 ∉W𝑏U∉Wb for every integer 𝑏 ≥2b≥2, such that 𝑋𝐴(𝑏)XA(b) is irrational for every infinite 𝐴 ⊆𝑈A⊆U and every integer 𝑏 ≥2b≥2.

Proof. Let 𝑈 =𝐸 ∪𝑉U=E∪V, with 𝐸,𝑉E,V as in Proposition 5.1. A cover of 𝑈U is also a cover of 𝐸E, while convergence of the weighted sum over 𝑈U would imply convergence of the sum over 𝑉V for the same prime set, since all summands are nonnegative. Thus 𝑈U belongs to neither individual class. Theorem 4.1 gives irrationality for every infinite subset of 𝑈U at every integer base 𝑏 ≥2b≥2. ◻

Weighted supports are also closed under finite unions: take the union of their prime sets: ℎ𝑃(𝑎)hP(a) can only increase, and ℎ/(𝑏ℎ −1)h/(bh−1) decreases with ℎh. Both criteria are hereditary and admit finite supports. Their mixed class is therefore closed under finite unions and finite changes, already with the fixed dyadic weights in (V). Countable unions need not remain in the mixed class: every prime singleton is admitted, but full prime support fails the small-displacement condition, as shown in Section 6.
