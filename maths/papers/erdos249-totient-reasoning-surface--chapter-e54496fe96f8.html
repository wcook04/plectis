Long paper

The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Reduced coefficients

Reduction modulo 𝑚m restores the boundedness missing from 𝜑φ. The short paper isolates a prescribed nonzero coefficient between two long zero blocks: a hypothetical rational denominator forces the right tail to vanish, after which the left block makes the nonzero centre too small. CRT creates the blocks by imposing a separate prime divisor congruent to 1(mod𝑚)1(modm) at each neighbour. We extend that proof in two directions. Changing the integer base changes only the geometric tail bound. Combining dilations is subtler: one must first ensure that their contributions cannot cancel at the chosen centre.

Proposition 1.10 (Residue series in every integer base). Let 𝑡 ≥2t≥2 be an integer.

  1. For every 𝑚 ≥3m≥3, ∑𝑛≥1(𝜑(𝑛)mod𝑚) 𝑡−𝑛∑n≥1(φ(n)modm)t−n is irrational (radix_residue_series_irrational).

  2. For 𝑘 ≥1k≥1 and 𝑓 :ℤ/2𝑘ℤ →ℚf:Z/2kZ→Q, the series ∑𝑛≥1𝑓(𝜑(𝑛)mod2𝑘) 𝑡−𝑛∑n≥1f(φ(n)mod2k)t−n is rational exactly when 𝑓f is constant on the even residue classes, and its value is then (𝑡 +1)𝑓(1)/𝑡2 +𝑓(0)/(𝑡2(𝑡 −1))(t+1)f(1)/t2+f(0)/(t2(t−1)) (rational_zmod_radix_observable_iff, positiveRadixValue_eq_of_even_constant).

Proof. For coefficients bounded by 𝐶C, the right-block tail is at most 𝐶𝑡−𝐿/(𝑡 −1) ≤𝐶𝑡−𝐿Ct−L/(t−1)≤Ct−L. Choose 𝐿L with 𝐶𝑡−𝐿 <1/𝑞Ct−L<1/q; the rational-tail lattice forces that tail to be zero. The left-block tail is then the isolated coefficient times 𝑡−𝐿−1t−L−1, giving the same contradiction as in the short paper. In (b), the two exceptional terms contribute (𝑡 +1)𝑓(1)/𝑡2(t+1)f(1)/t2 and the constant tail contributes 𝑓(0)/(𝑡2(𝑡 −1))f(0)/(t2(t−1)), since all totients after 𝑛 =2n=2 are even. ◻

For example, in base 22 the function modulo 44 with 𝑓(0) =𝑓(2) =5f(0)=f(2)=5, 𝑓(1) =7f(1)=7 and 𝑓(3) = −2f(3)=−2 gives the rational value 3 ⋅7/4 +5/4 =13/23⋅7/4+5/4=13/2: the class 33 never occurs. In contrast, the indicator of the class 2(mod4)2(mod4) gives an irrational value. This classification uses the fact that the prime centres 𝑠 −1s−1, with 𝑠s a unit, cover every even class modulo 2𝑘2k. Modulo 66 they cover only 00 and 44. The least-residue assertion needs one nonzero accessible centre, but the same argument does not classify all rational-valued functions for a general modulus.

    𝑐+∑𝑑∈𝐷∑𝑛≥1𝑓𝑑(𝜑(𝑛)mod2𝑘𝑑)𝑡−𝑑𝑛c+∑d∈D∑n≥1fd(φ(n)mod2kd)t−dn

    is rational exactly when every 𝑓𝑑fd is constant on the even residue classes (rational_mixed_moduli_with_constant_iff).

Proof. (a) A relation gives integers 𝑐0c0 and 𝑐𝑑cd, 𝑑d in a finite set 𝐷D of positive integers, with some 𝑐𝑑 ≠0cd≠0 and 𝑐0 +∑𝑁≥1𝑏𝑁𝑡−𝑁 =0c0+∑N≥1bNt−N=0, where 𝑏𝑁 =∑𝑑∈𝐷,𝑑∣𝑁𝑐𝑑(𝜑(𝑁/𝑑)mod𝑚)bN=∑d∈D,d∣Ncd(φ(N/d)modm) is bounded. Discard the 𝑑d with 𝑐𝑑 =0cd=0 and let 𝛿δ be the least remaining one. Different dilations can contribute to the same exponent 𝑁N, so isolating a prime in one summand is not enough. Choose instead 𝑁 =𝛿𝑝N=δp with 𝑝 >max𝐷p>maxD prime. A contributing 𝑑d divides 𝛿𝑝δp; since 𝑝 ∤𝑑p∤d, it divides 𝛿δ. Minimality then forces 𝑑 =𝛿d=δ. The centre now receives exactly one active contribution, while CRT will remove all contributions at its neighbours.

Given 𝐿L, choose distinct primes 𝑞𝑗 ≡1(mod𝑚)qj≡1(modm), 0 <|𝑗| ≤𝐿0<|j|≤L, each larger than 𝐿L and than every element of 𝐷D. Impose

The inverse exists because 𝑞𝑗 >𝛿qj>δ, and the latter residue is nonzero because 𝑞𝑗 >|𝑗|qj>|j|. Together with the unit residue −1−1 modulo 𝑚m, these congruences define a reduced progression. CRT and Dirichlet’s theorem give a prime 𝑝p as large as needed. At the centre, 𝑏𝛿𝑝 =𝑐𝛿(𝑚 −2) ≠0bδp=cδ(m−2)≠0. (b) Each 𝑓𝑑fd that is constant on the even classes contributes a rational number, by Proposition 1.10(b) in base 𝑡𝑑td. Remove these contributions, lift the remaining functions to one modulus 2𝐾2K, subtract their values at zero, and clear a common denominator. None of these operations changes rationality. For the least remaining dilation 𝛿δ, choose an even residue 𝑟r with 𝑓𝛿(𝑟) ≠𝑓𝛿(0)fδ(r)≠fδ(0). Use the construction in (a), now with 𝑞𝑗 ≡1(mod2𝐾)qj≡1(mod2K) and 𝑝 ≡𝑟 +1(mod2𝐾)p≡r+1(mod2K). Minimality again leaves just the nonzero contribution from 𝛿δ at the centre, while every neighbouring totient is zero modulo 2𝐾2K and hence contributes zero after the subtraction. The same bounded integer-coefficient argument applies. ◻

Remark 1.13 (Earlier results and the reduced coefficients). Proposition 1.10(a), and with it the first assertion of the short paper’s residue theorem, already follows from Erdős’s Lemma 1 of [6]: if integers 𝑎𝑘 ≥0ak≥0 have ∑𝑘≤𝑛𝑎𝑘 =𝑂(𝑛)∑k≤nak=O(n), infinitely many 𝑎𝑘 ≠0ak≠0, and lim inf𝑛#{𝑘 ≤𝑛 :𝑎𝑘 >0}/𝑛 =0lim infn#{k≤n:ak>0}/n=0, then ∑𝑘𝑎𝑘𝑡−𝑘∑kakt−k is irrational for every integer 𝑡 ≥2t≥2. Erdős notes that this is the case 𝑏𝑘 =0bk=0 of his Lemma 4 (stated pp. 215–216, proved pp. 216–218). For 𝑎𝑛 =𝜑(𝑛)mod𝑚an=φ(n)modm the hypotheses hold: 𝑎𝑛 ≠0an≠0 forces 𝑛n to have no prime factor 𝑝 ≡1(mod𝑚)p≡1(modm), and such 𝑛n have density zero because ∑𝑝≡1 (𝑚)1/𝑝∑p≡1 (m)1/p diverges, while 𝑎𝑝 =𝑚 −2ap=m−2 for every prime 𝑝 ≡ −1(mod𝑚)p≡−1(modm). Erdős’s Lemma 4′′ (p. 218, proof omitted there) and Theorem 3 of Kaneko, Suzuki and Tachiya [7] give the same conclusion; the latter paper proves Lemma 4′′ as its Theorem A (pp. 2 and 18). The same argument covers Proposition 1.10(b) and Proposition 1.11 when the values 𝑓(𝑟) −𝑓(0)f(r)−f(0), respectively the coefficients 𝑐𝑑cd, have one sign. With both signs present, Lemmas 4 and 4′′ and Theorem 3 of [7] require an additional spacing condition on the negative terms, which is not checked here; the isolated coefficient needs none. Theorem 3 of [7], with 𝑏 =0b=0 and 𝑧𝑛 =log2⁡log⁡𝑥𝑛zn=log2⁡log⁡xn, also gives the irrational direction of Remark 1.12 once the 𝑛 <𝑥n<x with 𝑣2(𝜑(𝑛)) <log2⁡log⁡𝑥v2(φ(n))<log2⁡log⁡x are shown to number 𝑜(𝑥/log⁡log⁡𝑥)o(x/log⁡log⁡x); this record does not write out that density estimate. Wong’s base-equals-modulus theorem [5] is the case 𝑡 =𝑚t=m of Proposition 1.10(a).
