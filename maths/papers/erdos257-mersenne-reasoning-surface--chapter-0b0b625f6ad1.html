Long paper

Reciprocal Mersenne Subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 153 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A weighted summability criterion proves irrationality of reciprocal Mersenne subseries: if h is the part of a supported on a finite nonempty prime set P, every infinite A with ∑ ℎ(𝑎)/(𝑎(𝑏ℎ(𝑎) −1))h(a)/(a(bh(a)−1)) finite has irrational ∑ (𝑏𝑎 −1)−1(ba−1)−1. The base-two condition gives the conclusion at every integer base for every infinite subset, and permits an example with divergent reciprocal sum, while excluding full support and all odd exponents. The proof averages over a finite part of the support and over dyadic observation lengths. Erdős's weaker reciprocal-summable theorem is proved in full, and a common window extends both arguments to unions of weighted and positive-cover supports. Finite-denominator and achievement-set calculations formulate tests for the targets 1/21/2 and 1/211/21; those tests do not decide either target or the universal question. Prime support at base 2 and squarefree support at power-of-two bases are attributed as existing work.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #257: selected checked premises, finite computations, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, whose universal and half-value questions remain open.

𝑟=rem(𝑛),𝑜=𝑄(𝐵𝑛,2𝑛)−𝑇𝑛,r=rem(n),o=Q(Bn,2n)−Tn,

Proof. Applying the one-step quotient identity twice gives

𝑞(2𝑛+2,𝑑)=4𝑞(2𝑛,𝑑)+2𝟏𝑑∣2𝑛+1+𝟏𝑑∣2𝑛+2.q(2n+2,d)=4q(2n,d)+21d∣2n+1+1d∣2n+2.

The new capacity is 𝑇𝑛+1 =4𝑇𝑛 +2𝑛+1Tn+1=4Tn+2n+1, and the new terminal weight, at exponent 𝑛n, is 𝑞(2𝑛 +2,𝑛) =2𝑛+2 +4q(2n+2,n)=2n+2+4. Write 𝑔 =2𝑛+1g=2n+1. The old quotient sums are separated by at least 𝑔g, so the corrected sums remain separated by at least 4𝑔 −2(𝑛 −2) >2𝑔 +44g−2(n−2)>2g+4. This exceeds the new terminal weight. The upper word exists: already the terms at 𝑑 =2,3,4d=2,3,4 have sum greater than 𝑇𝑛Tn for 𝑛 ≥5n≥5, since 1/3 +1/7 +1/15 =19/35 >1/21/3+1/7+1/15=19/35>1/2 and their total floor error is less than 33. Consequently, adding the terminal weight to a lower old word cannot pass the next corrected old word. Moreover, the increment 𝑇𝑛+1 −4𝑇𝑛 =𝑔Tn+1−4Tn=g is smaller than this separation, so no old word beyond the adjacent upper one can fit. It is therefore enough to compare the two adjacent old words and the terminal weight. The upper word fits precisely under the condition UU; its remainder is then too small to take the terminal weight. Otherwise the lower word is used, and comparison with the terminal weight gives MM or RR. Substitution yields the displayed formulas. ◻

Only the MM and RR formulas have the asserted small correction to a fixed affine map. To rewrite UU in that form, let 𝛾𝑛 =𝑟 +𝑜 −2𝑛+1 ≥0γn=r+o−2n+1≥0 be the excess of the actual adjacent spacing over its lower bound. Then

𝜆𝑛+1=2𝜆𝑛−2−4𝛾𝑛+𝑝+2𝑛+1(U).λn+1=2λn−2−4γn+p+2n+1(U).

There is no 𝑂(𝑛)O(n) bound on 𝛾𝑛γn supplied by the separation inequality. At 𝑛 =13n=13, exact calculation gives

𝑟=15147,𝑜=3997,𝑝+=4,𝛾13=2760,rem(14)=392.r=15147,o=3997,p+=4,γ13=2760,rem(14)=392.

Thus 𝜆14 −2𝜆13 +2 = −2761/4096λ14−2λ13+2=−2761/4096. In particular, the proposed uniform error bound |𝛿𝑛| ≤(2𝑛 +6)2−𝑛|δn|≤(2n+6)2−n in a three-branch signed-digit formula is false already at this row. The adjacent-spacing term cannot be absorbed into the divisor correction.

Iterating the RR formula is still legitimate along a run of right branches. Its accumulated correction and endpoint indices must be retained in a reset estimate. These exact recurrences do not identify the proposed ceiling 𝐿𝑟 <(𝑟 −3)/2Lr<(r−3)/2 with a converse to the conditional reset square-root estimate.
