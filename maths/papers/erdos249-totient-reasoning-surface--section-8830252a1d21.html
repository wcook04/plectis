The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 160 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Exact dyadic rank and the limits of a rank argument

The dyadic case gives an explicit basis for each finite level and for the union of all levels. Coons proved nonregularity in every base 𝑘 ≥2k≥2 [2]; here the rank is computed at each finite level and the basis is given explicitly. Martin’s Theorem 1 supplies independence for the positive-residue affine forms [4], leaving the two zero-residue sections to be treated separately. The all-base independence proof and the integral relation proof are given separately in the short paper.

Construction.

Theorem 10.7 (Exact dyadic rank and infinite-dimensionality). For every 𝑒 ≥1e≥1, the family

{𝑛↦𝜑(𝑛), 𝑛↦𝜑(2𝑛)} ∪ {𝑛↦𝜑(2𝑗𝑛+𝑟):1≤𝑗≤𝑒, 0<𝑟<2𝑗, 𝑟 odd}{n↦φ(n), n↦φ(2n)} ∪ {n↦φ(2jn+r):1≤j≤e, 0<r<2j, r odd}

is linearly independent over ℚQ. It contains 2𝑒 +12e+1 sequences and spans the sections through level 𝑒e by the zero- and even-residue reductions.

The two zero-residue sections need separate treatment because their arguments 𝑛n and 2𝑛2n are proportional. Restrict a proposed relation to 𝑛 =2𝑚 +1n=2m+1, where 𝜑(2𝑛) =𝜑(𝑛)φ(2n)=φ(n). The odd-residue sections, together with 𝜑(𝑛)φ(n), now have pairwise nonproportional affine arguments. The evaluation-matrix construction chooses one input per row. At that input a selected primitive form is prime, while each competing form has a prime divisor whose predecessor is divisible by a fixed auxiliary prime. The matrix is diagonal and nonzero on the diagonal modulo that auxiliary prime. The check that the selected prime progression is reduced is part of the construction.

Independence removes the positive-residue coefficients and leaves 𝐴 +𝐵 =0A+B=0. The same remaining relation at 𝑛 =2n=2 gives 𝐴 +2𝐵 =0A+2B=0, so both coefficients vanish. This is the dyadic specialisation of the short paper’s proof. The Lean proof is unconditional; it uses the Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet’s theorem on primes in arithmetic progressions from Mathlib. Consequently the full infinite family spans an infinite-dimensional ℚQ-space (not_finiteDimensional_span_fullTotientKernel). A separate elementary observation concerns a proposed integer identity. There are no 𝑄,𝑣 ∈ℕ>0Q,v∈N>0 and 𝐴,𝑏 ∈ℤA,b∈Z with 𝑄𝑣𝐴 =𝑏QvA=b, 𝐴 ≠0A≠0, and |𝑏| <𝑄𝑣|b|<Qv: the identity forces |𝑏| =𝑄𝑣|𝐴| ≥𝑄𝑣|b|=Qv|A|≥Qv (false_of_compressedAdjointCertificate).

The Lean independence theorem is also stated for 𝑒 =0e=0, but its auxiliary canonical index then still contains the two zero-residue sections 𝜑(𝑛)φ(n) and 𝜑(2𝑛)φ(2n). It is therefore not the actual truncation through level zero, which consists only of 𝜑(𝑛)φ(n) and has dimension one.

Integral relations.

The independence statement also determines the arithmetic of the section lattice. Fix 𝑒 ≥1e≥1, write 𝐹𝑗,𝑟(𝑛) =𝜑(2𝑗𝑛 +𝑟)Fj,r(n)=φ(2jn+r), and let 𝐼𝑒 ={(𝑗,𝑟) :0 ≤𝑗 ≤𝑒, 0 ≤𝑟 <2𝑗}Ie={(j,r):0≤j≤e, 0≤r<2j}. Retain 𝐹0,0F0,0, 𝐹1,0F1,0, and the odd-residue sections at levels 1,…,𝑒1,…,e; call this retained index set 𝐽𝑒Je. The totient identities reduce every omitted section to 𝐹𝑖 =𝑎𝑖𝐹𝑗(𝑖)Fi=aiFj(i) for a retained section 𝑗(𝑖)j(i) and an integer 𝑎𝑖 ≥0ai≥0. Thus the retained family generates the lattice 𝐿𝑒 =∑𝑖∈𝐼𝑒ℤ𝐹𝑖Le=∑i∈IeZFi. Its rational independence gives integer independence as well: every element of 𝐿𝑒Le has unique integer coordinates in these 2𝑒 +12e+1 retained sections.

To describe all integral relations, let 𝐸𝑖Ei denote the formal coordinate vector in ℤ𝐼𝑒ZIe and use the evaluation map

ev𝑒:ℤ𝐼𝑒⟶𝐿𝑒,(𝑐𝑖)⟼∑𝑖∈𝐼𝑒𝑐𝑖𝐹𝑖.eve:ZIe⟶Le,(ci)⟼∑i∈IeciFi.

For each omitted index put 𝑅𝑖 =𝐸𝑖 −𝑎𝑖𝐸𝑗(𝑖)Ri=Ei−aiEj(i). Then

ker⁡(ev𝑒)=⨁𝑖∈𝐼𝑒\𝐽𝑒ℤ𝑅𝑖,rankℤ⁡ker⁡(ev𝑒)=2𝑒−2.ker⁡(eve)=⨁i∈Ie\JeZRi,rankZ⁡ker⁡(eve)=2e−2.

Indeed, subtracting ∑𝑖∉𝐽𝑒𝑐𝑖𝑅𝑖∑i∉JeciRi removes all omitted coordinates. At a retained index 𝑗j, the remaining coefficient is 𝑐𝑗 +∑𝑖∉𝐽𝑒,𝑗(𝑖)=𝑗𝑎𝑖𝑐𝑖cj+∑i∉Je,j(i)=jaici. These are the integral coordinates of the original combination. If it is a relation, independence makes every one of them zero. Conversely, the coefficient of 𝐸𝑖Ei in ∑ℎ∉𝐽𝑒𝑏ℎ𝑅ℎ∑h∉JebhRh is exactly 𝑏𝑖bi, proving uniqueness. Each relation row has coefficient 11 in its own omitted coordinate and 00 in every other omitted coordinate. No division is needed, so the conclusion holds over ℤZ. The depth-two base-six example in the short paper illustrates these two-term rows. The distinction between the retained basis and the redundant spanning family of all sections already matters for 𝑘k-regular sequences that are not eventually zero: their growth exponent is the base-𝑘k logarithm of the joint spectral radius of their section matrices in a basis, whereas a redundant spanning family gives only an upper bound (Coons [3]). The totient is not regular, so that theorem does not apply; the short paper records how the section maps act on the retained bases from one level to the next.

The integral normal form and the full dyadic rational basis are formally checked in Lean. The formal statement gives the same construction for every integer base 𝑘 ≥2k≥2, with relation rank ∑𝑒−1𝑗=1𝑘𝑗∑j=1e−1kj. The full dyadic assembly separately gives a basis for the infinite rational span and for its finitely supported rational relations. These finite and infinite statements should not be confused with a claim about infinite sums of relations.

Relation to the series value. Infinite section rank does not imply irrationality of 𝑆S. The result quantifies the nonregularity already cited above: it gives the rank, the retained sections and their integral relations. Martin’s simultaneous inequalities supply another proof for pairwise nonproportional affine forms with positive slopes [4]. His theorem does not separate 𝑛n from 2𝑛2n; the two evaluations above do that. The linked Lean proof is independent of Martin’s theorem.

The same independence argument also bounds the rank of an integral carry. If 𝑆 ∈ℚS∈Q, choose an integer 𝑣 >0v>0 with 𝑣𝑆 ∈ℤvS∈Z and put 𝑢𝑁 =𝑣𝑅𝑁uN=vRN, where 𝑅𝑁 =∑𝑗≥1𝜑(𝑁 +𝑗)2−𝑗RN=∑j≥1φ(N+j)2−j. Then 𝑢𝑁 ∈ℤuN∈Z,

𝑢𝑁+1=2𝑢𝑁−𝑣𝜑(𝑁+1),0≤𝑢𝑁≤𝑣(𝑁+2),𝑢𝑁/2𝑁⟶0.uN+1=2uN−vφ(N+1),0≤uN≤v(N+2),uN/2N⟶0.

For every 𝑒 ≥0e≥0,

dimℚ⁡span⁡{𝑛↦𝑢2𝑗𝑛+𝑟:1≤𝑗≤𝑒, 0≤𝑟<2𝑗} ≥ 2𝑒−1.dimQ⁡span⁡{n↦u2jn+r:1≤j≤e, 0≤r<2j} ≥ 2e−1.

This is the finite family in the linked statement (not_irrational_totientSeries_implies_unbounded_carryRank_unconditional); it contains all carry residues at levels 1,…,𝑒1,…,e, not just odd residues or an unspecified retained subfamily. Indeed, for each odd 𝑟r with 0 <𝑟 <2𝑗0<r<2j, the recurrence gives

𝑣𝜑(2𝑗𝑛+𝑟)=2𝑢2𝑗𝑛+𝑟−1−𝑢2𝑗𝑛+𝑟.vφ(2jn+r)=2u2jn+r−1−u2jn+r.

These 2𝑒 −12e−1 totient sections are independent and belong to the span of the displayed carry sections after multiplication by 𝑣 ≠0v≠0. The rank bound follows. The boundary condition 𝑢𝑁/2𝑁 →0uN/2N→0 identifies the canonical carry, but is not used in this rank-transfer argument.

To contradict rationality by rank, one would also need an incompatible upper bound. The comparison series with value 5/45/4 in Section 12.6 shows why rationality, a binary carry recurrence and linear coefficient growth alone do not supply such a bound. Its coefficients agree with 𝜑φ at odd indices but differ at even indices; they are not the totient sequence. Thus this comparison does not exclude an argument using additional totient identities.

A separate finite-dimensional calculation concerns the matrix 𝑈𝑁(𝑖,𝑗) =𝜇((𝑖 +1)/(𝑗 +1))UN(i,j)=μ((i+1)/(j+1)) when (𝑗 +1) ∣(𝑖 +1)(j+1)∣(i+1) and zero otherwise, for 0 ≤𝑖,𝑗 <𝑁0≤i,j<N. It is lower triangular with diagonal entries one. Hence det𝑈𝑁 =1detUN=1 and the map 𝑥 ↦𝑈𝑁𝑥x↦UNx is injective (incidenceMobius_det_eq_one). This proves injectivity of that particular map, not the impossibility of every finite-dimensional argument for irrationality.

The evaluation-matrix argument. For functions 𝑓1,…,𝑓𝑠f1,…,fs with values in a field, points 𝑛1,…,𝑛𝑠n1,…,ns for which det(𝑓𝑗(𝑛𝑖)) ≠0det(fj(ni))≠0 prove linear independence: evaluating a relation gives an invertible linear system for its coefficients. This elementary criterion is Proposition 6.24. It does not, by itself, construct the points. Here the totient formula, CRT and Dirichlet’s theorem provide them for nonproportional affine forms, followed by the separate zero-residue argument. Another arithmetic function would require its own argument for nonvanishing of the evaluation determinant.
