Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

Erdős #1041 By Will Cook 29 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 86 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For a monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc, any two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1} by a path of length less than 2. For any squarefree monic polynomial of degree 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, an area-growth argument gives such a path when the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/2513/25; scaling gives length less than (5/2)𝜇1/𝑛(5/2)μ1/n inside {|𝑓| <(25/13)𝜇}{|f|<(25/13)μ}. The record develops this argument, an inverse-ray averaging estimate, and a construction using an isolated simple critical value, together with results for collinear roots and several coefficient families. A weighted Poisson identity gives a sharp mean bound on critical values; examples distinguish that bound from path-length control. The final sections examine inverse-sheet topology and refute an earlier spanning-tree estimate. These results do not prove the unrestricted length-2 assertion.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #1041, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or the current status of Erdős #1041, including the later one-polynomial Formal Conjectures path-image Hausdorff-length check and its unreviewed historical curve-length correspondence; those distinctions belong to docs/claims.json in the mathematics repository.

Unformalised remark: a coefficient condition for four-term polynomials

One more monomial introduces a partial sum that need not lie in the unit disc. Bounding that sum gives the following ordinary coefficient criterion. Let

𝑔(𝑤)=𝑤𝑚+𝑎𝑤𝑟+𝑏𝑤𝑠+𝑐,𝑚>𝑟>𝑠≥1,g(w)=wm+awr+bws+c,m>r>s≥1,

assume all roots of 𝑔g lie in the open unit disk, and let 𝑤1,𝑤2w1,w2 be roots of the two smallest moduli. If

|𝑐|+|𝑏||𝑤2|𝑠<1,|c|+|b||w2|s<1,

then the complete radial spokes from 𝑤1w1 and 𝑤2w2 to the origin lie in {|𝑔| <1}{|g|<1}, and their broken line has length strictly below 22. The simpler coefficient condition

|𝑏|+|𝑐|≤1|b|+|c|≤1

makes every root-to-origin segment contained; the coefficient 𝑎a has no additional restriction beyond the assumed root locations. This coefficient condition includes 𝑏 =0b=0 and |𝑐| ≤1|c|≤1, but is not automatic for all four-term polynomials with roots in the disc.

The mechanism is visible in one identity. At a root 𝑤w and for 0 ≤𝑢 ≤10≤u≤1,

𝑔(𝑢𝑤)=(1−𝑢𝑠)𝑐+(𝑢𝑠−𝑢𝑟)(𝑐+𝑏𝑤𝑠)+(𝑢𝑟−𝑢𝑚)(−𝑤𝑚).g(uw)=(1−us)c+(us−ur)(c+bws)+(ur−um)(−wm).

Here the root equation has eliminated 𝑎𝑤𝑟awr before taking absolute values. The three weights are nonnegative and sum to 1 −𝑢𝑚1−um. The controlled terms are 𝑐c, 𝑐 +𝑏𝑤𝑠c+bws and −𝑤𝑚−wm; their moduli are strictly below one under the root-dependent condition. The same identity explains why no bound on 𝑎a is needed. Lean checks the factorization and the resulting strict radial-segment theorem under the exact weak exponent hypotheses 1 ≤𝑠 ≤𝑟 ≤𝑚1≤s≤r≤m; it also checks the coefficient-only corollary above. Its weak bound |𝑏| +|𝑐| ≤1|b|+|c|≤1 still gives strict containment: the root-disc hypothesis implies |𝑐| <1|c|<1, and, if 𝑏 ≠0b≠0, |𝑤|𝑠 <1|w|s<1 gives |𝑐| +|𝑏||𝑤|𝑠 <|𝑐| +|𝑏| ≤1|c|+|b||w|s<|c|+|b|≤1. If 𝑏 =0b=0, the required bound is simply |𝑐| <1|c|<1.

An alternative criterion uses a complex power sum to select two indices. Let 𝑆S index a finite family of roots, 𝑁 =|𝑆| ≥2N=|S|≥2, and

𝑀=∑𝑖∈𝑆𝑤𝑠𝑖.M=∑i∈Swis.

The squared-modulus identity gives

∑𝑖∈𝑆|𝑐+𝑏𝑤𝑠𝑖|2=𝑁|𝑐|2+|𝑏|2∑𝑖∈𝑆|𝑤𝑠𝑖|2+2Re⁡(――𝑐𝑏𝑀).∑i∈S|c+bwis|2=N|c|2+|b|2∑i∈S|wis|2+2Re⁡(c―bM).

Consequently, if every |𝑤𝑖| <1|wi|<1, |𝑐| <1|c|<1, and

𝑁(|𝑏|2+|𝑐|2)+2Re⁡(――𝑐𝑏𝑀)<𝑁−1,N(|b|2+|c|2)+2Re⁡(c―bM)<N−1,

then two distinct indices 𝑖,𝑗 ∈𝑆i,j∈S satisfy |𝑐 +𝑏𝑤𝑠𝑖| <1|c+bwis|<1 and |𝑐 +𝑏𝑤𝑠𝑗| <1|c+bwjs|<1. Indeed, the displayed identity bounds the sum of squared moduli strictly below 𝑁 −1N−1. If at most one index had modulus below one, the other 𝑁 −1N−1 terms would each contribute at least one. This explains both the threshold 𝑁 −1N−1 and why the criterion selects two indices. Applying the resulting contained-segment theorem to these inequalities proves that both root segments lie strictly in {|𝑔| <1}{|g|<1}. Retaining the signed cross term permits the inequality to hold for configurations outside the coefficient-only triangle |𝑏| +|𝑐| ≤1|b|+|c|≤1. The formal hypotheses do not require 𝑖 ↦𝑤𝑖i↦wi to be injective, so distinct indices need not denote distinct root values. For the polynomial consequence, the family must be a submultiset of the zeros, respecting their algebraic multiplicities. A repeated zero then already gives a constant path between two occurrences. Repeating a simple zero in an arbitrary indexed family cannot supply a second occurrence. The two sufficient conditions are not ordered by strength. For example, 𝑔(𝑤) =𝑤5 +19/20g(w)=w5+19/20 has all roots in the open unit disc and satisfies the coefficient condition with 𝑏 =0b=0. For its full five-root list, however, the left side of the power-sum condition is 5(19/20)2 =361/80 >4 =𝑁 −15(19/20)2=361/80>4=N−1, so that condition fails. Its benefit is the use of cancellation in the cross term, not inclusion of every case covered by the coefficient condition. Conversely,

𝑔(𝑤)=(𝑤2−1/2)(𝑤3+4/5)=𝑤5−12𝑤3+45𝑤2−25g(w)=(w2−1/2)(w3+4/5)=w5−12w3+45w2−25

has all roots of modulus 1/√21/2 or (4/5)1/3(4/5)1/3, hence below one, but |𝑏| +|𝑐| =6/5 >1|b|+|c|=6/5>1. Its full root list has 𝑀 =∑𝑖𝑤2𝑖 =1M=∑iwi2=1: the two quadratic roots contribute 11, and the three cubic roots contribute 00. The power-sum left side is 5(16/25 +4/25) −16/25 =84/25 <45(16/25+4/25)−16/25=84/25<4. Thus this criterion does cover root-disc polynomials excluded by the coefficient condition.

For 𝑞 ≥2q≥2 and 𝑓(𝑧) =𝑔((𝑧 −ℎ)𝑞)f(z)=g((z−h)q), assume also that all roots of 𝑓f lie in the open unit disc. If 𝑦𝑞 =𝑤yq=w and 𝜔 =𝑒2𝜋𝑖/𝑞ω=e2πi/q, the roots ℎ +𝜔𝑗𝑦h+ωjy satisfy

1𝑞𝑞−1∑𝑗=0|ℎ+𝜔𝑗𝑦|2=|ℎ|2+|𝑦|2<1.1q∑j=0q−1|h+ωjy|2=|h|2+|y|2<1.

Thus every quotient root has |𝑤| =|𝑦|𝑞 <1|w|=|y|q<1, which supplies the hypothesis on the zeros used above, and every lifted segment has length |𝑦| <1|y|<1. Two selected spokes join through ℎh with total length below 22; repeated root occurrences instead permit the constant path. The Lean theorem assumes the finite root family, its complex power sum 𝑀M, and the displayed inequality. It neither derives that inequality from arbitrary coefficients nor verifies the family’s polynomial multiplicities, the cyclic lifting, or the final path construction. Completeness of the root list is not required for the selection argument: a submultiset respecting multiplicity suffices. Without one of the power-sum, root-dependent, or coefficient-only inequalities, the tetranomial case is not covered by this argument; this family does not solve unrestricted Erdős #1041.
