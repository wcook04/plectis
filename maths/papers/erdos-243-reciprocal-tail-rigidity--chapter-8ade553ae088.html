Short paper

Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 21 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For strictly increasing positive integers with 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3), the reciprocal sum is irrational. The argument extracts a cubic polynomial from an integer tail numerator, restricts its coefficients using a square in a cubic field, and excludes the remaining cases modulo seven. The unrestricted Erdős–Graham question remains open.

This paper owns the cubic-rate irrationality theorem and its proof; the long paper develops the separate Sylvester-tail criteria and broader recurrence questions.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

An eventual cubic polynomial

We use 𝐹𝑛 =𝑛(𝑛 +1)(𝑛 +2)Fn=n(n+1)(n+2) rather than 𝑛3n3: its successive ratio is exactly 1 +3/𝑛1+3/n, so dividing by it removes the model without introducing lower-order errors. Write (3) as 𝐶𝑛+1 =(1 +3/𝑛 +𝜀𝑛)𝐶𝑛Cn+1=(1+3/n+εn)Cn, where 𝜀𝑛 =𝑜(𝑛−3)εn=o(n−3). Dividing out the exact model gives

𝑧𝑛=𝐶𝑛𝐹𝑛,𝑧𝑛+1𝑧𝑛=1+𝜀𝑛1+3/𝑛.zn=CnFn,zn+1zn=1+εn1+3/n.

The logarithmic increments are 𝑜(𝑛−3)o(n−3), so their absolute tails are 𝑜(𝑛−2)o(n−2). Hence 𝑧𝑛 →𝐴 >0zn→A>0 with 𝑧𝑛 −𝐴 =𝑜(𝑛−2)zn−A=o(n−2). Multiplying by 𝐹𝑛Fn gives

𝐶𝑛=𝐴𝐹𝑛+𝛿𝑛,𝛿𝑛=𝑜(𝑛).Cn=AFn+δn,δn=o(n).

An 𝑜(𝑛)o(n) error can still oscillate too much for finite differences. To control its first difference, subtract the numerator update for 𝐶𝑛Cn and the comparison update for 𝐴𝐹𝑛AFn:

Δ𝛿𝑛=3𝑛𝛿𝑛+𝜀𝑛𝐶𝑛=𝑜(1),Δℎ𝑛=ℎ𝑛+1−ℎ𝑛.Δδn=3nδn+εnCn=o(1),Δhn=hn+1−hn.

Here 𝐶𝑛 =𝑂(𝑛3)Cn=O(n3) makes the second term 𝑜(1)o(1). Three more differences still tend to zero and kill the cubic model:

Δ4𝐶𝑛=Δ3(Δ𝛿𝑛)⟶0.Δ4Cn=Δ3(Δδn)⟶0.

Integrality turns this limit into eventual vanishing. Newton interpolation then expresses 𝐶𝑛Cn as a rational polynomial of degree at most three. Since its difference from 𝐴𝐹𝑛AFn is 𝑜(𝑛)o(n), the cubic, quadratic and linear coefficients agree; only a constant can remain:

𝐶𝑛=𝐴𝑛(𝑛+1)(𝑛+2)+𝐵,𝐴∈ℚ>0,𝐵∈ℚ.(4)(4)Cn=An(n+1)(n+2)+B,A∈Q>0,B∈Q.

The precision matters at the subtraction step. The integer sequence 𝐹𝑛 +( −1)𝑛Fn+(−1)n has ratio 1 +3/𝑛 +𝑂(𝑛−3)1+3/n+O(n−3) and never agrees eventually with a polynomial; its error has an oscillating first difference. This example concerns polynomial extraction alone. It is not asserted to satisfy the denominator recurrence.
