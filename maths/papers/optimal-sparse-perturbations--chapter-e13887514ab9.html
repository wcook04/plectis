Cross-problem paper

Reading Eight Erdős Problems Together

Eight Erdős problems read together By Will Cook 22 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 38 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub

Précis. One account of the mathematics developed across the programmes: an exact tail-capacity criterion under eventual congruences, its sharp factorial support-gap threshold, rational and irrational Lambert subsums across bases, and the limits of finite greedy tests and other irrationality methods. Full arguments, exact counterexamples, unsuccessful routes and attribution are retained together. The principal proofs are ordinary mathematics; cited Lean ingredients have their own stated scope. Historical novelty and independent expert review are not established.

This paper owns the synthesis exposition and ordinary proofs across the covered problems: capacity and congruence constructions, Lambert subsums, rational-point counts, method limits and their research record.

It is not authority for a solution to any original Erdős target, historical novelty, independent expert review, or a full Lean proof of the analytic capacity criterion or Lambert-chain theorem.

Divisibility cuts at every algebraic base

A support need not be a chain for the prefix-clearing argument to work. What it needs is a place to cut: every earlier exponent divides one integer 𝐿L, while every later exponent is a multiple of a larger integer 𝑀M. The prefix then becomes a polynomial of degree at most 𝐿L, and the tail has a bounded number of possible initial coefficient patterns in powers of 𝑡−𝑀t−M. Keeping several of those powers as separate coordinates pays for the height of the prefix, including all its algebraic conjugates.

They also allow arbitrarily large sets of pairwise incomparable exponents in one block, as the example below shows. They do not hold for the full positive support. The theorem therefore does not settle the rationality of the full Lambert series at 3/23/2.

The proof uses the number-field Subspace Theorem in the normalization of Evertse and Ferretti [44]. It extends the preceding argument based on the method of Corvaja and Zannier [23]. This is an ordinary proof; neither a Lean proof of the transcendence conclusion nor historical priority is asserted.

These are the required cuts, with 𝑀𝑗 =2𝐿𝑗Mj=2Lj. Dyadic grouping gives ∑𝑘≤2𝑁𝑗1/𝑘 ≥𝑁𝑗/2∑k≤2Nj1/k≥Nj/2, so each block contributes at least 1/21/2 to the reciprocal sum. In the upper half of the 𝑗jth block, two different coefficients have ratio less than two and neither divides the other. The resulting antichains have unbounded cardinality. A union of finitely many chains cannot contain them. Apply the theorem. ◻

For a smaller example take 𝐻 =⋃𝑗≥0{12𝑗,2 ⋅12𝑗,3 ⋅12𝑗}H=⋃j≥0{12j,2⋅12j,3⋅12j}, with cuts 𝐿𝑗 =6 ⋅12𝑗Lj=6⋅12j, 𝑀𝑗 =12𝑗+1Mj=12j+1. Every infinite thinning satisfies the theorem, including the terms 2 ⋅12𝑝,3 ⋅12𝑝2⋅12p,3⋅12p for prime 𝑝p at base 3/23/2. The theorem is hereditary under thinning because the same cuts continue to work. Arbitrary supports do not have this property; the earlier rational subsums below base two remain a necessary warning.
