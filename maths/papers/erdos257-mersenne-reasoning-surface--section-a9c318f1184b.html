Reciprocal Mersenne Subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 153 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A weighted summability criterion proves irrationality of reciprocal Mersenne subseries: if h is the part of a supported on a finite nonempty prime set P, every infinite A with ∑ ℎ(𝑎)/(𝑎(𝑏ℎ(𝑎) −1))h(a)/(a(bh(a)−1)) finite has irrational ∑ (𝑏𝑎 −1)−1(ba−1)−1. The base-two condition gives the conclusion at every integer base for every infinite subset, and permits an example with divergent reciprocal sum, while excluding full support and all odd exponents. The proof averages over a finite part of the support and over dyadic observation lengths. Erdős's weaker reciprocal-summable theorem is proved in full, and a common window extends both arguments to unions of weighted and positive-cover supports. Finite-denominator and achievement-set calculations formulate tests for the targets 1/21/2 and 1/211/21; those tests do not decide either target or the universal question. Prime support at base 2 and squarefree support at power-of-two bases are attributed as existing work.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #257: selected checked premises, finite computations, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, whose universal and half-value questions remain open.

Bounds for the remaining binary positions

Definition 6.92 (The sharper binary bound). Let 𝑐 ≥4c≥4, 𝐷 ⊆{2,…,𝑐 −1}D⊆{2,…,c−1}, and 𝑋𝐷(2) <1/2XD(2)<1/2. The sharper bound is the condition

𝑆(𝐷,1,2𝑐−2)<2𝑐−2.S(D,1,2c−2)<2c−2.

It permits a binary completion using ranks 𝑐 +1,…,2𝑐 −2c+1,…,2c−2, with rank 𝑐c omitted, as explained below.

Theorem 6.93 (Equivalent forms of the sharper bound). Under the hypotheses of Definition 6.92:

𝑆(𝐷,1,2𝑐−2)<2𝑐−2⟺2(2𝑐−2)−1≤𝑄(𝐷∪{𝑐}, 2𝑐−2).S(D,1,2c−2)<2c−2⟺2(2c−2)−1≤Q(D∪{c}, 2c−2).

The threshold on the right is one greater than the exact-row target 22𝑐−3 −122c−3−1. Thus this is a test for omitting rank 𝑐c, not for including it in the completed row.

Proof. Put 𝑀 =2𝑐 −2M=2c−2. Since 𝑋𝐷(2) <1/2XD(2)<1/2, one has 𝑄(𝐷,𝑀) <2𝑀−1Q(D,M)<2M−1, and the integer remainder is 𝑆(𝐷,1,𝑀) =2𝑀−1 −𝑄(𝐷,𝑀) −1 ≥0S(D,1,M)=2M−1−Q(D,M)−1≥0 without truncation. The geometric quotient identity gives 𝑞(𝑀,𝑑) =2𝑀−𝑑q(M,d)=2M−d for 𝑐 ≤𝑑 ≤𝑀c≤d≤M, since 2𝑑 >𝑀2d>M. In particular 𝑞(𝑀,𝑐) =2𝑐−2q(M,c)=2c−2, so

𝑆(𝐷,1,𝑀)<2𝑐−2⟺𝑄(𝐷∪{𝑐},𝑀)≥2𝑀−1.S(D,1,M)<2c−2⟺Q(D∪{c},M)≥2M−1.

For the completion, use the ranks 𝑐 +1,…,𝑀c+1,…,M instead. Their quotient weights are 2𝑐−3,…,12c−3,…,1 and represent every integer from 00 to 2𝑐−2 −12c−2−1. A subset of them therefore has quotient sum 𝑆(𝐷,1,𝑀)S(D,1,M) and completes 𝐷D to the exact target 2𝑀−1 −12M−1−1 while omitting 𝑐c. These finite identities prove the equivalence and explain the sharper bound. They do not assert that a completed row stays below one half in real value. ◻

For example, 𝐷 ={2,3}D={2,3} and 𝑐 =5c=5 give 𝑀 =8M=8, 𝑄(𝐷,8) =121Q(D,8)=121 and remainder 6 <86<8. Inserting rank 55 adds 88, giving 129129 rather than the exact target 127127. The correct completion adds ranks 66 and 77, whose quotients are 44 and 22.

Theorem 6.94 (An unconditional bound with one extra bit). Unconditionally, for every 𝑐 ≥4c≥4 and every below-half core 𝐷 ⊆[2,𝑐)D⊆[2,c) with deficit <𝑤(𝑐)<w(c):

𝑆(𝐷,1,2𝑐−2)<2𝑐−1.S(D,1,2c−2)<2c−1.

The bound holds uniformly in 𝑐c, but is twice the threshold needed in Theorem 6.93 to apply Theorem 6.75. Before using |𝐷| ≤𝑐 −2 ≤2𝑐−2|D|≤c−2≤2c−2, the proof gives the sharper additive estimate 𝑆(𝐷,1,2𝑐 −2) <2𝑐−2 +|𝐷|S(D,1,2c−2)<2c−2+|D|. Thus the critical-capacity inequality would follow by excluding the integer band [2𝑐−2, 2𝑐−2 +𝑐 −3][2c−2,2c−2+c−3], which contains 𝑐 −2c−2 integers. This is the same band shape as Theorem 6.79’s dyadic-band condition. Its width is linear in 𝑐c, whereas the square-root reset condition discussed in Section 12.5 has width 2(𝑟+5)/22(r+5)/2 in a different parameter 𝑟r. Comparing these widths alone proves no implication between the two hypotheses.
