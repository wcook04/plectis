Geometric Moments and Rational Lambert Values: Proofs and Further Results

Erdős #1049 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 81 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. Specialising Zudilin's 2004 linear forms shows F(a/b) irrational for coprime 𝑎 >𝑏 ≥1a>b≥1 when θ=log b / log a is less than θ^*=0.4056830213840605…, with irrationality-exponent bound (1−θ)/(θ*−θ) for every positive integral power. In particular the bound is less than 301 for every positive integral power of 31/431/4. The constants are Zudilin's; the proof cancels common cyclotomic factors before rational specialisation. For the 2016 Hankel construction with x=z=1, the first nonzero term of the normalised determinant is computed at every rank. A separate positive-measure argument proves positivity and a size estimate for each fixed real 0<q<1. The final sections explain the limits of the stated clearing and congruence arguments at 3/23/2: no small nonzero sequence of divided remainders is constructed there, and the full rational-base conjecture remains open.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #1049, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #1049, which remains open.

What a larger region would require.

At 3/23/2 the logarithmic parameter is

log⁡2log⁡3=0.6309297535714574…,log⁡2log⁡3=0.6309297535714574…,

which exceeds 𝜃∗θ∗ by 0.2252467…0.2252467…. Theorem 2.2 gives no conclusion when log⁡𝑏/log⁡𝑎 ≥𝜃∗log⁡b/log⁡a≥θ∗. The same argument can apply to another family once its polynomial integrality, degrees and fixed-base remainder estimates are proved. A smaller ratio of the resulting constants 𝐶1/𝐶0C1/C0 would enlarge the reciprocal-cutoff region. For example, 𝐶1/𝐶0 ≤2.4234C1/C0≤2.4234 would give a cutoff greater than 0.41260.4126 and admit 29/429/4. An integer-base irrationality-exponent bound alone does not supply the polynomial data needed for this specialisation. The existing formal proofs establish irrationality and measure bounds for the constructed forms; they do not supply a different family whose divided remainder is small and nonzero at 3/23/2.

The next restriction concerns one polynomial family whose estimates hold at every fixed 𝑥 >1x>1, with the same leading constants but possibly base-dependent error terms. This quantifier is essential: the proof chooses a large integer base at which remainder decay would outpace coefficient growth. Estimates only at 3/23/2, or for a different family at each base, do not permit that choice. The 2004 family satisfies the hypotheses. The conclusion concerns forms in 𝐹(𝑥)F(x) and 11, not simultaneous forms in several independent target values. Here height means the largest absolute coefficient. To compare it with the sum of the absolute coefficients, note that for a polynomial of degree at most 𝑑d,

max𝑗|𝑝𝑗|≤∑𝑗|𝑝𝑗|≤(𝑑+1)max𝑗|𝑝𝑗|.maxj|pj|≤∑j|pj|≤(d+1)maxj|pj|.

For a nonzero polynomial of degree 𝑑 =𝑂(𝑛2)d=O(n2), the logarithms of these norms differ by 𝑂(log⁡𝑛)O(log⁡n). They have the same leading quadratic growth rate, but the literal zero-height conditions are not identical: 1 +𝑋1+X has maximum coefficient 11 and coefficient sum 22. In particular, when ℎ =0h=0, the displayed bound ℎ𝑛2(1 +𝑜(1))hn2(1+o(1)) is zero, not an arbitrary 𝑜(𝑛2)o(n2) term. The proof below also works with the additive bound ℎ𝑛2 +𝑜(𝑛2)hn2+o(n2); its evaluation estimate absorbs the 𝑂(log⁡𝑛)O(log⁡n) difference. Alternatively, either norm convention implies the hypothesis for the other norm after replacing ℎh by any larger positive constant. The degree conclusion is independent of that replacement. In the rational-base conclusions, write 𝑎/𝑏a/b with integers 𝑎 >𝑏 ≥1a>b≥1. The estimates hold without coprimality; a reduced representation gives the smaller denominator-clearing factor.

This strengthens the failure of a sufficient-cutoff test to an exclusion of decay under the stated all-base hypotheses. It does not supply an actual-degree limit or assert divergence. If 𝑑𝑛/𝑛2 →𝑑dn/n2→d, the separate exact-degree result gives the stronger conclusion |𝑏𝑑𝑛Λ𝑛(𝑎/𝑏)| →∞|bdnΛn(a/b)|→∞ in the same strict region. Equality 𝑎 =𝑏2a=b2 remains unclassified. The corollary concerns the undivided forms; it does not exclude base-dependent content division or other irrationality methods outside its hypotheses.

A degree bound alone would not supply the evaluation estimate: even degree-zero polynomials can have arbitrarily large integer coefficients. This explains the height hypothesis (3), without establishing that it cannot be weakened or omitted. At log⁡𝑏/log⁡𝑎 =𝜎/(𝜎 +𝑑)log⁡b/log⁡a=σ/(σ+d), the normalised logarithm is zero and the hypotheses decide neither behaviour. Theorem 2.5 is a restriction on the stated families, not on all Padé constructions.
