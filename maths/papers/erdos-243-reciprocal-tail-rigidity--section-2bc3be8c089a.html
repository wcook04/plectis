Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 21 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For strictly increasing positive integers with 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3), the reciprocal sum is irrational. The argument extracts a cubic polynomial from an integer tail numerator, restricts its coefficients using a square in a cubic field, and excludes the remaining cases modulo seven. The unrestricted Erdős–Graham question remains open.

This paper owns the cubic-rate irrationality theorem and its proof; the long paper develops the separate Sylvester-tail criteria and broader recurrence questions.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

num⁡(𝑛,𝑎)=(𝑛+1)𝑎2−(𝑛+2)𝑎+(𝑛+3),num⁡(n,a)=(n+1)a2−(n+2)a+(n+3),

the forced numerator. We stop at the first nonintegral quotient. This procedure checks divisibility only; it does not impose the positivity conditions required of an infinite reciprocal-tail sequence. The formal exact-division condition requires exact division at each step.

Example A.1 (a nonintegral second step). Starting from 𝑎0 =3a0=3, the first numerator num⁡(0,3) =6num⁡(0,3)=6 is divisible by 22, giving 𝑎1 =3a1=3. The next numerator num⁡(1,3) =13num⁡(1,3)=13 is not divisible by 33, so the recursion stops.

Direct iteration can produce very large intermediate integers. To decide whether the first ℎh divisions are exact, however, it suffices to know the initial value modulo (ℎ +1)!(h+1)!. Computing a pseudo-greedy orbit through residues modulo a shrinking product modulus is Koizumi’s method [10]; for the forced numerator that product is a factorial.

Theorem A.2 (factorial residue reduction). Let ℎh be a nonnegative integer and let 𝑎,𝑏a,b be integers with 𝑎 ≡𝑏(mod(ℎ +1)!)a≡b(mod(h+1)!). The first ℎh steps of the recursion 𝑎𝑛+1 =num⁡(𝑛,𝑎𝑛)/(𝑛 +2)an+1=num⁡(n,an)/(n+2), starting at index zero, are integral for 𝑎0 =𝑎a0=a if and only if they are integral for 𝑎0 =𝑏a0=b.

Proof. Division by 𝑖 +2i+2 consumes that factor of the modulus; no modular inverse is required. Define 𝑀(0,𝑖) =1M(0,i)=1 and 𝑀(ℎ +1,𝑖) =(𝑖 +2)𝑀(ℎ,𝑖 +1)M(h+1,i)=(i+2)M(h,i+1). Induct on ℎh, allowing any starting index 𝑖i: inputs congruent modulo 𝑀(ℎ,𝑖)M(h,i) either both complete ℎh integral steps or neither does. The case ℎ =0h=0 is immediate.

If 𝑎 ≡𝑏(mod(𝑖 +2)𝑀(ℎ,𝑖 +1))a≡b(mod(i+2)M(h,i+1)), the integer polynomial num⁡(𝑖, ⋅)num⁡(i,⋅) preserves this congruence. Its values are therefore either both divisible by 𝑖 +2i+2 or neither is. In the latter case both recursions stop. In the former, exact division leaves the quotients congruent modulo 𝑀(ℎ,𝑖 +1)M(h,i+1), and induction supplies the remaining ℎh steps. Finally 𝑀(ℎ,0) =(ℎ +1)!M(h,0)=(h+1)!. ◻
