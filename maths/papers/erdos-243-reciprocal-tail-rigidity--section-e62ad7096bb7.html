Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 21 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For strictly increasing positive integers with 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3), the reciprocal sum is irrational. The argument extracts a cubic polynomial from an integer tail numerator, restricts its coefficients using a square in a cubic field, and excludes the remaining cases modulo seven. The unrestricted Erdős–Graham question remains open.

This paper owns the cubic-rate irrationality theorem and its proof; the long paper develops the separate Sylvester-tail criteria and broader recurrence questions.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

Arithmetic compatibility

The remaining estimate requires the denominator recurrence as well as control of the error. For example, 𝐶𝑛 =𝑛2 +1Cn=n2+1, 𝐸𝑛 = −(2𝑛 +1)En=−(2n+1) has vanishing relative error and the necessary scalar pattern. But at 𝑛 =2,3,4n=2,3,4 its numerators are 5,10,175,10,17. The update 10 =5𝑎2 −𝐷210=5a2−D2 forces 5 ∣𝐷25∣D2, hence 5 ∣𝐷3 =𝑎2𝐷25∣D3=a2D2; the next update would force 5 ∣175∣17. Thus these numerators cannot satisfy (1). The companion’s Section 14 also constructs sequences avoiding sparse fixed moduli and distinguishes that avoidance from compatibility with all later denominators. Neither property alone supplies (13).

Conversely, suppose integer sequences indexed from zero satisfy (1), with 𝑎𝑛 >1an>1, 𝐶𝑛 >0Cn>0, 𝐷𝑛 ≥0Dn≥0 and 𝐸𝑛/𝐶𝑛 →0En/Cn→0. Section 4 of the companion proves that 𝐷𝑛 >0Dn>0, 𝐶𝑛/𝐷𝑛 →0Cn/Dn→0, ∑𝑛1/𝑎𝑛 =𝐶0/𝐷0∑n1/an=C0/D0 and 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1. The vanishing remainder 𝐶𝑛/𝐷𝑛 →0Cn/Dn→0 turns the finite reciprocal identities into an infinite series. A scalar profile or finite prefix does not supply it.

Problem 7.3 (growth of repeated denominator factors). For every rational-tail orbit satisfying the hypotheses but not the conclusion of Problem 1.1, must

lim sup𝑛→∞log⁡𝑀𝑛𝑛>0?lim supn→∞log⁡Mnn>0?

Equivalently, must there be a 𝐾 ≥1K≥1 for which

2𝑛≤𝑀𝐾𝑛2n≤MnK

at infinitely many indices?

Section 2 gives 𝐶𝑛+1/𝐶𝑛 →1Cn+1/Cn→1; averaging logarithms therefore gives log⁡𝐶𝑛 =𝑜(𝑛)log⁡Cn=o(n). Since 1 ≤𝑀𝑛 ≤𝐶𝑛1≤Mn≤Cn, every such orbit already satisfies log⁡𝑀𝑛/𝑛 →0log⁡Mn/n→0. A positive answer therefore holds exactly when no such orbit exists: proving it from failure of Sylvester behaviour would exclude every counterexample.
