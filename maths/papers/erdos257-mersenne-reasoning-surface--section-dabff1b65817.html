Reciprocal Mersenne Subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 153 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. A weighted summability criterion proves irrationality of reciprocal Mersenne subseries: if h is the part of a supported on a finite nonempty prime set P, every infinite A with ∑ ℎ(𝑎)/(𝑎(𝑏ℎ(𝑎) −1))h(a)/(a(bh(a)−1)) finite has irrational ∑ (𝑏𝑎 −1)−1(ba−1)−1. The base-two condition gives the conclusion at every integer base for every infinite subset, and permits an example with divergent reciprocal sum, while excluding full support and all odd exponents. The proof averages over a finite part of the support and over dyadic observation lengths. Erdős's weaker reciprocal-summable theorem is proved in full, and a common window extends both arguments to unions of weighted and positive-cover supports. Finite-denominator and achievement-set calculations formulate tests for the targets 1/21/2 and 1/211/21; those tests do not decide either target or the universal question. Prime support at base 2 and squarefree support at power-of-two bases are attributed as existing work.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #257: selected checked premises, finite computations, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, whose universal and half-value questions remain open.

A sufficient test for a safe skip

Definition 6.119 (Three geometric terms in the tail lower bound). For 𝑘 ≥1k≥1 put ℓ3(𝑘) =1/2𝑘 +1/(3 ⋅4𝑘) +1/(7 ⋅8𝑘)ℓ3(k)=1/2k+1/(3⋅4k)+1/(7⋅8k). Then ℓ3(𝑘) <𝑅(𝑘)ℓ3(k)<R(k): this is a strict rational lower bound obtained by truncating the Lambert-type series at three terms.

Theorem 6.120 (A sufficient inequality for a safe skip). Let 𝑘,𝑢,𝐿 ≥1k,u,L≥1 be integers, set 𝑎 =2𝐿 −(2𝑘 −1)𝑢a=2L−(2k−1)u, and suppose 𝑎 >0a>0. For the skipped rational remainder 𝜌 =𝑢/(2𝐿)ρ=u/(2L), 𝜌 ≤2−𝑘ρ≤2−k is equivalent to 𝑢 ≤𝑎u≤a, whereas

2𝑢≤3𝑎⟹𝜌<𝑅𝑘.2u≤3a⟹ρ<Rk.

The latter implication uses the three-term lower bound above. More explicitly, with 𝑡 =2𝑘 ≥2t=2k≥2,

(1𝑡+13𝑡2+17𝑡3)−33𝑡−1=2𝑡−321𝑡3(3𝑡−1)>0.(1t+13t2+17t3)−33t−1=2t−321t3(3t−1)>0.

The inequality 2𝑢 ≤3𝑎2u≤3a gives 𝜌 ≤3/(3𝑡 −1)ρ≤3/(3t−1), proving the claim. The first two terms alone would not prove this comparison. This is a sufficient exclusion of the current tail-mass deficit, not an exact test for membership in the remaining achievement set.

The sufficient condition is weaker than 𝑢 ≤𝑎u≤a. A realizable example is (𝑘,𝑢,𝐿,𝑎) =(2,7,13,5)(k,u,L,a)=(2,7,13,5); it passes 2𝑢 ≤3𝑎2u≤3a but not 𝑢 ≤𝑎u≤a. The linked scalar example (𝑢,𝑎) =(3,2)(u,a)=(3,2) proves strict containment of the inequalities, but is not integral rational data for this substitution. For comparison, the exact mass threshold is 𝑎/𝑢 ≥𝑅−1𝑘 −(2𝑘 −1)a/u≥Rk−1−(2k−1); its right-hand side lies strictly between 00 and 2/32/3. None of these comparisons asserts that the half-greedy orbit satisfies the sufficient inequality at every skipped rank.

Proposition 6.121 (Two unconditional safety cases). Under the positive-integer and skipped-step hypotheses of Theorem 6.120, 𝑢 =1u=1 implies 𝑎 ≥1a≥1, so the current remainder is less than 𝑅𝑘Rk. Conversely, a fatal tail-mass deficit forces 3𝑎 <2𝑢3a<2u, hence 𝑢 ≥2u≥2, or 𝑢 ≥3u≥3 when 𝑢u is odd. These are statements about the current step, not infinite survival.
