Prime-Gap Dyadic Series: Perturbations, Exact Criteria and Certificates

Erdős #251 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 47 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A convergent dyadic series with nonnegative integer coefficients can be made rational by sparse nonnegative corrections of upper Banach density zero, eventually bounded by any f(n)→∞, while preserving every fixed modulus and, for a logarithmic allowance, short-block empirical distributions. For prime gaps the cumulative positions remain asymptotic to n log n; no primality conclusion is asserted. Summation by parts reduces the prime series to the gap series, and the tails 𝑇𝑁 =∑𝑗≥1𝑔𝑁+𝑗2−𝑗TN=∑j≥1gN+j2−j satisfy 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1. That recurrence characterises rationality and yields finite tests with explicit remainder bounds. An exact finite certificate shows that a rational value of the prime series would have denominator at least 239998 >1012040239998>1012040. The bound does not establish irrationality.

Remaining prime-gap estimates

For the actual tails, Theorem 5.3 makes the first question equivalent to irrationality. The second requires two small differences and a gap mismatch at the same index. A finite certificate verifies one instance; both questions require witnesses arbitrarily late for every positive shift.

Problem 10.1 (nonintegral tail differences of each positive length). For every ℎ ≥1h≥1 and every 𝑁0N0, prove that some 𝑁 ≥𝑁0N≥N0 satisfies

Problem 10.2 (two small tail differences at arbitrarily large indices). For every ℎ ≥1h≥1 and every 𝑁0N0, prove that some 𝑁 ≥𝑁0N≥N0 satisfies

| | |𝜎ℎ(𝑁)| | |<1,| | |𝜎ℎ(𝑁+1)| | |<1,𝑔𝑁+ℎ+1≠𝑔𝑁+1.(10)(10)|σh(N)|<1,|σh(N+1)|<1,gN+h+1≠gN+1.

Problem 10.1, like the congruent-index criterion of Theorem 5.5, is exact; Problem 10.2 is only sufficient. The counterexamples in Section 8 show why the retained coefficient data do not supply the missing tail information. They are not counterexamples about actual primes.

The successful indices have density zero by Theorem 8.7, so the required existence theorem must detect a sparse set. A finite prefix is compatible with a rational comparison series. Supplemented by a proved remainder bound, however, it can certify one actual pair, as in Proposition 6.6; it does not establish occurrence beyond every cutoff.

At this depth, the test bounds the signed integer 2𝐿𝐹ℎ,𝑁,𝐿2LFh,N,L, not just its residue modulo 2𝐿2L, and also requires 𝑔𝑁+ℎ+1 −𝑔𝑁+1 = ±2gN+h+1−gN+1=±2. For fixed ℎh and 𝜀ε, checking one certificate uses 𝑂𝜀(log⁡𝑁)Oε(log⁡N) gap values and the explicit remainder bound. This counts data, not bit operations. The unproved input is the existence of certified witnesses beyond every cutoff for each fixed ℎh; constants uniform in ℎh are not required.

The following published results control gap sizes and clusters, not the signed weighted sums required by this criterion. Zhang’s bounded-gap theorem [21] produces infinitely many bounded consecutive-prime gaps. Maynard bounds lim inf𝑛(𝑝𝑛+𝑚 −𝑝𝑛)lim infn(pn+m−pn) for every fixed 𝑚m, giving bounded clusters of every fixed size [16], with the explicit unconditional bound lim inf𝑛(𝑝𝑛+1 −𝑝𝑛) ≤600lim infn(pn+1−pn)≤600 [16]. Polymath subsequently improved this to 246246 [35]; neither numerical bound controls the signed weighted tails required here. The large-gap theorem of Ford, Green, Konyagin, Maynard and Tao gives an effective lower bound for the largest single consecutive-prime gap below 𝑋X [8]. A theorem giving bounded clusters at each fixed cluster size does not by itself supply a weighted condition on blocks whose length grows with the basepoint, and none of these results is used as a proof input here.

Conditionally the picture is different. Tao’s comment names uniform quantitative prime-tuples control of about log⁡log⁡𝑛log⁡log⁡n consecutive gaps as a possible approach [23], and Land’s draft carries that out under Kuperberg’s uniform prime-tuples conjecture [13]. The counterexamples in Section 8 show that the listed size, congruence and block-statistical properties alone do not force irrationality. These constructions are not shown to enumerate the consecutive primes, so they do not rule out arguments that combine those properties with further information about primes.
