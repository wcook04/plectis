Geometric Moments and Rational Lambert Values: Proofs and Further Results

Erdős #1049 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 80 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Summary. The record proves the fixed-base Hankel asymptotic for Zudilin's normalised moments and the rational-base irrationality criterion log b/log a<0.4056830213840605…. The determinant proof separates geometric node configurations from positive weights; the irrationality proof instead specialises Zudilin's 2004 polynomial forms after cancellation. Coefficient moments, evaluated lattices and congruence conditions distinguish integer divisibility from nonzero remainder decay. A broader ratio-limit argument and a rational-value model remain unformalised. None of these arguments settles 𝐹(3/2)F(3/2).

This paper covers the sharp fixed-base Hankel asymptotic and rational-base irrationality proofs, coefficient moments and arithmetic obstructions, with ordinary ratio-limit and rational-value models distinguished from checked results.

It does not establish the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #1049, which remains open.

Where the earlier criterion stops.

Both regions are cut out by the same quantity. The published cutoff 1/2 −1/𝜋21/2−1/π2 of [6] is the reciprocal of 𝜇BV =2𝜋2/(𝜋2 −2)μBV=2π2/(π2−2), the constant Van Assche later recovered as an integer-base irrationality-exponent bound for 𝐹(𝑝)F(p) [18], and the cutoff 𝜃∗θ∗ of Theorem 2.2 is the reciprocal of the smaller integer-base bound 𝜇 =𝐶1/𝐶0μ=C1/C0 printed at [8]. For Zudilin’s family this reciprocal relation uses both the exact degree limit 𝑑 =𝐶1 −𝐶0d=C1−C0 and the stronger evaluation bound |𝑈𝑛(𝑝)| ≤𝑝𝑊𝑛exp⁡(𝑂𝑝(𝑛))|Un(p)|≤pWnexp⁡(Op(n)) proved in Corollary 2.4, together with decay exponent 𝜎 =𝐶0σ=C0. A general exponential coefficient-height bound would contribute an additional term to the integer-base exponent estimate; it cannot simply be discarded. The discussion following Theorem 2.5 makes that distinction explicit. The rational-base proof clears the denominators of the already cancelled polynomials. It does not infer value irrationality merely by taking the reciprocal of a published exponent bound. The bases gained are exactly the strip 𝑠𝜇 <𝑟 ≤𝑠𝜇BVsμ<r≤sμBV, which is empty for 𝑠 =2s=2 and 𝑠 =3s=3 and is first occupied at 𝑠 =4s=4 by the single coprime numerator 3131. The integer comparisons and rational bracket in Section 2.5 establish membership in the region. The irrationality conclusion in Theorem 2.3 also uses the constructed forms and their proved analytic estimates. This application does not improve either inherited integer-base exponent bound.
