Prime-Gap Dyadic Series: Perturbations, Exact Criteria and Certificates

Erdős #251 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 47 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A convergent dyadic series with nonnegative integer coefficients can be made rational by sparse nonnegative corrections of upper Banach density zero, eventually bounded by any f(n)→∞, while preserving every fixed modulus and, for a logarithmic allowance, short-block empirical distributions. For prime gaps the cumulative positions remain asymptotic to n log n; no primality conclusion is asserted. Summation by parts reduces the prime series to the gap series, and the tails 𝑇𝑁 =∑𝑗≥1𝑔𝑁+𝑗2−𝑗TN=∑j≥1gN+j2−j satisfy 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1. That recurrence characterises rationality and yields finite tests with explicit remainder bounds. An exact finite certificate shows that a rational value of the prime series would have denominator at least 239998 >1012040239998>1012040. The bound does not establish irrationality.

Arithmetic-progression reformulations of integrality

A longer observed block can appear to provide a stronger arithmetic test. Here it does not: the recurrence inserts the same block sum into the quantity tested and into the comparison progression, so that sum cancels. Thus (16) holds for real integer-coefficient recurrences under the same bound and growth hypothesis. The same is true of (13), and of (14) when the coefficient differences are even. These extensions follow from the printed argument; the Lean proof covers the rational case stated in the theorem.

The first test is exactly even integrality of 𝐷𝑁DN, regardless of 𝑟r. The fixed-progression test compares 2𝑟dist⁡(𝐷𝑁,ℤ)2rdist⁡(DN,Z) with the bound on the remaining tail. When 𝐷𝑁DN is nonintegral, the growth hypothesis makes the former larger than twice the latter at a suitable scale. Rewriting the test does not provide an estimate about the distribution of consecutive primes.

A signed comparison with more analytic data

A signed variant appears in the totient companion, The Binary Totient Series, under “Signed interpolation with entire Dirichlet defects”. We recall its different hypotheses to delimit the comparison. Around the totient sequence, signed integer changes bounded by any positive 𝑓(𝑛) →∞f(n)→∞ fill an interval at every reciprocal integer base. They can preserve the odd coefficients, every fixed eventual congruence, all arithmetic-progression Dirichlet singularities, and prescribed finite root-of-unity jets. The proof takes finite differences of increasing order on separated finite sets of indices. The support of the changes has upper Banach density zero. The union of the intervals spanned by those sets has ordinary density zero and upper Banach density one.

This theorem allows the bound on the corrections to grow arbitrarily slowly and retains more analytic observations. It uses both signs, however, and does not subsume the one-sided prime-gap perturbation theorem proved here. In particular, it constructs comparison integer sequences, not consecutive prime gaps, and neither theorem supplies an irrationality proof for the actual prime series. The companion contains a full ordinary proof and the precise block estimate in terms of the prescribed bound; no infinite formal theorem is claimed.
