Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 61 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. For rational reciprocal sums under 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, the record also develops sufficient conditions for the eventual recurrence 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1, controlling either increases of an integer tail numerator or a convergent sum at steps setting new maxima. Examples separate numerical growth bounds from the exact recurrences. The cubic proof is distinguished from the theorems formalised in Lean. None of the additional bounds is derived for every rational reciprocal sum in Erdős #243.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #243, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

Further questions

We collect the restrictions on a possible counterexample and the estimates which would exclude it. The constant and periodic arguments concern all-negative tails. They leave open a periodic pattern seen only along the negative indices of a mixed-sign sequence. The unrestricted problem remains unresolved here.

Proposition 14.1 (necessary conditions on a counterexample). For the integer tail attached to any counterexample to Problem 1.1,

𝐸𝑛≠0eventually,|𝐸𝑛|𝐶𝑛⟶0,En≠0eventually,|En|Cn⟶0,

and

lim sup𝑛→∞𝐸𝑛<0(−𝐸𝑛)=∞,∞∑𝑛=0(−𝐸𝑛)+𝐶𝑛=∞.lim supn→∞En<0(−En)=∞,∑n=0∞(−En)+Cn=∞.

In particular, negative indices occur infinitely often. Thus the remaining regime consists of unbounded negative errors along exact reciprocal tails for which the sum of relative increases diverges.

Proof. Koizumi’s construction gives vanishing relative error after a finite shift, hence |𝐸𝑛| <𝐶𝑛|En|<Cn eventually. Absorption then makes 𝐸𝑛 ≠0En≠0 eventually, since eventual zero would give the Sylvester recurrence. Descent excludes an eventually nonnegative error. Theorem 12.1 excludes a bounded negative part, and Theorem 13.1 excludes convergence of the sum of relative increases. These statements are unchanged by deleting a finite prefix. ◻

Proposition 14.1 does not assert infinitely many prime divisors of the error magnitudes. Nor are its numerical conditions alone a reformulation of the problem: the numerator and denominator must satisfy the exact recurrences. With those recurrences, positivity and vanishing relative error, Section 4 supplies the reciprocal-series realisation. The following exact-orbit question is therefore equivalent to the original problem.

Problem 14.2 (unbounded negative errors with divergent relative sum). Exclude, or construct, natural-number sequences (𝑎,𝐷,𝐶)(a,D,C) satisfying (19), with 𝑎𝑛 >1an>1 and 𝐶𝑛 >0Cn>0, whose multipliers satisfy lim𝑛𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 =1limnan+1/an2=1 and whose error satisfies vanishing relative error, and which is negative infinitely often with magnitudes unbounded along that infinite set and

∑𝑛(−𝐸𝑛)+𝐶𝑛=∞.∑n(−En)+Cn=∞.

Such an orbit fails the bounded-negative-part and summable-relative-increase hypotheses. By Proposition 14.1 the integer tail of a counterexample is such an orbit after deletion of a finite prefix, so an exclusion would settle Problem #243. Conversely, a global orbit with the displayed properties has 𝐶𝑛/𝐷𝑛 →0Cn/Dn→0 by Section 4. The reciprocal-series realisation then identifies its reciprocal sum as 𝐶0/𝐷0C0/D0. After deletion of a finite prefix the multipliers are strictly increasing, and the infinitely many negative errors rule out a Sylvester tail. It is therefore a counterexample to Problem #243. Finite admissible prefixes alone do not provide such a construction.

Remark. The recurrences alone admit unbounded negative errors for which ∑𝑛( −𝐸𝑛)+/𝐶𝑛∑n(−En)+/Cn diverges. The following example fails both the relative-error and growth assumptions of Problem 14.2. Put

𝐶𝑛=2𝑛,𝑏0=2,𝑏𝑛+1=12𝑏𝑛(𝑏𝑛+2),𝑎𝑛=𝑏𝑛+2,𝐷𝑛=𝑏𝑛𝐶𝑛,Cn=2n,b0=2,bn+1=12bn(bn+2),an=bn+2,Dn=bnCn,

A scalar profile need not satisfy the recurrences.

The example 𝐶𝑛 =𝑛2 +1Cn=n2+1, 𝐸𝑛 = −(2𝑛 +1)En=−(2n+1) satisfies the numerator update, |𝐸𝑛|/𝐶𝑛 →0|En|/Cn→0, log⁡𝐶𝑛 =𝑜(𝑛)log⁡Cn=o(n) and ∑𝑛(𝐸𝑛/𝐶𝑛)2 <∞∑n(En/Cn)2<∞. It is not an exact reciprocal-tail orbit. Indeed, 𝐶2 =5C2=5, 𝐶3 =10C3=10 and 𝐶4 =17C4=17. The first numerator update forces 5 ∣𝐷25∣D2; the multiplicative denominator update gives 5 ∣𝐷35∣D3; the next numerator update then forces 5 ∣𝐶45∣C4, a contradiction. This is the failed-route example referred to at the end of the short note: subexponential size and a small relative error do not supply arithmetic compatibility.

Growth of the factors repeated in the denominator

To compare repeated prime factors in the denominator with its total size, define

𝐿0=𝐷0,𝐿𝑛+1=lcm⁡(𝐿𝑛,𝑎𝑛),𝑀𝑛=𝐷𝑛𝐿𝑛.L0=D0,Ln+1=lcm⁡(Ln,an),Mn=DnLn.

The integer 𝑀𝑛Mn records prime factors counted repeatedly in 𝐷𝑛 =𝐷0∏𝑗<𝑛𝑎𝑗Dn=D0∏j<naj but only to their largest exponent in 𝐿𝑛Ln; thus 𝑀𝑛𝐿𝑛 =𝐷𝑛MnLn=Dn. Problem 14.3 asks whether failure of the Sylvester recurrence forces exponential growth of 𝑀𝑛Mn, contradicting its subexponential upper bound. The following recovery estimate is local and does not supply that lower bound.

Cancellation during recovery intervals

The LCM quotient 𝑀𝑛Mn need not equal the common divisor 𝐺𝑛Gn removed by reduction to lowest terms. Since 𝑀𝑛Mn divides both 𝐶𝑛Cn and 𝐷𝑛Dn, we have 𝑀𝑛 ∣𝐺𝑛Mn∣Gn, but equality is not assumed. The following estimate concerns the successive reduction factors, over an interval where the reduced numerator regains its starting value. In the calculation below, ℎ𝑛hn is the factor removed at step 𝑛n, 𝑐𝑛cn is a positive integer with 𝑐2𝑛 ∣ℎ𝑛cn2∣hn, and ˜𝑒𝑛e~n denotes the signed reduced error, as in Section 7. For a fixed number of steps, the same estimate has a simpler consequence: a sufficiently late recovery cannot include any cancellation. If 𝐾|˜𝑒𝑛| <𝑢𝑛K|e~n|<un eventually for every 𝐾K, then for each fixed 𝐿 >0L>0 there is an 𝑁N such that every recovery 𝑢𝑟 ≤𝑢𝑟+𝐿ur≤ur+L with 𝑟 ≥𝑁r≥N satisfies

This includes a recovery followed by another fall before the last endpoint. The fixed-length conclusion is formalised as absence of late cancellation during recovery intervals of fixed length. The threshold 𝑁N depends on 𝐿L: late cancellations require longer recoveries, but have not been excluded. A global contradiction needs control of those varying lengths and a lower bound on the moduli’s least common multiple; the interval estimate supplies neither.

Problem 14.3 (growth of repeated denominator factors). For every rational-tail orbit satisfying the hypotheses but not the conclusion of Problem 1.1, must

lim sup𝑛→∞log⁡𝑀𝑛𝑛>0?lim supn→∞log⁡Mnn>0?

Equivalently, must there be a 𝐾 ≥1K≥1 for which

2𝑛≤𝑀𝐾𝑛2n≤MnK

at infinitely many indices?

The two displayed formulations are equivalent up to changing the positive constant; the second is not a weaker target. Section 6 already gives 1 ≤𝑀𝑛 ≤𝐶𝑛1≤Mn≤Cn, hence log⁡𝑀𝑛/𝑛 →0log⁡Mn/n→0 on every orbit under consideration. A positive answer would therefore be a contradiction, not a further compatible necessary condition. It would prove Problem #243. The missing step is to force exponential growth of 𝑀𝑛Mn from failure of the Sylvester recurrence.

There is also a more local-looking question whose content is nevertheless the entire prefix. Put 𝐴𝑛 =∏𝑗<𝑛𝑎𝑗An=∏j<naj, so 𝐷𝑛 =𝐷0𝐴𝑛Dn=D0An. From

𝐷0𝐴𝑛=(𝑎𝑛−1)𝐶𝑛+𝐸𝑛D0An=(an−1)Cn+En

one immediately obtains

gcd(𝐴𝑛,𝑎𝑛−1)∣𝐸𝑛.gcd(An,an−1)∣En.

The checked tail-height estimate and vanishing relative error make |𝐸𝑛||En| subexponential in 𝑛n.

Problem 14.4 (common factors of the prefix and 𝑎𝑛 −1an−1). In every rational-tail orbit satisfying the hypotheses but not the conclusion of Problem 1.1, is

lim sup𝑛→∞log⁡gcd(𝐴𝑛,𝑎𝑛−1)𝑛>0?lim supn→∞log⁡gcd(An,an−1)n>0?

Equivalently, do there exist 𝜂 >0η>0 and infinitely many 𝑛n such that gcd(𝐴𝑛,𝑎𝑛 −1) ≥𝑒𝜂𝑛gcd(An,an−1)≥eηn?

The direct analytic question

Problem 14.5 (summability from rationality). Let (𝑎𝑛)(an) satisfy the hypotheses of Problem 1.1, and let (𝐷𝑛,𝐶𝑛,𝐸𝑛)(Dn,Cn,En) be its integer tail. Must

∞∑𝑛=0(−𝐸𝑛)+𝐶𝑛<∞?∑n=0∞(−En)+Cn<∞?

An affirmative answer closes Problem #243 by Theorem 13.1. Since the summands tend to zero and log⁡(1 +𝑡) ∼𝑡log⁡(1+t)∼t, convergence is equivalent to bounded partial products ∏𝑛<𝑁(1 +( −𝐸𝑛)+/𝐶𝑛)∏n<N(1+(−En)+/Cn). A negative answer requires the full growth and rationality hypotheses, not merely a locally admissible orbit. By Theorem 6.2, finiteness of (21) for one nonincreasing weight with divergent integral and one fixed 𝐵 ≥0B≥0 also suffices. The equivalent record-only sum in Theorem 6.2 weights the positive excess of each actual upward jump over 𝐵B by 𝑓(𝑈𝑛)f(Un).

Remark. One further question is not left open here, since it is answered in [12]: whether vanishing relative error follows from 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1 and rationality. It does. After deleting a finite prefix, Koizumi’s Corollary 3  [12] makes the sequence the pseudo-greedy expansion of its own reciprocal sum and gives 𝜀𝑛 →0εn→0 under exactly the hypotheses of Problem 1.1; rationality is not needed for that step, only summability of ∑1/𝑎𝑛∑1/an. The matched-index dictionary in Section 3 gives 𝜀𝑛 =𝐸𝑛/𝐶𝑛εn=En/Cn on the restarted tail. For a rational sum, Lemma 4(2)  [12] also gives −𝑐𝑛/2 ≤𝑒𝑛 <𝑐𝑛/2−cn/2≤en<cn/2 at every index of that expansion. Thus |𝐸𝑛| <𝐶𝑛|En|<Cn holds beyond the restart, as required by Theorem 12.1; it need not hold at every original index. The eventual inequality |𝐸𝑛| <𝐶𝑛|En|<Cn also follows directly from vanishing relative error with 𝐾 =1K=1. What remains unsupplied is the eventual lower bound on 𝐸𝑛En in Theorem 12.1. That additional hypothesis is why the theorem is still conditional.

Unbounded upward increments

Proposition 14.6 (small increases when the prime moduli are sparse). There exist strictly increasing primes 𝑝𝑖pi and a strictly increasing positive integer sequence 𝑢𝑛 →∞un→∞ such that gcd(𝑢𝑛,𝑝𝑖) =1gcd(un,pi)=1 for all 𝑖,𝑛i,n and

The set of such integers is therefore unbounded. Enumerate it increasingly as (𝑢𝑛)(un) and apply the same bound with 𝑥 =𝑢𝑛 +1x=un+1 to obtain 𝑢𝑛+1 −𝑢𝑛 ≤𝐿(𝑢𝑛 +1)un+1−un≤L(un+1). Proof. Write 𝑘(𝑧) =#{𝑗 :𝑚𝑗 ≤𝑧} =ℓ(𝑧) +𝑂(1)k(z)=#{j:mj≤z}=ℓ(z)+O(1). For a fixed prefix length 𝑇T, set

Here 𝜃𝑇 →0θT→0. By the Chinese remainder theorem, avoiding the first 𝑇T whole moduli selects precisely the fraction 𝜎𝑇σT of the residues modulo 𝑀𝑇MT.

For the upper bound, fix 𝑇T before sending the interval to infinity, so the prefix error 𝑀𝑇MT stays constant. An integer interval [𝑥,𝑥 +𝐿)[x,x+L) contains at least 𝜎𝑇𝐿 −𝑀𝑇σTL−MT integers avoiding that prefix. The remaining moduli cover at most 𝐿𝜃𝑇 +𝑘(𝑥 +𝐿)LθT+k(x+L) integers. Choose 𝑇T large enough that 𝜎𝑇 >𝜃𝑇σT>θT, and then any 𝑐 >(𝜎𝑇 −𝜃𝑇)−1c>(σT−θT)−1. For 𝐿 =⌈𝑐ℓ(𝑥)⌉L=⌈cℓ(x)⌉, the number left is positive for all sufficiently large 𝑥x, since 𝑘(𝑥 +𝐿) =ℓ(𝑥) +𝑂(1)k(x+L)=ℓ(x)+O(1). Thus the set being enumerated is unbounded, and every sufficiently late such interval meets it. Applying this at 𝑥 =𝑢𝑛 +1x=un+1 gives

lim sup𝑛𝑢𝑛+1−𝑢𝑛ℓ(𝑢𝑛)≤(𝜎𝑇−𝜃𝑇)−1.lim supnun+1−unℓ(un)≤(σT−θT)−1.

For the lower bound, fix 𝑇T and let the integer length 𝐿L tend to infinity. Among the offsets 0 ≤𝑗 <𝐿0≤j<L, precisely 𝐾𝐿 =𝜎𝑇𝐿 +𝑂(𝑀𝑇)KL=σTL+O(MT) avoid the first 𝑇T moduli. For the Fermat moduli 𝑚𝑗 =22𝑗 +1mj=22j+1, pairwise coprimality and the finite-product identity

𝑁∏𝑗=0(1−122𝑗+1)=22𝑁+1−122𝑁+1−1∏j=0N(1−122j+1)=22N+1−122N+1−1

They do not construct multipliers or numerator and denominator sequences satisfying the exact recurrences. In particular, the static proportion 𝜎σ must not be substituted for 𝜑(𝑣𝑇)/𝑣𝑇φ(vT)/vT: the first excludes multiples of the whole moduli, while the second excludes every prime factor of the reduced denominator.
