Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

Erdős #1041 By Will Cook 29 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 86 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For a monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc, any two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1} by a path of length less than 2. For any squarefree monic polynomial of degree 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, an area-growth argument gives such a path when the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/2513/25; scaling gives length less than (5/2)𝜇1/𝑛(5/2)μ1/n inside {|𝑓| <(25/13)𝜇}{|f|<(25/13)μ}. The record develops this argument, an inverse-ray averaging estimate, and a construction using an isolated simple critical value, together with results for collinear roots and several coefficient families. A weighted Poisson identity gives a sharp mean bound on critical values; examples distinguish that bound from path-length control. The final sections examine inverse-sheet topology and refute an earlier spanning-tree estimate. These results do not prove the unrestricted length-2 assertion.

Separating the rays of critical values

A constant perturbation translates the critical values and leaves the critical points fixed. We can therefore separate their positive rays by describing the exceptional translation parameters: two nonzero values collide in argument exactly when their ratio is positive real.

Along a Newton trajectory the argument of 𝑓f stays constant while its modulus decreases. By Corollary 11.2, arranging pairwise distinct arguments of the nonzero critical values is therefore a sufficient way to exclude finite connections between critical points. It is not asserted to be necessary. This condition imposes no lower bound on the distances between critical values and no upper bound on their moduli.

The weaker conditions really can coexist with a connection, even when all roots lie in the open unit disc. Take

𝑓(𝑧)=𝑧3−316𝑧+364.f(z)=z3−316z+364.

On |𝑧| =1|z|=1, the lower terms have total modulus at most 15/64 <115/64<1, so Rouché’s theorem places all three roots inside. The critical points are −1/4−1/4 and 1/41/4, with distinct positive values 5/645/64 and 1/641/64. On the intervening real interval 𝑓f is positive and strictly decreasing. The equation

𝑓(𝑥(𝑡))=564𝑒−𝑡,0<𝑡<log⁡5,f(x(t))=564e−t,0<t<log⁡5,

therefore defines a differentiable path with 𝑥′(𝑡) = −𝑓(𝑥(𝑡))/𝑓′(𝑥(𝑡))x′(t)=−f(x(t))/f′(x(t)). Its endpoint limits are −1/4−1/4 and 1/41/4. It is an actual critical-to-critical Newton trajectory, not merely a pair of values on one ray. The time convention is that of the value equation [7]; the singular field is not evaluated at the endpoints.

A common translation of distinct values can separate their arguments. The next calculation describes the translations to avoid.

Theorem 12.1 (ray-collision locus). Let 𝑎 ≠𝑏a≠b be complex. Every common translation 𝛽β for which 𝑎 +𝛽a+β and 𝑏 +𝛽b+β lie on the same positive ray has the form

𝛽=𝑟𝑎−𝑏1−𝑟,𝑟∈ℝ>0, 𝑟≠1.β=ra−b1−r,r∈R>0, r≠1.

Indeed, write 𝑏 +𝛽 =𝑟(𝑎 +𝛽)b+β=r(a+β) with 𝑟 >0r>0. Since 𝑎 ≠𝑏a≠b, one has 𝑟 ≠1r≠1, and solving for 𝛽β gives the displayed formula. Equivalently, 𝛽 = −𝑎 +(𝑎 −𝑏)/(1 −𝑟)β=−a+(a−b)/(1−r), so the forbidden translations lie on the real affine line through −𝑎−a and −𝑏−b.

A finite union of these loci has empty interior. Adding a constant to 𝑓f translates all critical values by that constant and leaves the critical points unchanged. Section 15 records the formal avoidance and root-retention estimates. A constant cannot separate equal critical values; coefficient perturbation and control of the remaining geometric margins are separate requirements.
