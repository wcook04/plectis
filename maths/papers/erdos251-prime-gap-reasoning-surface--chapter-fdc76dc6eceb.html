Prime-Gap Dyadic Series: Perturbations, Exact Criteria and Certificates

Erdős #251 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 47 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A convergent dyadic series with nonnegative integer coefficients can be made rational by sparse nonnegative corrections of upper Banach density zero, eventually bounded by any f(n)→∞, while preserving every fixed modulus and, for a logarithmic allowance, short-block empirical distributions. For prime gaps the cumulative positions remain asymptotic to n log n; no primality conclusion is asserted. Summation by parts reduces the prime series to the gap series, and the tails 𝑇𝑁 =∑𝑗≥1𝑔𝑁+𝑗2−𝑗TN=∑j≥1gN+j2−j satisfy 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1. That recurrence characterises rationality and yields finite tests with explicit remainder bounds. An exact finite certificate shows that a rational value of the prime series would have denominator at least 239998 >1012040239998>1012040. The bound does not establish irrationality.

Finite numerical searches

An enclosure must control both the omitted tail and arithmetic error. The scans supply no bound for accumulated double-precision rounding, so their counts remain observations even when the omitted tail is rigorously bounded. By contrast, Proposition 6.6 and Section 7 use exact integer comparisons with linked Lean checks.

Over the 6 841 6486841648 primes below 1.2 ×1081.2×108, the scan tests ℎ =1,…,16h=1,…,16 separately. For each offset it records indices satisfying the signed inequalities in Theorem 6.4, with observed proportions between 0.004180.00418 and 0.0082480.008248 and nearly balanced signs. The reported counts do not require one index to work for all offsets. At ℎ =1h=1 it records 56 42756427 hits among 6 841 5646841564 candidate indices, the last at the prime 119 995 753119995753. Multiplying the observed proportion in each band by that band’s mean of log⁡𝑝log⁡p gives 0.176710.17671 in the first band and 0.131480.13148 in the last. This finite observation establishes no asymptotic counting law and no occurrence beyond every cutoff. The search output is public.

A second scan uses the 1 270 6071270607 primes below 2 ×1072×107 and pairs of indices in the half-open range 1 143 545 ≤𝑛,𝑚 <1 270 5401143545≤n,m<1270540. It searches each residue class modulo each 1 ≤𝑡 ≤201≤t≤20 for the condition of Theorem 5.5, selecting pairs with a next-gap difference of 22 and a tail difference in the specified interval. At 𝑡 =20t=20, every residue class has at least 90 15090150 counted pairs; pairs may share indices. For each class, the program also records the larger index of a selected late witness. The minimum of those recorded indices is 1 270 5201270520; it need not come from the class with the fewest pairs. For the saved sample pairs, floating-point arithmetic gives both small-difference inequalities. The scan supplies neither arbitrary moduli nor witnesses beyond every prescribed cutoff.
