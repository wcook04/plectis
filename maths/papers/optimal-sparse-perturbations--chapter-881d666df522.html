Cross-problem paper

Reading Eight Erdős Problems Together

Eight Erdős problems read together By Will Cook 22 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 38 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub

Précis. One account of the mathematics developed across the programmes: an exact tail-capacity criterion under eventual congruences, its sharp factorial support-gap threshold, rational and irrational Lambert subsums across bases, and the limits of finite greedy tests and other irrationality methods. Full arguments, exact counterexamples, unsuccessful routes and attribution are retained together. The principal proofs are ordinary mathematics; cited Lean ingredients have their own stated scope. Historical novelty and independent expert review are not established.

This paper owns the synthesis exposition and ordinary proofs across the covered problems: capacity and congruence constructions, Lambert subsums, rational-point counts, method limits and their research record.

It is not authority for a solution to any original Erdős target, historical novelty, independent expert review, or a full Lean proof of the analytic capacity criterion or Lambert-chain theorem.

A common-divisor test for irrationality

The distinction between small and large allowances also appears directly in rationality. Let 𝑏𝑛 ≥2bn≥2 be integers, put 𝑄0 =1Q0=1 and 𝑄𝑛 =𝑏1⋯𝑏𝑛Qn=b1⋯bn, and consider a Cantor series.

Theorem 3.6. Suppose 0 ≤𝑎𝑛 ≤𝐴0≤an≤A and 𝑒𝑛 ≥0en≥0 are integers, 𝑒𝑛 ≤𝐶𝑏𝑛en≤Cbn eventually, and 𝑎𝑛 +𝑒𝑛an+en is nonzero infinitely often. If

lim sup𝑛→∞gcd(𝑏𝑛,𝑒𝑛)=∞,lim supn→∞gcd(bn,en)=∞,

then ∑𝑛≥1(𝑎𝑛 +𝑒𝑛)/𝑄𝑛∑n≥1(an+en)/Qn is irrational.

Proof. For large 𝑛n, the scaled tail satisfies

𝑇𝑛=𝐷𝑄𝑛(𝑝𝐷−∑𝑘≤𝑛𝑎𝑘+𝑒𝑘𝑄𝑘)Tn=DQn(pD−∑k≤nak+ekQk)

would be positive integers eventually bounded by 𝐷(𝐴 +2𝐶)D(A+2C). No hypothesis that 𝐷 ∣𝑄𝑛D∣Qn is needed: the extra factor 𝐷D clears it. The recurrence

𝑏𝑛𝑇𝑛−1=𝐷(𝑎𝑛+𝑒𝑛)+𝑇𝑛bnTn−1=D(an+en)+Tn

implies gcd(𝑏𝑛,𝑒𝑛) ∣𝐷𝑎𝑛 +𝑇𝑛gcd(bn,en)∣Dan+Tn. The integer on the right is positive and eventually at most 𝐷(2𝐴 +2𝐶)D(2A+2C), contradicting the unbounded gcd. ◻

For factorial denominators, 𝑏𝑛 =𝑛bn=n, and eventual divisibility of 𝑒𝑛en by each fixed integer makes gcd(𝑛,𝑒𝑛)gcd(n,en) arbitrarily large arbitrarily late: choose a late multiple of that integer. Hence bounded nonnegative digits that are nonzero infinitely often cannot be rationalised by nonnegative 𝑂(𝑛)O(n) corrections satisfying (2). Conversely any allowance 𝐹(𝑛)F(n) with 𝐹(𝑛)/𝑛 →∞F(n)/n→∞ permits interval filling under (4): apply the positive construction with 𝑟𝑛 =𝑛rn=n, replacing 𝑛𝑐nc by 𝐹(𝑛)F(n).

The arithmetic hypothesis cannot be replaced by 𝑏𝑛 →∞bn→∞, even if both congruences (4) are retained. Set 𝑎𝑛 =1an=1, 𝑒1 =2e1=2, and 𝑒𝑛 =(𝑛 +1)! −𝑛!en=(n+1)!−n! for 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, and let 𝑏𝑛 =𝑒𝑛 +2bn=en+2. Then ∑𝑘≤𝑛𝑒𝑘 =(𝑛 +1)!∑k≤nek=(n+1)!, so (4) holds, and 𝑒𝑛 <𝑏𝑛en<bn. Nevertheless

∑𝑛≥1𝑎𝑛+𝑒𝑛𝑄𝑛=∑𝑛≥1𝑏𝑛−1𝑄𝑛=1.∑n≥1an+enQn=∑n≥1bn−1Qn=1.
