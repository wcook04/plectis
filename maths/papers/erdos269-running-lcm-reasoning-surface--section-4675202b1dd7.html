Running least common multiples: distinct values and multiplicities

Erdős #269 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 5 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 73 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The reciprocal three-prime running-LCM kernel has nonsingular minors of every order: row and column rescaling leaves a two-valued matrix, and density supplies the required threshold pattern. The record computes the rank of every finite restriction and the least uniform approximation error over all matrices of finite separated rank. For the repeated {2,3,5} sum it derives an integer-coefficient tail recurrence and a residue criterion equivalent to irrationality. The unproved step is to find such windows for every positive multiplier coprime to 30 and after every prescribed start. It also gives the full two-prime comparison, following Fan's earlier Hecke–Mahler reduction, and identifies the boundary terms that survive a proposed finite-difference cancellation.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #269, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #269, which remains open.

Two sums from running least common multiples

Let 𝑃P be a finite set of at least two primes, and let S𝑃SP consist of 11 and all positive integers whose prime factors belong to 𝑃P. For 𝑥 ≥1x≥1, put

L⁡(𝑥)=lcm⁡{𝑢∈S𝑃:𝑢≤𝑥},H𝑃⁡(𝑥)=∏𝑝∈𝑃𝑝⌊log𝑝⁡𝑥⌋.L⁡(x)=lcm⁡{u∈SP:u≤x},HP⁡(x)=∏p∈Pp⌊logp⁡x⌋.

Every integer in this finite set divides H𝑃⁡(𝑥)HP⁡(x), and the set contains the largest power of each prime in 𝑃P below or equal to 𝑥x. Their product therefore equals the least common multiple: L⁡(𝑥) =H𝑃⁡(𝑥)L⁡(x)=HP⁡(x). The value changes only at a positive prime power. Summing its distinct reciprocals gives

D𝑃=1+∑𝑡=𝑝𝑛𝑝∈𝑃, 𝑛≥11H𝑃⁡(𝑡).DP=1+∑t=pnp∈P, n≥11HP⁡(t).

We begin with an irrationality result for three primes.

Theorem 1.1 (the sum over distinct running LCMs at {2,3,5}{2,3,5}). The sum D{2,3,5}D{2,3,5} is irrational.

The proof in Section 2 gives the primes three different roles. Powers of 22 delimit the blocks; powers of 55 occur often enough to separate the five affine tail images; powers of 33 have an irrational block frequency. Separation makes each tail determine the next block and tail. Under rationality there are only finitely many tails, so repetition would force an impossible periodic block sequence.

Erdős asserted the general conclusion for every finite 𝑃P with |𝑃| ≥2|P|≥2 in his letter of 1 January 1973, without printing a proof [3]. The theorem above is a special case of that assertion. The general argument in Section 5.1 compares nearby tails and finds an isolated interchange of jumps on a torus. We retain it as an ordinary argument awaiting formalisation, with its intermediate claims stated as remarks. The specialised five-map proof is independent of that argument.

The question in Erdős Problem #269 retains multiplicities. If 𝑎1 <𝑎2 <⋯a1<a2<⋯ enumerates S𝑃SP, it asks about

R𝑃=∑𝑛≥11[𝑎1,…,𝑎𝑛]=∑𝑢∈S𝑃1L⁡(𝑢)RP=∑n≥11[a1,…,an]=∑u∈SP1L⁡(u)

[1][2]; the numbering is Bloom’s [4]. For 𝑃 ={2,3,5}P={2,3,5}, the integers 55 and 66 both have running LCM 6060. They contribute 2/602/60 to R𝑃RP and 1/601/60 to D𝑃DP. Thus Theorem 1.1 concerns the sum over distinct values, whereas the problem concerns the sum with multiplicity. The arguments in this paper leave R𝑃RP unresolved when 𝑃P is finite and |𝑃| ≥3|P|≥3.

For two primes the conclusion is stronger.

Theorem 1.2 (two-prime transcendence). Let 𝑝p and 𝑞q be distinct primes. Then R{𝑝,𝑞}R{p,q} and D{𝑝,𝑞}D{p,q} are transcendental.

Fan’s post of 26 June 2026 gave the finite-prime running-LCM identity, the factorisation of R{𝑝,𝑞}R{p,q}, and the Hecke–Mahler reduction with its transcendence conclusion [17]. We reproduce that calculation in Section 4. Both values are nonconstant polynomials over ℚQ in one boundary sum, whose transcendence follows from Loxton and van der Poorten [16], in the formulation of Bugeaud and Laurent [15]. No priority is claimed for this deduction.

Retaining multiplicities preserves the bases but changes the numerators. The repeated tails satisfy 𝑋𝑎+1 =𝑏𝑎𝑋𝑎 −𝑚𝑎Xa+1=baXa−ma, with 𝑏𝑎 ∈{2,6,10,30}ba∈{2,6,10,30}, while both 𝑚𝑎ma and 𝑋𝑎Xa grow quadratically. The preceding finite-tail repetition argument no longer applies. Rationality would still make every sufficiently late 𝐵𝑋𝑎BXa integral, where 𝐵B is the denominator part coprime to 3030. A finite segment excludes such an integer tail when its least positive residue exceeds the quadratic bound. Irrationality is equivalent to finding these segments for every 𝐵B beyond every prescribed onset. The finite computations do not establish either unbounded quantifier.

The five-map proof comes first. Sections 3 and 4 then supply the running LCM geometry and two-prime formulae. For general distinct sums, Section 5.1 uses comparison of two tails rather than recovery from one; the finite-state, subseries and linear-flow extensions follow as ordinary arguments pending formalisation. Section 7 separates exact kernel rank from approximation. For the repeated target, Sections 8–10 derive the coefficients, bounds and residue criterion; the remaining sections distinguish finite certificates from missing arithmetic inputs. Section 12.5 records formal coverage. The short paper concentrates on the five-map theorem and the repeated-tail criterion.

We use ℕ ={0,1,2,…}N={0,1,2,…} for exponents and braces for fractional parts. In the finite-geometry section and in the kernel calculation, 𝑃 ={𝑝,𝑞,𝑟}P={p,q,r} consists of three pairwise distinct primes, H =H𝑃H=HP, and

K⁡(𝑖,𝑗,𝑘)=H⁡(𝑝𝑖𝑞𝑗𝑟𝑘)−1.K⁡(i,j,k)=H⁡(piqjrk)−1.

The other sections specify their prime sets separately. For {2,3,5}{2,3,5}, S𝑃SP consists of the usual 55-smooth integers. This fixed support differs from a varying smoothness bound in Ψ(𝑥,𝑦)Ψ(x,y) [11]. Tijdeman and Meijer [28] and the two-prime study of Languasco, Luca, Moree and Togbé [29] give the relevant lattice-counting context; the estimates below use no smooth-number density asymptotic or gap theorem. Kovač and Tao [13] study other unit-fraction series by elementary methods, without providing an input to these proofs.

Keywords. irrationality; transcendence; least common multiple; smooth numbers; Cantor series; separated rank. MSC 2020. 11J72 (primary); 11A05, 11N25, 68V20 (secondary).
