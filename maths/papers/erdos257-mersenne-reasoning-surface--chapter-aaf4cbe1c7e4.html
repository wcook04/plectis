Long paper

Reciprocal Mersenne Subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 153 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A weighted summability criterion proves irrationality of reciprocal Mersenne subseries: if h is the part of a supported on a finite nonempty prime set P, every infinite A with ∑ ℎ(𝑎)/(𝑎(𝑏ℎ(𝑎) −1))h(a)/(a(bh(a)−1)) finite has irrational ∑ (𝑏𝑎 −1)−1(ba−1)−1. The base-two condition gives the conclusion at every integer base for every infinite subset, and permits an example with divergent reciprocal sum, while excluding full support and all odd exponents. The proof averages over a finite part of the support and over dyadic observation lengths. Erdős's weaker reciprocal-summable theorem is proved in full, and a common window extends both arguments to unions of weighted and positive-cover supports. Finite-denominator and achievement-set calculations formulate tests for the targets 1/21/2 and 1/211/21; those tests do not decide either target or the universal question. Prime support at base 2 and squarefree support at power-of-two bases are attributed as existing work.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #257: selected checked premises, finite computations, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, whose universal and half-value questions remain open.

Divisor residues and the changing skip set

Expanding each divisor count separates its residue conditions. This is a finite counting identity. It does not prescribe whether an eventual proof should be analytic, combinatorial, or use another method.

A finite divisor cutoff makes the averaging question precise. For integers 𝐷 ≥2D≥2 and 𝐿 ≥1L≥1, define

Ψ𝐿,𝐷(𝑥)=𝐷∑𝑑=2𝐿∑𝑖=12−𝑖𝟏𝑑∣𝑥+𝑖,𝑄𝐷=lcm⁡(1,…,𝐷).ΨL,D(x)=∑d=2D∑i=1L2−i1d∣x+i,QD=lcm⁡(1,…,D).

This is the cutoff sum of Lemma 11.2. It is periodic in 𝑥x with period 𝑄𝐷QD, and Θ𝐿(𝑀) =Ψ𝐿,𝐷(𝑀)ΘL(M)=ΨL,D(M) whenever 𝑀 ≥1M≥1 and 𝐷 ≥𝑀 +𝐿D≥M+L. Averaging each indicator over a complete period gives

1𝑄𝐷𝑄𝐷−1∑𝑥=0Ψ𝐿,𝐷(𝑥)=(1−2−𝐿)𝐷∑𝑑=21𝑑.1QD∑x=0QD−1ΨL,D(x)=(1−2−L)∑d=2D1d.

The mean diverges as 𝐷 →∞D→∞, even for fixed 𝐿L. Thus this family does not converge to a finite integrable observable whose limiting mean could simply be used in an equidistribution argument.

The same issue arises if one uses the profinite integers ˆℤZ^, whose Haar probability measure is uniform on each finite residue quotient. The finite-cutoff functions are continuous there. Their increasing limit is infinite almost everywhere: the events 𝑝 ∣𝑥 +1p∣x+1 are independent over primes by the Chinese remainder theorem, have probabilities 1/𝑝1/p, and occur infinitely often almost surely. Each contributes 1/21/2 to the term at offset 11. The positive integer inputs 𝑀M, for which Θ𝐿(𝑀)ΘL(M) is finite, do not justify treating this divergent limit as a continuous or integrable function. Only the finite period average above is used in the present argument.

There is a related residue expression in Theorem 5.15, but its index and divisor set differ:

∑𝑑∈𝐷𝑛{4𝑛2𝑑−1}=∑𝑑∈𝐷𝑛2−𝑖𝑑(2𝑛)1−2−𝑑.∑d∈Dn{4n2d−1}=∑d∈Dn2−id(2n)1−2−d.

Indeed 𝑖𝑑(2𝑛) =𝑑 −(2𝑛mod𝑑)id(2n)=d−(2nmodd), with value 𝑑d when the remainder is zero. The argument is 2𝑛2n, not 𝑛n, because the quotient scale is 4𝑛 =22𝑛4n=22n. For example, at 𝑛 =7,𝑑 =3n=7,d=3 the fractional part is 4/74/7; using 𝑖3(7)i3(7) would instead give 2/72/7. This sum uses the selected finite set 𝐷𝑛Dn, whereas Θ𝐿ΘL uses all divisors with offsets truncated at 𝐿L. Their common geometric summand does not make the two sums equal.

To keep the changing support visible, put 𝑆𝑛 =∑𝑑∈Skip𝑛⌊4𝑛/(2𝑑 −1)⌋Sn=∑d∈Skipn⌊4n/(2d−1)⌋. The master identity Δ𝑛 =𝐾(2𝑛) +𝑆𝑛Δn=K(2n)+Sn and the recurrence for 𝐾K give, for 𝑛 ≥6n≥6 and ℓ ≥1ℓ≥1,

2−2ℓΔ𝑛+ℓ=Δ𝑛−Θ2ℓ(2𝑛)+2−2ℓ𝑆𝑛+ℓ−𝑆𝑛.2−2ℓΔn+ℓ=Δn−Θ2ℓ(2n)+2−2ℓSn+ℓ−Sn.

A phase estimate would have to control this additional term and identify the required reset rows before implying the reset bound. No such implication is proved here. The scales also differ from a logarithmic-window assertion: ℓℓ quotient transitions use 2ℓ2ℓ binary steps starting at 𝑀 =2𝑛M=2n. If ℓℓ is proportional to 𝑛n, the phase length is comparable to 𝑀M, not to log⁡𝑀log⁡M. A theorem for logarithmic windows would need a separate argument connecting its range to the reset condition.

Finite residue expansions, divisor-sum estimates and probabilistic local estimates are possible sources of further information. None is an input already supplied here. An application must state the divisor cutoff, offset length, error at the required precision, and control on the changing skip-set sum, rather than appeal to the name of a method or to equidistribution of a different statistic.
