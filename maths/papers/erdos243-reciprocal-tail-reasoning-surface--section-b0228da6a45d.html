Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 61 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. For rational reciprocal sums under 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, the record also develops sufficient conditions for the eventual recurrence 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1, controlling either increases of an integer tail numerator or a convergent sum at steps setting new maxima. Examples separate numerical growth bounds from the exact recurrences. The cubic proof is distinguished from the theorems formalised in Lean. None of the additional bounds is derived for every rational reciprocal sum in Erdős #243.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #243, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

Growth of the running maximum

Theorem 7.3 (increments of the running maximum). Let Θ =lim sup𝑛(𝐻𝑛+1 −𝐻𝑛)/ℓ(𝐻𝑛)Θ=lim supn(Hn+1−Hn)/ℓ(Hn) on a rational-tail orbit under the standing hypotheses, and suppose 𝐸𝑛En is not eventually zero. Then either 𝐺𝑛Gn is unbounded and ΘΘ is infinite, or 𝐺𝑛Gn stabilises at a value 𝑔g and Θ ≥𝑔 𝑣𝑇/𝜑(𝑣𝑇)Θ≥gvT/φ(vT) for every late 𝑇T, so that Θ >𝑔 ≥1Θ>g≥1. For any orbit under the standing hypotheses, therefore, Θ =0Θ=0 or Θ >1Θ>1, and Θ ≤1Θ≤1 forces the eventual Sylvester recurrence.

The constant in the next condition is measured against a double logarithm, not against 𝐶𝑛Cn. This grows much more slowly than any positive power of 𝐶𝑛Cn. Every fixed bound on ( −𝐸𝑛)+(−En)+ satisfies it on a nonzero tail because 𝐶𝑛 →∞Cn→∞; it also permits unbounded negative parts on the double-logarithmic scale, with the coefficient specified below. In contrast, an error of size √𝐶𝑛Cn has relative size tending to zero but violates the condition. These are comparisons of bounds, not constructions of reciprocal tails.

Corollary 7.4 (the double-logarithmic bound). If

lim sup𝑛(−𝐸𝑛)+ℓ(𝐶𝑛)≤1,lim supn(−En)+ℓ(Cn)≤1,

then the sequence is eventually Sylvester. Every counterexample therefore satisfies

lim sup𝑛(−𝐸𝑛)+ℓ(𝐶𝑛)>1.lim supn(−En)+ℓ(Cn)>1.

Proof of Theorem 7.3 and Corollary 7.4. We first prove the auxiliary lower bound Θ ≥1Θ≥1. This part needs only an exact positive integer orbit with 𝑎𝑛 >1an>1, 𝐷0 ≥1D0≥1, |𝐸𝑛|/𝐶𝑛 →0|En|/Cn→0 and error not eventually zero. In particular, it will also apply to the abstract orbit in Theorem 7.11. The key input is that, outside a set of indices of density zero, 𝑎𝑛an is coprime to the entire preceding denominator 𝐷𝑛Dn. This supplies almost one new coprime modulus per step. The denominator growth then places the resulting CRT block at the required double-logarithmic height.

Integer solutions of the recurrences.

The strict bound Θ >1Θ>1 is also a Lean theorem for solutions of (19) with 𝑎𝑛 >1an>1, 𝐶𝑛 >0Cn>0, 𝐷0 ≥1D0≥1 and vanishing relative error whose error is not eventually zero (strict record bound). The implication of Corollary 7.4, that lim sup𝑛( −𝐸𝑛)+/ℓ(𝐶𝑛) ≤1lim supn(−En)+/ℓ(Cn)≤1 forces 𝐸𝑛 =0En=0 eventually, is a Lean theorem for solutions of (19) with 𝑎𝑛 >1an>1, 𝐶𝑛 >0Cn>0 and vanishing relative error (double-logarithmic bound for an exact orbit); there 𝐷0 ≥1D0≥1 follows from the vanishing relative error. The Lean sources also contain pointwise-rise versions of the arithmetic steps: persistence of a common divisor, a CRT block starting in [𝑃,2𝑃)[P,2P), the landing of a slowly rising numerator in that block and the resulting exclusion. Their landing bound controls increments of 𝐶𝑛Cn; the argument above controls increments of 𝐻𝑛Hn.

No upper bound on ΘΘ is proved. If 𝐺𝑛Gn is unbounded, the theorem forces Θ = +∞Θ=+∞. If 𝐺𝑛 =𝑔Gn=g eventually, it proves only Θ ≥𝑔 𝑣𝑇/𝜑(𝑣𝑇)Θ≥gvT/φ(vT) for every late 𝑇T.
