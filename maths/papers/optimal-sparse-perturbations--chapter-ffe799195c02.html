Cross-problem paper

Reading Eight Erdős Problems Together

Eight Erdős problems read together By Will Cook 22 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 38 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub

Précis. One account of the mathematics developed across the programmes: an exact tail-capacity criterion under eventual congruences, its sharp factorial support-gap threshold, rational and irrational Lambert subsums across bases, and the limits of finite greedy tests and other irrationality methods. Full arguments, exact counterexamples, unsuccessful routes and attribution are retained together. The principal proofs are ordinary mathematics; cited Lean ingredients have their own stated scope. Historical novelty and independent expert review are not established.

This paper owns the synthesis exposition and ordinary proofs across the covered problems: capacity and congruence constructions, Lambert subsums, rational-point counts, method limits and their research record.

It is not authority for a solution to any original Erdős target, historical novelty, independent expert review, or a full Lean proof of the analytic capacity criterion or Lambert-chain theorem.

Proof of the capacity criterion

The necessity and sufficiency use different parts of the expansion. Prefixes supply an arithmetic obstruction; a common interval of possible future remainders makes the residue choices compatible with exact representation.

Bounded capacity along a subsequence

If 𝑈𝑁UN does not tend to infinity, there are a constant 𝐵 >0B>0 and arbitrarily large 𝑁N with 𝑈𝑁 ≤𝐵UN≤B. Choose one integer 𝑞 >𝐵q>B. Fix a cutoff 𝐾K after which the allowance and divisibility by 𝑞q hold, and fix the finite prefix, of value 𝑦0y0. The set 𝑉V of resulting sums is compact by summability and the finite choices at each remaining position. For every such 𝑁 >𝐾N>K,

𝑉⊆𝑦0+𝑞𝑄𝑁ℤ+[0,𝐵/𝑄𝑁].V⊆y0+qQNZ+[0,B/QN].

Indeed the scaled prefix after 𝐾K is a multiple of 𝑞q and the remaining tail is at most 𝑈𝑁/𝑄𝑁UN/QN. These grids leave holes inside every fixed open interval for all sufficiently large selected 𝑁N, so 𝑉V is nowhere dense. For a bounded interval 𝐽J they also give |𝑉 ∩𝐽| ≤(𝐵/𝑞)|𝐽| +2𝐵/𝑄𝑁|V∩J|≤(B/q)|J|+2B/QN. Passing to the subsequence and covering 𝑉V by finitely many intervals with total length at most |𝑉| +𝜀|V|+ε gives |𝑉| ≤(𝐵/𝑞)(|𝑉| +𝜀)|V|≤(B/q)(|V|+ε), hence |𝑉| =0|V|=0. A countable union over the cutoffs and integer prefixes covers 𝐸𝑄(𝐹)EQ(F).

One continuation interval for every cumulative residue

Suppose 𝑈𝑁 →∞UN→∞ and put ℎ𝑁 =inf𝑘≥𝑁𝑈𝑘hN=infk≥NUk. For sufficiently large 𝑛n, let 𝑀𝑛Mn be the largest factorial at most min{𝑛,√ℎ⌊𝑛/2⌋}min{n,h⌊n/2⌋}, and set the finitely many earlier moduli equal to one. These positive integers are nested under divisibility, tend to infinity, and are eventually divisible by each fixed integer. The delayed lower envelope controls the whole future cost of rounding, even when the denominator ratios are unbounded. Indeed, 𝑄𝑁/𝑄𝑘 ≤2𝑁−𝑘QN/Qk≤2N−k, so for sufficiently large 𝑁N,

𝐶𝑁:=𝑄𝑁∑𝑘>𝑁𝑀𝑘𝑄𝑘≤√ℎ𝑁2𝑁∑𝑘=𝑁+12𝑁−𝑘+∑𝑘>2𝑁𝑘2𝑁−𝑘≤√ℎ𝑁+(2𝑁+2)2−𝑁.CN:=QN∑k>NMkQk≤hN∑k=N+12N2N−k+∑k>2Nk2N−k≤hN+(2N+2)2−N.

The first bound uses ⌊𝑘/2⌋ ≤𝑁⌊k/2⌋≤N when 𝑘 ≤2𝑁k≤2N; the second uses 𝑀𝑘 ≤𝑘Mk≤k. Since 𝑈𝑁 ≥ℎ𝑁 →∞UN≥hN→∞ and 𝑀𝑁 ≤√ℎ𝑁MN≤hN, this proves 𝑈𝑁 −2𝐶𝑁 ≥𝑀𝑁UN−2CN≥MN for every sufficiently large 𝑁N. Start beyond this point and any prescribed cutoff.

Keep only the active positions 𝑆 ={𝑛 :𝐹𝑛 ≥2𝑀𝑛}S={n:Fn≥2Mn} after this cutoff. Define two common tail bounds

𝛼𝑁=∑𝑛>𝑁𝑛∈𝑆𝑀𝑛𝑄𝑛,𝛽𝑁=∑𝑛>𝑁𝑛∈𝑆𝐹𝑛−𝑀𝑛𝑄𝑛.αN=∑n>Nn∈SMnQn,βN=∑n>Nn∈SFn−MnQn.

Both are nonnegative and tend to zero. Discarding an inactive position loses less than 2𝑀𝑛2Mn; reserving both margins at an active position loses exactly 2𝑀𝑛2Mn. Thus the preceding estimate gives the decisive overlap inequality

𝑄𝑁(𝛽𝑁−𝛼𝑁)≥𝑈𝑁−2𝐶𝑁≥𝑀𝑁.(O)(O)QN(βN−αN)≥UN−2CN≥MN.

In particular the active set is infinite and each continuation interval has positive width.

Choose a target in [𝛼𝑁0,𝛽𝑁0][αN0,βN0] and set all preceding digits to zero. Suppose the current unweighted cumulative sum is 𝐶C and the remaining target before position 𝑛n lies in [𝛼𝑛−1,𝛽𝑛−1][αn−1,βn−1]. At an inactive position choose zero. At an active position, the permitted digits in the residue class 𝑒 ≡ −𝐶(mod𝑀𝑛)e≡−C(modMn), 0 ≤𝑒 ≤𝐹𝑛0≤e≤Fn, form a nonempty arithmetic progression of step 𝑀𝑛Mn. Its least digit is less than 𝑀𝑛Mn and its greatest digit exceeds 𝐹𝑛 −𝑀𝑛Fn−Mn. By (O), the intervals

𝑒𝑄𝑛+[𝛼𝑛,𝛽𝑛]eQn+[αn,βn]

for these digits overlap. Their union contains

[𝑀𝑛𝑄𝑛+𝛼𝑛,𝐹𝑛−𝑀𝑛𝑄𝑛+𝛽𝑛]=[𝛼𝑛−1,𝛽𝑛−1].[MnQn+αn,Fn−MnQn+βn]=[αn−1,βn−1].

Choose a digit whose interval contains the remaining target. The next remainder stays between 𝛼𝑛αn and 𝛽𝑛βn, and these bounds tend to zero, proving exact representation. This is precisely the common-interval feedback construction; no target-independent ordinary block sum is required.

After an active position 𝑛n, the cumulative sum is divisible by 𝑀𝑛Mn. Each later active digit is divisible by the previous active modulus, because both adjacent cumulative sums are; the moduli are nested. For a fixed 𝑞q, take one active position with 𝑞 ∣𝑀𝑛q∣Mn. After that position, all individual digits and all preceding cumulative sums are divisible by 𝑞q. This cutoff is common to all targets in the interval.

From one interval to every nonnegative target

The finite-exception convention in 𝐸𝑄(𝐹)EQ(F) strengthens the conclusion. Given 𝑦 >0y>0, choose 𝑁N arbitrarily late with 𝛼𝑁 <𝑦αN<y and put

𝑝=𝑀𝑁⌊𝑄𝑁(𝑦−𝛼𝑁)𝑀𝑁⌋.p=MN⌊QN(y−αN)MN⌋.

Then 𝑝 ≥0p≥0, 𝑀𝑁 ∣𝑝MN∣p, and (O) gives 𝑦 −𝑝/𝑄𝑁 ∈[𝛼𝑁,𝛽𝑁]y−p/QN∈[αN,βN]. Set 𝑒𝑁 =𝑝eN=p and all earlier digits to zero. Run the same continuation construction after 𝑁N, with initial cumulative sum 𝐶 =𝑝C=p; its interval covering worked for every 𝐶C. All later digits obey their allowances. The zero target uses zero digits.

The congruence cutoffs can also be common to all 𝑦y. For fixed 𝑞q, choose an active position 𝑗j with 𝑞 ∣𝑀𝑗q∣Mj. If the target’s exceptional index 𝑁N precedes 𝑗j, the repair at 𝑗j gives the required divisibilities after 𝑗j. If 𝑁 ≥𝑗N≥j, all preceding digits are zero, 𝑞 ∣𝑀𝑁 ∣𝑝q∣MN∣p, and all subsequent repairs preserve divisibility by 𝑞q. Thus 𝑗 +1j+1 is a valid cutoff for every target. The allowance-exception index itself may depend on the target. This completes Theorem 3.1.

The growth hypothesis has content. If repeated denominators are allowed, take 𝑄𝑛 =2⌊√𝑛⌋Qn=2⌊n⌋ and 𝐹𝑛 =1Fn=1. The allowance series converges, while 𝑈𝑁 ≥⌊√𝑁⌋UN≥⌊N⌋ by counting the next complete denominator block. Yet eventual divisibility by two forces all sufficiently late digits to vanish, so the attainable set is countable. Imposing all cumulative congruences as well leaves only zero. Thus nestedness alone does not justify the criterion.
