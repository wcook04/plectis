Running least common multiples: distinct values and multiplicities

Erdős #269 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 74 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. The reciprocal three-prime running-LCM kernel has nonsingular minors of every order: row and column rescaling leaves a two-valued matrix, and density supplies the required threshold pattern. The record computes the rank of every finite restriction and the least uniform approximation error over all matrices of finite separated rank. For the repeated {2,3,5} sum it derives an integer-coefficient tail recurrence and a residue criterion equivalent to irrationality. The unproved step is to find such windows for every positive multiplier coprime to 30 and after every prescribed start. It also gives the full two-prime comparison, following Fan's earlier Hecke–Mahler reduction, and identifies the boundary terms that survive a proposed finite-difference cancellation.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #269, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

The logarithmic flow.

Consider

For 𝜃 ∈𝕋θ∈T, a 𝑞q-crossing is a time 𝑡t at which 𝜃𝑞 +𝑡 ∈(log⁡𝑞)ℤθq+t∈(log⁡q)Z. At 𝜃𝑘 =Θ(log⁡𝑡𝑘)θk=Θ(log⁡tk) the positive crossings occur at log⁡(𝑞𝑗/𝑡𝑘)log⁡(qj/tk) with 𝑞𝑗 >𝑡𝑘qj>tk, so their labels, read in time order, are 𝑞𝑘+1𝑞𝑘+2⋯qk+1qk+2⋯. Crossings at this phase never coincide.

Let 𝑇0T0 be the closure of Θ(ℝ)Θ(R). Every forward orbit in 𝑇0T0 is dense there. Indeed, the pigeonhole principle gives arbitrarily large 𝑑 >0d>0 with Θ(𝑑)Θ(d) arbitrarily close to 00, so negative times are limits of positive ones. Translation then proves the same assertion for every starting point and every later starting time.

In the normalised coordinates 𝜑𝑞 =𝜃𝑞/log⁡𝑞φq=θq/log⁡q, Kronecker’s theorem identifies 𝑇0T0 with the image of the smallest rational subspace 𝐿 ⊆ℝ𝑃L⊆RP containing 𝜔 =(1/log⁡𝑞)𝑞∈𝑃ω=(1/log⁡q)q∈P. Any two coordinate forms 𝜑𝑎,𝜑𝑏φa,φb are linearly independent on 𝐿L. To see this, their dependence would put a nonzero vector supported on {𝑎,𝑏}{a,b} in the rational annihilator of 𝐿L. That intersection is rational, so it would contain a nonzero integer vector. Its pairing with 𝜔ω would force log⁡𝑎/log⁡𝑏 ∈ℚlog⁡a/log⁡b∈Q, contrary to unique factorisation. In particular, (log⁡𝑏)𝜑𝑏 −(log⁡𝑎)𝜑𝑎(log⁡b)φb−(log⁡a)φa is a nonzero form on 𝐿L. We write it as 𝜃𝑏 −𝜃𝑎θb−θa when using real representatives.

To interchange just two jumps, we seek a phase at which exactly those two crossings coincide. The next argument finds such a phase inside 𝑇0T0; it does not require this orbit closure to be the whole torus 𝕋T.

Remark 5.5 (two walls). If |𝑃| ≥4|P|≥4, there is 𝑥 ∈𝑇0x∈T0 with 𝜃𝑞(𝑥) =0θq(x)=0 for exactly two primes 𝑞 ∈𝑃q∈P.

Proof. Choose a rational plane in 𝐿L on which every pair of coordinate forms remains independent. Such a plane exists: for each pair, the determinant on 𝐿 ×𝐿L×L is a nonzero polynomial, and finitely many of these polynomials cannot vanish on all rational pairs. An integer basis of the plane identifies its image with a two-dimensional subtorus of 𝑇0T0. On this subtorus each wall 𝜃𝑞 =0θq=0 has the equation ⟨𝛾𝑞,𝑠⟩ ∈ℤ⟨γq,s⟩∈Z, where 𝛾𝑞 ∈ℤ2 ∖{0}γq∈Z2∖{0}, and the vectors 𝛾𝑞γq are pairwise nonparallel. A wall may have several components if 𝛾𝑞γq is not primitive.

The walls form a finite arrangement of closed geodesics. Let 𝑘𝑣kv count the wall families through a vertex 𝑣v. Each such family contributes two incident half-edges, so 𝐸 =∑𝑣𝑘𝑣E=∑vkv. Two families alone divide the torus into open parallelograms: their two integer linear forms give a finite covering of the standard torus. Adding the remaining straight lines subdivides these parallelograms into convex polygons, each with at least three sides. Thus 2𝐸 ≥3𝐹2E≥3F, while Euler’s formula gives 𝑉 −𝐸 +𝐹 =0V−E+F=0. Together these imply

∑𝑣(𝑘𝑣−3)=𝐸−3𝑉≤0.∑v(kv−3)=E−3V≤0.

Every vertex belongs to at least two wall families, and the last inequality says that the average number of families through a vertex is at most three. The origin belongs to all |𝑃| ≥4|P|≥4 families. Another vertex must therefore belong to exactly two, as required. ◻
