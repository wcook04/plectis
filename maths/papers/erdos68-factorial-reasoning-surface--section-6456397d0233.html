Factorial Linear Forms and Denominators: Detailed Proofs and Rationality Criteria

Erdős #68 By Will Cook 1 September 2026; revised 7 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 51 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The record proves liminf (log LN)/(N3/2 log N) ≥2√2/3≥22/3 for L_N=lcm(2!−1,…,N!−1), by comparing a terminal block of denominators with pairwise gcds rather than an external multiplicity theorem. Specialising a Louwsma–Martino valuation, it exhibits primes 139 and 2593 absent from all later reduced denominators. Coefficient constructions give integer forms MS+k; at fixed moment the remainder changes only by an integer. Rationality criteria use factorial digits and the next integer above a scaled partial sum; the digit argument also recovers the irrationality of e. Two exact calculations give q∤299999! and 𝑞 ≥239990 >1012038q≥239990>1012038. These exclusions do not prove irrationality.

Carries and factorial digits

Instead of clearing the partial sum, we now track the only integer that could equal 𝑚!𝑆m!S. Under rationality, 𝑚!𝑆m!S is integral for all large 𝑚m; since the positive tail is less than one, it must be the strict successor of 𝑚!𝐻𝑚m!Hm. 𝑍𝑚=⌊𝑚!𝐻𝑚⌋+1,Δ𝑚=𝑍𝑚−1−(𝑚−1)!𝐻𝑚−1(𝑚≥3).Zm=⌊m!Hm⌋+1,Δm=Zm−1−(m−1)!Hm−1(m≥3).

Compare the factorial scaling in Hančl and Tijdeman’s tail-integrality lemma for factorial series with integer coefficients [11]. Their scaled partial sums are integers; ours need not be, since already 3!𝐻3 =36/53!H3=36/5. The next proof instead uses the short positive tail to identify the least integer above a scaled partial sum under a rationality assumption. The carry-defect expansion in §B.2 gives a separate, direct application of factorial-series rationality criteria.

The adjacent-unit window condition

Two successive unit carries imply a prime-power size condition after clearing denominators in their defining inequalities. The calculation below shows that the condition follows already from those carries. An additional arithmetic estimate is therefore needed to turn it into a contradiction.

Factorial digits of 𝑆 −𝑒 +2S−e+2

The small perturbation from the reciprocal-factorial series can also be isolated term by term. Subtracting 𝑒 −2e−2 leaves the absolutely convergent correction 𝐶 =∑𝑛≥2(𝑛!(𝑛! −1))−1C=∑n≥2(n!(n!−1))−1. The identity 1/(𝑛! −1) =1/𝑛! +1/(𝑛!(𝑛! −1))1/(n!−1)=1/n!+1/(n!(n!−1)) gives

The canonical factorial digits of a real 𝑥x are 𝑎𝑚(𝑥) =⌊𝑚!𝑥⌋ −𝑚⌊(𝑚 −1)!𝑥⌋ ∈{0,…,𝑚 −1}am(x)=⌊m!x⌋−m⌊(m−1)!x⌋∈{0,…,m−1} for 𝑚 ≥2m≥2. At a rational endpoint, the floor convention selects the terminating representation. For example, 1/2 =1/2!1/2=1/2! also equals ∑𝑚≥3(𝑚 −1)/𝑚!∑m≥3(m−1)/m!, but its canonical digits are 𝑎2 =1a2=1 and 𝑎𝑚 =0am=0 for 𝑚 ≥3m≥3, not an eventually maximal tail. With this convention, 𝑥x is rational exactly when its digits vanish from some index on. The criterion goes back to Cantor [5]; Galambos treats the rationality of Cantor series in [6], and Koepf and Schmersau prove the irrationality direction for nonterminating factorial expansions whose digits are not eventually maximal [9].

The proof applies to every real 𝐶C, not just this series: eventual digits 𝑎𝑚(𝐶) =𝑚 −2am(C)=m−2 characterize rationality of 𝐶 +𝑒 −2C+e−2. Recovering the finite carries.

Write 𝜃𝑚 ={𝑚! 𝑆}θm={m!S}. The next criterion compares a fractional part with the actual tail: 𝜃𝑚−1θm−1 must leave [0,𝐸𝑚/𝑚)[0,Em/m) at arbitrarily large indices. Since 5252 is composite, the example supplies no prime-index instance.

The series with denominators 𝑛! +𝑡n!+t

The digit argument also applies to the other shifts mentioned by Erdős. For an integer 𝑡 ≥ −1t≥−1, put 𝑆𝑡 =∑𝑛≥21/(𝑛! +𝑡)St=∑n≥21/(n!+t) and 𝐶𝑡 =∑𝑛≥21/(𝑛!(𝑛! +𝑡))Ct=∑n≥21/(n!(n!+t)), so that 𝑆𝑡 = −𝑡𝐶𝑡 +(𝑒 −2)St=−tCt+(e−2).

The restriction 𝑡 ≥ −1t≥−1 keeps every denominator positive for 𝑛 ≥2n≥2; 𝑡 = −2t=−2 is excluded because its first denominator is zero. All series in this identity converge absolutely. The familiar case 𝑡 =0t=0 is 𝑒 −2e−2; this example will make the residue condition explicit.

Theorem 4.4 (a criterion for the shifts 𝑡 ≥ −1t≥−1). For every integer 𝑡 ≥ −1t≥−1, the series 𝑆𝑡St is rational exactly when

⌈𝑡𝑚!𝐶𝑡⌉≡2(mod𝑚)⌈tm!Ct⌉≡2(modm)

for all sufficiently large 𝑚m, and irrational exactly when that residue is missed cofinally. The member 𝑡 = −1t=−1 is 𝑆S, and the member 𝑡 =0t=0 is 𝑒 −2e−2, for which the scaled correction is always 00 and hence misses the residue class at every 𝑚 ≥3m≥3, proving the irrationality of 𝑒e.

Proof. Put 𝑌 = −𝑡𝐶𝑡Y=−tCt, so that 𝑆𝑡 =𝑌 +𝑒 −2St=Y+e−2. The proof of Proposition 4.2 applies to any real 𝑌Y: rationality of 𝑌 +𝑒 −2Y+e−2 forces ⌊𝑚!𝑌⌋ ≡ −2(mod𝑚)⌊m!Y⌋≡−2(modm) eventually. Conversely, that congruence forces the canonical digits of 𝑌Y to equal 𝑚 −2m−2 eventually; adding the factorial series of 𝑒 −2e−2 leaves an eventually telescoping tail, hence a rational sum. Finally ⌈𝑡𝑚!𝐶𝑡⌉ = −⌊𝑚!𝑌⌋⌈tm!Ct⌉=−⌊m!Y⌋ translates the congruence. Negation gives the cofinal statement. For 𝑡 =0t=0, the left side is zero, which is not congruent to 22 for 𝑚 ≥3m≥3. ◻

The 𝑡 =0t=0 specialisation recovers the classical irrationality of 𝑒e. For 𝑡 ≠0t≠0, no estimate proving failure of the displayed congruence at arbitrarily large indices is supplied here. This is a statement about the scope of the present argument, not an assertion that every other member has the same current literature status.
