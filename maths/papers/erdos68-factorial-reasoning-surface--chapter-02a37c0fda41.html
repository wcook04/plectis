Factorial Linear Forms and Denominators: Detailed Proofs and Rationality Criteria

Erdős #68 By Will Cook 1 September 2026; revised 5 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 51 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The record proves liminf (log LN)/(N3/2 log N) ≥2√2/3≥22/3 for L_N=lcm(2!−1,…,N!−1), by comparing a terminal block of denominators with pairwise gcds rather than an external multiplicity theorem. Specialising a Louwsma–Martino valuation, it exhibits primes 139 and 2593 absent from all later reduced denominators. Coefficient constructions give integer forms MS+k; at fixed moment the remainder changes only by an integer. Rationality criteria use factorial digits and the next integer above a scaled partial sum; the digit argument also recovers the irrationality of e. Two exact calculations give q∤299999! and 𝑞 ≥239990 >1012038q≥239990>1012038. These exclusions do not prove irrationality.

Further arithmetic questions

It remains to prove 𝑚 ∤𝑍𝑚m∤Zm at arbitrarily large indices. Section 4 gives equivalent formulations through the actual remainders in (20) and the finite condition (21), when all integer indices are allowed. The estimates below concern the remaining arithmetic, not further ways to solve the coefficient equations. None establishes the required unbounded set. Restricting indices to primes is an extra condition; selected congruences likewise do not identify the sequence defined by 𝑆S without its floor relations.

Value sets do not determine the weighted residue sum.

Garaev, Luca and Shparlinski’s harmonic-sum estimate [20] concerns unconditioned harmonic sums, not sums restricted to the indices where 𝑛! ≡1(mod𝑞)n!≡1(modq). The value-set literature addresses different invariants. Banks, Luca, Shparlinski and Stichtenoth [25] provide historical missing-value context; Klurman and Munsch [26] distinguish unconditional averages from GRH-dependent improvements. Grebennikov, Sagdeev, Semchankau and Vasilevskii [27] study sizes of value and quotient sets, as do the factorial-residue and representation results of Hu [22] and Garaev and Pardo [23]. None is a noncancellation theorem for the reciprocal unit weights here. A lower bound for the number of distinct residues does give an upper bound on the multiplicity of residue 11, since every other value needs at least one index. But that bound does not determine the reciprocal unit weights or test whether they cancel modulo 𝑞q. The two explicit cancellations in §2 illustrate why the weights matter. Hu’s September preprint [24] considers pairwise distinct maps 𝑇𝜃(𝑥) =𝑏𝜃 +𝑐𝜃/𝑥Tθ(x)=bθ+cθ/x over 𝔽𝑝Fp, with 𝑐𝜃 ≠0cθ≠0. Its incidence estimate comes from Stevens and de Zeeuw [28]. To apply this to the residue classes of indices satisfying 𝑛! ≡1(mod𝑞)n!≡1(modq), one would first have to specify the set 𝐴A and the transition maps. The number of such indices is not automatically a lower bound for 𝑀M: different indices may produce the same pair (𝑥,𝜃)(x,θ), and their factorial values are all 11. One must prove that enough distinct transitions remain, as well as bound the ordered-pair multiplicity 𝜇μ. The unconditioned value-set theorem does not provide these additional facts.

Stewart’s gap-product estimate [8] requires the two products to be unequal. He supplies that step separately: in the proof of his bound (14), a prime-distribution argument separates the products when the common divisor is large; the smaller-divisor case is immediate [8].

The non-power theorem gives a different argument when the gaps are adjacent. The wider equal-product problem has a separate history [29]; the doubled-length theorem discussed by Nguyen Xuan Tho [30] is another special case, not arbitrary near-equal-length nonvanishing. No stronger lcm exponent is inferred from these local observations.

Theorem 3.4 measures the cost of clearing all summands before reduction; it does not bound cancellation in their sum. Theorem 2.1 tests whether a maximal prime power survives reduction. Neither supplies the upper bound for a clearing integer, relative to its remainder, that these arguments need.
