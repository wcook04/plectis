Long paper

The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Propagation of nonintegral tail differences

Write

𝑆=∑𝑛≥1𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛,𝑅𝑁=∑𝑗≥1𝜑(𝑁+𝑗)2−𝑗,Δℎ(𝑁)=𝑅𝑁+ℎ−𝑅𝑁.S=∑n≥1φ(n)2−n,RN=∑j≥1φ(N+j)2−j,Δh(N)=RN+h−RN.

2𝑁𝑆=𝑁∑𝑛=1𝜑(𝑛)2𝑁−𝑛+𝑅𝑁2NS=∑n=1Nφ(n)2N−n+RN

has an integer finite sum. Subtracting the identities at 𝑁 +ℎN+h and 𝑁N gives

Δℎ(𝑁)≡2𝑁(2ℎ−1)𝑆(mod)𝑍.(6)(6)Δh(N)≡2N(2h−1)S(mod)Z.

In particular, a nonintegral tail difference excludes every rational value whose denominator divides 2𝑁(2ℎ −1)2N(2h−1).

The finite test.

If 𝐾(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿)K(h,N,L) holds, the interval [𝐷ℎ,𝑁,𝐿 −𝐵,𝐷ℎ,𝑁,𝐿 +𝐵][Dh,N,L−B,Dh,N,L+B] lies strictly between two consecutive multiples of 2𝐿2L. It contains 2𝐿Δℎ(𝑁)2LΔh(N), so Δℎ(𝑁) ∉ℤΔh(N)∉Z. Such a choice exists: ℎ,𝑁h,N are fixed, the distance is positive, and 𝐵 =𝑁 +ℎ +𝐿 +2B=N+h+L+2 grows only linearly in 𝐿L. The error estimate then puts the residue of 𝐷ℎ,𝑁,𝐿Dh,N,L more than 𝐵B from either endpoint. Thus nonintegrality is equivalent to a certificate at some unrestricted depth. This is a one-sided finite test: success proves nonintegrality, but failure at a particular depth does not prove integrality. The converse guarantees eventual success only after a fixed nonintegral difference has been supplied.

Theorem A.1 (Propagation of one nonintegral tail difference). Fix 𝑑 ≥1d≥1 and 𝑁 ≥0N≥0. If Δ𝑑(𝑁) ∉ℤΔd(N)∉Z, then every sufficiently late pair {𝑡,𝑡 +1}{t,t+1} contains an 𝑚m such that 𝐾(𝑚𝑑,𝑁,𝑚𝑑)K(md,N,md) holds. Consequently

∃𝑡≥1: 𝐾(𝑡𝑑,𝑁,𝑡𝑑)⟺Δ𝑑(𝑁)∉ℤ.∃t≥1: K(td,N,td)⟺Δd(N)∉Z.

Requiring this for every 𝑑 ≥1,𝑁 ≥0d≥1,N≥0 is equivalent to 𝑆 ∉ℚS∉Q.

Unrestricted completeness cannot simply set 𝐿L equal to the shift: it supplies a depth only after the shift is fixed. The theorem couples them by varying both along multiples of 𝑑d. The recurrence below prevents two adjacent failures. Its starting hypothesis is still just one nonintegral difference; only the requirement for every 𝑑,𝑁d,N is equivalent to irrationality.
