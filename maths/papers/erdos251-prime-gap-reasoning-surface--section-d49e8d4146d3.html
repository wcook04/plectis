Prime-Gap Dyadic Series: Perturbations, Exact Criteria and Certificates

Erdős #251 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 47 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A convergent dyadic series with nonnegative integer coefficients can be made rational by sparse nonnegative corrections of upper Banach density zero, eventually bounded by any f(n)→∞, while preserving every fixed modulus and, for a logarithmic allowance, short-block empirical distributions. For prime gaps the cumulative positions remain asymptotic to n log n; no primality conclusion is asserted. Summation by parts reduces the prime series to the gap series, and the tails 𝑇𝑁 =∑𝑗≥1𝑔𝑁+𝑗2−𝑗TN=∑j≥1gN+j2−j satisfy 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1. That recurrence characterises rationality and yields finite tests with explicit remainder bounds. An exact finite certificate shows that a rational value of the prime series would have denominator at least 239998 >1012040239998>1012040. The bound does not establish irrationality.

The tail recurrence and the exact criteria

We rescale each tail so that its first denominator is again 22. For a convergent series ∑𝑖≥0𝑎𝑖2−𝑖−1∑i≥0ai2−i−1 with 𝑎𝑖 ∈ℤai∈Z, put

𝑇𝑁=2𝑁+1∑𝑖>𝑁𝑎𝑖2−𝑖−1=𝑎𝑁+12+𝑎𝑁+24+⋯.TN=2N+1∑i>Nai2−i−1=aN+12+aN+24+⋯.

Thus 𝑇𝑁TN starts with coefficient 𝑎𝑁+1aN+1, not 𝑎𝑁aN. Doubling and removing that first coefficient gives 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑎𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−aN+1. Modulo integers, a step is simply doubling; the detailed values of the integer coefficients disappear. This is why the recurrence gives a rationality criterion for arbitrary integer coefficients.

At 𝑁 =0N=0 we have 𝑇0 =2∑𝑖≥0𝑎𝑖2−𝑖−1 −𝑎0T0=2∑i≥0ai2−i−1−a0. In particular, for the prime gaps 𝑇0 =2𝑆 −1 =2Π −5T0=2S−1=2Π−5, so 𝑇0T0, 𝑆S and ΠΠ have the same rationality status. We first analyse the recurrence abstractly. We then impose the vanishing boundary condition that singles out complete tails and apply the criterion to these prime-gap tails.

Definition 5.1. Let 𝑔 :ℕ →ℤg:N→Z and 𝑇 :ℕ →ℚT:N→Q or 𝑇 :ℕ →ℝT:N→R. Say 𝑇T satisfies the dyadic tail recurrence with coefficients 𝑔g when 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1 for every 𝑁N. Write 𝜎ℎ(𝑁) =𝑇𝑁+ℎ −𝑇𝑁σh(N)=TN+h−TN for the shift of length ℎh at 𝑁N, and call a real number integral when it belongs to ℤZ.

For a complete-tail example, take 𝑔𝑛 =2gn=2 for odd 𝑛 ≥1n≥1 and 𝑔𝑛 =4gn=4 for even 𝑛 ≥1n≥1. Its complete tail at index 00 is 𝑇0 =(2/2 +4/4)/(1 −1/4) =8/3T0=(2/2+4/4)/(1−1/4)=8/3, and the tails alternate between 8/38/3 and 10/310/3. Thus 𝜎1(𝑁)σ1(N) is always 2/32/3 or −2/3−2/3, whereas 𝜎2(𝑁) =0σ2(N)=0 for every 𝑁N. Nonintegrality at one prescribed shift length does not imply irrationality, even for positive even coefficients and genuine tails.

An integral shift returns to the same fractional part. To track the integer accumulated in ℎh steps, define 𝐵0,𝑁 =0B0,N=0 and 𝐵ℎ+1,𝑁 =2𝐵ℎ,𝑁 +𝑔𝑁+ℎ+1Bh+1,N=2Bh,N+gN+h+1, so that 𝐵ℎ,𝑁 =𝑔𝑁+12ℎ−1 +⋯ +𝑔𝑁+ℎBh,N=gN+12h−1+⋯+gN+h is the weighted integer sum accumulated in those ℎh steps.

Theorem 5.2 (block identity). For every 𝑁N and ℎh,

𝑇𝑁+ℎ=2ℎ𝑇𝑁−𝐵ℎ,𝑁,hence𝜎ℎ(𝑁)=(2ℎ−1)𝑇𝑁−𝐵ℎ,𝑁.TN+h=2hTN−Bh,N,henceσh(N)=(2h−1)TN−Bh,N.

Proof. The case ℎ =0h=0 follows from 𝐵0,𝑁 =0B0,N=0. If the first identity holds at ℎh, then

𝑇𝑁+ℎ+1=2(2ℎ𝑇𝑁−𝐵ℎ,𝑁)−𝑔𝑁+ℎ+1=2ℎ+1𝑇𝑁−𝐵ℎ+1,𝑁.TN+h+1=2(2hTN−Bh,N)−gN+h+1=2h+1TN−Bh+1,N.

Subtracting 𝑇𝑁TN gives the second identity. ◻

Subtracting the tail recurrences at 𝑁 +ℎN+h and 𝑁N gives

𝜎ℎ(𝑁+1)=2𝜎ℎ(𝑁)−(𝑔𝑁+ℎ+1−𝑔𝑁+1),(7)(7)σh(N+1)=2σh(N)−(gN+h+1−gN+1),

which is the recurrence for the tail differences. Since 𝐵ℎ,𝑁Bh,N is an integer, Theorem 5.2 also gives the integrality criterion: 𝜎ℎ(𝑁)σh(N) is integral if and only if (2ℎ −1)𝑇𝑁(2h−1)TN is integral. For fixed ℎh in this general recurrence, 𝑇𝑁TN therefore determines whether the shift is integral, regardless of the later integer coefficients. For a complete tail, however, changing those coefficients also changes 𝑇𝑁TN; the observation does not permit arbitrary changes to prime gaps.

For rational 𝑇𝑁 =𝑢/𝑣TN=u/v in lowest terms, the recurrence gives 𝑇𝑁+1 =(2𝑢 −𝑔𝑁+1𝑣)/𝑣TN+1=(2u−gN+1v)/v. The numerator has greatest common divisor gcd(2,𝑣)gcd(2,v) with 𝑣v, because gcd(𝑢,𝑣) =1gcd(u,v)=1. Thus

den⁡(𝑇𝑁+1)=den⁡(𝑇𝑁)gcd(2,den⁡(𝑇𝑁)),𝜎ℎ(𝑁)∈ℤ⟺den⁡(𝑇𝑁)∣2ℎ−1.(8)(8)den⁡(TN+1)=den⁡(TN)gcd(2,den⁡(TN)),σh(N)∈Z⟺den⁡(TN)∣2h−1.

The power of two in the denominator determines how many initial steps must pass before a return is possible; the remaining odd factor 𝑑d determines the possible return lengths. Once that factor is reached, Euler’s congruence supplies 𝑑 ∣2𝜑(𝑑) −1d∣2φ(d)−1. The least positive return length is the multiplicative order of 22 modulo 𝑑d, taken to be 11 when 𝑑 =1d=1. Before the power of two is removed there is no return: for example, 𝑇𝑁 =1/2TN=1/2 gives no integral shift at 𝑁N, since 2ℎ −12h−1 is always odd. This periodicity concerns fractional parts, not full tail values or coefficients.

Theorem 5.3 (exact rationality classification). Let 𝑇 :ℕ →ℝT:N→R satisfy 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1 with integer coefficients 𝑔g. The following are equivalent:

  1. 𝑇0T0 is rational;

  2. 𝜎ℎ(𝑁)σh(N) is an integer for some ℎ ≥1h≥1 and some 𝑁N;

  3. for some fixed ℎ ≥1h≥1, 𝜎ℎ(𝑁)σh(N) is an integer at every sufficiently large 𝑁N.

Consequently 𝑇0T0 is irrational if and only if every positive-length shift is nonintegral at every index, equivalently if and only if for every ℎ ≥1h≥1 and every cutoff some later 𝑁N has 𝜎ℎ(𝑁) ∉ℤσh(N)∉Z.

Proof. For (i)⇒⇒(iii), the block identity makes every 𝑇𝑁TN rational. By (8), its denominator is a fixed odd integer 𝑑d for all sufficiently large 𝑁N. Choose ℎ =𝜑(𝑑)h=φ(d); Euler’s congruence and the same formula show that every subsequent 𝜎ℎ(𝑁)σh(N) is integral. The implication (iii)⇒⇒(ii) is immediate. For (ii)⇒⇒(i), the block identity gives 𝜎ℎ(𝑁) =(2ℎ −1)𝑇𝑁 −𝐵ℎ,𝑁σh(N)=(2h−1)TN−Bh,N with 𝐵ℎ,𝑁Bh,N and 𝜎ℎ(𝑁)σh(N) integers and 2ℎ −1 ≠02h−1≠0, so 𝑇𝑁TN is rational, and 𝑇𝑁 =2𝑁𝑇0 −𝐵𝑁,0TN=2NT0−BN,0 then makes 𝑇0T0 rational. Negating the three conditions gives the two irrationality formulations. ◻

Lemma 5.4 (the boundary condition for the tail recurrence). Let 𝑎1,𝑎2,…a1,a2,… be real numbers with ∑𝑗≥1|𝑎𝑗|2−𝑗 <∞∑j≥1|aj|2−j<∞, and let 𝑈𝑁+1 =2𝑈𝑁 −𝑎𝑁+1UN+1=2UN−aN+1. Then

𝑈𝑁=∑𝑗≥1𝑎𝑁+𝑗2−𝑗for every 𝑁⟺2−𝑁𝑈𝑁⟶0.UN=∑j≥1aN+j2−jfor every N⟺2−NUN⟶0.

Proof. Iteration gives 2−𝑁𝑈𝑁 =𝑈0 −∑𝑁𝑗=1𝑎𝑗2−𝑗2−NUN=U0−∑j=1Naj2−j. The limit is zero exactly when 𝑈0U0 equals the full series; subtracting its first 𝑁N terms then gives the claimed tail formula. Without the boundary condition, one may add 𝐶2𝑁C2N to every 𝑈𝑁UN without changing the recurrence. ◻

For instance, zero coefficients and initial value 𝑈0 =1/12U0=1/12 give 112,16,13,23,43,…112,16,13,23,43,…, with 𝑈𝑁+ℎ −𝑈𝑁 =(2ℎ −1)2𝑁/12UN+h−UN=(2h−1)2N/12. Shifts of length 22 become integral at index 22; shifts of length 11 never do. These are not complete tails of the zero sequence, since 2−𝑁𝑈𝑁 =1/122−NUN=1/12 does not tend to zero. The denominator calculation remains valid, but the boundary condition excludes this extra homogeneous term.

The actual prime-gap tails.

Put

𝑇𝑁=∑𝑗≥1𝑔𝑁+𝑗2𝑗,TN=∑j≥1gN+j2j,

which converges by Theorem 4.3. Reindexing identifies this sum with the rescaled complete tail of 𝑆S, as in the shifted-gap series identity. Splitting off the first term gives 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1 without a rationality hypothesis, as formalised in the real tail recurrence. Since 𝑇0 =2𝑆 −1 =2.349287932…T0=2S−1=2.349287932…, the numbers ΠΠ, 𝑆S and 𝑇0T0 have the same rationality status, and Theorem 5.3 specialises to the actual series: irrationality of ΠΠ is equivalent to nonintegrality arbitrarily far out for each positive shift of 𝑇T. The formal statement is the irrationality equivalence for the actual prime tails. Thus the passage from the series to its complete tails is proved. What remains is to establish the required nonintegrality for these prime-gap tails.

Pairs of congruent indices and least-common-multiple shifts

Instead of prescribing the offset 𝑀 −𝑁M−N, we may prescribe a positive modulus 𝑡t and allow any pair with 𝑀 ≡𝑁(mod𝑡)M≡N(modt). This gives more freedom in finding a witness, but the test must still work for every 𝑡t and beyond every cutoff.

Theorem 5.5 (pairs of congruent indices). 𝑆S is irrational if and only if for every 𝑡 ≥1t≥1 and every 𝑁0N0 there are 𝑁,𝑀 ≥𝑁0N,M≥N0 with 𝑀 ≡𝑁(mod𝑡)M≡N(modt) and 𝑇𝑀 −𝑇𝑁 ∉ℤTM−TN∉Z.

Proof. If 𝑆S is irrational, take 𝑀 =𝑁 +𝑡M=N+t for the 𝑁N supplied by Theorem 5.3 at shift length 𝑡t. Conversely, suppose 𝑆S is rational. By (8) the orbit reaches an index 𝑁0N0 beyond which the reduced denominator is a fixed odd 𝑑d; let 𝑡t be the multiplicative order of 22 modulo 𝑑d, taking 𝑡 =1t=1 when 𝑑 =1d=1. For 𝑁,𝑀 ≥𝑁0N,M≥N0 with 𝑀 ≡𝑁M≡N modulo 𝑡t, the difference 𝑇𝑀 −𝑇𝑁TM−TN is then an integer, contradicting the stated property at this 𝑡t and this cutoff. ◻

The obstruction under rationality is therefore one particular modulus: the order of 22 modulo the eventual odd denominator. A successful pair for some other modulus does not exclude it.

Equal tail values do not help this criterion, since their difference is zero. Section D.4 shows that rationality and the prime number theorem would in fact force many equal tail values. It also explains why, beyond the rational denominator cutoff, an interval condition excluding integers already contradicts rationality at indices congruent modulo the corresponding multiplicative order. These are ordinary deductions, not additional formalised statements.

Alternatively, one can fix the pairs in advance. Let 𝐿0 =1L0=1 and 𝐿𝑗 =lcm⁡(1,…,𝑗)Lj=lcm⁡(1,…,j). These numbers eventually exceed any starting cutoff and are eventually divisible by any proposed period.

Theorem 5.6 (criterion using least common multiples). Let 𝑔 :ℕ →ℤg:N→Z and 𝑇 :ℕ →ℝT:N→R satisfy 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1. Then 𝑇0T0 is irrational if and only if 𝑇2𝐿𝑗 −𝑇𝐿𝑗 ∉ℤT2Lj−TLj∉Z for every 𝑗 ≥0j≥0.

Proof. Theorem 5.3 gives the forward implication. Conversely, if 𝑇0T0 is rational then some positive shift length ℎh is integral at every basepoint beyond an index 𝑁0N0. Choose 𝑗j so large that 𝑁0 ≤𝐿𝑗N0≤Lj and ℎ ∣𝐿𝑗h∣Lj. The telescoping identity 𝜎𝑎+𝑏(𝑁) =𝜎𝑎(𝑁) +𝜎𝑏(𝑁 +𝑎)σa+b(N)=σa(N)+σb(N+a) shows by induction that every positive multiple of ℎh is integral at every such basepoint, so 𝑇2𝐿𝑗 −𝑇𝐿𝑗T2Lj−TLj is an integer. ◻

One could use factorials instead of least common multiples, since they have the same eventual divisibility property. The least common multiples are no larger at each index. Theorems 5.5 and 5.6 change which tail differences one must test, but remain exact equivalences for integer-coefficient recurrences. Neither proves the needed nonintegrality for the actual prime gaps.
