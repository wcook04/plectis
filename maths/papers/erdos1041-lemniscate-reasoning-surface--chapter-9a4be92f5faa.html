Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

Erdős #1041 By Will Cook 29 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 86 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For a monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc, any two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1} by a path of length less than 2. For any squarefree monic polynomial of degree 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, an area-growth argument gives such a path when the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/2513/25; scaling gives length less than (5/2)𝜇1/𝑛(5/2)μ1/n inside {|𝑓| <(25/13)𝜇}{|f|<(25/13)μ}. The record develops this argument, an inverse-ray averaging estimate, and a construction using an isolated simple critical value, together with results for collinear roots and several coefficient families. A weighted Poisson identity gives a sharp mean bound on critical values; examples distinguish that bound from path-length control. The final sections examine inverse-sheet topology and refute an earlier spanning-tree estimate. These results do not prove the unrestricted length-2 assertion.

Equation (8) already gives 𝑘 ≥2𝑥/𝛿(𝑎)k≥2x/δ(a): the required sum is at least 𝑥x, and each root contributes at most 𝛿(𝑎)/2δ(a)/2. Improving this estimate requires the pairwise separation, not just the distance of each root from the origin. Testing only consecutive pairs in angular order loses essential information. For 𝑘 =2𝑚k=2m, put 𝑚m points at hyperbolic radius 𝑑lowdlow and 𝑚m at radius 𝑑low +𝐷dlow+D, alternating in cyclic order. The even number of points is needed for cyclic alternation. These point configurations test only the consecutive-pair constraints; they need not satisfy separation for nonconsecutive pairs or arise from a polynomial component. To use the missing pairwise information, we impose the packing bound on every circle centred at the origin.

A nonnegative weighted sum of the circle-slice inequalities bounds every admissible configuration at once. The weights give a usable bound only when ΣΣ and the supremum 𝑈U have rigorous bounds. In particular, checking the expression on a finite grid does not bound the supremum between grid points. Zero weights recover only the individual bound 𝑘 ≥2𝑥/𝛿(𝑎)k≥2x/δ(a); nonzero weights incorporate the circle-packing information and can improve the result. A certificate at area 𝑎′ ≥𝑎a′≥a also applies at 𝑎a. Indeed, 𝑑low(𝑎) ≥𝑑low(𝑎′)dlow(a)≥dlow(a′), so the admissible range of 𝑑d only shrinks. Also 𝐷(𝑎) ≥𝐷(𝑎′)D(a)≥D(a′), which enlarges every slice angle 𝑤(𝑑,𝑟𝑖)w(d,ri) at fixed 𝑑,𝑟𝑖d,ri. Since the weights are nonnegative, the expression defining 𝑈U can only decrease. Thus the same certified pair (Σ,𝑈)(Σ,U) remains valid. The numerical comparison also uses two bounds that do not involve chosen weights. They are useful where the circle-slice certificate is weaker. We derive them here so that every root-count input to the comparison is visible.
