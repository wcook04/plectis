Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

Erdős #1041 By Will Cook 29 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 86 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For a monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc, any two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1} by a path of length less than 2. For any squarefree monic polynomial of degree 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, an area-growth argument gives such a path when the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/2513/25; scaling gives length less than (5/2)𝜇1/𝑛(5/2)μ1/n inside {|𝑓| <(25/13)𝜇}{|f|<(25/13)μ}. The record develops this argument, an inverse-ray averaging estimate, and a construction using an isolated simple critical value, together with results for collinear roots and several coefficient families. A weighted Poisson identity gives a sharp mean bound on critical values; examples distinguish that bound from path-length control. The final sections examine inverse-sheet topology and refute an earlier spanning-tree estimate. These results do not prove the unrestricted length-2 assertion.

An isolated simple critical value

A disc containing 00 and one simple critical value selects a component of degree two. Taking a square root removes its local branching and gives a coordinate in which the proposed connecting curve is a real segment. The ordinary argument below then bounds its length by area and capacity. Lean assumes this combined analytic construction as DiscSepBergmanArea; it does not derive it from the polynomial hypotheses. We therefore record the path criterion and its numerical consequences as unformalised remarks.

The simplicity assumption means that the chosen critical point is a simple zero of the derivative, so two inverse branches meet there. A multiple chosen critical point is excluded; the other critical points need not be simple. Also, separation of roots in the 𝑧z-plane is not enough: different critical points can have nearly equal values. An exact quartic example below makes this distinction explicit. For a positive example, take 𝑓(𝑧) =𝑧3 −3𝑎2𝑧f(z)=z3−3a2z with 0 <𝑎 <1/√30<a<1/3. Its roots 0, ±√3𝑎0,±3a lie in the unit disc. At 𝑐 =𝑎c=a the critical value is 𝑣 = −2𝑎3v=−2a3, and the other normalised critical value is −1−1. Thus the disc centred at 11 with radius 4/34/3 contains 00 and 11 but excludes the other critical value. Radius 22 is allowed as well: the other critical value then lies on the boundary. The proof must therefore allow a nonregular outer level.
