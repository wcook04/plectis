Long paper

The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

The rank quantities.

Finite-dimensional span of all dyadic sections is the definition of a 22-regular sequence in the sense of Allouche and Shallit [1]. For integer-valued sequences this is equivalent to finite generation of the ℤZ-module generated by the sections: if their span has dimension 𝑑d, some 𝑑d evaluations embed it in ℚ𝑑Qd and that module in ℤ𝑑Zd. Coons [2] proved that 𝜑φ is not 𝑘k-regular for any 𝑘 ≥2k≥2. The unconditional Lean theorem (Erdos249257.not_finiteDimensional_span_fullTotientKernel) proves the case 𝑘 =2k=2 independently. For integer coefficient and carry sequences, the recurrence transfers non-22-regularity of the coefficients to non-22-regularity of the carry. A proof must include the zero-residue sections, not just the positive residues. The following argument gives an explicit dimension bound.

For a rational-valued sequence 𝑓f, let 𝑉𝑒(𝑓)Ve(f) be the span of all its sections through levels 0,…,𝑒0,…,e. A level-𝑗j section of 𝐵𝑓Bf is a section of 𝑓f if its residue is positive. At residue zero it is the backward shift of 𝑛 ↦𝑓(2𝑗𝑛 +2𝑗 −1)n↦f(2jn+2j−1). Hence

𝑉𝑒(𝐵𝑓)⊆𝑉𝑒(𝑓)+𝐵(𝑉𝑒(𝑓)).Ve(Bf)⊆Ve(f)+B(Ve(f)).

In particular, bounded carry ranks would force bounded coefficient ranks. Non-22-regularity of 𝑐c therefore forces unbounded carry ranks. This is an ordinary proof, not a claim of a new Lean declaration. It also gives the generic bound dimℚ⁡𝑉𝑒(𝑢) ≥2𝑒−1dimQ⁡Ve(u)≥2e−1 for 𝑐 =𝜑c=φ and 𝑒 ≥1e≥1, using the coefficient rank 2𝑒 +12e+1. The factor 22 is not an additive 𝑂(1)O(1) loss, and this bound concerns all sections, including level zero.

The theorem for 𝜑φ in Proposition 1.6 is sharper: the carry sections at all residues of levels 1,…,𝑒1,…,e have rank at least 2𝑒 −12e−1. It uses the independence of the odd-residue totient sections. Neither lower bound is an irrationality proof, since rational coefficient series can have nonregular carries. The separate 5/45/4 comparison verifies this directly. Any proposed rank upper bound must use additional totient-specific hypotheses not satisfied by the 5/45/4 comparison sequence.
