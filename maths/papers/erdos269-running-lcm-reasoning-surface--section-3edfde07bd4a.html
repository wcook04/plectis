Running least common multiples: distinct values and multiplicities

Erdős #269 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 5 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 73 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The reciprocal three-prime running-LCM kernel has nonsingular minors of every order: row and column rescaling leaves a two-valued matrix, and density supplies the required threshold pattern. The record computes the rank of every finite restriction and the least uniform approximation error over all matrices of finite separated rank. For the repeated {2,3,5} sum it derives an integer-coefficient tail recurrence and a residue criterion equivalent to irrationality. The unproved step is to find such windows for every positive multiplier coprime to 30 and after every prescribed start. It also gives the full two-prime comparison, following Fan's earlier Hecke–Mahler reduction, and identifies the boundary terms that survive a proposed finite-difference cancellation.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #269, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #269, which remains open.

Separation of the three-prime kernel

We test an exact kernel identity: can finitely many products separate one exponent from the other two? A single binary carry rules this out for three primes. Diagonal rescaling leaves threshold columns; independent row and column choices produce staircase minors of every order. This says nothing yet about identities after summation or approximation, which we treat separately. The initial two-generator identity is valid for real generators; distinct primes enter the later density argument.

Scope of the rank theorem.

Fan’s comment of 26 June 2026 [17] notes that the two-prime argument does not seem to generalise immediately to |𝑃| ≥3|P|≥3. The theorem above gives a precise obstruction: no finite exact separation of the stated form exists. The same finite-dimensional proof works for real or complex factors, without continuity or boundedness assumptions. This does not exclude approximation or imply irrationality or transcendence of the sum. The threshold construction needs log𝑟⁡𝑝logr⁡p and log𝑟⁡𝑞logr⁡q irrational, but not 𝑝 ≠𝑞p≠q. The latter condition is needed for the three-prime running-LCM interpretation, not for the two independent dense orbits.

Uniform approximation of the rescaled kernel

Exact rank does not decide approximation. For the rescaled kernel, infinitely many columns remain a fixed distance apart, giving a uniform lower bound against every finite rank. For the original kernel, decay instead permits summable truncation. We prove these statements for the two different matrices. Write

The same bound controls the supremum of the entrywise errors, so these finite separated-rank approximants converge both in ℓ1ℓ1 and uniformly to the original kernel. The positive uniform lower bound in Theorem 7.6 belongs to the rescaled matrix. Diagonal rescaling preserves exact rank, but the rescaling factors here are unbounded and do not preserve uniform error estimates. Neither the rank obstruction nor these approximation bounds decide whether the scalar sum is rational.
