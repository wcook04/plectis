Irrationality criteria for Lambert subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 26 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. The series ∑𝑎∈𝐴(𝑏𝑎 −1)−1∑a∈A(ba−1)−1 is irrational for every integer 𝑏 ≥2b≥2 and infinite A contained in E∪V when E satisfies a finite-prime weighted summability condition and V has a summable positive divisor cover. The two classes are incomparable, and their union reaches supports in neither class. A common average over dyadic lengths produces one index with both displacements small. Arbitrary infinite supports at base two remain unresolved.

This paper owns the weighted, divisor-cover and mixed support criteria with complete proofs and method limits; the long paper treats finite denominators, the achievement set and rational membership.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, which remains open.

Authorship and AI use. Will Cook built and directed the research infrastructure and maintains the public release. He reviewed claims when he could. AI agents did most of the research and drafting. Cook did not independently verify every claim.

The greedy recurrence

For 𝑥 ≥0x≥0, the greedy rule starts with 𝑟0 =𝑥r0=x and, at rank 𝑛 ≥1n≥1, selects 𝑛n when 𝑟𝑛−1 ≥(2𝑛 −1)−1rn−1≥(2n−1)−1, subtracting that weight if selected. Let 𝐴𝑥Ax be the resulting support and 𝑐𝑥(𝑛) =#{𝑎 ∈𝐴𝑥 :𝑎 ∣𝑛}cx(n)=#{a∈Ax:a∣n}. Set

𝑃0=0,𝑃𝑁+1=2𝑃𝑁+𝑐𝑥(𝑁+1),𝑄𝑁=⌊2𝑁𝑥⌋−𝑃𝑁.P0=0,PN+1=2PN+cx(N+1),QN=⌊2Nx⌋−PN.

Thus 𝑃𝑁 =∑𝑁𝑗=12𝑁−𝑗𝑐𝑥(𝑗)PN=∑j=1N2N−jcx(j) is the integer truncation of the Lambert expansion (6). It is at most 2𝑁𝑋𝐴𝑥(2) ≤2𝑁𝑥2NXAx(2)≤2Nx, so 𝑄𝑁 ≥0QN≥0. These integer remainders obey

𝑄𝑁+1=2𝑄𝑁+𝛽𝑁−𝑐𝑥(𝑁+1),𝛽𝑁=⌊2𝑁+1𝑥⌋−2⌊2𝑁𝑥⌋∈{0,1}.(10)(10)QN+1=2QN+βN−cx(N+1),βN=⌊2N+1x⌋−2⌊2Nx⌋∈{0,1}.

The reason to test for nonincreases is a difference in growth rates. For a represented target, 𝑄𝑁QN is bounded by a scaled coefficient tail of order √𝑁N; integrality prevents strict increase throughout a sufficiently long window. For an unrepresented target, the positive deficit 𝑥 −𝑋𝐴𝑥(2)x−XAx(2) contributes exponential growth, and the recurrence eventually forces strict increase.

Theorem C.1 (membership and nonincreasing integer remainders). For every real 𝑥 ≥0x≥0, the following are equivalent:

𝑥∈A;∀𝐾≥0 ∃𝑁≥𝐾:𝑄𝑁+1≤𝑄𝑁;∀𝐾≥0 ∃𝑁∈[𝐾,𝐾+2⌊√𝐾⌋+12):𝑄𝑁+1≤𝑄𝑁.x∈A;∀K≥0 ∃N≥K:QN+1≤QN;∀K≥0 ∃N∈[K,K+2⌊K⌋+12):QN+1≤QN.

Proof. Strict domination of each Mersenne weight over its tail implies that a represented target is recovered by the greedy rule. For such a target,

0≤𝑄𝑁≤∑𝑟≥1𝑐𝑥(𝑁+𝑟)2−𝑟≤2√𝑁+4,0≤QN≤∑r≥1cx(N+r)2−r≤2N+4,

since 𝑐𝑥(𝑛) ≤𝜏(𝑛) ≤2√𝑛cx(n)≤τ(n)≤2n and √𝑁+𝑟 ≤√𝑁 +√𝑟 ≤√𝑁 +𝑟N+r≤N+r≤N+r. Put 𝑠 =⌊√𝐾⌋s=⌊K⌋ and 𝑇 =2𝑠 +12T=2s+12. Strict increase at all 𝑇T steps would imply 𝑄𝐾+𝑇 ≥𝑇QK+T≥T. But 𝐾 +𝑇 <(𝑠 +4)2K+T<(s+4)2 gives 𝑄𝐾+𝑇 <2𝑠 +12 =𝑇QK+T<2s+12=T. This proves the window condition and hence cofinal nonincreases.

Conversely, if 𝑥 ∉Ax∉A, then 𝛿 =𝑥 −𝑋𝐴𝑥(2) >0δ=x−XAx(2)>0. The Lambert prefix is at most 2𝑁𝑋𝐴𝑥(2)2NXAx(2), so 𝑄𝑁 ≥2𝑁𝛿 −1QN≥2Nδ−1. Eventually this exceeds 𝑐𝑥(𝑁 +1) ≤𝑁 +1cx(N+1)≤N+1. Equation (10) then makes 𝑄𝑁QN strictly increasing, contradicting cofinal nonincreases. ◻

The nonincreases must occur along the target’s own greedy sequence. For 𝑥 =0x=0, no exponent is selected and 𝑄𝑁 =0QN=0 at every rank. A finite subseries sum is likewise represented and satisfies the criterion. The target 𝑥 =3/4x=3/4 lies strictly between 𝑅1 <2/3R1<2/3 and 𝑤1 =1w1=1, so it is not represented and its integer remainders eventually increase strictly. For 1/21/2 and 1/211/21, the theorem does not establish which alternative occurs.

For 𝑇 =⌈𝐶𝜀(𝐾 +1)𝜀⌉T=⌈Cε(K+1)ε⌉, strict increase at all 𝑇T steps would give

𝑇≤𝑄𝐾+𝑇≤𝐷𝜀(𝐾+𝑇+1)𝜀≤𝐷𝜀(𝐶𝜀+3)𝜀(𝐾+1)𝜀<𝑇,T≤QK+T≤Dε(K+T+1)ε≤Dε(Cε+3)ε(K+1)ε<T,

a contradiction. The converse still follows from exponential growth when 𝑥x is not represented. This shortens the window for represented targets without deciding whether a specified target is represented. The linked formal proof establishes the square-root window.

At 𝑥 =1/2x=1/2, the digits 𝛽𝑁βN vanish for 𝑁 ≥1N≥1; at 𝑥 =1/21x=1/21 they are six-periodic. Thus the unresolved arithmetic input is
