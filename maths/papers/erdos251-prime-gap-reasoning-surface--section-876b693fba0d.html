Prime-Gap Dyadic Series: Perturbations, Exact Criteria and Certificates

Erdős #251 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 47 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A convergent dyadic series with nonnegative integer coefficients can be made rational by sparse nonnegative corrections of upper Banach density zero, eventually bounded by any f(n)→∞, while preserving every fixed modulus and, for a logarithmic allowance, short-block empirical distributions. For prime gaps the cumulative positions remain asymptotic to n log n; no primality conclusion is asserted. Summation by parts reduces the prime series to the gap series, and the tails 𝑇𝑁 =∑𝑗≥1𝑔𝑁+𝑗2−𝑗TN=∑j≥1gN+j2−j satisfy 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1. That recurrence characterises rationality and yields finite tests with explicit remainder bounds. An exact finite certificate shows that a rational value of the prime series would have denominator at least 239998 >1012040239998>1012040. The bound does not establish irrationality.

Bounded residue-preserving perturbations

For one fixed positive modulus 𝑀M, we may add either 00 or 𝑀M after a prescribed prefix. The weighted choices form a binary expansion, so we can choose a rational sum while preserving every coefficient modulo 𝑀M. This construction permits changes at every index after the prefix; it imposes neither density-zero support nor eventual preservation of all moduli.

Theorem 8.1 (bounded perturbations preserving congruences). Let 𝑎𝑛an be natural numbers with ∑𝑛≥0𝑎𝑛2−(𝑛+1)∑n≥0an2−(n+1) convergent. For every integer 𝑀 ≥1M≥1 and every cutoff 𝐾K there are digits 𝜀𝑛 ∈{0,1}εn∈{0,1}, zero for 𝑛 <𝐾n<K, such that ∑𝑛≥0(𝑎𝑛 +𝑀𝜀𝑛)2−(𝑛+1)∑n≥0(an+Mεn)2−(n+1) is rational.

Proof. Write 𝐴 =∑𝑛≥0𝑎𝑛2−(𝑛+1)A=∑n≥0an2−(n+1) and choose a rational 𝑟 ∈(𝐴,𝐴 +𝑀2−𝐾)r∈(A,A+M2−K). A binary expansion of (𝑟 −𝐴)/𝑀(r−A)/M has the form ∑𝑛≥𝐾𝜀𝑛2−(𝑛+1)∑n≥Kεn2−(n+1) with 𝜀𝑛 ∈{0,1}εn∈{0,1}; set 𝜀𝑛 =0εn=0 for 𝑛 <𝐾n<K. The perturbed series converges and equals 𝑟r. ◻

Hence a property shared by every allowed perturbation cannot by itself force irrationality, since one such perturbation has a rational sum; this consequence is not stated separately in Lean. Lean also checks the construction at the actual consecutive prime gaps, for every 𝑀 ≥1M≥1 and every 𝐾K: the perturbed prime-gap series is rational, and each perturbed gap lies in [𝑔𝑛,𝑔𝑛 +𝑀][gn,gn+M] and is congruent to 𝑔𝑛gn modulo 𝑀M. For each fixed 𝑘 ≥1k≥1, the sum of 𝑘k consecutive gaps increases by at most 𝑘𝑀kM. Thus infinitely many bounded gaps and bounded clusters of each fixed size survive, but their original numerical bounds need not. Nonnegative corrections also preserve lower bounds for large gaps. Taking 𝑀 =2𝑞M=2q preserves parity and the cumulative positions modulo a prescribed 𝑞q, hence any limiting residue frequencies of those positions. These conclusions concern the size and residue information in the results discussed in Section 10; they establish neither primality of the new positions nor preservation of every quantitative assertion of those theorems.
