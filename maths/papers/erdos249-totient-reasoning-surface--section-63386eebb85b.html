The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 160 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Authorship and AI use. Will Cook built and directed the research infrastructure and maintains the public release. He reviewed claims when he could. AI agents did most of the research and drafting. Cook did not independently verify every claim.

A general squared-Lambert identity

The same geometric summation applies to any weight with the stated linear bound.

Proposition 5.16 (The squared-Lambert identity). For 𝑤 :ℕ →ℝw:N→R with |𝑤(𝑑)| ≤𝑑|w(d)|≤d for all 𝑑 >0d>0, and 0 ≤𝑟 <10≤r<1:

∑𝑑≥1𝑤(𝑑)(𝑟𝑑1−𝑟𝑑)2=∑𝑛≥1(∑𝑒∣𝑛𝑤(𝑒)(𝑛𝑒−1))𝑟𝑛.∑d≥1w(d)(rd1−rd)2=∑n≥1(∑e∣nw(e)(ne−1))rn.

The identity follows by expanding 𝑟𝑑/(1 −𝑟𝑑)rd/(1−rd) as a geometric series and interchanging absolutely convergent sums. It is the squared analogue of the classical divisor-sum Lambert identity ∑𝑛𝑎𝑛𝑞𝑛/(1 −𝑞𝑛) =∑𝑚(∑𝑑∣𝑚𝑎𝑑)𝑞𝑚∑nanqn/(1−qn)=∑m(∑d∣mad)qm [16].

At 𝑟 =1/2r=1/2, the choices 𝑤 =1w=1 and 𝑤 =𝜑w=φ give the following two identities. They help distinguish the role of the weight from that of the squared denominator.

Proposition 5.17 (The divisor-sum identity).

∑𝑑≥11(2𝑑−1)2=∑𝑛≥1𝜎(𝑛)−𝜏(𝑛)2𝑛=𝜁𝑞(2)−𝜁𝑞(1) at 𝑞=12,∑d≥11(2d−1)2=∑n≥1σ(n)−τ(n)2n=ζq(2)−ζq(1) at q=12,

where 𝜎σ is the sum-of-divisors function and 𝜏τ the number-of-divisors function. The identity is machine-checked. With 𝜁𝑞(𝑠) =∑𝑛≥1𝑛𝑠−1𝑞𝑛/(1 −𝑞𝑛)ζq(s)=∑n≥1ns−1qn/(1−qn), Postelmans and Van Assche prove that 1,𝜁𝑞(1),𝜁𝑞(2)1,ζq(1),ζq(2) are linearly independent over ℚQ for 𝑞 =1/𝑝q=1/p with an integer 𝑝 ≥2p≥2 [12]; at 𝑞 =12q=12 this gives the irrationality of the value 𝜁𝑞(2) −𝜁𝑞(1)ζq(2)−ζq(1). Two irrational numbers can have a rational difference, so the separate irrationality of the two values would not suffice.

Proposition 5.18 (The gcd-moment identity).

∑𝑑≥1𝜑(𝑑)(2𝑑−1)2=∑𝑛≥1(𝑃(𝑛)−𝑛)⋅2−𝑛=𝔼[gcd(𝑋,𝑌)],∑d≥1φ(d)(2d−1)2=∑n≥1(P(n)−n)⋅2−n=E[gcd(X,Y)],

where 𝑃(𝑛) :=∑𝑒∣𝑛𝜑(𝑒) ⋅(𝑛/𝑒) =(𝜑 ∗Id)(𝑛)P(n):=∑e∣nφ(e)⋅(n/e)=(φ∗Id)(n) is Pillai’s gcd-sum function 𝑃(𝑛) =∑𝑘≤𝑛gcd(𝑘,𝑛)P(n)=∑k≤ngcd(k,n) [15] and 𝑋,𝑌X,Y are the independent fair-coin waiting times of Proposition 5.9. The identity itself is machine-checked; irrationality of 𝔼[gcd(𝑋,𝑌)]E[gcd(X,Y)] is not proved here. This is a different series from 𝑆S.

Remark 5.19 (Related series). Writing 𝐿(𝑓) :=∑𝑑𝑓(𝑑)/(2𝑑 −1)L(f):=∑df(d)/(2d−1) and 𝐿2(𝑓) :=∑𝑑𝑓(𝑑)/(2𝑑 −1)2L2(f):=∑df(d)/(2d−1)2, the preceding identities give 𝐿(𝜇) =12L(μ)=12 (rational), 𝐿(1) =𝐸L(1)=E (Erdős–Borwein, irrational), 𝐿(𝜑) =2L(φ)=2 (rational); 𝐿2(𝜇) =𝑆 −12L2(μ)=S−12 (irrationality open; this is #249), 𝐿2(1) =𝜁𝑞(2) −𝜁𝑞(1)L2(1)=ζq(2)−ζq(1) (irrational by the cited theorem, Proposition 5.17), 𝐿2(𝜑) =𝔼[gcd(𝑋,𝑌)]L2(φ)=E[gcd(X,Y)] (irrationality open, Proposition 5.18). These examples show why the denominator shape alone does not determine irrationality: changing the weight can produce either rational or irrational values.
