Running least common multiples: distinct values and multiplicities

Erdős #269 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 5 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 73 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The reciprocal three-prime running-LCM kernel has nonsingular minors of every order: row and column rescaling leaves a two-valued matrix, and density supplies the required threshold pattern. The record computes the rank of every finite restriction and the least uniform approximation error over all matrices of finite separated rank. For the repeated {2,3,5} sum it derives an integer-coefficient tail recurrence and a residue criterion equivalent to irrationality. The unproved step is to find such windows for every positive multiplier coprime to 30 and after every prescribed start. It also gives the full two-prime comparison, following Fan's earlier Hecke–Mahler reduction, and identifies the boundary terms that survive a proposed finite-difference cancellation.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #269, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #269, which remains open.

Intervals of constancy of the running LCM

We first describe which smooth integers contribute the same running value. The LCM is determined by the largest exponent of each prime in the prefix. For the unrestricted prefix this gives lcm⁡(1,…,𝑁) =∏𝑡≤𝑁𝑡⌊log𝑡⁡𝑁⌋lcm⁡(1,…,N)=∏t≤Nt⌊logt⁡N⌋, with 𝑡t running over primes, and hence log⁡lcm⁡(1,…,𝑁) =𝜓(𝑁)log⁡lcm⁡(1,…,N)=ψ(N); see Apostol [10] and Montgomery and Vaughan [12]. We apply the same exponent rule to a fixed prime support, keeping track of the intervals on which the running LCM is constant.

The smooth numbers up to 𝑥x are indexed by the exponent triples (𝑖,𝑗,𝑘)(i,j,k) with 𝑖 ≤⌊log𝑝⁡𝑥⌋i≤⌊logp⁡x⌋, 𝑗 ≤⌊log𝑞⁡𝑥⌋j≤⌊logq⁡x⌋, 𝑘 ≤⌊log𝑟⁡𝑥⌋k≤⌊logr⁡x⌋ and 𝑝𝑖𝑞𝑗𝑟𝑘 ≤𝑥piqjrk≤x. The coordinate bounds alone need not describe the prefix: each prime-power factor may be at most 𝑥x while their product exceeds 𝑥x. For instance, at 𝑥 =6x=6 the factors 44, 33 and 55 satisfy their coordinate bounds, but the corresponding smooth number is 6060.

Theorem 3.1 (the running least common multiple). Let 𝑝,𝑞,𝑟p,q,r be pairwise distinct primes and 𝑥 ≥1x≥1. Then L⁡(𝑥) =H⁡(𝑥)L⁡(x)=H⁡(x).

Proof. Every smooth 𝑛 ≤𝑥n≤x has exponents bounded by the corresponding integer logarithms, so 𝑛 ∣H⁡(𝑥)n∣H⁡(x) and hence L⁡(𝑥) ∣H⁡(𝑥)L⁡(x)∣H⁡(x). Conversely the three pure powers 𝑝⌊log𝑝⁡𝑥⌋p⌊logp⁡x⌋, 𝑞⌊log𝑞⁡𝑥⌋q⌊logq⁡x⌋ and 𝑟⌊log𝑟⁡𝑥⌋r⌊logr⁡x⌋ are themselves smooth numbers not exceeding 𝑥x, so each divides L⁡(𝑥)L⁡(x), and distinct primes have coprime powers, so their product divides L⁡(𝑥)L⁡(x) as well. ◻

At (𝑝,𝑞,𝑟) =(2,3,5)(p,q,r)=(2,3,5) the first ten values are

𝑥12345678910L⁡(𝑥)12612606060120360360x12345678910L⁡(x)12612606060120360360

So L⁡(6) =4 ⋅3 ⋅5 =60L⁡(6)=4⋅3⋅5=60, which exceeds 66: the running value at a smooth cutoff already contains powers of the other two primes that the cutoff itself does not. The kernel must therefore account for all three maximal powers, not just the factorisation of the cutoff. Also H⁡(𝑥) ≤𝑥3H⁡(x)≤x3, since each factor is at most 𝑥x. The exponent is the number of generating primes.

Say that 𝑥x and 𝑦y lie in the same logarithmic cell when ⌊log𝑏⁡𝑥⌋ =⌊log𝑏⁡𝑦⌋⌊logb⁡x⌋=⌊logb⁡y⌋ for each of 𝑏 =𝑝,𝑞,𝑟b=p,q,r. By Theorem 3.1 the running value depends on 𝑥x only through the three integer logarithms, so it is constant on cells and moves only where one logarithm moves.

Proposition 3.2 (constancy and jump ratios). If 𝑥,𝑦 ≥1x,y≥1 lie in the same logarithmic cell then L⁡(𝑥) =L⁡(𝑦)L⁡(x)=L⁡(y), and the same holds for the kernel at two smooth points of one cell. If ⌊log𝑝⁡𝑦⌋ =⌊log𝑝⁡𝑥⌋ +1⌊logp⁡y⌋=⌊logp⁡x⌋+1 while the other two logarithms agree, then L⁡(𝑦) =𝑝 L⁡(𝑥)L⁡(y)=pL⁡(x), and similarly with 𝑞q or 𝑟r in place of 𝑝p.

Proof. Both parts are immediate from Theorem 3.1: the running LCM depends on 𝑥x only through the three integer logarithms, and advancing one of them multiplies exactly one factor by its base. ◻

Proposition 3.3 (jump count). Let 𝑛 ≥0n≥0. The set of the first 𝑛n positive powers of 𝑝p, of 𝑞q and of 𝑟r has exactly 3𝑛3n elements, and adjoining the common origin 11 gives exactly 3𝑛 +13n+1.

Proof. For a fixed prime 𝑏b, the powers 𝑏,𝑏2,…,𝑏𝑛b,b2,…,bn are distinct. A common value for two different primes would contradict unique factorisation. Finally 11 is not a positive power of any prime. ◻

The later tail estimates use two elementary counting facts. Write B(ℎ𝑝,ℎ𝑞,ℎ𝑟)B(hp,hq,hr) for the exponent triples with 𝑖 ≤ℎ𝑝i≤hp, 𝑗 ≤ℎ𝑞j≤hq and 𝑘 ≤ℎ𝑟k≤hr, and 𝐹(𝐻)F(H) for the points of this box whose running LCM equals 𝐻H.

Proposition 3.4 (grouping equal running LCMs). For every box BB,

∑(𝑖,𝑗,𝑘)∈BK⁡(𝑖,𝑗,𝑘)=∑𝐻#𝐹(𝐻)/𝐻,∑(i,j,k)∈BK⁡(i,j,k)=∑H#F(H)/H,

the outer sum ranging over the running LCMs attained on BB.

Proof. Partition BB into the fibres of the running LCM map. On 𝐹(𝐻)F(H) every summand is 1/𝐻1/H, so the fibre contributes #𝐹(𝐻)/𝐻#F(H)/H. ◻

Now fix an interval [𝜆,𝜂)[λ,η) with 0 ≤𝜆 <𝜂0≤λ<η and write SS for the exponent triples of B(ℎ𝑝,ℎ𝑞,ℎ𝑟)B(hp,hq,hr) whose value 𝑝𝑖𝑞𝑗𝑟𝑘piqjrk lies in it. In the next lemma, the exponents 𝑎,𝑎′a,a′ are nonnegative integers, and 𝑤 ≥0w≥0 is the product of the fixed factors.

Lemma 3.5 (uniqueness in a short interval). Let 𝑏 ≥1b≥1 and 𝜂 ≤𝑏 𝜆η≤bλ. If 𝑏𝑎𝑤baw and 𝑏𝑎′𝑤ba′w both lie in [𝜆,𝜂)[λ,η) then 𝑎 =𝑎′a=a′.

Proof. If 𝑎 <𝑎′a<a′ then 𝜂 ≤𝑏 𝜆 ≤𝑏𝑎+1𝑤 ≤𝑏𝑎′𝑤 <𝜂η≤bλ≤ba+1w≤ba′w<η, which is impossible; the case 𝑎 >𝑎′a>a′ is symmetric. ◻

The short-interval condition says that multiplying by the omitted base moves a point beyond the interval. It holds for [𝐿,2𝐿)[L,2L) when that base is at least two. A wider interval need not have this property: 11 and 22 both lie in [1,3)[1,3) and have the same odd part. Thus the ratio bound, not just finiteness of the interval, permits the injective projection.

Proposition 3.6 (counting a block by two coordinates). If 𝜂 ≤𝑟 𝜆η≤rλ then #S ≤(ℎ𝑝 +1)(ℎ𝑞 +1)#S≤(hp+1)(hq+1), and if 𝜂 ≤𝑝 𝜆η≤pλ then #S ≤(ℎ𝑞 +1)(ℎ𝑟 +1)#S≤(hq+1)(hr+1). If moreover 𝜂 ≤𝑟 𝜆η≤rλ and ℎ𝑝 ≤ℎ𝑞 ≤ℎ𝑟hp≤hq≤hr with ℎ𝑝 +ℎ𝑞 +ℎ𝑟 =𝑗hp+hq+hr=j, then 9 #S ≤(𝑗 +3)29#S≤(j+3)2.

Proof. Suppose 𝜂 ≤𝑟 𝜆η≤rλ. If two triples of SS agree in their first two coordinates, Lemma 3.5 with 𝑏 =𝑟b=r and 𝑤 =𝑝𝑖𝑞𝑗w=piqj forces their third coordinates to agree, so the projection forgetting the third coordinate is injective on SS and its image lies in a rectangle with (ℎ𝑝 +1)(ℎ𝑞 +1)(hp+1)(hq+1) points. The other case is the same with the first coordinate projected away. Under the sorting hypothesis the two surviving coordinates are the two smallest, so it suffices that 𝑎 ≤𝑏 ≤𝑐a≤b≤c with 𝑎 +𝑏 +𝑐 =𝑗a+b+c=j gives 9(𝑎 +1)(𝑏 +1) ≤(𝑗 +3)29(a+1)(b+1)≤(j+3)2. From 𝑎 ≤𝑏 ≤𝑐a≤b≤c we get 𝑎 +2𝑏 ≤𝑗a+2b≤j, so it is enough that 9(𝑎 +1)(𝑏 +1) ≤(𝑎 +2𝑏 +3)29(a+1)(b+1)≤(a+2b+3)2; writing 𝑏 =𝑎 +𝑑b=a+d with 𝑑 ≥0d≥0, the difference of the two sides is 𝑑(3𝑎 +4𝑑 +3) ≥0d(3a+4d+3)≥0. ◻
