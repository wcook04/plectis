Prime-Gap Dyadic Series: Perturbations, Exact Criteria and Certificates

Erdős #251 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 47 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A convergent dyadic series with nonnegative integer coefficients can be made rational by sparse nonnegative corrections of upper Banach density zero, eventually bounded by any f(n)→∞, while preserving every fixed modulus and, for a logarithmic allowance, short-block empirical distributions. For prime gaps the cumulative positions remain asymptotic to n log n; no primality conclusion is asserted. Summation by parts reduces the prime series to the gap series, and the tails 𝑇𝑁 =∑𝑗≥1𝑔𝑁+𝑗2−𝑗TN=∑j≥1gN+j2−j satisfy 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1. That recurrence characterises rationality and yields finite tests with explicit remainder bounds. An exact finite certificate shows that a rational value of the prime series would have denominator at least 239998 >1012040239998>1012040. The bound does not establish irrationality.

Summation by parts, with the endpoint retained

We return to the actual prime gaps 𝑔𝑛 =𝑝𝑛+1 −𝑝𝑛gn=pn+1−pn, with 𝑝0 =2p0=2 and 𝑔0 =1g0=1. We write

Π=∑𝑛≥0𝑝𝑛2−𝑛−1,𝑆=∑𝑛≥0𝑔𝑛2−𝑛−1.Π=∑n≥0pn2−n−1,S=∑n≥0gn2−n−1.

Here 𝑆S denotes the gap sum. The set of indices used in Section 2 will not be needed below. The short paper calls this same sum 𝐺G. The two calculations below have different jobs: summation by parts gives Π =2 +𝑆Π=2+S, and deleting the first gap gives the initial tail 𝑇0 =2𝑆 −1T0=2S−1.

We begin with a finite identity, so no convergence hypothesis is needed. Its endpoint identifies the estimate required before passing to the infinite series. The linked definitions are the dyadic partial sum and the weighted sum of consecutive differences. The formula below writes both sums explicitly.

Proposition 4.1 (finite summation by parts). For every rational sequence 𝑃P and every 𝑛 ≥0n≥0,

𝑛∑𝑖=0𝑃(𝑖)2𝑖+1=𝑃(0)+𝑛−1∑𝑖=0𝑃(𝑖+1)−𝑃(𝑖)2𝑖+1−𝑃(𝑛)2𝑛+1.∑i=0nP(i)2i+1=P(0)+∑i=0n−1P(i+1)−P(i)2i+1−P(n)2n+1.

Proof. At 𝑛 =0n=0 both sides equal 𝑃(0)/2P(0)/2. When 𝑛n is replaced by 𝑛 +1n+1, the change on the right is

𝑃(𝑛+1)−𝑃(𝑛)2𝑛+1−𝑃(𝑛+1)2𝑛+2+𝑃(𝑛)2𝑛+1=𝑃(𝑛+1)2𝑛+2.P(n+1)−P(n)2n+1−P(n+1)2n+2+P(n)2n+1=P(n+1)2n+2.

This is the next term on the left, which proves the identity by induction. ◻

Specialising to 𝑃(𝑖) =𝑝𝑖P(i)=pi, whose first value is 𝑝0 =2p0=2, and writing 𝑔𝑖 =𝑝𝑖+1 −𝑝𝑖gi=pi+1−pi for the zero-based gaps, gives the reformulation.

Theorem 4.2 (prime-gap reformulation). Let 𝑝0 =2,𝑝1 =3,…p0=2,p1=3,… be the primes in increasing order and 𝑔𝑖 =𝑝𝑖+1 −𝑝𝑖gi=pi+1−pi. For every 𝑛 ≥0n≥0,

𝑛∑𝑖=0𝑝𝑖2𝑖+1=2+𝑛−1∑𝑖=0𝑔𝑖2𝑖+1−𝑝𝑛2𝑛+1.∑i=0npi2i+1=2+∑i=0n−1gi2i+1−pn2n+1.

The leading 22 is the first prime, not a normalising constant. At 𝑛 =2n=2, for instance, the left side is 2/2 +3/4 +5/8 =19/82/2+3/4+5/8=19/8 and the right side is 2 +(1/2 +2/4) −5/8 =19/82+(1/2+2/4)−5/8=19/8.

The infinite identity and the irrationality equivalence

To pass to the infinite series, let

𝑢𝑛=𝑝𝑛2𝑛+1,𝑣𝑛=𝑔𝑛2𝑛+1.un=pn2n+1,vn=gn2n+1.

Thus ∑𝑛𝑢𝑛 =Π∑nun=Π. The termwise identity 𝑣𝑛 =2𝑢𝑛+1 −𝑢𝑛vn=2un+1−un is the dyadic discrete derivative. It expresses each gap term as an integer combination of two consecutive prime terms, so once (𝑢𝑛)(un) is summable the gap series can be summed after proving absolute convergence, which justifies shifting and subtracting the prime series.

Theorem 4.3 (infinite prime-gap identity). Both series converge and

∑𝑛≥0𝑝𝑛2𝑛+1=2+∑𝑛≥0𝑔𝑛2𝑛+1.∑n≥0pn2n+1=2+∑n≥0gn2n+1.

Proof. The bound 𝑝𝑛 ≤1250(𝑛 +1)4pn≤1250(n+1)4 of Appendix A makes (𝑢𝑛)(un) summable, and 0 <𝑔𝑛 ≤𝑝𝑛+10<gn≤pn+1 then makes (𝑣𝑛)(vn) summable. The shifted sequence (𝑢𝑛+1)(un+1) is summable, so summing 𝑣𝑛 =2𝑢𝑛+1 −𝑢𝑛vn=2un+1−un and using 𝑢0 =1u0=1 gives

∑𝑛≥0𝑣𝑛=2(∑𝑛≥0𝑢𝑛−𝑢0)−∑𝑛≥0𝑢𝑛=∑𝑛≥0𝑢𝑛−2.◻∑n≥0vn=2(∑n≥0un−u0)−∑n≥0un=∑n≥0un−2.◻

Corollary 4.4 (exact irrationality reformulation). ΠΠ is irrational if and only if 𝑆 =∑𝑛≥0𝑔𝑛2−(𝑛+1)S=∑n≥0gn2−(n+1) is irrational. The corresponding zero-based series with denominator 2𝑛2n equals 4 +2𝑆4+2S and has the same irrationality status.

Proof. The identity just proved gives Π =2 +𝑆Π=2+S. Adding a rational constant or multiplying by a nonzero rational number preserves rationality and irrationality. ◻

Concretely, Π =3.674643966…Π=3.674643966… is irrational if and only if 𝑆 =1.674643966…S=1.674643966… is. Neither irrationality statement is established here.
