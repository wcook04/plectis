Long paper

The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Relations between the representations

Binary tails, Möbius sums, rational approximations and exponential sums express related information about 𝑆S. We give the identities that connect them and then check which assumptions survive each change. This is necessary before transferring a counterexample to another representation, as the finite-state decoding and polynomial denominator examples illustrate.

Binary-weighted series and tails.

The coefficients in 𝑆 =∑𝑛≥1𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛S=∑n≥1φ(n)2−n are unbounded; this expression is not the base-two digit expansion of 𝑆S. The tail 𝑅𝑁RN, finite difference numerator 𝐷(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿)D(h,N,L) and test C(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿)C(h,N,L) retain this coefficient information. The identity

𝑆=12+∑𝑑≥1𝜇(𝑑)(2𝑑−1)2S=12+∑d≥1μ(d)(2d−1)2

(tsum_totient_half_pow_eq_half_add_moebius_sq) and its partial-sum version (mobius_square_series_eq_partial_add_tail) express the same value by Möbius sums. An equality of values does not by itself transfer a claim about the original coefficient subsequences.

Lambert sums and finite numerator polynomials.

The Lambert identities for 𝜇μ, 11 and 𝜑φ give the series relations in Section 5. For a finite divisor sum, the polynomial evaluation (mobiusNumeratorPolynomial_eval_two) and the definition (mobiusNumerator) put its numerator into integer polynomial arithmetic. This evaluates a finite contribution, not the complementary infinite tail.

Coprimality probability.

Let 𝑋,𝑌X,Y be independent with Pr(𝑋 =𝑛) =Pr(𝑌 =𝑛) =2−𝑛Pr(X=n)=Pr(Y=n)=2−n for 𝑛 ≥1n≥1. Then 𝑆 −12 =Pr(gcd(𝑋,𝑌) =1)S−12=Pr(gcd(X,Y)=1). The divisibility probabilities are Pr(𝑑 ∣𝑋, 𝑑 ∣𝑌) =(2𝑑 −1)−2Pr(d∣X, d∣Y)=(2d−1)−2 (tsum_pos_pair_both_dvd_half_eq_inv_mersenne_sq). Möbius inversion converts these to the coprimality probability. The decomposition by the greatest common divisor also gives the weighted coprime-pair identity (tsum_pos_coprime_inv_mersenne_eq_one). Counting coprime pairs by their sum gives the totient expression directly (tsum_coprime_pair_pow_eq_tsum_totient_mul_pow).

Cyclotomic factors of finite denominators.

The coefficient formula for 𝑃𝑟Pr (mobiusNumeratorPolynomial_coeff) and the cyclotomic divisibility statement (cyclotomicValue_dvd_baseMobiusShadow_den) identify factors in the reduced denominators of the specified finite sums. Growing finite denominators alone do not exclude cancellation with the remaining tail.

Binary tails and Möbius sums

Erdős’s argument for ∑𝑛1/(2𝑛 −1)∑n1/(2n−1) chooses congruences so that divisor counts along a block are divisible by increasing powers of the base. A tail bound then forces a long run of zero digits [11]. It uses no finite automaton.

A different proposal would recover each later block from a fixed finite summary of the preceding history. The counterexample of Proposition 2.11(i) (balancedPulse_no_autonomous_decoder) shows why that proposal fails for the family constructed there. For 𝑚 ≥2m≥2, its members have the same earlier history but different successors. A state that identifies all those histories cannot recover every successor. More generally, an exact label-and-decoder pair for that family needs at least ⌊(𝑚 +1)/2⌋ +1⌊(m+1)/2⌋+1 distinct labels. This is a lower bound on the number of labels, not a linear lower bound on the number of bits, and it is not a state-complexity theorem for the actual totient sequence.

The implication from the quantified certificate condition (irrational_totient_series_of_certificate_supply) is a separate theorem. The counterexample above neither proves its hypothesis nor makes it the only possible route to irrationality. Likewise, Proposition 10.4 shows that inverse-phase column weights can preserve a determinant, and Theorem 6.3 gives the exact growing ranks of totient sections. These are different statements with different hypotheses; none is a general prohibition on other representations of 𝑆S.

The Möbius representation instead allows the squared-Lambert identity of Proposition 5.16 to be applied to 𝜇μ, since |𝜇(𝑑)| ≤𝑑|μ(d)|≤d for 𝑑 ≥1d≥1. The polynomial identities of Propositions 6.37–6.41 operate at each finite radical without an autonomous decoder. The exact reduced denominator and its lower bound are then given by Propositions 6.8 and 6.7. Those formulas concern a finite rational contribution. They do not exclude cancellation by the complementary tail and hence do not settle irrationality. The representations are equal expressions for 𝑆S; what differs is the information supplied by the particular arguments.
