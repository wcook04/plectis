Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

Erdős #1041 By Will Cook 29 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 86 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For a monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc, any two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1} by a path of length less than 2. For any squarefree monic polynomial of degree 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, an area-growth argument gives such a path when the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/2513/25; scaling gives length less than (5/2)𝜇1/𝑛(5/2)μ1/n inside {|𝑓| <(25/13)𝜇}{|f|<(25/13)μ}. The record develops this argument, an inverse-ray averaging estimate, and a construction using an isolated simple critical value, together with results for collinear roots and several coefficient families. A weighted Poisson identity gives a sharp mean bound on critical values; examples distinguish that bound from path-length control. The final sections examine inverse-sheet topology and refute an earlier spanning-tree estimate. These results do not prove the unrestricted length-2 assertion.

Numerical estimates for the packing inequality

These computations suggest that this particular relaxation may stop near 𝑒−0.607 =0.545e−0.607=0.545; they do not prove that it does, and they exclude neither a stronger lower bound on the root count nor a larger certified range.

The search uses only ten or fourteen radii in its two settings and a coarse 𝑎a-grid with upward rounding. The reported loss from the first restriction at 𝑎 =1a=1, 𝑘 =8k=8 is about one per cent; the reported rounding loss is comparable. These are empirical estimates, not certified error bounds relative to the unrestricted continuous optimum.

The continuous optimisation remains unevaluated: maximise ∑𝑗𝜆(𝑑𝑗)∑jλ(dj) subject to 𝑑𝑗 ≥𝑑lowdj≥dlow and ∑𝑗𝑤(𝑑𝑗,𝑟) ≤𝜋∑jw(dj,r)≤π for every 𝑟 >0r>0, allowing the relaxed radial distributions used in the computation. The numerical optimiser spreads mass over several radii and suggests logarithmic growth in 𝑘k. Neither that growth law nor its coefficient is proved. Evaluating the relaxation would clarify this packing argument; its effect on the final path threshold would still need to be established.
