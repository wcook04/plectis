Reciprocal-Summable Support Irrationality at Every Integer Base

Erdős #257 14 pp Equations typeset from the exact TeX
PDFOpen the PDF · 14 pp TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis

For every infinite set A of positive integers with sum of 1/a finite, the paper proves that the reciprocal Mersenne-type subseries is irrational at every integer base b at least 2. Averaged modular divisor atoms give arbitrarily small positive displacements, contradicting the fixed lattice forced by rationality. This settles the reciprocal-summable regime, including the coprimality-free all-base extension stated by Erdős. Arbitrary infinite supports outside that regime, and hence universal Erdős #257, remain open.

This paper owns problem-specific mathematical exposition for Erdős #257: exact finite periods, settled support families, method limits, and the open half-value edge.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, which remains open.

In the reverse direction, disjoint prime blocks with reciprocal mass of order 2𝑘2k, placed in frames {2𝑘𝑑 :𝑑 ∣𝑀𝑘}{2kd:d∣Mk}, give a support 𝐴𝑊AW satisfying (W); its first logarithmic moments diverge, so (4) excludes every strengthened cover. Exact constructions and the scalar minimisation are referred to First Logarithmic Moment of Positive Covers, which is not in this checkout. These comparisons concern the individual sufficient classes.

Proof of the combination step. Freeze a sufficiently large finite prefix from each component with one modulus. Rational values and scaled tails

The displacement identity gives a fixed arithmetic lattice under rationality. Its recurrence is

𝑧𝑁+1=2𝑧𝑁−𝑣𝑐𝐴(𝑁+1),𝑧𝑁=𝑣∑𝑟≥1𝑐𝐴(𝑁+𝑟)2−𝑟∈ℤzN+1=2zN−vcA(N+1),zN=v∑r≥1cA(N+r)2−r∈Z

when 𝑋𝐴(2) =𝑝/𝑣XA(2)=p/v. The sequence 𝑐𝐴cA is determined by one Boolean selector.

Two elementary facts explain why size information alone has little force here. If 𝑎0 =min𝐴a0=minA, every 𝑎0a0 consecutive integers contain a multiple of 𝑎0a0, so a divisor-incidence zero window has length at most 𝑎0 −1a0−1. If 𝐴A is infinite, choose 𝑘k exponents and a common multiple 𝐿L; then 𝑐𝐴(𝐿) ≥𝑘cA(L)≥k and 𝑇(2)𝐿−1 ≥𝑘/2TL−1(2)≥k/2. Both facts hold without rationality. The arithmetic information lies in the lattice and in the compatibility of all coefficients with the same selector.

Precisely, Dirichlet convolution gives 𝜇 ∗𝑐𝐴 =𝟏𝐴μ∗cA=1A, where 𝜇μ is the Möbius function. A putative recurrence with integral forcing must therefore satisfy (𝜇 ∗𝑐𝐴)(𝑛) ∈{0,1}(μ∗cA)(n)∈{0,1} for all 𝑛n. Where the return mechanism stops

The preceding criteria establish irrationality through small positive returns. This mechanism already fails at the full support:

Δ2,ℕ>0(𝑁)>(2𝑁−1)∑𝑎>𝑁2−𝑎=1−2−𝑁≥12(𝑁≥1).Δ2,N>0(N)>(2N−1)∑a>N2−a=1−2−N≥12(N≥1).

The full-support value is nevertheless irrational . A universal proof must therefore control arithmetic behaviour beyond small returns.

The distinction also appears in the squarefree support. Its series is known to be irrational at powers-of-two bases by Duverney–Tachiya . The zero-window obstruction for one normalised certificate scheme concerns that scheme’s hypotheses. It gives no obstruction to irrationality of the value. The precise normalisation counterexamples are retained in the long record, together with the complete catalogue of known support families.

A related independence boundary concerns prime-incidence arguments. Writing 𝑓𝐴(𝑛) =∑𝑎∈𝐴𝟏𝑎∣𝑛fA(n)=∑a∈A1a∣n, the local identity

Further questions

The preceding arguments isolate two different tasks. For new irrational supports, one needs a summable or uniformly averaged control of the shifted divisor atoms, or an arithmetic functional that also detects supports whose positive displacements stay separated from zero. For a proposed rational target, one needs the actual-selector inequality (7); synthetic recurrences satisfying only growth and integrality conditions do not provide it.

Theorem 7 (fatal-branch quotient refinement at 1/211/21). The following statements hold.

  1. 1/21 ∈A1/21∈A if and only if F21F21 does not hold.

  2. If there is an unbounded sequence of ranks 𝑅R with 𝑠𝑅 ≤2𝑅sR≤2R, then 1/21 ∈A1/21∈A.

  3. On F21F21, eventually 𝑠𝑅 >2𝑅sR>2R, the boundary rank 𝑅 +1R+1 belongs to 𝐷𝑅+1DR+1, and (𝐷𝑅,𝑠𝑅)(DR,sR) follows one exact affine recurrence.
