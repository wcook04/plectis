Long paper

Running least common multiples: distinct values and multiplicities

Erdős #269 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 5 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 73 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The reciprocal three-prime running-LCM kernel has nonsingular minors of every order: row and column rescaling leaves a two-valued matrix, and density supplies the required threshold pattern. The record computes the rank of every finite restriction and the least uniform approximation error over all matrices of finite separated rank. For the repeated {2,3,5} sum it derives an integer-coefficient tail recurrence and a residue criterion equivalent to irrationality. The unproved step is to find such windows for every positive multiplier coprime to 30 and after every prescribed start. It also gives the full two-prime comparison, following Fan's earlier Hecke–Mahler reduction, and identifies the boundary terms that survive a proposed finite-difference cancellation.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #269, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #269, which remains open.

Integer recurrences in their general form

Consider an arbitrary integer recurrence with positive integer radices. Divisibility and the existence of segments satisfying the residue inequality must now be supplied as hypotheses. For the actual three-prime radices, the criterion below shows that each integer solution is eventually divisible by the smooth part of a denominator. It still leaves the residue-inequality hypothesis to be proved. The comparison with Cantor-series criteria is in Section 9.

Let 𝐷D and 𝐵B be positive integers with 𝐷 =𝐷sm𝐵D=DsmB, where 𝐷sm =2𝑢3𝑣5𝑤Dsm=2u3v5w, 𝑢,𝑣,𝑤 ∈ℕu,v,w∈N and gcd(𝐵,30) =1gcd(B,30)=1. For these general sequences, form 𝑊W and 𝐹F by the recursions in Section 10; a segment satisfying the residue inequality must have 𝑊 ≠0W≠0, and its modulus is |𝑊||W|. In the factorisation below the quotients 𝑑𝑛dn are required to be integers; writing 𝑐𝑛 =𝐷sm𝑑𝑛cn=Dsmdn asserts divisibility, not just an identity in ℚQ.

Proposition 13.2 (segments satisfying the residue inequality exclude a positive bounded integer solution). Let (𝑏𝑛)(bn) and (𝑚𝑛)(mn) be sequences of nonnegative integers, let 𝐺 :ℕ>0 ×ℕ →ℕG:N>0×N→N, and assume the residue condition (20) for these sequences and 𝐺G, using |𝑊ℓ,ℎ| >0|Wℓ,h|>0 as the modulus. Fix 𝐵 >0B>0 coprime to 3030. There is no integral sequence (𝑑𝑛)(dn) satisfying simultaneously 𝑑𝑛+1 =𝑏𝑛𝑑𝑛 −𝐵𝑚𝑛dn+1=bndn−Bmn, 𝑑𝑛 >0dn>0 and |𝑑𝑛| ≤𝐺(𝐵,𝑛)|dn|≤G(B,n) for every 𝑛 ≥0n≥0.

Proof. Choose one segment satisfying the residue inequality (ℓ,ℎ)(ℓ,h). The iterated recurrence gives 𝑑ℓ+ℎ ≡ −𝐵𝐹ℓ,ℎdℓ+h≡−BFℓ,h modulo |𝑊ℓ,ℎ||Wℓ,h|. The endpoint value is positive and at most 𝐺(𝐵,ℓ +ℎ)G(B,ℓ+h), whereas the least positive residue of the right-hand side exceeds that bound, so Proposition 10.1 applies. ◻

Product growth gives room for a large residue; it does not put the actual residue there. For the constant data 𝑏𝑛 =2bn=2, 𝑚𝑛 =1mn=1, the positive solution is 𝑑𝑛 =𝐵dn=B. Every segment has 𝑊 =2ℎW=2h, 𝐹 =2ℎ −1F=2h−1 and −𝐵𝐹 ≡𝐵(mod𝑊)−BF≡B(modW). Its least positive residue is therefore at most 𝐵B, however large 𝑊W becomes, so no bound 𝐺(𝐵,𝑛) ≥𝐵G(B,n)≥B can pass the test. For the actual three-prime coefficients and 𝐺 =𝐾G=K, Theorem 10.2 makes the residue-inequality hypothesis equivalent to irrationality of 𝑆S. That equivalence does not hold for an arbitrary choice of 𝐺G.

Coprimality with 3030 selects the denominators covered by the hypothesis; once a segment is fixed, the finite contradiction does not use it. The conventions cover the edge cases: a zero product of the bases is excluded, a zero residue is represented by the full modulus, and positivity prevents the endpoint value from vanishing. The version stated with a nonzero smooth factor assumes the exact factorisation 𝑐𝑛 =𝐷sm𝑑𝑛cn=Dsmdn, the corresponding recurrence for (𝑐𝑛)(cn) and the positive upper bound for (𝑑𝑛)(dn), and gives the same contradiction.
