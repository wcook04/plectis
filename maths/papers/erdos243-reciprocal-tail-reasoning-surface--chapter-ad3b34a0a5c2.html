Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 61 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. For rational reciprocal sums under 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, the record also develops sufficient conditions for the eventual recurrence 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1, controlling either increases of an integer tail numerator or a convergent sum at steps setting new maxima. Examples separate numerical growth bounds from the exact recurrences. The cubic proof is distinguished from the theorems formalised in Lean. None of the additional bounds is derived for every rational reciprocal sum in Erdős #243.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #243, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

Cancellation during recovery intervals

The LCM quotient 𝑀𝑛Mn need not equal the common divisor 𝐺𝑛Gn removed by reduction to lowest terms. Since 𝑀𝑛Mn divides both 𝐶𝑛Cn and 𝐷𝑛Dn, we have 𝑀𝑛 ∣𝐺𝑛Mn∣Gn, but equality is not assumed. For a fixed number of steps, the same estimate has a simpler consequence: a sufficiently late recovery cannot include any cancellation. If 𝐾|˜𝑒𝑛| <𝑢𝑛K|e~n|<un eventually for every 𝐾K, then for each fixed 𝐿 >0L>0 there is an 𝑁N such that every recovery 𝑢𝑟 ≤𝑢𝑟+𝐿ur≤ur+L with 𝑟 ≥𝑁r≥N satisfies

The same 𝐾K works for every length 1 ≤𝑗 ≤𝐿1≤j≤L, since (1 +1/𝐾)𝑗 ≤(1 +1/𝐾)𝐿 <2(1+1/K)j≤(1+1/K)L<2. Thus, after a sufficiently late step with ℎ𝑛 >1hn>1, the numerator cannot regain its starting value at any of the next 𝐿L indices. This includes a recovery followed by another fall before the last endpoint. The fixed-length conclusion is formalised as absence of late cancellation during recovery intervals of fixed length. The threshold 𝑁N depends on 𝐿L: late cancellations require longer recoveries, but have not been excluded. A global contradiction needs control of those varying lengths and a lower bound on the moduli’s least common multiple; the interval estimate supplies neither.

Problem 14.3 (growth of repeated denominator factors). For every rational-tail orbit satisfying the hypotheses but not the conclusion of Problem 1.1, must

lim sup𝑛→∞log⁡𝑀𝑛𝑛>0?lim supn→∞log⁡Mnn>0?

Equivalently, must there be a 𝐾 ≥1K≥1 for which

2𝑛≤𝑀𝐾𝑛2n≤MnK

at infinitely many indices?

The two displayed formulations are equivalent up to changing the positive constant; the second is not a weaker target. Section 6 already gives 1 ≤𝑀𝑛 ≤𝐶𝑛1≤Mn≤Cn, hence log⁡𝑀𝑛/𝑛 →0log⁡Mn/n→0 on every orbit under consideration. A positive answer would therefore be a contradiction, not a further compatible necessary condition. It would prove Problem #243. The missing step is to force exponential growth of 𝑀𝑛Mn from failure of the Sylvester recurrence.

The checked tail-height estimate and vanishing relative error make |𝐸𝑛||En| subexponential in 𝑛n.

lim sup𝑛→∞log⁡gcd(𝐴𝑛,𝑎𝑛−1)𝑛>0?lim supn→∞log⁡gcd(An,an−1)n>0?
