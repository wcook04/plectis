Geometric Moments and Rational Lambert Values: Proofs and Further Results

Erdős #1049 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 81 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. Specialising Zudilin's 2004 linear forms shows F(a/b) irrational for coprime 𝑎 >𝑏 ≥1a>b≥1 when θ=log b / log a is less than θ^*=0.4056830213840605…, with irrationality-exponent bound (1−θ)/(θ*−θ) for every positive integral power. In particular the bound is less than 301 for every positive integral power of 31/431/4. The constants are Zudilin's; the proof cancels common cyclotomic factors before rational specialisation. For the 2016 Hankel construction with x=z=1, the first nonzero term of the normalised determinant is computed at every rank. A separate positive-measure argument proves positivity and a size estimate for each fixed real 0<q<1. The final sections explain the limits of the stated clearing and congruence arguments at 3/23/2: no small nonzero sequence of divided remainders is constructed there, and the full rational-base conjecture remains open.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #1049, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #1049, which remains open.

Direct clearing of a partial sum.

A more naive attempt is to suppose 𝐹(𝑏) =𝑝/𝑞F(b)=p/q, cut at 𝑁N, and multiply the remaining identity by 𝑞𝑏𝑁qbN. This does not by itself trap a positive integer below 11: the first uncleared tail contribution is 𝑞𝜏(𝑁 +1)/𝑏qτ(N+1)/b. The clearing conditions below describe one bounded-window version of this attempt. They fail at 3/23/2.

Write 𝛽 =𝑟/𝑠β=r/s for the base and 𝑐(𝑛)c(n) for the coefficient of 𝛽−𝑛β−n, so that 𝑐 =𝜏c=τ in the case at hand. The term 𝑐(𝑛)𝛽−𝑛c(n)β−n is 𝑐(𝑛)𝑠𝑛/𝑟𝑛c(n)sn/rn. Clearing the power of 𝑟r leaves the numerator factor 𝑠𝑛sn in place. That factor is invisible when 𝑠 =1s=1 and grows geometrically when 𝑠 ≥2s≥2. At 3/23/2 it is 2𝑛2n.

We return to the attempt to clear successive terms of the series. We retain just two necessary demands: the last coordinate in the window must clear, and the coefficient-free lower bound from the first omitted positive term must be smaller than 11. Even these weaker tests are incompatible at 3/23/2. The argument below uses six natural-number parameters; it excludes the stated clearing scheme, not other approximations to 𝐹(3/2)F(3/2).

Definition 6.1 (clearing conditions). For natural numbers 𝑎,𝑏,𝑁,𝐾,𝑄,𝐷a,b,N,K,Q,D, the clearing conditions are

𝑎>0,𝑄>0,𝐷>0,𝐷≤𝑁+𝐾,𝑎𝐾∣𝑄𝐷,𝑄𝑏𝑁+𝐾+1<𝑎𝐾+1.a>0,Q>0,D>0,D≤N+K,aK∣QD,QbN+K+1<aK+1.

The reading is: 𝑎a and 𝑏b are the numerator and denominator of the base, playing the roles of 𝑟r and 𝑠s in the partial-sum calculation above, so that (𝑎,𝑏) =(3,2)(a,b)=(3,2) is the case of interest; 𝑁N is the shift, 𝐾K is the width of the cleared window, 𝑄Q is the accumulated clearing factor, and 𝐷D is the final coefficient being cleared. The bound 𝐷 ≤𝑁 +𝐾D≤N+K is the only property of the coefficient used; for the divisor-counting coefficient it holds because 𝜏(𝑛) ≤𝑛τ(n)≤n. The divisibility 𝑎𝐾 ∣𝑄𝐷aK∣QD tests the final coordinate of the cleared window; it does not by itself clear every earlier coordinate. For a reduced base 𝑎/𝑏a/b, the denominator of 𝑄𝑎𝑁𝐷(𝑏/𝑎)𝑁+𝐾QaND(b/a)N+K is cleared exactly when 𝑎𝐾 ∣𝑄𝐷aK∣QD, since 𝑎a and 𝑏b are coprime. For example, at 𝑎 =2a=2, 𝑏 =1b=1, 𝑁 =21N=21, 𝐾 =3K=3 and 𝑄 =1Q=1, the final term clears because 23 ∣𝜏(24) =823∣τ(24)=8, but the preceding scaled term is 𝜏(23)/22 =1/2τ(23)/22=1/2 and is not integral.

The final inequality is a necessary smallness test, not a sufficient bound for the entire tail. For 𝑎 >𝑏 ≥1a>b≥1 and 𝑄 >0Q>0, positivity and 𝜏(𝑚) ≥1τ(m)≥1 give

𝑄𝑎𝑁∑𝑚≥𝑁+𝐾+1𝜏(𝑚)(𝑏/𝑎)𝑚>𝑄𝑏𝑁+𝐾+1𝑎𝐾+1.QaN∑m≥N+K+1τ(m)(b/a)m>QbN+K+1aK+1.

A tail smaller than 11 must therefore satisfy the final inequality. The divisibility gives 𝑎𝐾 ≤𝑄(𝑁 +𝐾)aK≤Q(N+K), while the smallness test requires 𝑄𝑏𝑁+𝐾+1 <𝑎𝐾+1QbN+K+1<aK+1. The same 𝑄Q must satisfy both. These conditions are defined in Lean by the six clearing conditions.

Theorem 6.2 (a necessary inequality for clearing). If (𝑎,𝑏,𝑁,𝐾,𝑄,𝐷)(a,b,N,K,Q,D) satisfies the clearing conditions, then

𝑏𝑁+𝐾+1<𝑎(𝑁+𝐾).bN+K+1<a(N+K).

Proof. Since 𝑄𝐷 >0QD>0 and 𝑎𝐾 ∣𝑄𝐷aK∣QD, we have 𝑎𝐾 ≤𝑄𝐷aK≤QD, and 𝐷 ≤𝑁 +𝐾D≤N+K gives 𝑎𝐾 ≤𝑄(𝑁 +𝐾)aK≤Q(N+K). Hence

𝑄𝑏𝑁+𝐾+1<𝑎𝐾+1=𝑎𝐾⋅𝑎≤𝑄(𝑁+𝐾)⋅𝑎,QbN+K+1<aK+1=aK⋅a≤Q(N+K)⋅a,

and cancelling the positive factor 𝑄Q gives the claim. ◻

The necessary inequality in Theorem 6.2 shows the difference between these clearing tests at integer and noninteger rational bases. At 𝑏 =1b=1 it reads 1 <𝑎(𝑁 +𝐾)1<a(N+K), which holds for every 𝑎 ≥2a≥2 and every nonempty window; this necessary inequality imposes no obstruction. The other clearing conditions still have to be checked. At 𝑏 ≥2b≥2 the left side is exponential in 𝑁 +𝐾N+K and the right side is linear, so the conditions can hold only for bounded 𝑁 +𝐾N+K at each fixed base. At (𝑎,𝑏) =(3,2)(a,b)=(3,2) the necessary inequality already fails when 𝑁 =𝐾 =1N=K=1.

Example 6.3. Take the smallest window with 𝑁,𝐾 ≥1N,K≥1, namely 𝑁 =𝐾 =1N=K=1, and numerator 𝑎 =3a=3. At 𝑏 =1b=1 the tuple (3,1,1,1,3,1)(3,1,1,1,3,1) satisfies the conditions: 𝐷 =1 ≤2D=1≤2, the divisibility reads 3 ∣33∣3, and the last inequality reads 3 ⋅13 =3 <32 =93⋅13=3<32=9. At 𝑏 =2b=2 no choice works. The last inequality becomes 𝑄 ⋅23 <32Q⋅23<32, which forces 𝑄 =1Q=1; the divisibility then reads 3 ∣𝐷3∣D, and the only candidates are 𝐷 =1D=1 and 𝐷 =2D=2, neither divisible by 33. This is the proof of Theorem 6.2 in miniature: it derives 𝑎𝐾 ≤𝑄(𝑁 +𝐾)aK≤Q(N+K), here 3 ≤23≤2.

Feasibility of these necessary conditions is not a successful tail estimate. For (𝑎,𝑏,𝑁,𝐾,𝑄,𝐷) =(2,1,1,1,1,2)(a,b,N,K,Q,D)=(2,1,1,1,1,2) all six conditions hold, and 𝐷 =𝜏(𝑁 +𝐾)D=τ(N+K). Nevertheless,

2∑𝑚≥3𝜏(𝑚)2𝑚>2(223+324+225)=1.2∑m≥3τ(m)2m>2(223+324+225)=1.

The failure at 3/23/2 is useful because it already occurs at the weaker, necessary tests; their feasibility at an integer base proves no bound for the full remainder.

Proposition 6.4. For every natural number 𝑥 ≥2x≥2 we have 3𝑥 <2𝑥+13x<2x+1.

Proof. Induct on 𝑥x, starting from 6 <86<8 at 𝑥 =2x=2. For the step, 2𝑥+1 ≥22 >32x+1≥22>3 when 𝑥 ≥1x≥1, so 3(𝑥 +1) =3𝑥 +3 <2𝑥+1 +2𝑥+1 =2𝑥+23(x+1)=3x+3<2x+1+2x+1=2x+2. ◻

Theorem 6.5 (failure of the stated clearing conditions at 3/23/2). For all 𝑁 ≥1N≥1 and 𝐾 ≥1K≥1 and all natural 𝑄,𝐷Q,D, the tuple (3,2,𝑁,𝐾,𝑄,𝐷)(3,2,N,K,Q,D) does not satisfy the clearing conditions.

Proof. The clearing conditions would give 2𝑁+𝐾+1 <3(𝑁 +𝐾)2N+K+1<3(N+K) by Theorem 6.2, contradicting Proposition 6.4 applied to 𝑥 =𝑁 +𝐾 ≥2x=N+K≥2. ◻

Theorem 6.5 excludes the divisibility pattern and necessary tail test in Definition 6.1 at 3/23/2. It gives no conclusion about the rationality of 𝐹(3/2)F(3/2) or about clearing schemes with different conditions.
