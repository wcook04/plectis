Reciprocal Mersenne Subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 153 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A weighted summability criterion proves irrationality of reciprocal Mersenne subseries: if h is the part of a supported on a finite nonempty prime set P, every infinite A with ∑ ℎ(𝑎)/(𝑎(𝑏ℎ(𝑎) −1))h(a)/(a(bh(a)−1)) finite has irrational ∑ (𝑏𝑎 −1)−1(ba−1)−1. The base-two condition gives the conclusion at every integer base for every infinite subset, and permits an example with divergent reciprocal sum, while excluding full support and all odd exponents. The proof averages over a finite part of the support and over dyadic observation lengths. Erdős's weaker reciprocal-summable theorem is proved in full, and a common window extends both arguments to unions of weighted and positive-cover supports. Finite-denominator and achievement-set calculations formulate tests for the targets 1/21/2 and 1/211/21; those tests do not decide either target or the universal question. Prime support at base 2 and squarefree support at power-of-two bases are attributed as existing work.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #257: selected checked premises, finite computations, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, whose universal and half-value questions remain open.

Integer quotients and their remainder identity

The quotient rows are finite integer approximations to the real greedy rule, not identical finite stages of that rule. Their normalised weights tend to the Mersenne weights and their targets tend to 1/21/2. Proposition 8.1 proves agreement at each fixed prefix once the row index is sufficiently large; it does not assert simultaneous agreement at all ranks. Fix a row 𝑛 ≥6n≥6. The integer quotient weights at row 𝑛n are

𝑤(𝑛,𝑑):=⌊4𝑛2𝑑−1⌋,𝑑=2,…,𝑛−1,w(n,d):=⌊4n2d−1⌋,d=2,…,n−1,

and the integer target is 𝑇𝑛 :=22𝑛−1 −2𝑛Tn:=22n−1−2n. The integer greedy process takes ranks 𝑑 =2,…,𝑛 −1d=2,…,n−1 in ascending order against 𝑇𝑛Tn exactly as in Definition 5.11, producing a take set 𝐷𝑛 ⊆{2,…,𝑛 −1}Dn⊆{2,…,n−1} and skip set Skip𝑛 :={2,…,𝑛 −1}\𝐷𝑛Skipn:={2,…,n−1}\Dn. Define the integer remainder rem(𝑛) :=𝑇𝑛 −∑𝑑∈𝐷𝑛𝑤(𝑛,𝑑)rem(n):=Tn−∑d∈Dnw(n,d) and the scaled remainder deviation Δ𝑛 :=rem(𝑛) −2𝑛Δn:=rem(n)−2n.

Definition 5.13 (The full-support quotient recurrence). For 𝑀 ≥2M≥2, define

𝐾(𝑀):=2𝑀−1−𝑀∑𝑑=2⌊2𝑀2𝑑−1⌋.K(M):=2M−1−∑d=2M⌊2M2d−1⌋.

This depends only on 𝑀M, not on the selected support in a quotient row. The floor-quotient recurrence gives

𝐾(2)=1,𝐾(𝑀+1)=2𝐾(𝑀)−(𝜏(𝑀+1)−1).K(2)=1,K(M+1)=2K(M)−(τ(M+1)−1).

Here 𝜏(𝑛)τ(n) counts the positive divisors of 𝑛n. To see the correction term, double the quotients for 2 ≤𝑑 ≤𝑀2≤d≤M and add one for each such 𝑑d dividing 𝑀 +1M+1. The new endpoint 𝑑 =𝑀 +1d=M+1 contributes one more quotient. Together these are exactly the 𝜏(𝑀 +1) −1τ(M+1)−1 divisors at least two. This is an identity for every 𝑀 ≥2M≥2, not merely a checked finite recurrence.

Theorem 5.14 (Decomposition of the integer remainder). For every quotient row 𝑛 ≥6n≥6,

Δ𝑛=𝐾(2𝑛)+∑𝑑∈Skip𝑛⌊4𝑛2𝑑−1⌋.(I)(I)Δn=K(2n)+∑d∈Skipn⌊4n2d−1⌋.

Here the take and skip sets, capacity and remainder are those defined at the start of this subsection. The identity separates the contribution of the full exponent range from that of the omitted ranks. It makes no claim that there are few omitted ranks; controlling their sum is a separate part of any application.
Proof. For 𝑑 =𝑛d=n the quotient is 2𝑛 +12n+1, and for 𝑛 <𝑑 ≤2𝑛n<d≤2n it is 22𝑛−𝑑22n−d. Hence

2𝑛∑𝑑=𝑛⌊4𝑛2𝑑−1⌋=2𝑛+1+𝑛−1∑𝑗=02𝑗=2𝑛+1.∑d=n2n⌊4n2d−1⌋=2n+1+∑j=0n−12j=2n+1.

Substitute this in the definition of 𝐾(2𝑛)K(2n), split {2,…,𝑛 −1} =𝐷𝑛 ⊔Skip𝑛{2,…,n−1}=Dn⊔Skipn, and use Δ𝑛 =22𝑛−1 −2𝑛+1 −∑𝑑∈𝐷𝑛𝑤(𝑛,𝑑)Δn=22n−1−2n+1−∑d∈Dnw(n,d). This gives (I). Certification: rows 6 ≤𝑛 ≤2006≤n≤200 rechecked with zero mismatches by an exact program (§5.7).
Use: (I) permits substitution of information about 𝐾(2𝑛)K(2n). A resulting estimate for Δ𝑛Δn must also retain or bound the skip-set sum; information about 𝐾K alone need not control their cancellation.
