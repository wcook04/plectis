The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Counterexamples and limitations

A residue or coefficient condition may hold for a rational comparison series as well as for 𝑆S. Each example below records the information it preserves and the deduction it disproves. The irrationality of 𝑆S remains open, and an argument using further totient identities is unaffected by a comparison that does not preserve them.

Observation 6.114 (The prime-power restriction in the unit-gap refinement). The ordinary interval test excludes a rational denominator when its possible numerator interval contains no integer. Reduced fractions permit a refinement: every numerator is coprime to its denominator, so an interval containing only nonunits can also be excluded. 𝑉=(𝐾∑𝑟=1𝜑(𝑁+𝑟)2𝐾−𝑟)mod2𝐾,𝑢𝑉=𝐽2𝐾+𝑊,0≤𝑊<2𝐾,𝐿=⌊𝑊+𝑢(𝑁+𝐾+2)2𝐾⌋.V=(∑r=1Kφ(N+r)2K−r)mod2K,uV=J2K+W,0≤W<2K,L=⌊W+u(N+K+2)2K⌋.

Consequently any positive integer 𝐷D for which 𝐷𝐴(𝑛)/𝑛 ∈ℤDA(n)/n∈Z for all 1 ≤𝑛 ≤𝑁1≤n≤N must be divisible by every odd prime 𝑝 ≤𝑁p≤N, every prime square 𝑝2 ≤𝑁p2≤N, and by 44 once 𝑁 ≥4N≥4. No fixed 𝐷D can do this for all 𝑛n, since there are infinitely many odd primes. In periodic-point counting, the quantities 𝐴(𝑛)/𝑛A(n)/n would count orbits of length 𝑛n; their integrality and nonnegativity characterise realisable periodic-point sequences [28]. Here only the denominator obstruction is used. It excludes a fixed integer clearing all these particular coordinates, not every possible Euler-product representation or an argument with a multiplier growing with 𝑁N. It is not an irrationality proof for 𝑆S. no_fixed_integral_primitive_euler_index

Observation 6.119 (A finite exclusion is not a membership test). A successful residue certificate proves the exclusion stated in its soundness theorem. Failure of that sufficient condition at finitely many depths does not prove integrality of the tail difference. In particular, a finite unsuccessful search cannot be substituted for the eventual-period assertion needed to prove rationality. The same logical distinction occurs for the linked greedy test in a separate Mersenne achievement-set problem: its finite inequality implies nonmembership. The linked object is a definition, with a soundness theorem in the same file, not a theorem asserting a universal impossibility of finite rationality proofs for Problem #249. Membership or rationality established by some additional exact identity is not excluded by this observation.
