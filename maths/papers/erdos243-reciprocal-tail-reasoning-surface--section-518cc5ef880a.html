Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 61 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. For rational reciprocal sums under 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, the record also develops sufficient conditions for the eventual recurrence 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1, controlling either increases of an integer tail numerator or a convergent sum at steps setting new maxima. Examples separate numerical growth bounds from the exact recurrences. The cubic proof is distinguished from the theorems formalised in Lean. None of the additional bounds is derived for every rational reciprocal sum in Erdős #243.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #243, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

Reduction and lower density

For 𝑆 ⊆ℕS⊆N write 𝑑――(𝑆) =lim inf𝑋#(𝑆 ∩[1,𝑋])/𝑋d―(S)=lim infX#(S∩[1,X])/X. Thus 𝑑――(𝑆) =0d―(S)=0 gives cutoffs along which the exceptional proportion tends to zero, not eventual agreement. We cannot replace 𝐶𝑛Cn by its proposed polynomial at arbitrary indices; the following lemma supplies the finite windows where such a replacement is valid. For a sequence 𝐹F, let Δ𝐹𝑛 =𝐹𝑛+1 −𝐹𝑛ΔFn=Fn+1−Fn; higher powers of ΔΔ mean repeated forward differences. This operator is distinct from the later indexed quantity Δ𝑛Δn, the Sylvester defect.

Lemma 2.4 (periodic obstruction principle). Let ℎ,𝐿h,L be positive integers and let 𝑗1,…,𝑗𝐿j1,…,jL be fixed integer offsets. Suppose that for every sufficiently large 𝑛n in one residue class modulo ℎh at least one of 𝑛 +𝑗1,…,𝑛 +𝑗𝐿n+j1,…,n+jL lies in 𝑆S. Then 𝑑――(𝑆) ≥1/(𝐿ℎ)d―(S)≥1/(Lh).

Proof. There are 𝑋/ℎ +𝑂(1)X/h+O(1) relevant starting indices below 𝑋X. Their witnesses lie below 𝑋 +𝑂(1)X+O(1) because the offsets are fixed, and each element of 𝑆S serves as a witness for at most 𝐿L starting indices. Hence 𝐿#(𝑆 ∩[1,𝑋]) ≥𝑋/ℎ −𝑂(1)L#(S∩[1,X])≥X/h−O(1), which gives the claimed lower density. ◻

Lemma 2.6 (coprimality and irreducibility). On the primitive tail, gcd(𝑎𝑛,𝑣𝑛) =1gcd(an,vn)=1, the multipliers at distinct indices are pairwise coprime, infinitely many of them exceed 11, and the cubic 𝑄𝑚,𝑐Qm,c of (3) is irreducible over ℚQ.

Proof. A prime dividing 𝑎𝑛an and 𝑣𝑛vn would divide 𝑢𝑛+1un+1 and 𝑣𝑛+1vn+1, against gcd(𝑢𝑛+1,𝑣𝑛+1) =1gcd(un+1,vn+1)=1; and every earlier multiplier divides every later 𝑣𝑛vn, so distinct multipliers are coprime. If 𝑎𝑛 =1an=1 for all large 𝑛n then 𝑣𝑛vn is eventually a positive constant and 𝑢𝑛+1 =𝑢𝑛 −𝑣𝑛un+1=un−vn decreases without bound, against positivity. So infinitely many distinct primes divide late multipliers.

Suppose 𝑄𝑚,𝑐Qm,c had a rational root. Choose a prime ℓ ∤6𝑚ℓ∤6m dividing a late multiplier 𝑎𝑁aN, large enough that the root reduces modulo ℓℓ.
