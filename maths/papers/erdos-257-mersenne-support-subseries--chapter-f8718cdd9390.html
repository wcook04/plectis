Short paper

Irrationality criteria for Lambert subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 26 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The series ∑𝑎∈𝐴(𝑏𝑎 −1)−1∑a∈A(ba−1)−1 is irrational for every integer 𝑏 ≥2b≥2 and infinite A contained in E∪V when E satisfies a finite-prime weighted summability condition and V has a summable positive divisor cover. The two classes are incomparable, and their union reaches supports in neither class. A common average over dyadic lengths produces one index with both displacements small. Arbitrary infinite supports at base two remain unresolved.

This paper owns the weighted, divisor-cover and mixed support criteria with complete proofs and method limits; the long paper treats finite denominators, the achievement set and rational membership.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, which remains open.

Introduction

For a set 𝐴A of positive integers and a real number 𝑏 >1b>1, write

𝑋𝐴(𝑏)=∑𝑎∈𝐴1𝑏𝑎−1.XA(b)=∑a∈A1ba−1.

This series converges by comparison with a geometric series, since (𝑏𝑎 −1)−1 ≤𝑏1−𝑎/(𝑏 −1)(ba−1)−1≤b1−a/(b−1). Erdős proved irrationality for full support at integer bases [5], and later proved the same conclusion for pairwise coprime supports of finite reciprocal sum [7]. On that page he stated that the coprimality assumption could be removed, without giving the details. The question for arbitrary infinite support in base two is Problem #257:

Problem 1.1 (Erdős #257). Is 𝑋𝐴(2)XA(2) irrational for every infinite 𝐴 ⊆ℕ>0A⊆N>0?

We seek conditions inherited by every infinite subset of the support. The first replaces reciprocal summability by a divisibility-sensitive weight. For 𝑎 =2𝑘𝑚a=2km with 𝑚m odd, it uses 1/[𝑚(𝑏2𝑘 −1)]1/[m(b2k−1)] in place of 1/(2𝑘𝑚)1/(2km): a large power of 22 can compensate for many odd cofactors. More generally, fix a finite set 𝑃P of primes and write ℎ(𝑎) =∏𝑝∈𝑃𝑝𝑣𝑝(𝑎)h(a)=∏p∈Ppvp(a) for the 𝑃P-part of 𝑎a, where 𝑣𝑝(𝑎)vp(a) is the exponent of 𝑝p in 𝑎a. The prime powers may grow with 𝑎a, but the prime set is fixed.

Theorem 1.2 (a weighted condition on the support). Let 𝑏 ≥2b≥2 be an integer, let 𝐴 ⊆ℕ>0A⊆N>0 be infinite, and let 𝑃P be a finite nonempty set of primes. Set ℎ(𝑎) =∏𝑝∈𝑃𝑝𝑣𝑝(𝑎)h(a)=∏p∈Ppvp(a). If

𝑊𝑏,𝑃(𝐴):=∑𝑎∈𝐴ℎ(𝑎)𝑎(𝑏ℎ(𝑎)−1)<∞,(1)(1)Wb,P(A):=∑a∈Ah(a)a(bh(a)−1)<∞,

then 𝑋𝐴(𝑏)XA(b) is irrational. In particular, the base-two condition

∑𝑎∈𝐴ℎ(𝑎)𝑎(2ℎ(𝑎)−1)<∞(W)(W)∑a∈Ah(a)a(2h(a)−1)<∞

implies that 𝑋𝐴(𝑏)XA(b) is irrational for every integer 𝑏 ≥2b≥2. Both conclusions are hereditary under passage to infinite subsets.

Consider the support

𝐴⋆={2𝑘𝑚:𝑘≥1, 𝑚 odd, 𝑚≤22𝑘}.A⋆={2km:k≥1, m odd, m≤22k}.

For a fixed 𝑘k, the sum of 1/𝑚1/m over the odd cofactors is of order 2𝑘2k. Thus the corresponding terms contribute at least 1/41/4 to ∑𝑎∈𝐴⋆1/𝑎∑a∈A⋆1/a, but at most 21−𝑘21−k to (W). The calculation in Section 2 proves both bounds. Every infinite subset of 𝐴⋆A⋆ consequently gives an irrational value at every integer base. Since ℎ/(2ℎ −1) ≤1h/(2h−1)≤1, the same theorem also includes reciprocal-summable supports. Section A gives a direct proof of Erdős’s stated extension.

The second condition uses joint divisibility: it bounds fractional powers of divisor counts by positive divisor sums (Theorem 3.1). Neither condition implies the other, but they combine: every infinite subset of 𝐸 ∪𝑉E∪V has irrational sum when 𝐸E satisfies the base-two weighted condition and 𝑉V has a summable divisor cover (Theorem 4.1). Two irrational subseries could sum to a rational number, so we must control their errors at the same index. The separating constructions in Proposition 5.1 give such a union outside both individual classes (Corollary 5.2). These are sufficient conditions, not a characterisation of the supports in Problem 1.1.

The proofs compare a multiple of 𝑋𝐴(𝑏)XA(b) with an integer. For a positive integer 𝑁N, put

Δ𝑏,𝐴(𝑁)=∑𝑎∈𝐴𝑏𝑁mod𝑎−1𝑏𝑎−1=(𝑏𝑁−1)𝑋𝐴(𝑏)−𝐽𝑏,𝐴(𝑁),𝐽𝑏,𝐴(𝑁)=∑𝑎∈𝐴𝑎≤𝑁⌊𝑁/𝑎⌋∑𝑗=1𝑏𝑁−𝑗𝑎.(D)(D)Δb,A(N)=∑a∈AbNmoda−1ba−1=(bN−1)XA(b)−Jb,A(N),Jb,A(N)=∑a∈Aa≤N∑j=1⌊N/a⌋bN−ja.

Division of 𝑁N by each 𝑎a gives the identity. At an integer base, 𝐽𝑏,𝐴(𝑁)Jb,A(N) is an integer; for infinite 𝐴A, the displacement is positive because some 𝑎 ∈𝐴a∈A exceeds 𝑁N. Thus, if 𝑋𝐴(𝑏) =𝑝/𝑞XA(b)=p/q with 𝑝 ∈ℤp∈Z and 𝑞 ≥1q≥1 an integer, then 𝑞Δ𝑏,𝐴(𝑁)qΔb,A(N) is a positive integer and Δ𝑏,𝐴(𝑁) ≥1/𝑞Δb,A(N)≥1/q. We seek displacements tending to zero; Δ𝑏,𝐴(𝑁)Δb,A(N) itself is not a fractional part and may exceed 11. Erdős also proposed using fractional parts of powers times the series value [7].

Divisibility cancels finitely many exponents when 𝑁N is a multiple of their least common multiple. The remaining terms have small complete-period means when their gcd with the modulus is large. Incomplete-period bounds lack a factor 1/𝑎1/a. Averaging over dyadic lengths restores it: an exponent enters the truncated error only when the sampled range reaches it, and the reciprocals of subsequent lengths form a geometric tail bounded by a multiple of 1/𝑎1/a. Exponents beyond the sampled range are estimated separately. Explicit modulus dependence lets both criteria share this average.

Sections 2–4 prove the three criteria; Sections 5–6 separate their support classes and explain the method’s limits. The appendices give the direct reciprocal-summable proof, finite-denominator results and rational-membership tests. The last of these decide neither 1/21/2 nor 1/211/21.
