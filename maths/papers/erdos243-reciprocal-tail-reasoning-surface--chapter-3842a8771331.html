Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 61 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. For rational reciprocal sums under 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, the record also develops sufficient conditions for the eventual recurrence 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1, controlling either increases of an integer tail numerator or a convergent sum at steps setting new maxima. Examples separate numerical growth bounds from the exact recurrences. The cubic proof is distinguished from the theorems formalised in Lean. None of the additional bounds is derived for every rational reciprocal sum in Erdős #243.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #243, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

Further questions

We collect the restrictions on a possible counterexample and the estimates which would exclude it. The constant and periodic arguments concern all-negative tails. They leave open a periodic pattern seen only along the negative indices of a mixed-sign sequence. The unrestricted problem remains unresolved here.

Proposition 14.1 (necessary conditions on a counterexample). For the integer tail attached to any counterexample to Problem 1.1,

𝐸𝑛≠0eventually,|𝐸𝑛|𝐶𝑛⟶0,En≠0eventually,|En|Cn⟶0,

and

lim sup𝑛→∞𝐸𝑛<0(−𝐸𝑛)=∞,∞∑𝑛=0(−𝐸𝑛)+𝐶𝑛=∞.lim supn→∞En<0(−En)=∞,∑n=0∞(−En)+Cn=∞.

In particular, negative indices occur infinitely often. Thus the remaining regime consists of unbounded negative errors along exact reciprocal tails for which the sum of relative increases diverges.

Proof. Koizumi’s construction gives vanishing relative error after a finite shift, hence |𝐸𝑛| <𝐶𝑛|En|<Cn eventually. Absorption then makes 𝐸𝑛 ≠0En≠0 eventually, since eventual zero would give the Sylvester recurrence. Descent excludes an eventually nonnegative error. Theorem 12.1 excludes a bounded negative part, and Theorem 13.1 excludes convergence of the sum of relative increases. These statements are unchanged by deleting a finite prefix. ◻

Proposition 14.1 does not assert infinitely many prime divisors of the error magnitudes. Nor are its numerical conditions alone a reformulation of the problem: the numerator and denominator must satisfy the exact recurrences. With those recurrences, positivity and vanishing relative error, Section 4 supplies the reciprocal-series realisation. The following exact-orbit question is therefore equivalent to the original problem.

Problem 14.2 (unbounded negative errors with divergent relative sum). Exclude, or construct, natural-number sequences (𝑎,𝐷,𝐶)(a,D,C) satisfying (19), with 𝑎𝑛 >1an>1 and 𝐶𝑛 >0Cn>0, whose multipliers satisfy lim𝑛𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 =1limnan+1/an2=1 and whose error satisfies vanishing relative error, and which is negative infinitely often with magnitudes unbounded along that infinite set and

∑𝑛(−𝐸𝑛)+𝐶𝑛=∞.∑n(−En)+Cn=∞.

Such an orbit fails the bounded-negative-part and summable-relative-increase hypotheses. By Proposition 14.1 the integer tail of a counterexample is such an orbit after deletion of a finite prefix, so an exclusion would settle Problem #243. Conversely, a global orbit with the displayed properties has 𝐶𝑛/𝐷𝑛 →0Cn/Dn→0 by Section 4. The reciprocal-series realisation then identifies its reciprocal sum as 𝐶0/𝐷0C0/D0. After deletion of a finite prefix the multipliers are strictly increasing, and the infinitely many negative errors rule out a Sylvester tail. It is therefore a counterexample to Problem #243. Finite admissible prefixes alone do not provide such a construction.

Remark. The recurrences alone admit unbounded negative errors for which ∑𝑛( −𝐸𝑛)+/𝐶𝑛∑n(−En)+/Cn diverges. The following example fails both the relative-error and growth assumptions of Problem 14.2. Put

𝐶𝑛=2𝑛,𝑏0=2,𝑏𝑛+1=12𝑏𝑛(𝑏𝑛+2),𝑎𝑛=𝑏𝑛+2,𝐷𝑛=𝑏𝑛𝐶𝑛,Cn=2n,b0=2,bn+1=12bn(bn+2),an=bn+2,Dn=bnCn,

so that (𝑏𝑛) =2,4,12,84,3612,…(bn)=2,4,12,84,3612,… and (𝑎𝑛) =4,6,14,86,3614,…(an)=4,6,14,86,3614,…. Every 𝑏𝑛bn is a positive even integer, since 𝑏𝑛 =2𝑘bn=2k gives 𝑏𝑛+1 =2𝑘(𝑘 +1)bn+1=2k(k+1), so all four sequences are ℕN-valued. They satisfy both recurrences: 𝐶𝑛+1 +𝐷𝑛 =2𝑛(2 +𝑏𝑛) =𝑎𝑛𝐶𝑛Cn+1+Dn=2n(2+bn)=anCn and 𝐷𝑛+1 =𝑏𝑛+12𝑛+1 =𝑏𝑛(𝑏𝑛 +2)2𝑛 =𝑎𝑛𝐷𝑛Dn+1=bn+12n+1=bn(bn+2)2n=anDn. Its error is

𝐸𝑛=𝐷𝑛−(𝑎𝑛−1)𝐶𝑛=𝑏𝑛2𝑛−(𝑏𝑛+1)2𝑛=−𝐶𝑛,En=Dn−(an−1)Cn=bn2n−(bn+1)2n=−Cn,

so the negative magnitudes 2𝑛2n are unbounded and ∑𝑛( −𝐸𝑛)+/𝐶𝑛∑n(−En)+/Cn diverges. Here |𝐸𝑛| =𝐶𝑛|En|=Cn at every index, so both the strict inequality |𝐸𝑛| <𝐶𝑛|En|<Cn and the vanishing relative error fail. The multipliers satisfy 𝑎𝑛+1 =12(𝑎2𝑛 −2𝑎𝑛 +4)an+1=12(an2−2an+4), so 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →12an+1/an2→12 and the growth hypothesis fails as well. The example therefore says nothing about Problem 1.1, and it does not isolate the error bounds: 𝐸𝑛 = −𝐶𝑛En=−Cn at every index forces that multiplier recurrence, so growth fails with them. It is recorded only to show that the two recurrences alone do not force the required conclusion.

A scalar profile need not satisfy the recurrences.
