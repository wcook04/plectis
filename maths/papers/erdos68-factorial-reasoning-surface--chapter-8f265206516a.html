Factorial Linear Forms and Denominators: Detailed Proofs and Rationality Criteria

Erdős #68 By Will Cook 1 September 2026; revised 7 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 51 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The record proves liminf (log LN)/(N3/2 log N) ≥2√2/3≥22/3 for L_N=lcm(2!−1,…,N!−1), by comparing a terminal block of denominators with pairwise gcds rather than an external multiplicity theorem. Specialising a Louwsma–Martino valuation, it exhibits primes 139 and 2593 absent from all later reduced denominators. Coefficient constructions give integer forms MS+k; at fixed moment the remainder changes only by an integer. Rationality criteria use factorial digits and the next integer above a scaled partial sum; the digit argument also recovers the irrationality of e. Two exact calculations give q∤299999! and 𝑞 ≥239990 >1012038q≥239990>1012038. These exclusions do not prove irrationality.

An elementary criterion for any real number

The fractional-part condition in (20) is an instance of Cantor’s termination criterion. For any real 𝑥x and positive sequence 𝑐𝑚cm satisfying 𝑐𝑚 ≤1/𝑚cm≤1/m eventually, we have

𝑥∉ℚ⟺{(𝑚−1)!𝑥}≥𝑐𝑚 for arbitrarily large 𝑚.x∉Q⟺{(m−1)!x}≥cm for arbitrarily large m.

For rational 𝑥x the fractional parts vanish eventually. Conversely, if {(𝑚 −1)!𝑥} <𝑐𝑚 ≤1/𝑚{(m−1)!x}<cm≤1/m for all large 𝑚m, then 𝑚{(𝑚 −1)!𝑥} <1m{(m−1)!x}<1, so every sufficiently late canonical digit is zero. Cantor’s criterion then makes 𝑥x rational. Equality 𝑐𝑚 =1/𝑚cm=1/m is allowed: it is failure of the displayed weak inequality that makes 𝑚{(𝑚 −1)!𝑥}m{(m−1)!x} strictly less than one. For 𝑥 =𝑆x=S, the choice 𝑐𝑚 =𝐸𝑚/𝑚cm=Em/m gives exactly (20); indeed 0 <𝐸𝑚 <10<Em<1 ensures the required threshold bound. The finite test (21) is a separate sufficient condition at one index. Its cofinal converse uses the carry theorem, as shown in §4, not substitution into this general criterion. Neither argument proves that 𝑆S meets the test at arbitrarily large indices.

The threshold 1/𝑚1/m cannot be replaced by 𝜆/𝑚λ/m for a fixed 𝜆 >1λ>1. For 𝑥 =𝑒x=e and 𝑚 ≥3m≥3, the factorial tail gives

𝑚{(𝑚−1)!𝑒}=1+𝑚!∑𝑛>𝑚1𝑛!<1+1𝑚<𝜆eventually.m{(m−1)!e}=1+m!∑n>m1n!<1+1m<λeventually.

Thus the irrational number 𝑒e would never meet the enlarged threshold at sufficiently large indices. Positivity is also essential: with 𝑐𝑚 =0cm=0, rational numbers would meet the threshold eventually. These examples explain the two assumptions without imposing any equidistribution hypothesis.
