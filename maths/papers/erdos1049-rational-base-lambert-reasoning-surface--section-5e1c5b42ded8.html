Geometric Moments and Rational Lambert Values: Proofs and Further Results

Erdős #1049 By Will Cook September 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 79 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. Specialising Zudilin's 2004 linear forms shows F(a/b) irrational for coprime 𝑎 >𝑏 ≥1a>b≥1 when θ=log b / log a is less than θ^*=0.4056830213840605…, with irrationality-exponent bound (1−θ)/(θ*−θ) for every positive integral power. In particular the bound is less than 301 for every positive integral power of 31/431/4. The constants are Zudilin's; the proof cancels common cyclotomic factors before rational specialisation. For the 2016 Hankel construction with x=z=1, the first nonzero term of the normalised determinant is computed at every rank. A separate positive-measure argument proves positivity and a size estimate for each fixed real 0<q<1. The final sections explain the limits of the stated clearing and congruence arguments at 3/23/2: no small nonzero sequence of divided remainders is constructed there, and the full rational-base conjecture remains open.

The height criterion at 7/27/2

The base 7/27/2 already lies in the irrationality region of Bundschuh and Väänänen [6]. We check their height condition in their notation, with 𝑞q denoting the base. At 𝛼 = −1α=−1 it reads

𝜆<(12+1𝜋2)−1.λ<(12+1π2)−1.

Every denominator is positive, so the reciprocal inequalities have the displayed directions. ◻

Numerically the two sides are 1.5533…1.5533… and 1.6630…1.6630…, so the condition holds with a margin of about 0.110.11. The Lean proof factors the estimate through the explicit height-region predicate and the integer certificate 218 <77218<77, the resulting logarithmic ratio bound log⁡2/log⁡7 <7/18log⁡2/log⁡7<7/18, the 𝜋π-bound 1/𝜋2 <1/91/π2<1/9, and the strict margin

log⁡2log⁡7<12−1𝜋2.log⁡2log⁡7<12−1π2.

The final height inequality then rewrites log⁡(7/2) =log⁡7 −log⁡2log⁡(7/2)=log⁡7−log⁡2 and closes the displayed condition.

This formalises the complete elementary parameter check at 𝑞 =7/2q=7/2; it does not formalise Bundschuh and Väänänen’s analytic irrationality theorem, whose proof occupies pp. 189–193 of the source. The conclusion that 𝐹(7/2)F(7/2) is irrational is consequently cited from that theorem, not claimed as a Lean theorem here.

The criterion does not cover 3/23/2, and the results of Sections 6 and 7 give no evidence either way about whether 𝐹(3/2)F(3/2) is irrational.
