Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 61 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. For rational reciprocal sums under 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, the record also develops sufficient conditions for the eventual recurrence 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1, controlling either increases of an integer tail numerator or a convergent sum at steps setting new maxima. Examples separate numerical growth bounds from the exact recurrences. The cubic proof is distinguished from the theorems formalised in Lean. None of the additional bounds is derived for every rational reciprocal sum in Erdős #243.

Comparison with Duverney’s reduced parameters.

For the positive reciprocal series, the eventually reduced parameters in Duverney’s Theorem 3.1 and Section 5.2 [2] can be taken as follows. The symbols 𝑝𝑛,𝑞𝑛pn,qn in this comparison are Duverney’s auxiliary integers, not the fixed numerator and denominator of the full reciprocal sum:

𝑝𝑛=𝑢𝑛,𝑞𝑛=𝑤𝑛=𝑎𝑛𝑢𝑛−𝑣𝑛=ℎ𝑛𝑢𝑛+1,𝐸𝑛=𝐺𝑛(𝑝𝑛−𝑞𝑛).pn=un,qn=wn=anun−vn=hnun+1,En=Gn(pn−qn).

Indeed, gcd(𝑢𝑛,𝑤𝑛) =gcd(𝑢𝑛,𝑣𝑛) =1gcd(un,wn)=gcd(un,vn)=1. Since 𝑎𝑛𝑣𝑛 =𝑎2𝑛𝑢𝑛 −𝑎𝑛𝑤𝑛anvn=an2un−anwn, reduction of the next tail gives

ℎ𝑛=gcd(𝑤𝑛,𝑎𝑛𝑣𝑛)=gcd(𝑤𝑛,𝑎2𝑛),𝑝𝑛+1∣𝑞𝑛.hn=gcd(wn,anvn)=gcd(wn,an2),pn+1∣qn.

For the gcd equality, subtract the multiple 𝑎𝑛𝑤𝑛anwn and use the coprimality of 𝑢𝑛,𝑤𝑛un,wn; the divisibility follows from 𝑞𝑛 =ℎ𝑛𝑝𝑛+1qn=hnpn+1. Eliminating 𝑣𝑛+1vn+1 then gives Duverney’s recurrence:

𝑎𝑛+1=𝑝𝑛𝑞𝑛𝑎2𝑛−𝑎𝑛+𝑞𝑛+1𝑝𝑛+1.an+1=pnqnan2−an+qn+1pn+1.

Thus 𝑞𝑛/𝑝𝑛+1 =ℎ𝑛qn/pn+1=hn is precisely the cancellation factor, and 𝑞𝑛/𝑝𝑛 →1qn/pn→1. This identifies the reduced parameters, not every possible unreduced choice in the original theorem. An upper bound on 𝑞𝑛 −𝑝𝑛qn−pn controls the increase before cancellation in the reduced coordinates. To transfer that bound directly to ( −𝐸𝑛)+ =𝐺𝑛(𝑞𝑛 −𝑝𝑛)+(−En)+=Gn(qn−pn)+ would also require control of 𝐺𝑛Gn.

An old prime power can be lost only when the multiplier supplies exactly its exponent and subtraction cancels more. The formula below separates this case from unequal exponents.

Lemma 7.1 (the denominator valuation transition). Let 𝑢,𝑣,𝑎u,v,a be positive integers, gcd(𝑢,𝑣) =1gcd(u,v)=1, and 𝑤 =𝑎𝑢 −𝑣 >0w=au−v>0. Put ℎ =gcd(𝑤,𝑎𝑣)h=gcd(w,av) and 𝑣′ =𝑎𝑣/ℎv′=av/h. For a prime 𝑝p, write 𝑟 =𝜈𝑝(𝑎)r=νp(a), 𝑠 =𝜈𝑝(𝑣)s=νp(v) and 𝑡 =𝜈𝑝(𝑤)t=νp(w). Then

𝜈𝑝(𝑣′)={max(𝑟,𝑠),𝑟≠𝑠,max(0,2𝑠−𝑡),𝑟=𝑠.νp(v′)={max(r,s),r≠s,max(0,2s−t),r=s.

In particular 𝜈𝑝(𝑣′) ≤max(𝑟,𝑠)νp(v′)≤max(r,s). A strict loss relative to 𝑠s requires 𝑟 =𝑠 ≥1r=s≥1 and 𝑡 >𝑠t>s.

Proof. Always 𝜈𝑝(𝑣′) =𝑟 +𝑠 −min(𝑡,𝑟 +𝑠)νp(v′)=r+s−min(t,r+s). If 𝑟 ≠𝑠r≠s, primitivity implies 𝑡 =min(𝑟,𝑠)t=min(r,s): when 𝑠 >0s>0, 𝑢u is a 𝑝p-adic unit, and when 𝑠 =0 <𝑟s=0<r, 𝑣v is a unit. If 𝑟 =𝑠 >0r=s>0, both terms in 𝑎𝑢 −𝑣au−v are divisible by 𝑝𝑠ps, so 𝑡 ≥𝑠t≥s; substituting gives the second case. If 𝑟 =𝑠 =0r=s=0, the same formula gives zero without needing 𝑢u to be a unit. ◻

Cancellation is measured against the enlarged denominator 𝑎𝑣av, not against 𝑣v. Thus ℎ >1h>1 need not mean that an old prime power is lost. For example, (𝑢,𝑣,𝑎) =(2,15,9)(u,v,a)=(2,15,9) gives 𝑤 =3w=3, ℎ =3h=3 and (𝑢′,𝑣′) =(1,45)(u′,v′)=(1,45). The numerator before cancellation exceeds 𝑢u by only 𝑤 −𝑢 =1w−u=1, yet the 33-adic valuation of the reduced denominator rises from 11 to 22. This is a finite exact step, not an infinite counterexample.

Corollary 7.2 (persistence of a prime power). Suppose 𝑝𝑘 ∣𝑣𝑠pk∣vs. If 𝑤𝑛 <𝑝𝑘+1wn<pk+1 at every step from 𝑠s through 𝑡 −1t−1, then 𝑝𝑘 ∣𝑣𝑡pk∣vt.

Proof. At a first loss of divisibility by 𝑝𝑘pk, the current exponent is some 𝑗 ≥𝑘j≥k and the valuation lemma forces 𝜈𝑝(𝑤𝑛) >𝑗νp(wn)>j. This would give 𝑤𝑛 ≥𝑝𝑗+1 ≥𝑝𝑘+1wn≥pj+1≥pk+1, contrary to the hypothesis. ◻

The bound on 𝑤𝑛wn protects the old prime power despite varying cancellation factors. It is needed only until the crossing under study.
