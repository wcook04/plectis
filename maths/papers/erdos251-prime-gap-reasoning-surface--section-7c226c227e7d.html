Prime-Gap Dyadic Series: Perturbations, Exact Criteria and Certificates

Erdős #251 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 47 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A convergent dyadic series with nonnegative integer coefficients can be made rational by sparse nonnegative corrections of upper Banach density zero, eventually bounded by any f(n)→∞, while preserving every fixed modulus and, for a logarithmic allowance, short-block empirical distributions. For prime gaps the cumulative positions remain asymptotic to n log n; no primality conclusion is asserted. Summation by parts reduces the prime series to the gap series, and the tails 𝑇𝑁 =∑𝑗≥1𝑔𝑁+𝑗2−𝑗TN=∑j≥1gN+j2−j satisfy 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1. That recurrence characterises rationality and yields finite tests with explicit remainder bounds. An exact finite certificate shows that a rational value of the prime series would have denominator at least 239998 >1012040239998>1012040. The bound does not establish irrationality.

Polynomial coefficients and telescoping sums

We can construct further rational sums by starting with a polynomial 𝑈𝑛Un and defining 𝑐𝑛+1 =2𝑈𝑛 −𝑈𝑛+1cn+1=2Un−Un+1. Polynomial growth gives the vanishing dyadic boundary condition, so the recurrence identifies 𝑈𝑛Un as the complete tail. A quadratic choice already makes the coefficients positive and increasing.

Proposition 8.9 (a quadratic sequence with integral tails). Put 𝑐𝑛 =2(𝑛2 +4𝑛 +2)cn=2(n2+4n+2) and 𝑈𝑛 =2(𝑛 +4)2Un=2(n+4)2. Then 𝑐𝑛cn is positive, even and strictly increasing, 𝑈𝑛+1 =2𝑈𝑛 −𝑐𝑛+1Un+1=2Un−cn+1, every shift 𝑈𝑁+ℎ −𝑈𝑁UN+h−UN is integral, 𝑐𝑛+1 −𝑐𝑛 =4𝑛 +10cn+1−cn=4n+10 is never ±2±2, and

∑𝑗≥1𝑐𝑗2𝑗=32.∑j≥1cj2j=32.

Positivity, parity, strict growth, unboundedness and nonperiodicity are therefore jointly compatible with rationality, and the adjacent-gap condition of Theorem 6.4 fails at every index. The telescoping mechanism is the one used in the note of ChatGPT 5.4 Pro (orchestrated by Vjeko Kovač) against the variable-denominator expectation of Erdős [24]; the sequence is not a result about Problem #251.

Rationality also fails to force the coefficients to repeat. Let 𝐾 :ℕ →ℚK:N→Q be arbitrary and put 𝜅𝑛 =2𝐾𝑛 −𝐾𝑛+1κn=2Kn−Kn+1: the coefficient of the telescoping series.

The identity 𝜅𝑛 =2𝐾𝑛 −𝐾𝑛+1κn=2Kn−Kn+1 has the algebraic form used in the rationality criterion of Erdős and Straus when 𝑎𝑛 =2an=2. That criterion has additional hypotheses: for integers 𝑏𝑛bn and positive integers 𝑎𝑛an with 𝑎𝑛 >1an>1 for all large 𝑛n and |𝑏𝑛|/(𝑎𝑛−1𝑎𝑛) →0|bn|/(an−1an)→0, the series ∑𝑛𝑏𝑛/(𝑎1⋯𝑎𝑛)∑nbn/(a1⋯an) is rational exactly when some positive integer 𝐵B and integers 𝑐𝑛cn satisfy 𝐵𝑏𝑛 =𝑐𝑛𝑎𝑛 −𝑐𝑛+1Bbn=cnan−cn+1 and |𝑐𝑛+1| <𝑎𝑛/2|cn+1|<an/2 for all large 𝑛n [7]. With 𝑎𝑛 =2an=2, the smallness hypothesis becomes |𝑏𝑛|/4 →0|bn|/4→0. Since the 𝑏𝑛bn are integers, this forces 𝑏𝑛 =0bn=0 eventually. Thus the criterion in this specialisation does not apply even to a bounded integer sequence that is nonzero infinitely often, let alone to the positive prime gaps. Proposition 8.10, by contrast, imposes no growth condition on 𝐾K. The infinite series converges precisely when 2−𝑛𝐾𝑛2−nKn has a finite limit, and then its sum is 𝐾0 −lim𝑛→∞2−𝑛𝐾𝑛K0−limn→∞2−nKn. The sum equals 𝐾0K0 precisely when that limit is zero; polynomial growth suffices. For 𝐾𝑛 =2𝑛Kn=2n, every 𝜅𝑛κn is zero, so the series converges to 00, not to 𝐾0 =1K0=1.

Taking 𝐾0 =52K0=52 and 𝐾𝑛 =2𝑛 +2Kn=2n+2 for 𝑛 ≥1n≥1 gives 𝜅0 =1κ0=1 and 𝜅𝑛 =2𝑛κn=2n, so the coefficients 1,2,4,6,8,…1,2,4,6,8,… are positive, even after the first term, unbounded and not eventually periodic, while their dyadic sum is 5252. Rationality alone therefore cannot imply eventual periodicity even for a positive, parity-correct integer coefficient sequence, and rationality cannot be contradicted merely by Proposition 6.3.
