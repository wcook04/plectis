Reciprocal Mersenne Subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 153 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A weighted summability criterion proves irrationality of reciprocal Mersenne subseries: if h is the part of a supported on a finite nonempty prime set P, every infinite A with ∑ ℎ(𝑎)/(𝑎(𝑏ℎ(𝑎) −1))h(a)/(a(bh(a)−1)) finite has irrational ∑ (𝑏𝑎 −1)−1(ba−1)−1. The base-two condition gives the conclusion at every integer base for every infinite subset, and permits an example with divergent reciprocal sum, while excluding full support and all odd exponents. The proof averages over a finite part of the support and over dyadic observation lengths. Erdős's weaker reciprocal-summable theorem is proved in full, and a common window extends both arguments to unions of weighted and positive-cover supports. Finite-denominator and achievement-set calculations formulate tests for the targets 1/21/2 and 1/211/21; those tests do not decide either target or the universal question. Prime support at base 2 and squarefree support at power-of-two bases are attributed as existing work.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #257: selected checked premises, finite computations, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, whose universal and half-value questions remain open.

Introduction

We begin with these antecedents and prove two hereditary criteria which also admit supports of divergent reciprocal sum. If 𝑋𝐴(𝑏) =𝑝/𝑞XA(b)=p/q with 𝑞 >0q>0, then 𝑞Δ𝑏,𝐴(𝑁) ∈ℤqΔb,A(N)∈Z, so Δ𝑏,𝐴(𝑁) ≥1/𝑞Δb,A(N)≥1/q. We rule out rationality by making this difference arbitrarily small. In a finite average, the incomplete residue periods produce an error without the factor 1/𝑎1/a needed for summation over exponents. Averaging again over dyadic lengths restores this factor. The decisive point for the union theorem is stronger than proving the two criteria separately: both errors are controlled on the same finite distribution, and one index is chosen only after they have been added. Keeping the modulus dependence explicit makes that common distribution available to the cover estimate.

Part I gives the support theorems and the proofs followed by the short paper, Irrationality criteria for Lambert subseries. The subsequent parts concern a different question: whether a prescribed rational number belongs to the Mersenne achievement set. The finite identities in Sections 5–8 describe the integer quotients and their relation to the real greedy sequence. Sections 9–12 state the additional uniform or unbounded-depth assertions needed to apply them. Notation specific to a recurrence is introduced with that recurrence.

Section 13 returns to the support criteria. A logarithmic divisor cost cannot uniformly replace the fractional-cover cost when sampling on multiples of a prescribed modulus, even over complete periods. We prove the counterexample and an initial-interval estimate that survives it.

None of these arguments decides the universal problem, half-membership or 1/211/21-membership. The conditional results specify sufficient hypotheses or equivalent membership conditions, and the computations cover only the stated finite ranges. Each counterexample concerns the particular estimate under discussion.
