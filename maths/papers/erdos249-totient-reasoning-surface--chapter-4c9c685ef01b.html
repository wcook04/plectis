The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

The fixed Farey bound

Theorem 9.30. If 𝑆S is rational, its reduced denominator exceeds 7.963 964 664 670 137 532 335 577 487 583 105 3 ×10347.9639646646701375323355774875831053×1034. This is the finite denominator exclusion in the corpus, logically independent of the certificate-supply reduction.

Remark 9.31 (What a larger Farey bound requires). Unbounded valid denominator exclusions would imply 𝑆 ∉ℚS∉Q. For the single window 𝐾 =240K=240, however, the first-failure computation (gap_check_window_1_240_first_failure) proves that the residue inequality fails at the mediant denominator 𝑏 +𝑑 =𝑄0 +1b+d=Q0+1. This exact first failure prevents a larger cutoff from that same inequality at that same window. It is not an obstruction to other windows or to other tests using the finite data.

The existing proofs for individual windows use explicit totient residues and rational endpoint calculations. Extending that implementation calls for new finite data; a uniform proof of a growing bound need not use hard-coded convergents. If 𝑆S had reduced denominator 𝑞q, every valid exclusion bound would be smaller than 𝑞q. Thus unbounded exclusions suffice for irrationality; rationality would bound them, but would not force eventual constancy. No converse or comparison with the continued-fraction denominators of 𝑆S is proved here.

Observation 9.32 (The limited gain from a unit-gap refinement). The finite unit-gap condition of Observation 6.114 (ReducedDenominatorUnitGapCert) and its quantified version (PrimitiveReducedDenominatorUnitGapSupply) are definitions; the latter is not asserted to hold. Two separate results (reducedDenominatorUnitGapCert_prime_pow_iff, reducedDenominatorCandidateCount_prime_pow_le_one) show that, for 𝑝p prime and 𝑒 ≥1e≥1, a window at denominator 𝑝𝑒pe that passes the refined test contains at most one candidate integer. A window with two or more candidates fails the test. This is a condition for successful certification, not a bound on all windows or on the improvement in the denominator cutoff. It does not apply to all odd denominators: Observation 6.114 gives an actual totient window with two admissible nonunit candidates modulo 1515.

Proposition 9.33 (A sufficient extension). Suppose 𝑔(𝐾) →∞g(K)→∞ and, for every 𝐾K, the (𝑁 =1,𝐾)(N=1,K) gap check excludes every rational of reduced denominator at most 𝑔(𝐾)g(K). Then 𝑆S is irrational: any rational value of 𝑆S would have a fixed finite denominator, contradicted at a sufficiently large 𝐾K. Only unbounded exclusion bounds along a sequence of windows are needed for this argument. No equivalence with a bound for the continued-fraction denominators of 𝑆S is asserted; such a comparison would require its own proof connecting those convergents to the certified intervals.
