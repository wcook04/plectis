Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 61 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. For rational reciprocal sums under 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, the record also develops sufficient conditions for the eventual recurrence 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1, controlling either increases of an integer tail numerator or a convergent sum at steps setting new maxima. Examples separate numerical growth bounds from the exact recurrences. The cubic proof is distinguished from the theorems formalised in Lean. None of the additional bounds is derived for every rational reciprocal sum in Erdős #243.

Square specialisation

For the proposed cubic 𝑓(𝑇) =𝑇3 −𝑇 +6𝑐/𝑚f(T)=T3−T+6c/m, the recurrence will force 𝑟2 −1r2−1 to be a square whenever 𝑟r is a root modulo a good prime. We need the same conclusion for an algebraic root 𝛼α in ℚ(𝛼)Q(α). The issue is that a putative square root could lie in a quadratic extension of this field. Its nontrivial automorphism would fix 𝛼α and change the sign of the square root. Chebotarev supplies a prime with exactly that behaviour, contradicting the finite-field condition. The following lemma isolates this passage; in the cubic application we take 𝐻(𝑇) =𝑇2 −1H(T)=T2−1.

An alternative proof of Lemma 2.7 uses zeta functions. A nonsquare algebraic integer 𝑏b in a number field 𝐾K is nonsquare modulo infinitely many degree-one primes of 𝐾K. The underlying zeta-function argument first gives zero or a nonsquare. The zero case is excluded beyond the prime divisors of the nonzero constant coefficient of the minimal polynomial of 𝑏b. If 𝐻(𝛼)H(α) is not a square in 𝐾 =ℚ(𝛼)K=Q(α), this applies to 𝑏 =𝑚2𝐻(𝛼)b=m2H(α), where the nonzero integer 𝑚m makes 𝑏b an algebraic integer, and the residue map at such a prime, of norm ℓℓ, sends 𝛼α to a root 𝑟 ∈𝔽ℓr∈Fℓ of the reduction of 𝑓f at which 𝐻(𝑟)H(r) is a nonsquare. The prime-reduction argument (nonsquares modulo primes) is proved from the simple pole of the Dedekind zeta function at 𝑠 =1s=1, which is in Mathlib. If 𝑏b were a nonzero square modulo all but finitely many primes of degree one, these primes would split in 𝐾(√𝑏)K(b), and for real 𝑠 >1s>1 near 11 the zeta function of 𝐾(√𝑏)K(b) would be at least a positive constant times the square of the zeta function of 𝐾K, which the two simple poles forbid.

Both arguments use the condition at every root for all but finitely many primes. Testing finitely many primes supplies no such condition. The zeta-function argument is the one used in the linked formal proof.
