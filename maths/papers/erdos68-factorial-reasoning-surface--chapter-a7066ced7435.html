Factorial Linear Forms and Denominators: Detailed Proofs and Rationality Criteria

Erdős #68 By Will Cook 1 September 2026; revised 5 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 51 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The record proves liminf (log LN)/(N3/2 log N) ≥2√2/3≥22/3 for L_N=lcm(2!−1,…,N!−1), by comparing a terminal block of denominators with pairwise gcds rather than an external multiplicity theorem. Specialising a Louwsma–Martino valuation, it exhibits primes 139 and 2593 absent from all later reduced denominators. Coefficient constructions give integer forms MS+k; at fixed moment the remainder changes only by an integer. Rationality criteria use factorial digits and the next integer above a scaled partial sum; the digit argument also recovers the irrationality of e. Two exact calculations give q∤299999! and 𝑞 ≥239990 >1012038q≥239990>1012038. These exclusions do not prove irrationality.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #68, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #68, which remains open.

Finite denominator exclusions

Two recorded computations give different restrictions on a hypothetical rational denominator. The carry calculation concerns divisibility, and the continued-fraction enclosure concerns size:

𝑞∤299999!,𝑞≥239990>1012038(28)(28)q∤299999!,q≥239990>1012038

These restrictions hold simultaneously whenever 𝑆 =𝑎/𝑞S=a/q, with 𝑎 ∈ℤa∈Z and 𝑞 >0q>0; they are not two bounds to compare by taking a maximum. They also hold for an unreduced representation. The size bound passes to multiples of the reduced denominator, as does failure to divide a fixed factorial. The computations were performed outside Lean. The candidate finite-size certificate records that its computation has not been kernel checked, and the full carry certificate has the same outstanding formal obligation.

The two restrictions concern different properties of 𝑞q. The prime 300007300007 does not divide 299999!299999!, but lies below the size bound; 299999!299999! exceeds that bound, but fails the nondivisibility condition. A sufficiently high power of a small prime need not divide 299999!299999! either. Thus the carry exclusion is not an exclusion of all denominators with small prime factors. In Sondow’s terminology, it says that the least 𝑟r for which 𝑞 ∣𝑟!q∣r! is at least 300000300000 [21]. His approximation estimates for 𝑒e are not being applied to 𝑆S.
