Long paper

Running least common multiples: distinct values and multiplicities

Erdős #269 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 5 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 73 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The reciprocal three-prime running-LCM kernel has nonsingular minors of every order: row and column rescaling leaves a two-valued matrix, and density supplies the required threshold pattern. The record computes the rank of every finite restriction and the least uniform approximation error over all matrices of finite separated rank. For the repeated {2,3,5} sum it derives an integer-coefficient tail recurrence and a residue criterion equivalent to irrationality. The unproved step is to find such windows for every positive multiplier coprime to 30 and after every prescribed start. It also gives the full two-prime comparison, following Fan's earlier Hecke–Mahler reduction, and identifies the boundary terms that survive a proposed finite-difference cancellation.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #269, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #269, which remains open.

Hypotheses for auxiliary integer recurrences

For ordered primes 𝑝1 <⋯ <𝑝𝑠p1<⋯<ps, the interval (𝑝𝑎1,𝑝𝑎+11)(p1a,p1a+1) contains at most one power of each 𝑝𝑖 >𝑝1pi>p1. The radix including the final 𝑝1p1-jump consequently belongs to

{𝑝1𝑠∏𝑖=2𝑝𝜀𝑖𝑖:𝜀𝑖∈{0,1}}.{p1∏i=2spiεi:εi∈{0,1}}.

This finite alphabet determines neither the frequency of the radices nor the error terms in the weighted block counts. Estimates for those quantities, or for specified shifted kernels, would be additional arithmetic inputs. The conditional statements about integer recurrences below keep such inputs explicit.

Some auxiliary results explain why bounded integer approximations alone are insufficient. Here are their hypotheses in explicit form.

Let 𝑗(𝑛) ∈{2,3,5}j(n)∈{2,3,5} label a sequence of jumps, and suppose each label occurs. A perturbation 𝜀𝑛εn in an additive abelian group has sum zero on every interval [𝑎,𝑏)[a,b) whose endpoints have the same label exactly when

𝜀𝑛=𝑢(𝑗(𝑛+1))−𝑢(𝑗(𝑛))εn=u(j(n+1))−u(j(n))

for three potential values 𝑢(2),𝑢(3),𝑢(5)u(2),u(3),u(5). One direction follows by telescoping. Conversely, the zero-sum assumption makes ∑𝑘<𝑛𝜀𝑘∑k<nεk the same at any two indices with the same label. Assign that common value to 𝑢(𝑗(𝑛))u(j(n)); every label occurs, so all three values are defined. Consecutive partial sums then give the displayed identity. In the usual terminology, the perturbation is a coboundary of the function 𝑢u on the labels: it is the difference of successive potential values. This is a telescoping condition, not a bound on the size of the perturbation. It does not by itself force the perturbation to vanish: unequal potential values give nonzero differences when the label changes. If the perturbation also vanishes on a genuine 2 →32→3 transition and on a genuine 2 →52→5 transition, all three potential values agree, so every perturbation is zero.

For an integer sequence satisfying 𝑐𝑛+1 =𝑏𝑛𝑐𝑛 −𝐷𝑚𝑛cn+1=bncn−Dmn, compare it with an integer sequence 𝑧𝑛zn. Define the error 𝑒𝑛 =𝐷𝑧𝑛 −𝑐𝑛en=Dzn−cn and the perturbation 𝜀𝑛 =𝑏𝑛𝑧𝑛 −𝑧𝑛+1 −𝑚𝑛εn=bnzn−zn+1−mn. Then

𝑒𝑛+1=𝑏𝑛𝑒𝑛−𝐷𝜀𝑛.en+1=bnen−Dεn.

Under the preceding zero-sum and transition hypotheses, 𝜀𝑛 =0εn=0 and hence 𝑒𝑁 =(∏𝑛<𝑁𝑏𝑛)𝑒0eN=(∏n<Nbn)e0. If every 𝑏𝑛 ≥2bn≥2 and the integer 𝑒0e0 is nonzero, then |𝑒𝑁| ≥2𝑁|eN|≥2N. This contradicts even one bound |𝑒𝑁| <2𝑁|eN|<2N, and therefore also excludes a uniform bound for all 𝑁N. The nonzero initial error is essential: the identically zero error causes no contradiction.

A calculation at four consecutive indices gives a related obstruction. If 0 <𝑇𝑖 <10<Ti<1, 𝑧𝑖 ∈ℤzi∈Z and |𝑧𝑖 −𝑇𝑖| <1|zi−Ti|<1, then 𝑧𝑖zi is 00 or 11. For bases 2,3,2,52,3,2,5, the equalities 2𝑧0 −𝑧1 −1 =02z0−z1−1=0 and 2𝑧2 −𝑧3 −1 =02z2−z3−1=0 force all four integers to be 11. The sum of the first two perturbations is then

(2𝑧0−𝑧1−1)+(3𝑧1−𝑧2−1)=1,(2z0−z1−1)+(3z1−z2−1)=1,

not zero. Thus unit accuracy, the two exact equalities and a zero sum on that block cannot all hold. Unit accuracy alone does not give either exact equality.

No integer approximation to the tails of 𝑆S is constructed here that satisfies all these conditions. Rationality supplies integer carries of at most quadratic growth, but not the additional zero-sum and transition identities. The carries obtained from 𝑆S satisfy a weighted block identity instead. Applying the auxiliary results would require that identity to imply their hypotheses, or would require a different construction of the integer approximations.

There is also a conditional criterion for a sum supported on the powers of 22. Its elementary ingredients can be stated without introducing another formal vocabulary. From 𝑦′ =𝑏𝑦 −1y′=by−1 with 𝑏 >0b>0 and 0 <𝑦′ <10<y′<1 one gets 1/𝑏 <𝑦 <2/𝑏1/b<y<2/b. For integers 𝑎 >0a>0 and 𝑝 ≥3p≥3, if 𝑢 >1/𝑎u>1/a and 𝑣 <2/(𝑝𝑎)v<2/(pa), then 𝑢 −𝑣 >1/(3𝑎)u−v>1/(3a). Finally, a real number 𝑥x is irrational if, for every positive integer 𝑑d, there are integers 𝑛,𝑘n,k with 0 <|𝑛𝑥 −𝑘| <1/𝑑0<|nx−k|<1/d.

To use these observations for a series, the conditional result requires exact identities 𝑦𝑀 =𝐻𝑀𝑥 −𝑧𝑀yM=HMx−zM, with 𝐻𝑀,𝑧𝑀 ∈ℤHM,zM∈Z, and, for every positive 𝑑d, two indices with different 𝐻𝑀HM, different 𝑦𝑀yM, and |𝑦𝑀 −𝑦𝑀′| <1/𝑑|yM−yM′|<1/d. Their difference gives the required nonzero linear form. Equal tail values would give zero and would not suffice; a merely bounded gap would not suffice either. Indeed, for a rational 𝑥x of denominator 𝑑d, every nonzero difference of this form has size at least 1/𝑑1/d. No concrete series in this record is shown to satisfy all these clearing and arbitrarily small nonzero-gap conditions.
