Précis

The paper gives ordinary proofs of the length-less-than-2 conclusion for monic trinomials zn+a zm+b, with 1≤m<n and all roots in the open unit disc. It also proves that distinct concyclic zeros in degree n≥3 admit an adjacent chord shorter than 2 in {|f|<1} when their circle radius ρ satisfies 2ρn≤1; this alternation proof is not formalised in Lean. For squarefree monic polynomials, if the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/25, there is also such a connector. Without a root-location restriction, the scaled result gives a path shorter than (5/2) μ1/n in {|f|<(25/13)μ}. Separate length-area and critical-value estimates give further control. Exact counterexamples show why these ingredients do not yield the unrestricted connector theorem; Erdős #1041 remains open.

This paper owns the problem-specific exposition for Erdős #1041: ordinary all-degree trinomial paths, bounded-radius concyclic chords, a sharp critical-value mean, subsidiary Lean and Comparator checks, and exact obstructions that prevent promoting those estimates to a general connector theorem.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #1041, which remains open.

Critical proximity and straight-path obstructions

A critical point gives a different way to select two nearby roots, without assuming a sparse coefficient pattern. The logarithmic-derivative balance prevents one root from being arbitrarily closer to the critical point than all the others. This yields a bound on the sum of two distances. To turn that bound into a path theorem, both connecting segments would also have to stay inside the lemniscate; the examples below show why that extra step cannot be assumed.

Arguments, not moduli

It is tempting to arrange a generic perturbation so that the critical values are pairwise distinct, or that their moduli are pairwise distinct, and to conclude that saddle connections are excluded. Neither is enough.

Two distinct critical values can lie on one ray, and two critical values with distinct moduli certainly can: the ray records the argument, and the modulus is exactly the coordinate the flow contracts. By Corollary 19 the invariant that excludes connections is the argument. What a perturbation must therefore achieve is pairwise distinct critical-value arguments, which is a condition on 𝑛 −1n−1 points modulo the circle rather than on their positions in the plane.

The cost of that condition is also checked, and it is small.
