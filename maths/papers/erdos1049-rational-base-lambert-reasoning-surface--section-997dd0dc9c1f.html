Geometric Moments and Rational Lambert Values: Proofs and Further Results

Erdős #1049 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 81 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. Specialising Zudilin's 2004 linear forms shows F(a/b) irrational for coprime 𝑎 >𝑏 ≥1a>b≥1 when θ=log b / log a is less than θ^*=0.4056830213840605…, with irrationality-exponent bound (1−θ)/(θ*−θ) for every positive integral power. In particular the bound is less than 301 for every positive integral power of 31/431/4. The constants are Zudilin's; the proof cancels common cyclotomic factors before rational specialisation. For the 2016 Hankel construction with x=z=1, the first nonzero term of the normalised determinant is computed at every rank. A separate positive-measure argument proves positivity and a size estimate for each fixed real 0<q<1. The final sections explain the limits of the stated clearing and congruence arguments at 3/23/2: no small nonzero sequence of divided remainders is constructed there, and the full rational-base conjecture remains open.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #1049, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #1049, which remains open.

A restriction on Mahler functional equations

For the divisor generating series L(𝑧) =∑𝑛≥1𝜏(𝑛)𝑧𝑛L(z)=∑n≥1τ(n)zn, the classification of Bell and Smertnig excludes a 𝑘k-Mahler equation for every 𝑘 ≥2k≥2 [27]. We first explain its application to 𝜏τ. We then prove the weaker simultaneous 22/33 statement by combining the Adamczewski–Bell theorem with the functional nonrationality from Section 2.1. The cited 2026 works have the status of the identified preprints. These functional statements leave the arithmetic nature of an individual rational-base value undecided.

To apply the classification, note that 𝜏τ is multiplicative: for coprime integers 𝑚,𝑛m,n, each divisor of 𝑚𝑛mn has a unique factorisation into a divisor of 𝑚m and a divisor of 𝑛n. Suppose that LL were 𝑘k-Mahler. The classification would give a prime 𝑝p, an integer 𝑟 ≥0r≥0 and an eventually periodic function 𝜒χ with 𝜏(𝑚) =𝑚𝑟𝜒(𝑚)τ(m)=mrχ(m) whenever 𝑝 ∤𝑚p∤m. For a prime ℓ ≠𝑝ℓ≠p, this forces

𝜒(ℓ𝑗)=𝑗+1ℓ𝑗𝑟(𝑗≥0).χ(ℓj)=j+1ℓjr(j≥0).

If 𝑟 =0r=0, these values are unbounded. If 𝑟 >0r>0, they are nonzero and tend to zero. Both alternatives contradict the finite range of an eventually periodic function.

Here the ambient space is ℚ((𝑧))Q((z)), the field of formal Laurent series, viewed as a vector space over ℚ(𝑧)Q(z). Stability means that substituting 𝑧𝑘zk for 𝑧z sends each member of the subspace back into that subspace; this substitution is not a ℚ(𝑧)Q(z)-linear map.

Every series L(𝑧2𝑖)L(z2i) with 𝑖 ≥𝑗i≥j has exponents divisible by 2𝑗2j, so the relation splits by exponent residues modulo 2𝑗2j. Choose 𝑟r with 𝑄𝑗,𝑟 ≠0Qj,r≠0 and put 𝑢 =𝑧2𝑗u=z2j. The corresponding relation is

𝑑∑𝑖=𝑗𝑄𝑖,𝑟(𝑢)L(𝑢2𝑖−𝑗)=0,∑i=jdQi,r(u)L(u2i−j)=0,

with a nonzero coefficient of L(𝑢)L(u). Thus LL is 22-Mahler. The same argument with 33 in place of 22 makes it 33-Mahler. Since 22 and 33 are multiplicatively independent, a theorem of Adamczewski and Bell [16] then forces LL to be a rational function. Since L(𝑧) =𝐹(1/𝑧)L(z)=F(1/z) for 0 <𝑧 <10<z<1, this contradicts the nonrationality of 𝐹F proved in Section 2.1. Rivin’s periodic-coefficient corollary [24] gives the same nonrationality conclusion. ◻

Proposition 10.5 uses no property of the point 2/32/3: the obstruction is functional and appears before regularity at a particular point is considered. For this scalar function, Bell and Smertnig’s single-base classification already implies the proposition. The proof above instead derives it from simultaneous closure and elementary functional nonrationality, using the Adamczewski–Bell theorem. A construction using additional functions or functional relations must specify those functions and its closure conditions; the single-base statement is not an obstruction to every approximation method. The classification and the Adamczewski–Bell theorem are cited, not proved here; the needed nonrationality has the elementary proof given earlier.

The Lean proof of Proposition 10.5 (no finite simultaneous 2/32/3-system) uses neither cited theorem, and it uses only stability under 𝑧 ↦𝑧2z↦z2. As in the first step of the proof above, that stability gives polynomials 𝑃0,…,𝑃𝑑P0,…,Pd, not all zero, with ∑𝑑𝑖=0𝑃𝑖(𝑧)L(𝑧2𝑖) =0∑i=0dPi(z)L(z2i)=0, and this relation also holds between the values of these functions on the open unit disc. If 𝜁ζ is a primitive 𝑛nth root of unity, then (1 −𝑟)L(𝑟𝜁)/log⁡(1/(1 −𝑟)) →1/𝑛(1−r)L(rζ)/log⁡(1/(1−r))→1/n as 𝑟 ↑1r↑1. These limits, taken at roots of unity of suitable orders, force every 𝑃𝑖Pi to vanish. The same argument excludes, for every 𝑘 ≥2k≥2, a finite-dimensional space containing LL and stable under 𝑧 ↦𝑧𝑘z↦zk alone (single-base statement).
