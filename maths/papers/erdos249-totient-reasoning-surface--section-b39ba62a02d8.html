The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 160 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Authorship and AI use. Will Cook built and directed the research infrastructure and maintains the public release. He reviewed claims when he could. AI agents did most of the research and drafting. Cook did not independently verify every claim.

The sharp rank-one quotient bound

The quotients below are a particular proposed family of rational approximants to a Möbius-weighted value. We determine their minimum and show that they remain a fixed distance above that value. Put

This theorem treats averages after the quotients are formed. Signed coefficients, coupling before the quotient, and higher-rank kernels require separate arguments. For rational approximants 𝑝𝑗/𝑞𝑗pj/qj in lowest terms, with 𝑞𝑗 >0qj>0, a sufficient arithmetic target is

0<𝑞𝑗| | |Θ2−𝑝𝑗/𝑞𝑗| | |⟶0.0<qj|Θ2−pj/qj|⟶0.

The denominator sets the scale of the required error. Convergence alone does not control their product. For the elementary dyadic prefixes, the available bound is only 𝑞𝑗|𝑆 −𝑝𝑗/𝑞𝑗| ≤𝑁 +2qj|S−pj/qj|≤N+2 at depth 𝑁N, not a quantity tending to zero. A prescribed approximation family therefore needs a sharper error or reduced-denominator bound. When approximants may be chosen freely, continued-fraction convergents show that the criterion is equivalent to irrationality, just as (8) is. The theorem above excludes the displayed rank-one quotients and their positive averages, not other approximation families.
