Short paper

Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 21 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For strictly increasing positive integers with 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3), the reciprocal sum is irrational. The argument extracts a cubic polynomial from an integer tail numerator, restricts its coefficients using a square in a cubic field, and excludes the remaining cases modulo seven. The unrestricted Erdős–Graham question remains open.

This paper owns the cubic-rate irrationality theorem and its proof; the long paper develops the separate Sylvester-tail criteria and broader recurrence questions.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

Finite total relative increase

Summable relative increases first bound 𝐶𝑛Cn by an infinite product, then allow only finitely many rises; integer descent finishes. No denominator recurrence is needed. Relative increases tending merely to zero still allow 𝐶𝑛 =𝑛 +1Cn=n+1 or 𝐶𝑛 =(𝑛 +1)2Cn=(n+1)2.

Theorem 5.1 (a convergent sum of relative increases). Let 𝐶𝑛Cn be positive integers and 𝐸𝑛En integers satisfying 𝐶𝑛+1 =𝐶𝑛 −𝐸𝑛Cn+1=Cn−En. If

∑𝑛(−𝐸𝑛)+𝐶𝑛<∞,∑n(−En)+Cn<∞,

then 𝐸𝑛 =0En=0 eventually. Neither denominator dynamics nor vanishing relative error is required.

Proof. Set 𝛿𝑛 =( −𝐸𝑛)+/𝐶𝑛δn=(−En)+/Cn. Then

𝐶𝑁≤𝐶0∏𝑛<𝑁(1+𝛿𝑛)≤𝐶0exp(∑𝑛𝛿𝑛).CN≤C0∏n<N(1+δn)≤C0exp(∑nδn).

Choose an integer 𝐾K above this bound. A strict rise of the integer sequence has size at least one, so it contributes at least 1/𝐾1/K to ∑𝛿𝑛∑δn. Hence only finitely many rises occur. Thereafter 𝐶𝑛Cn is a nonincreasing positive integer sequence, and its eventual constancy gives 𝐸𝑛 =0En=0. ◻

The proof also allows positive real 𝐶𝑛Cn with integer 𝐸𝑛En: the values lie in 𝐶0 +ℤC0+Z, whose bounded positive part is finite. Discrete increments are essential to this argument. With real errors, 𝐶𝑛 =1 +1/(𝑛 +1)Cn=1+1/(n+1) decreases forever with zero relative-increase sum; without positivity, 𝐶𝑛 = −𝑛 −1Cn=−n−1, 𝐸𝑛 =1En=1 does so too. The companion’s Section 13 gives the details.

On an exact reciprocal-tail orbit the conclusion gives the Sylvester recurrence. For the gap sequence of the pseudo-greedy expansion, the same criterion, with the same product bound and integer descent, appears in the Erdős Problem a Day working report on Problem #243, dated 12 August 2026 [8]; the statement above is for any positive integer sequence with 𝐶𝑛+1 =𝐶𝑛 −𝐸𝑛Cn+1=Cn−En. This summability condition and the bound in Theorem 4.1 are different estimates. For exact reciprocal tails with |𝐸𝑛|/𝐶𝑛 →0|En|/Cn→0, each is equivalent to eventual vanishing of 𝐸𝑛En; neither estimate has been derived here from the unrestricted problem.
