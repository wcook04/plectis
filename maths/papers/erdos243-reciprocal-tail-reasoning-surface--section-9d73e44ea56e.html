Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 61 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. For rational reciprocal sums under 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, the record also develops sufficient conditions for the eventual recurrence 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1, controlling either increases of an integer tail numerator or a convergent sum at steps setting new maxima. Examples separate numerical growth bounds from the exact recurrences. The cubic proof is distinguished from the theorems formalised in Lean. None of the additional bounds is derived for every rational reciprocal sum in Erdős #243.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #243, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

Unbounded upward increments

Proposition 14.6 (small increases when the prime moduli are sparse). There exist strictly increasing primes 𝑝𝑖pi and a strictly increasing positive integer sequence 𝑢𝑛 →∞un→∞ such that gcd(𝑢𝑛,𝑝𝑖) =1gcd(un,pi)=1 for all 𝑖,𝑛i,n and

𝑢𝑛+1−𝑢𝑛=𝑂(√log⁡log⁡(𝑢𝑛+𝑒𝑒))=𝑜(log⁡log⁡(𝑢𝑛+3)).un+1−un=O(log⁡log⁡(un+ee))=o(log⁡log⁡(un+3)).

Thus the bounded-increment hypothesis of Theorem 11.3 cannot be replaced by an 𝑜(log⁡log⁡𝑢𝑛)o(log⁡log⁡un) bound without a quantitative restriction on the moduli.

Proof. Choose increasing primes 𝑝𝑖 >max{exp⁡(exp⁡((𝑖 +2)2)),2𝑖+3}pi>max{exp⁡(exp⁡((i+2)2)),2i+3}, for 𝑖 ≥0i≥0. Primes of arbitrarily large size suffice; no distribution theorem is used. Then 𝜃 =∑𝑖1/𝑝𝑖 <1/4θ=∑i1/pi<1/4 and 𝑘(𝑧) =#{𝑖 :𝑝𝑖 ≤𝑧} ≤√log⁡log⁡𝑧k(z)=#{i:pi≤z}≤log⁡log⁡z whenever 𝑧z is large. For integer 𝑥x put 𝐿(𝑥) =⌈4√log⁡log⁡(𝑥+𝑒𝑒) +8⌉L(x)=⌈4log⁡log⁡(x+ee)+8⌉. Since 𝐿(𝑥) =𝑜(𝑥)L(x)=o(x), eventually 𝐿(𝑥) >𝑘(𝑥 +𝐿(𝑥))/(1 −𝜃)L(x)>k(x+L(x))/(1−θ). The elementary window bound of Proposition 7.15 leaves an integer in [𝑥,𝑥 +𝐿(𝑥))[x,x+L(x)) divisible by no 𝑝𝑖pi. The set of such integers is therefore unbounded. Enumerate it increasingly as (𝑢𝑛)(un) and apply the same bound with 𝑥 =𝑢𝑛 +1x=un+1 to obtain 𝑢𝑛+1 −𝑢𝑛 ≤𝐿(𝑢𝑛 +1)un+1−un≤L(un+1). For prime moduli nondivisibility is exactly coprimality, proving every claim. Sparse moduli defeat the abstract coprimality argument, not the exact tail recurrences. The next theorem instead fixes the moduli’s double-exponential scale. ◻

We now keep the full family of moduli, rather than discarding a prefix as in Proposition 7.15. The resulting coefficient depends on 𝜎 =∏𝑗(1 −1/𝑚𝑗)σ=∏j(1−1/mj). For a finite prefix, the corresponding product is exactly the proportion of admissible residue classes by the Chinese remainder theorem. This explains the constant in the statement before the limiting argument is made.

Theorem 14.7 (largest gaps between integers avoiding given multiples). Let 𝑚0 <𝑚1 <⋯m0<m1<⋯ be pairwise coprime integers at least 22 with ℓ(𝑚𝑗) =𝑗 +𝑂(1)ℓ(mj)=j+O(1), where ℓ(𝑥) =log2⁡log2⁡max(4,𝑥)ℓ(x)=log2⁡log2⁡max(4,x). lim sup𝑛→∞𝑢𝑛+1−𝑢𝑛ℓ(𝑢𝑛)=𝜎−1.lim supn→∞un+1−unℓ(un)=σ−1.

This is a statement about avoidance of multiples of whole moduli. It is not a statement about coprimality to composite 𝑚𝑗mj, or about integer tail orbits.

𝑀𝑇=∏𝑗<𝑇𝑚𝑗,𝜎𝑇=∏𝑗<𝑇(1−1/𝑚𝑗),𝜃𝑇=∑𝑗≥𝑇1/𝑚𝑗.MT=∏j<Tmj,σT=∏j<T(1−1/mj),θT=∑j≥T1/mj.

Here 𝜃𝑇 →0θT→0. By the Chinese remainder theorem, avoiding the first 𝑇T whole moduli selects precisely the fraction 𝜎𝑇σT of the residues modulo 𝑀𝑇MT.

For the upper bound, fix 𝑇T before sending the interval to infinity, so the prefix error 𝑀𝑇MT stays constant. An integer interval [𝑥,𝑥 +𝐿)[x,x+L) contains at least 𝜎𝑇𝐿 −𝑀𝑇σTL−MT integers avoiding that prefix. The remaining moduli cover at most 𝐿𝜃𝑇 +𝑘(𝑥 +𝐿)LθT+k(x+L) integers. Choose 𝑇T large enough that 𝜎𝑇 >𝜃𝑇σT>θT, and then any 𝑐 >(𝜎𝑇 −𝜃𝑇)−1c>(σT−θT)−1. For 𝐿 =⌈𝑐ℓ(𝑥)⌉L=⌈cℓ(x)⌉, the number left is positive for all sufficiently large 𝑥x, since 𝑘(𝑥 +𝐿) =ℓ(𝑥) +𝑂(1)k(x+L)=ℓ(x)+O(1). Thus the set being enumerated is unbounded, and every sufficiently late such interval meets it. Applying this at 𝑥 =𝑢𝑛 +1x=un+1 gives

lim sup𝑛𝑢𝑛+1−𝑢𝑛ℓ(𝑢𝑛)≤(𝜎𝑇−𝜃𝑇)−1.lim supnun+1−unℓ(un)≤(σT−θT)−1.

Assign to these offsets distinct moduli 𝑚𝑇,…,𝑚𝑇+𝐾𝐿−1mT,…,mT+KL−1. Solve simultaneously 𝑥 ≡0(mod𝑀𝑇)x≡0(modMT) and 𝑥 ≡ −𝑗x≡−j modulo the modulus assigned to 𝑗j. Put 𝑄𝐿 =∏𝑖<𝑇+𝐾𝐿𝑚𝑖QL=∏i<T+KLmi and choose this solution in [𝑄𝐿,2𝑄𝐿)[QL,2QL). Every integer of [𝑥,𝑥 +𝐿)[x,x+L) is then divisible by a modulus: either an old one or its assigned new one. Let 𝑢u be the greatest admissible integer below 𝑥x; its successor is at least 𝑥 +𝐿x+L, so the gap is at least 𝐿L. The scale assumption gives

ℓ(2𝑄𝐿)≤𝑇+𝐾𝐿+𝑂(1),ℓ(2QL)≤T+KL+O(1),

because log2⁡𝑚𝑖 =2𝑖+𝑂(1)log2⁡mi=2i+O(1) and these upper bounds sum geometrically. Since 𝑢 <2𝑄𝐿u<2QL, the ratio for this gap is at least 𝐿/(𝑇 +𝐾𝐿 +𝑂(1))L/(T+KL+O(1)), tending to 𝜎−1𝑇σT−1. These 𝑢u tend to infinity: 𝑥 ≥𝑄𝐿 →∞x≥QL→∞ and admissible integers are unbounded. Hence the limsup is at least 𝜎−1𝑇σT−1 for every 𝑇T. Let 𝑇 →∞T→∞ to finish. ◻

For the Fermat moduli 𝑚𝑗 =22𝑗 +1mj=22j+1, pairwise coprimality and the finite-product identity

𝑁∏𝑗=0(1−122𝑗+1)=22𝑁+1−122𝑁+1−1∏j=0N(1−122j+1)=22N+1−122N+1−1

give 𝜎 =1/2σ=1/2, so the coefficient is exactly 22. These results describe gaps between integers avoiding fixed moduli. They do not construct multipliers or numerator and denominator sequences satisfying the exact recurrences. In particular, the static proportion 𝜎σ must not be substituted for 𝜑(𝑣𝑇)/𝑣𝑇φ(vT)/vT: the first excludes multiples of the whole moduli, while the second excludes every prime factor of the reduced denominator.
