The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

The norm hypothesis requires cancellation: if all phases are 11, the norm is 𝑋X and the bound fails; a balanced set of opposite phases has sum zero. If instead every phase is −1−1, the real part is −𝑋−X and satisfies the real-part bound, while the norm is 𝑋X and fails the norm bound. Thus a real-part estimate is strictly weaker for general phase families; these examples do not distinguish the two quantified conditions for the actual totient sequence. The same depth 𝐿L must work across the whole block.

Comparison with the preceding counterexamples.

The earlier counterexamples do not establish or refute this block estimate. The following distinctions explain why those particular arguments do not settle it.

  • The parameters grow. The size condition forces the truncation depth to increase with 𝑋X, so this is not a test of a fixed prefix.

  • The sample is prescribed: all 𝑁 ∈[𝑋,2𝑋)N∈[X,2X) are summed at one depth. Choosing two favourable indices does not establish this block estimate.

  • The sum uses the accumulated totient differences. It does not prescribe individual values through a congruence construction.

  • The estimate concerns the actual phases. Neither a bound on carry rank nor agreement only in parity gives it.

  • The proved implication uses the real-part threshold 9/109/10 and the constant in the size bound 1616. The stronger norm bound 21/2521/25 implies that real-part bound. These constants do not assert any distribution theorem for the totient phases.

Remark 3.2 (The finite-prefix example does not choose a method). The finite-prefix example does not select an analytic method. It shows only that a proof valid for every sequence 0 ≤𝑐(𝑛) ≤𝑛0≤c(n)≤n cannot deduce the required assertion from one fixed prefix. An argument using additional identities of 𝜑φ, a theorem propagating a finite base case, a Farey estimate, or a suitable exponential sum could provide information not contained in that prefix. The earlier finite audit cannot rank all such possibilities.

Remark 3.3 (Finite certificate observations). In the historical table of 2828 diagonal certificates through 𝑡 =64t=64, the reported depths exceed the necessary depth bound by a small additive constant. A separate enumeration at the indices where the least common multiple increases records a closest margin from the central interval’s boundary of approximately 0.0002210.000221 of the modulus, at 𝑡 =100t=100.
