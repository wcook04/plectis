Long paper

The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Relevant comparison estimates.

The following comparisons identify the estimates still needed; they do not rule out these methods.

Weyl differencing and van der Corput. The van der Corput inequality applies to arbitrary bounded complex sequences [23], but its use here requires estimates for shifted correlations. For the exact phases 𝑥𝑁 =2𝑁𝛼ℎxN=2Nαh of Lemma 12.1,

𝑥𝑁+𝑞−𝑥𝑁=(2𝑞−1)𝑥𝑁.xN+q−xN=(2q−1)xN.

Thus differencing gives another doubling orbit, not a polynomial phase of lower degree. The finite phase 𝐷(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿)/2𝐿D(h,N,L)/2L approximates this orbit modulo 11; it is not the exact orbit itself. No bound for the resulting correlations is established here.

The large sieve. A proposed application would need a suitable family of separated frequencies and a way to recover a bound for each fixed ℎh. Averaging only over ℎh would not suffice: the condition asks for a block estimate for every ℎh, not for most of them.

Bilinear sums over prime factors. Factoring 𝑁 +𝑡 =𝑚𝑝N+t=mp and using 𝜑(𝑚𝑝) =𝜑(𝑚)(𝑝 −1)φ(mp)=φ(m)(p−1) makes the selected totient contribution linear in the prime 𝑝p. Section 12.3 gives this decomposition and identifies the remaining weight. A bound for that weight is still needed before a Vaughan- or Vinogradov-type argument can be applied.

Erdős’s 1948 digit method. The comparison series is 𝐸 =∑𝑛≥11/(2𝑛 −1) =∑𝑁≥1𝑑(𝑁)/2𝑁E=∑n≥11/(2n−1)=∑N≥1d(N)/2N, whose irrationality Erdős proved [11]. For the totient series, the corresponding Möbius expansion is exact: 𝜑 =𝜇 ∗idφ=μ∗id gives

The identity follows by Möbius inversion; a numerical computation gives 𝑆 =1.3676308019850223…S=1.3676308019850223… to sixteen digits.

Erdős combines congruences forcing increasing powers of 22 to divide a block of divisor counts with an averaged tail bound, producing long zero blocks in the binary expansion [11]. To adapt that argument here, one would need congruences for the actual totient coefficients together with a compatible tail estimate. The identities alone provide neither combination. In (4), the coefficients 𝜇(𝑑)μ(d) can have either sign, so positivity of the original totient series does not give positivity term by term in this expansion. This is a limitation of the proposed direct transfer, not a proof that a digit argument or a different use of the Möbius expansion cannot work.

This example rules out the implication from irrationality and the stated coefficient bound to the block norm condition. It does not say that the totient sum is Liouville-like, or that every argument based on its digits fails. A proof of the block estimate must use information that distinguishes the actual totient phases from this comparison sequence.

The size of the required saving.

Under Lebesgue measure on the phase circle, the binary digit maps are independent fair bits. The digit-change proportion therefore tends to 1/21/2 for almost every phase. For almost every phase, the real-part block sum is also 𝑂(√𝑋log⁡log⁡𝑋)O(Xlog⁡log⁡X), by the law of the iterated logarithm of Erdős and Gál for lacunary series [29]. The block bound follows by subtracting two initial partial sums. An almost-everywhere theorem supplies no conclusion at the particular phase 𝛼ℎ =(2ℎ −1)𝑆αh=(2h−1)S. The requirement is 𝜌ℎ ≥0.11ρh≥0.11 and a saving of a constant factor. The finite values in Table 1 have 𝜌ℎρh near 0.500.50 and small block averages. They are compatible with square-root cancellation, but do not establish its asymptotic order. The sufficient condition asks only for the stated fixed saving on arbitrarily large blocks. No estimate in this record supplies it for the totient phases.
