Geometric Moments and Rational Lambert Values: Proofs and Further Results

Erdős #1049 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 81 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. Specialising Zudilin's 2004 linear forms shows F(a/b) irrational for coprime 𝑎 >𝑏 ≥1a>b≥1 when θ=log b / log a is less than θ^*=0.4056830213840605…, with irrationality-exponent bound (1−θ)/(θ*−θ) for every positive integral power. In particular the bound is less than 301 for every positive integral power of 31/431/4. The constants are Zudilin's; the proof cancels common cyclotomic factors before rational specialisation. For the 2016 Hankel construction with x=z=1, the first nonzero term of the normalised determinant is computed at every rank. A separate positive-measure argument proves positivity and a size estimate for each fixed real 0<q<1. The final sections explain the limits of the stated clearing and congruence arguments at 3/23/2: no small nonzero sequence of divided remainders is constructed there, and the full rational-base conjecture remains open.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #1049, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #1049, which remains open.

Introduction

For 𝑡 >1t>1, the Lambert series

𝐹(𝑡)=∑𝑛≥11𝑡𝑛−1=∑𝑛≥1𝜏(𝑛)𝑡𝑛F(t)=∑n≥11tn−1=∑n≥1τ(n)tn

has two useful descriptions: as a sum of simple fractions and as a divisor generating series. Here 𝜏(𝑛)τ(n) counts the positive divisors of 𝑛n. The equality follows by expanding each fraction, since all terms are nonnegative; the bound 𝜏(𝑛) ≤𝑛τ(n)≤n ensures convergence. Chowla conjectured that 𝐹(𝑡)F(t) is irrational for every rational 𝑡 >1t>1, as recorded by Erdős [2] and in Bloom’s catalogue [26]. Erdős proved the integer case in 1948 [1].

Two constructions of Zudilin enter, for different reasons. The 2016 approximation [9] supplies positive moments, whose Hankel determinants we evaluate asymptotically. The 2004 forms [8] supply polynomial coefficients whose cancellation and rational evaluation give an irrationality region. A denominator estimate for the second construction is not an estimate for the first; each coefficient family needs its own arithmetic analysis. The shorter paper, Hankel Determinants of Geometric Moments and Rational Lambert Values, begins with the determinant result. Here the polynomial forms come first, making their exact arithmetic available for the later comparisons. Readers following only the determinant argument may start with Section 3: its moment calculation does not use the rational-base transfer.

Rationality of 𝐹(𝑎/𝑏)F(a/b) would make a fixed integer multiple of every such value a positive integer, giving a contradiction. The constants and parameter direction are Zudilin’s; the cancellation and rational evaluation give the region used here. It contains 31/431/4 and all its positive integral powers. Bundschuh and Väänänen already treated a smaller rational-base region containing 7/27/2 [6]; Section 8 checks that specialisation. The value 3/23/2 lies outside both regions and remains unresolved by these arguments. It has the smallest height among reduced noninteger rationals greater than 11, where the height of 𝑟/𝑠r/s is max(𝑟,𝑠)max(r,s).

Section 2 gives the rational-base proof and its degree restrictions. Section 3 contains the determinant arguments and the coefficient-moment calculations. The coefficient pencil is treated for sizes 𝑁 ≤8N≤8 and every 𝑝 >1p>1; the computational ranges for shifted minors and coefficientwise positivity are specified there. Positivity for every 𝑁N and convergence of the largest root remain open. Sections 4–5 apply rescaling and additive congruences to divided forms, including a quantitative bounded-fibre criterion. Sections 6–9 give the clearing, tail and exponent calculations, and Section 10 distinguishes unrestricted reformulations of irrationality from questions about specified approximation families.

All logarithms are natural, and empty products and determinants equal 11. Constants in 𝑂𝑥( ⋅)Ox(⋅) may depend on the fixed base. We introduce the notation for each construction as it is needed. Appendix A identifies the formal sources and the separate finite computations.
