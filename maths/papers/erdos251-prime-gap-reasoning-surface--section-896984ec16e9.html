Prime-Gap Dyadic Series: Perturbations, Exact Criteria and Certificates

Erdős #251 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 47 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A convergent dyadic series with nonnegative integer coefficients can be made rational by sparse nonnegative corrections of upper Banach density zero, eventually bounded by any f(n)→∞, while preserving every fixed modulus and, for a logarithmic allowance, short-block empirical distributions. For prime gaps the cumulative positions remain asymptotic to n log n; no primality conclusion is asserted. Summation by parts reduces the prime series to the gap series, and the tails 𝑇𝑁 =∑𝑗≥1𝑔𝑁+𝑗2−𝑗TN=∑j≥1gN+j2−j satisfy 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1. That recurrence characterises rationality and yields finite tests with explicit remainder bounds. An exact finite certificate shows that a rational value of the prime series would have denominator at least 239998 >1012040239998>1012040. The bound does not establish irrationality.

Algebraic nonconcentration survives the rationalising perturbation

Schlage-Puchta’s lemma states that 𝐹(𝑔𝑛,…,𝑔𝑛+𝑘) ≠0F(gn,…,gn+k)≠0 for almost all 𝑛n, for every fixed nonzero 𝐹 ∈ℤ[𝑥0,…,𝑥𝑘]F∈Z[x0,…,xk] [18]. We say that an integer sequence has fixed-block nonconcentration if it satisfies this conclusion for every 𝑘 ≥0k≥0 and every such 𝐹F. A bounded integer perturbation preserves the property: there are only finitely many translated polynomials to consider for each fixed block. No randomness or independence assumption is required.

This nonconcentration condition excludes every bounded integer sequence: if its values lie in a finite set 𝐸E, the nonzero polynomial ∏𝑐∈𝐸(𝑥0 −𝑐)∏c∈E(x0−c) vanishes at every index. It also excludes a sequence satisfying a fixed nonzero polynomial relation on every short block, such as 𝑎𝑛 =𝑛an=n, for which 𝑥1 −𝑥0 −1x1−x0−1 vanishes. The density-zero exceptional set depends on the fixed polynomial. There cannot be one such set for all polynomials: 𝑥0 −𝑎𝑛x0−an vanishes on the block beginning at 𝑛n, so a common exceptional set would contain every index. No uniform estimate for growing families of polynomials or block lengths is asserted.

In the next proposition, 𝑁,𝐻N,H are nonnegative integers, ℎh is an integer, and all counts are over integer indices and integer triples.

Proposition 8.2 (finite counting for shifted gap differences). Write 𝑝𝑛pn for the primes indexed from 𝑝0 =2p0=2 and 𝑔𝑛 =𝑝𝑛+1 −𝑝𝑛gn=pn+1−pn. For ℎ ≥2h≥2 and 𝑟 ∈ℤr∈Z, let 𝑀ℎ,𝑟(𝑁)Mh,r(N) count 𝑛 <𝑁n<N with 𝑔𝑛+ℎ −𝑔𝑛 =𝑟gn+h−gn=r. Let 𝑄𝑁,𝐻,𝑟QN,H,r count triples (𝑥,𝑑,𝑠)(x,d,s) with 𝑥 <𝑝𝑁x<pN, 0 <𝑑 <𝑠 ≤𝐻0<d<s≤H, 𝑑 +𝑟 >0d+r>0, and all four integers 𝑥,𝑥 +𝑑,𝑥 +𝑠,𝑥 +𝑠 +𝑑 +𝑟x,x+d,x+s,x+s+d+r prime. Then, for every 𝑁,𝐻 ≥0N,H≥0,

(𝐻+1)𝑀ℎ,𝑟(𝑁)≤(ℎ+1)𝑝𝑁+ℎ+1+(𝐻+1)𝑄𝑁,𝐻,𝑟.(H+1)Mh,r(N)≤(h+1)pN+h+1+(H+1)QN,H,r.

Proof. For a counted index 𝑛n, put 𝑥 =𝑝𝑛x=pn, 𝑑 =𝑔𝑛d=gn and 𝑠 =𝑝𝑛+ℎ −𝑝𝑛s=pn+h−pn. If the total span 𝑝𝑛+ℎ+1 −𝑝𝑛pn+h+1−pn is at most 𝐻H, then 0 <𝑑 <𝑠 ≤𝐻0<d<s≤H, 𝑑 +𝑟 =𝑔𝑛+ℎ >0d+r=gn+h>0, and the four primes are precisely 𝑥,𝑥 +𝑑,𝑥 +𝑠,𝑥 +𝑠 +𝑑 +𝑟x,x+d,x+s,x+s+d+r. The count 𝑄𝑁,𝐻,𝑟QN,H,r need not require these primes to be consecutive: it is an upper bound, and 𝑥 =𝑝𝑛x=pn determines 𝑛n, making the map injective. For the remaining indices the integer span is at least 𝐻 +1H+1. Each gap appears in at most ℎ +1h+1 of the spans, so their total is at most (ℎ +1)∑𝑖<𝑁+ℎ𝑔𝑖 <(ℎ +1)𝑝𝑁+ℎ+1(h+1)∑i<N+hgi<(h+1)pN+h+1. Multiply the resulting bound on the exceptional count by 𝐻 +1H+1 and add the small-span contribution. ◻

Increasing 𝐻H reduces the bound for long spans but enlarges the prime-pattern count. To deduce zero density from this finite inequality, for each 𝜀 >0ε>0 and all large 𝑁N one needs a single choice of 𝐻H making the entire right side less than 𝜀𝑁(𝐻 +1)εN(H+1). For ℎ =1h=1, the same definitions give 𝑠 =𝑑s=d, so 𝑥 +𝑑 =𝑥 +𝑠x+d=x+s. The strict condition 𝑑 <𝑠d<s in the four-prime count fails; that case requires a three-prime count instead. Zero density itself is already known for every fixed ℎ ≥1h≥1 and 𝑟 ∈ℤr∈Z: apply Schlage-Puchta’s lemma above to the nonzero polynomial 𝑥ℎ −𝑥0 −𝑟xh−x0−r. Thus the finite reduction should not be read as leaving that upper bound open. Neither upper bound supplies the late occurrences or weighted-tail separation required for irrationality.

For the perturbation claim, first suppose that 𝑎a has fixed-block nonconcentration and every correction is either 00 or a fixed integer 𝑀 ≥1M≥1. For a fixed 𝑟 ∈ℤr∈Z, an equality 𝑏𝑛+1 −𝑏𝑛 =𝑟bn+1−bn=r then requires 𝑎𝑛+1 −𝑎𝑛 ∈{𝑟 −𝑀,𝑟,𝑟 +𝑀}an+1−an∈{r−M,r,r+M}. Each of these three equalities holds on a set of density zero. The general argument uses the same finite-union step for translated polynomials.

Theorem 8.3 (nonconcentration is perturbation-stable). Let 𝑎 :ℕ →ℤa:N→Z have fixed-block nonconcentration, let 𝐸 ⊂ℤE⊂Z be finite, and let 𝑏𝑛 =𝑎𝑛 +𝑒𝑛bn=an+en with 𝑒𝑛 ∈𝐸en∈E for every 𝑛n. Then 𝑏b has fixed-block nonconcentration.

Proof. Fix 𝑘 ≥0k≥0 and a nonzero 𝐹 ∈ℤ[𝑥0,…,𝑥𝑘]F∈Z[x0,…,xk]. For a tuple 𝐞 =(𝑒(0),…,𝑒(𝑘)) ∈𝐸𝑘+1e=(e(0),…,e(k))∈Ek+1 put 𝐹𝐞(𝑥0,…,𝑥𝑘) =𝐹(𝑥0 +𝑒(0),…,𝑥𝑘 +𝑒(𝑘))Fe(x0,…,xk)=F(x0+e(0),…,xk+e(k)). The substitution 𝑥𝑖 ↦𝑥𝑖 +𝑒(𝑖)xi↦xi+e(i) is a ring automorphism of ℤ[𝑥0,…,𝑥𝑘]Z[x0,…,xk] with inverse 𝑥𝑖 ↦𝑥𝑖 −𝑒(𝑖)xi↦xi−e(i), so 𝐹𝐞 ≠0Fe≠0. If 𝐹(𝑏𝑛,…,𝑏𝑛+𝑘) =0F(bn,…,bn+k)=0 then 𝐹𝐞(𝑎𝑛,…,𝑎𝑛+𝑘) =0Fe(an,…,an+k)=0 for the tuple 𝐞 =(𝑒𝑛,…,𝑒𝑛+𝑘)e=(en,…,en+k), whence

{𝑛:𝐹(𝑏𝑛,…,𝑏𝑛+𝑘)=0}⊆⋃𝐞∈𝐸𝑘+1{𝑛:𝐹𝐞(𝑎𝑛,…,𝑎𝑛+𝑘)=0}.{n:F(bn,…,bn+k)=0}⊆⋃e∈Ek+1{n:Fe(an,…,an+k)=0}.

Each set on the right has density zero by hypothesis, and there are only finitely many such sets because 𝐸𝑘+1Ek+1 is finite. Their union therefore has density zero. ◻

Finiteness of the correction set is essential to this argument; no independence between the corrections and the sequence is needed. Without a restriction on the corrections, the choice 𝑒𝑛 = −𝑎𝑛en=−an would give 𝑏𝑛 =0bn=0, which fails nonconcentration even for the polynomial 𝑥0x0.

Corollary 8.4 (nonconcentration does not force irrationality). Fix 𝑀 ≥1M≥1 and 𝐾 ≥0K≥0, and let 𝑏b be the perturbed sequence supplied by Theorem 8.1 at the actual prime gaps. Then ∑𝑛≥0𝑏𝑛2−(𝑛+1)∑n≥0bn2−(n+1) is rational, 𝑏𝑛 =𝑔𝑛bn=gn for 𝑛 <𝐾n<K, 𝑏𝑛 −𝑔𝑛 ∈{0,𝑀}bn−gn∈{0,M} and 𝑏𝑛 ≡𝑔𝑛(mod𝑀)bn≡gn(modM) for every 𝑛n, 𝑏b has fixed-block nonconcentration, and the cumulative sequence 𝑃𝑛 =2 +∑𝑖<𝑛𝑏𝑖Pn=2+∑i<nbi satisfies 𝑝𝑛 ≤𝑃𝑛 ≤𝑝𝑛 +𝑀𝑛pn≤Pn≤pn+Mn and hence 𝑃𝑛 ∼𝑛log⁡𝑛Pn∼nlog⁡n.

Proof. Theorem 8.1 gives the rational sum, prefix and congruence assertions. Summing 0 ≤𝑏𝑖 −𝑔𝑖 ≤𝑀0≤bi−gi≤M and using ∑𝑖<𝑛𝑔𝑖 =𝑝𝑛 −2∑i<ngi=pn−2 gives 𝑝𝑛 ≤𝑃𝑛 ≤𝑝𝑛 +𝑀𝑛pn≤Pn≤pn+Mn. Now 𝑝𝑛 ∼𝑛log⁡𝑛pn∼nlog⁡n [17] implies 𝑃𝑛 ∼𝑛log⁡𝑛Pn∼nlog⁡n. The perturbation takes values in the fixed two-element set 𝐸 ={0,𝑀}E={0,M}, so Theorem 8.3 applies to the actual gaps, which have fixed-block nonconcentration by Schlage-Puchta’s lemma [18]. ◻

All the corollary’s properties hold for one rational-sum sequence; the positions 𝑃𝑛Pn need not be prime. Taking 𝑀M even also preserves eventual evenness. In particular 𝑀 =2𝑞M=2q preserves parity and modulus 𝑞q, with 𝑏𝑛 ≤𝑔𝑛 +2𝑞bn≤gn+2q. Nonconcentration remains a fixed-polynomial, fixed-block assertion, not a uniform estimate for growing families.

Proposition 8.5 (nonconcentration under sparse changes). Let 𝑎,𝑏 :ℕ →ℤa,b:N→Z agree off a set 𝑆S of ordinary density zero. If 𝑎a has fixed-block nonconcentration, then so does 𝑏b. No boundedness assumption on 𝑎 −𝑏a−b is needed.

Proof. Fix 𝑘k and a nonzero 𝐹 ∈ℤ[𝑥0,…,𝑥𝑘]F∈Z[x0,…,xk]. The two blocks agree unless 𝑛 +𝑖 ∈𝑆n+i∈S for some 0 ≤𝑖 ≤𝑘0≤i≤k. Consequently

| | |{𝑛<𝑁:𝐹(𝑏𝑛,…,𝑏𝑛+𝑘)=0}| | |≤| | |{𝑛<𝑁:𝐹(𝑎𝑛,…,𝑎𝑛+𝑘)=0}| | |+𝑘∑𝑖=0|𝑆∩[𝑖,𝑁+𝑖)|.|{n<N:F(bn,…,bn+k)=0}|≤|{n<N:F(an,…,an+k)=0}|+∑i=0k|S∩[i,N+i)|.

The first count is 𝑜(𝑁)o(N) by nonconcentration for 𝑎a; the remaining finitely many counts are 𝑜(𝑁)o(N) because 𝑆S has density zero. The count concerns the event 𝐹 =0F=0, not the sizes of 𝐹F or the corrections. The sequence 𝑏b is already fixed, so this holds for every fixed 𝐹,𝑘F,k, without a uniform estimate for growing polynomial families or block lengths. ◻

Corollary 8.6 (a rational sum with the stated prime-gap statistics). For every prescribed finite prime-gap prefix and 0 <𝜀 ≤10<ε≤1, there is an altered sequence 𝑏 =𝑔 +𝑒b=g+e, with 𝑃𝑛 =2 +∑𝑖<𝑛𝑏𝑖Pn=2+∑i<nbi, for which one can simultaneously impose a rational dyadic value, nonnegative integer corrections eventually at most (log⁡(𝑛 +3))𝜀(log⁡(n+3))ε, all fixed eventual coefficient and cumulative congruences, fixed-block polynomial nonconcentration, and vanishing total variation distance between unnormalised block distributions for lengths 𝑜(log⁡log⁡𝑋)o(log⁡log⁡X). The cumulative positions satisfy

0≤𝑃𝑛−𝑝𝑛=𝑂𝜀(𝑛(log⁡(𝑛+3))𝜀log⁡log⁡𝑛),𝑃𝑛∼𝑛log⁡𝑛.0≤Pn−pn=Oε(n(log⁡(n+3))εlog⁡log⁡n),Pn∼nlog⁡n.

Proof. Apply Theorem 2.1 to the actual gaps, choose a rational target in its interval, and fix the resulting 𝑏 =𝑔 +𝑒b=g+e. Proposition 8.5 then transfers Schlage-Puchta’s nonconcentration [18] to this one sequence, for every fixed polynomial and block length. To obtain the cumulative support bound, split [√𝑛,𝑛)[n,n) into dyadic intervals, use log⁡log⁡𝑥 ≍log⁡log⁡𝑛log⁡log⁡x≍log⁡log⁡n there, and bound the first √𝑛n indices trivially. Thus |𝑆 ∩[0,𝑛)| =𝑂(𝑛/log⁡log⁡𝑛)|S∩[0,n)|=O(n/log⁡log⁡n). The pointwise correction bound gives the displayed estimate, including a fixed constant for the exceptional prefix. Since (log⁡𝑛)𝜀−1/log⁡log⁡𝑛 →0(log⁡n)ε−1/log⁡log⁡n→0 for 0 <𝜀 ≤10<ε≤1, the prime number theorem [17] gives the last conclusion. The position series is rational as well: reversing the order of summation of nonnegative terms gives

∑𝑛≥0𝑃𝑛2𝑛+1=2+∑𝑖≥0𝑏𝑖∑𝑛>𝑖2−𝑛−1=2+∑𝑖≥0𝑏𝑖2𝑖+1∈ℚ.∑n≥0Pn2n+1=2+∑i≥0bi∑n>i2−n−1=2+∑i≥0bi2i+1∈Q.

The finite value on the right also proves convergence on the left. ◻

The case 𝜀 >1ε>1 needs no new construction: use exponent 11. Its corrections satisfy the larger allowance, while the stronger error 𝑂(𝑛log⁡(𝑛 +3)/log⁡log⁡𝑛) =𝑜(𝑛log⁡𝑛)O(nlog⁡(n+3)/log⁡log⁡n)=o(nlog⁡n) still preserves cumulative growth. Thus every positive exponent is available. The construction supplies the support and correction estimates; Schlage-Puchta’s lemma supplies nonconcentration of the original gaps, and the prime number theorem supplies their cumulative asymptotic.

For each fixed integer 𝑦 ≥2y≥2, take modulus 𝑦!y!. Once the congruence holds and 𝑝𝑛 >𝑦pn>y, gcd(𝑃𝑛,𝑦!) =1gcd(Pn,y!)=1 excludes every prime divisor at most 𝑦y. This is stronger than eventual oddness. The cutoff depends on 𝑦y; primality would require this exclusion up to √𝑃𝑛Pn at the same index. Even prime-valued positions could omit intervening primes.

The bounded construction transfers nonconcentration by finitely many polynomial translates, not by preserving block frequencies. The sparse construction transfers block laws by leaving most blocks unchanged, with absolute error tending to zero for 𝑚 =𝑜(log⁡log⁡𝑋)m=o(log⁡log⁡X). Neither argument preserves relative rare-event frequencies or the quantitative prime-pattern hypotheses in Section 3.
