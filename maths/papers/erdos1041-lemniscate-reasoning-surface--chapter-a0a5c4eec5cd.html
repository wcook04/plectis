Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

Erdős #1041 By Will Cook 29 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 86 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For a monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc, any two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1} by a path of length less than 2. For any squarefree monic polynomial of degree 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, an area-growth argument gives such a path when the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/2513/25; scaling gives length less than (5/2)𝜇1/𝑛(5/2)μ1/n inside {|𝑓| <(25/13)𝜇}{|f|<(25/13)μ}. The record develops this argument, an inverse-ray averaging estimate, and a construction using an isolated simple critical value, together with results for collinear roots and several coefficient families. A weighted Poisson identity gives a sharp mean bound on critical values; examples distinguish that bound from path-length control. The final sections examine inverse-sheet topology and refute an earlier spanning-tree estimate. These results do not prove the unrestricted length-2 assertion.

Unformalised remark: a quartic composed with a power map

We next examine the ordinary lifting argument based on Pendyala’s degree-four theorem [11] and four-point radial lemma [11]. The cited quartic result alone does not formalise this higher-degree construction. Let 𝑃P be a monic quartic, let 𝑞 ≥2q≥2 be an integer, and set

𝑓(𝑧)=𝑃((𝑧−ℎ)𝑞).f(z)=P((z−h)q).

Assume that every root of 𝑓f lies in the open unit disc. A repeated root of 𝑓f gives the constant path, so suppose 𝑓f is squarefree. Then the roots of 𝑃P are distinct and nonzero. For a root 𝑤 =𝑦𝑞w=yq of 𝑃P, all points ℎ +𝜁𝑗𝑦h+ζjy, with 𝜁 =𝑒2𝜋𝑖/𝑞ζ=e2πi/q, are roots of 𝑓f. Hence the open-disc hypothesis gives

This proves the path assertion in every degree 4𝑞 ≥84q≥8. The fibre average gives a little more: |ℎ| <1|h|<1 and |𝑤𝑘|1/𝑞 <√1−|ℎ|2|wk|1/q<1−|h|2 for every quotient root. This refinement concerns the translated power-substitution family, not arbitrary degree-4𝑞4q polynomials.

Pendyala proves the quartic geometric theorem and its four-point radial lemma. The local Lean module checks the antitone density inequality, its integral, the strict powered endpoint-length bound, and the final length estimate. It does not formalize Pendyala’s geometric lemma, the continuous covering-space construction of the root lift, or the ordinary chord/radial case assembly. This is a family defined by a power substitution, not a proof of unrestricted Erdős #1041.

The joining point in the quartic construction.

Pendyala’s proof of [11] uses a smallest enclosing disc and joins the selected roots through its centre. For the power substitution we instead apply [11] to the centred disc |𝑤| ≤𝑅|w|≤R. That lemma requires only the disc and pairwise-distance hypotheses, not minimality of the disc. Keeping the joining point at 00 is what makes the radial lift above valid. The endpoint-length bound for a single chord would not justify lifting an arbitrary broken line through a nonzero quotient vertex.
