Short paper

Distinct running least common multiples

Erdős #269 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 17 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. For three distinct primes, the reciprocal running-LCM kernel has nonsingular minors of every order, with the same indices in every third-coordinate layer. Row and column rescaling reduces the proof to a matrix whose entries are 1 or the reciprocal of the third prime. Thus no finite sum of products separates one exponent from the other two. Separately, for the repeated {2,3,5} series the paper proves a tail recurrence and a residue criterion equivalent to irrationality. The unresolved step is to prove its inequalities for every positive multiplier coprime to 30 and arbitrarily late starts.

This paper owns the problem-specific exposition for Erdős #269: the two-prime transcendence deductions and attribution, three-prime product formula, cell and shell structure, non-separability, finite experiments, and remaining cofinal escape condition.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #269, which remains open.

Two sums from running least common multiples

The 55-smooth integers are the positive integers whose prime factors belong to {2,3,5}{2,3,5}. Their running least common multiples begin 1,2,6,12,60,60,120,360,360,…1,2,6,12,60,60,120,360,360,…. Positive powers of distinct primes never coincide, and geometric growth of the successive running LCMs gives convergence. We use ℕ ={0,1,2,…}N={0,1,2,…}.

Theorem 1.1 (the sum over distinct running LCMs for {2,3,5}{2,3,5}). The number D{2,3,5}D{2,3,5} is irrational.

If the sum were rational, its normalised tails would take only finitely many values. We show that each such value determines both the next block of jumps and the following tail. Repetition of a tail would then force the blocks to be eventually periodic, contrary to the irrational frequency of powers of 33. To recover a block from its tail, we use five affine maps whose images are separated by a bound obtained from the spacing of powers of 55.

Erdős asserted irrationality of D𝑃DP for every finite 𝑃P with |𝑃| ≥2|P|≥2 in a letter dated 1 January 1973, without printing a proof [3]. Thus the theorem above is a special case of that earlier assertion. We claim no priority for its conclusion. The companion’s general argument uses an isolated interchange of jumps on a torus. That argument is retained there as an ordinary proof awaiting formalisation. The proof below is independent of that argument.

The question in Erdős Problem #269 concerns the sum with multiplicity,

R𝑃=∑𝑢∈S𝑃1L⁡(𝑢),RP=∑u∈SP1L⁡(u),

as recorded in [1] and [2]. For example, L⁡(5) =L⁡(6) =60L⁡(5)=L⁡(6)=60 for 𝑃 ={2,3,5}P={2,3,5}, giving 2/602/60 in R𝑃RP and 1/601/60 in D𝑃DP. The theorem treats the sum over distinct values; the problem asks about the sum with multiplicity. Our arguments leave the latter unresolved for finite 𝑃P with |𝑃| ≥3|P|≥3. For 𝑃 ={𝑝}P={p} both sums equal 𝑝/(𝑝 −1)p/(p−1).

Section 2 proves the theorem. We then retain the multiplicities and examine what changes: the tails satisfy an integer recurrence, but their values are unbounded. Sections 3 and 4 derive that recurrence and a residue criterion for the repeated sum. For two primes, Fan’s factorisation [7] reduces both sums to a Hecke–Mahler value and yields transcendence (Section 5). Appendix A collects the elementary running LCM identities, and Appendix B explains why the three-prime kernel has no finite separated factorisation.

Approximation.

Truncating the original kernel to 𝑖 <𝑁i<N gives a sum of 𝑁N separated terms. The bound H⁡(𝑥) >𝑥3/(𝑝𝑞𝑟)H⁡(x)>x3/(pqr) makes the truncation error tend to zero both uniformly and in ℓ1(ℕ3)ℓ1(N3). Rescaling preserves exact rank but removes the decay responsible for the first approximation. The companion’s approximation theorem proves both assertions for their stated norms. Verification, reproducibility and sources

The five-map proof is in the distinct-value development and its block-series criterion. Its statement has no recorded Comparator comparison. The evidence record fixes the source versions; the named input for two-prime transcendence is stated beside that theorem.

The programs for sums over distinct values calculate the interval endpoints and finite-state examples using exact arithmetic; the proof in Section 2 derives its constants directly.

The companion’s account of this proof also compares one-jump and dyadic bounds. Its finite-state extension uses intervals depending on earlier blocks. The saved exact computations satisfy that criterion for every set of three primes at most 3131 and for 292292 of the 330330 four-prime sets in that range. The general criterion and those computations are recorded as remarks awaiting formalisation. For {2,3,5,7}{2,3,5,7} two admissible automaton words give the same affine map at every subsequent refinement, preventing that automaton from recovering its first block. This identity does not establish a collision between two words realised by the arithmetic sequence.

The companion’s source inventory also covers the ordinary general and subseries arguments. Its historical note records the 26 September 2026 AI check and the corrections made from it; independent human mathematical review has not been reported.

Further comparisons

The classical criteria of Erdős–Straus [9], Hančl–Tijdeman [10] and Diananda–Oppenheim [8] are compared with the bounded-base series in the companion. It also compares the bounded-ratio argument with Erdős–Taylor [11] and Fan [12], and the restrictions on integer recurrences with the polynomial Cantor-series results of Hančl–Tijdeman [13]. Koutsoukou-Argyraki and Li’s Isabelle development [19] formalises classical irrationality criteria. It does not treat the present repeated series.

For transcendence, the relevant comparisons are the finite-difference conditions of Luca–Ouaknine–Worrell [14], the echoing conditions in [15], the fixed-base complexity theorem of Adamczewski–Bugeaud [20], and multivariate Mahler theory [21]. Quadratic growth of 𝑚𝑎ma supplies none of the missing cancellation or recurrence hypotheses. The companion exhibits nonzero boundary terms in a proposed third-difference calculation and distinguishes variable-base digits from fixed-base expansions. Work on fixed-prime semigroups [16][17] and other Ahmes-series problems [18] provides context; no estimate from those papers enters our dyadic counting argument.
