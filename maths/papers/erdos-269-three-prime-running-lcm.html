Problem note

No Finite Separable Representation at Three Prime Generators

Erdős #269 12 pp By Will Cook July 2026; exposition revised 18 September 2026
PDFOpen the PDF · 12 pp TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis

For three distinct primes, the reciprocal running-LCM kernel has nonsingular minors of every order, with the same indices in every third-coordinate layer. Row and column rescaling reduces the proof to a matrix whose entries are 1 or the reciprocal of the third prime. Thus no finite sum of products separates one exponent from the other two. Separately, for the repeated {2,3,5} series the paper proves a tail recurrence and a residue criterion equivalent to irrationality. The unresolved step is to prove its inequalities for every positive multiplier coprime to 30 and arbitrarily late starts.

This paper owns the problem-specific exposition for Erdős #269: the two-prime transcendence deductions and attribution, three-prime product formula, cell and shell structure, non-separability, finite experiments, and remaining cofinal escape condition.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #269, which remains open.

Introduction

For a finite prime set 𝑃P, let S𝑃SP consist of the positive integers whose prime factors lie in 𝑃P, including 11. We use ℕ ={0,1,2,…}N={0,1,2,…} for exponent indices. For 𝑥 ≥1x≥1, put

Erdős asked whether R𝑃RP is irrational when |𝑃| ≥2|P|≥2 . For 𝑃 ={𝑝,𝑞,𝑟}P={p,q,r}, write

Prime-power divisibility gives L =HL=H (Proposition 4.1). Fan used this identity to separate the two-prime kernel . With three primes, even a finite sum of separated products is impossible. This is a statement about the kernel, not the arithmetic nature of its sum.

Exact rank is not an approximation obstruction.

Truncating the original kernel to 𝑖 <𝑁i<N gives a sum of 𝑁N separated terms. The bound H⁡(𝑥) >𝑥3/(𝑝𝑞𝑟)H⁡(x)>x3/(pqr) makes the truncation error tend to zero both uniformly and in ℓ1(ℕ3)ℓ1(N3). Rescaling preserves exact rank but removes the decay responsible for the first approximation. The companion proves both assertions, including the distinction between finite restrictions and the full infinite matrix, in Section 4.1.

The two-prime comparison

Fan’s post of 26 June 2026 gives the repeated two-prime factorisation, its quadratic expression in one Hecke–Mahler value and its transcendence conclusion [7]. Guide to the formal sources

There are two separate arguments. The rank proof uses the height formula, row and column rescaling, and a threshold determinant. The arithmetic argument uses the shell coefficients, the tail recurrence, denominator clearing and least positive residues. The Lean proof of Theorem 5.2 establishes the equivalence of the residue condition with irrationality; it does not establish the residue condition itself.

Library declarations.

The margin marks link each result to its Lean proof. The main steps rest on the following library declarations, each opened at a fixed revision. Running LCM: the exact height formula; constancy on each logarithmic cell; the jumps by 𝑝p, 𝑞q and 𝑟r; exactly 3 count +13count+1 jump values; the quadratic bound on exponent shells. Dyadic blocks: at most one internal pure-power jump per channel; the radix is 22, 66, 1010 or 3030; the radix lies in [2,30][2,30]. Kernel rank: the kernel is not of rank one; the smallest 2 ×22×2 minor equals −1/15−1/15; the tail-state recurrence. Residues and carries: no bounded positive state represents an escaping residue (integer form); the local-window escape condition and its exclusion of a positive reduced carry; a bounded-radix orbit is integral or cofinally far from the integers; no uniformly bounded nonzero carry lift; block-null words are channel coboundaries; the carry as a residue digit plus a coboundary; a finite-dimensional factorisation gives a finite realised span.

Scope of the supplementary literature.

The companion develops the comparisons omitted from the main argument. Its Section 5 relates the bounded-ratio argument to Erdős–Taylor [10] and Fan [11]; Section 6 compares the exact carry restrictions and polynomial-radix assumptions of Hančl–Tijdeman [12]. The Isabelle development of Koutsoukou-Argyraki and Li [18] verifies classical criteria, not the present series.

Section 9 of the companion separates the finite-difference hypotheses of Luca–Ouaknine–Worrell [13] from quadratic growth alone, and checks the obstacles to using the echoing , digit-complexity [19] and multivariate Mahler [20] results. These comparisons do not establish the missing hypotheses for 𝑆S.

For broader background, the companion distinguishes work on fixed-prime semigroups [15][16] from other unit-fraction irrationality problems [17]. No estimate from those papers is used in this note’s counting argument.
