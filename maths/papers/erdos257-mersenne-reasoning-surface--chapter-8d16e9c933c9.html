Reciprocal Mersenne Subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 153 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A weighted summability criterion proves irrationality of reciprocal Mersenne subseries: if h is the part of a supported on a finite nonempty prime set P, every infinite A with ∑ ℎ(𝑎)/(𝑎(𝑏ℎ(𝑎) −1))h(a)/(a(bh(a)−1)) finite has irrational ∑ (𝑏𝑎 −1)−1(ba−1)−1. The base-two condition gives the conclusion at every integer base for every infinite subset, and permits an example with divergent reciprocal sum, while excluding full support and all odd exponents. The proof averages over a finite part of the support and over dyadic observation lengths. Erdős's weaker reciprocal-summable theorem is proved in full, and a common window extends both arguments to unions of weighted and positive-cover supports. Finite-denominator and achievement-set calculations formulate tests for the targets 1/21/2 and 1/211/21; those tests do not decide either target or the universal question. Prime support at base 2 and squarefree support at power-of-two bases are attributed as existing work.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #257: selected checked premises, finite computations, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, whose universal and half-value questions remain open.

Exact finite sums at unbounded depths

Definition 12.6. For every integer 𝑁N, require some 𝑛 ≥max{𝑁,1}n≥max{N,1} and 𝐷 ⊆{2,…,𝑛}D⊆{2,…,n} with

∑𝑑∈𝐷⌊2𝑛2𝑑−1⌋=2𝑛−1−1.∑d∈D⌊2n2d−1⌋=2n−1−1.

(CofinalExactLocalMersenneHalfRows). This condition is unproved. Supports at different depths need not agree. This is the condition in Definition 6.66; no strict weakening of half-membership is asserted.

The implication from this condition to HALFHALF is proved unconditionally via compactness of AA (a continuous image of the space of 0/10/1 sequences under the digit-coding map, hence closed) (half_mem_mersenneAchievementSet_of_cofinalExactLocalRows). The cited theorem already proves that a representing support exists, because that is what membership of AA means. It need not choose a support by a specified algorithm from the row witnesses. An ordinary diagonal subsequence argument gives a compatible limiting support: successively fix each coordinate along an infinite subsequence, then use the uniform tail bound to pass to its value. This uses continuity of the digit map, also recorded in continuous_positiveMersenneDigitValue. The diagonal argument preserves 2 ∈𝐴2∈A, since all sufficiently deep exact rows contain exponent two (two_mem_of_exact_localMersenneQuotient). This describes an ordinary existence proof; no additional formal extraction theorem or effective algorithm from purely existential row data is claimed.

Proposition 2.7(c) already identifies this condition as logically equivalent to 1/2 ∈A1/2∈A. Equivalence of a membership condition, constructive extraction of a coherent support, and a rejected sufficient strengthening are not interchangeable descriptions. Absence of a Lean extraction theorem does not make this condition a strictly weaker substitute for HALFHALF. The remaining work here is to supply exact rows at unbounded depths, or any equivalent membership condition.

The finite construction uses a (𝑐 −2)(c−2)-bit capacity test. Write

( ∗ ∗)(∗∗) is exactly one bit sharper and, via localBinarySuffix_two_mul_sub_two_lt_criticalCapacity_iff (under the stated core hypotheses), is equivalent to 𝑄(𝐷 ∪{𝑐},2𝑐 −2) ≥22𝑐−3Q(D∪{c},2c−2)≥22c−3. Here 𝑐c is the added exponent; the quotient is evaluated at depth 2𝑐 −22c−2, not at depth 𝑐c. The proof of ( ∗)(∗) gives the more informative additive bound 𝑆 <2𝑐−2 +|𝐷|S<2c−2+|D| before using |𝐷| ≤𝑐 −2|D|≤c−2. (capacity band)∀𝑁 ∃𝑐≥max(𝑁,4) ∃𝐷⊆{2,…,𝑐−1},0<1/2−𝑉(𝐷)<𝑤𝑐,𝑆(𝐷,1,2𝑐−2)∉[2𝑐−2,2𝑐−2+𝑐−3].(capacity band)∀N ∃c≥max(N,4) ∃D⊆{2,…,c−1},0<1/2−V(D)<wc,S(D,1,2c−2)∉[2c−2,2c−2+c−3].

The excluded band contains 𝑐 −2c−2 integers. The sharp capacity bound itself allows 2𝑐−22c−2 nonnegative integer values. The numerical widths differ from those in Section 12.5, but the conditions concern different quantities. Width alone does not prove a logical implication between them.

The sharp-fill implication exists_exactRowStrictUpperFill_of_skippedCoreSharpCapacity assumes ( ∗ ∗)(∗∗) for a below-half core 𝐷 ⊆{2,…,𝑐 −1}D⊆{2,…,c−1}, but does not require a critical crossing or identify that core with a real greedy prefix. This gives another formulation in which to seek the required finite rows. It does not establish a strict logical weakening of half-membership, nor does it supply cores satisfying the bound at unbounded ranks. Moreover, the integer gap separation fixes the resulting exact row whenever it exists; the formulation does not create multiple exact rows at a fixed depth.

The complete induction from the endpoint-six seed to exact sums at unbounded depths is already formalised (cofinalExactLocalMersenneHalfRows_of_criticalQuotientSupply); it uses the sharp capacity input, in its reduced canonical form

𝑐≥4, 𝑐∉𝐺⟹𝑄((𝐺∩{2,…,𝑐−1})∪{𝑐},2𝑐−2)≥22𝑐−3.c≥4, c∉G⟹Q((G∩{2,…,c−1})∪{c},2c−2)≥22c−3.

(HalfGreedySkippedCriticalQuotientSupply), at essentially every step; this input is unproved. This condition is required along the specified greedy prefixes. That restriction on the witnesses does not by itself prove a strict logical ordering with the cofinal condition above.
