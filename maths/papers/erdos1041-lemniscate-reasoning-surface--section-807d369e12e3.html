Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

Erdős #1041 By Will Cook 29 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 86 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For a monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc, any two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1} by a path of length less than 2. For any squarefree monic polynomial of degree 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, an area-growth argument gives such a path when the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/2513/25; scaling gives length less than (5/2)𝜇1/𝑛(5/2)μ1/n inside {|𝑓| <(25/13)𝜇}{|f|<(25/13)μ}. The record develops this argument, an inverse-ray averaging estimate, and a construction using an isolated simple critical value, together with results for collinear roots and several coefficient families. A weighted Poisson identity gives a sharp mean bound on critical values; examples distinguish that bound from path-length control. The final sections examine inverse-sheet topology and refute an earlier spanning-tree estimate. These results do not prove the unrestricted length-2 assertion.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #1041, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or the current status of Erdős #1041, including the later one-polynomial Formal Conjectures path-image Hausdorff-length check and its unreviewed historical curve-length correspondence; those distinctions belong to docs/claims.json in the mathematics repository.

The proof of Remark 5.1 averages over levels in [𝜇,𝜆𝜇][μ,λμ]. To keep the path at the first critical level instead, one would need a perimeter estimate of the following kind. The regular-level argument (CF1) gives no finite pointwise bound at the critical level: even at a simple critical point, the area derivative diverges logarithmically.

The addendum [26] reports a Runge-approximation construction of monic level-one components with one simple zero and arbitrarily long analytic boundaries. It imposes neither a unit root disc nor a global lower bound on critical-value moduli. Its proof is not available, and the report is not an input to the path estimates here. The question below requires a stronger hypothesis than the existence of one unramified component: the level must lie below every critical-value modulus of the polynomial. A construction without that global condition cannot by itself answer the question.

Sources and scope.

The inputs of the construction in the proof of Remark 5.1 are the capacity identity for polynomial preimages [2], Pólya’s area inequality [2], Cauchy–Schwarz, the Koebe distortion theorem for univalent maps, and averaging over adjacent boundary endpoints. The public source of record is the full area and boundary-length argument, which carries every rational verification quoted above. Its Section 10 gives a weaker conditional bound involving an auxiliary parameter and a logarithm; Remark 5.6 states the stronger 𝛽𝜌βρ consequence proved here. That section’s proposed numerical perimeter constant is refuted by the degree-eight example above and is not assumed in the theorem. The unconditional construction retains level 2𝜇2μ and constant 71/1071/10. The scaled low-critical corollary has both a smaller constant and a smaller level. The proof retained here is ordinary, with component-sensitive consequences on their stated regions; its rational cutoffs are exact integer inequalities. The earlier perimeter samples concerned only a selected component. They do not test the stronger condition that the level lie below every critical value. The existence of a root-to-root curve of length less than 22 is not established for the unrestricted class by the arguments in this record. The adjacent classical literature on lemniscate length concerns the arclength of the level curve {|𝑝| =1}{|p|=1}, which is Erdős #114. Fryntov and Nazarov recall its history [15]: Dolzhenko’s bound 4𝜋𝑛4πn, Pommerenke’s bound 74𝑛274n2 of 1961, and Borwein’s bound 8𝜋𝑒𝑛8πen [13]. Eremenko and Hayman proved 9.173𝑛9.173n [14], Fryntov and Nazarov the asymptotically sharp 2𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛)2n+o(n) [15], and Tao resolved the problem for large 𝑛n [16]. These results bound the length of a level curve, not the least internal root-pair path. This distinction identifies the quantity being estimated; no claim of novelty follows from the scope or outcome of a literature search.
