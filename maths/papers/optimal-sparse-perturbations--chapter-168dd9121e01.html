Cross-problem paper

Reading Eight Erdős Problems Together

Eight Erdős problems read together By Will Cook 22 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 38 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub

Précis. One account of the mathematics developed across the programmes: an exact tail-capacity criterion under eventual congruences, its sharp factorial support-gap threshold, rational and irrational Lambert subsums across bases, and the limits of finite greedy tests and other irrationality methods. Full arguments, exact counterexamples, unsuccessful routes and attribution are retained together. The principal proofs are ordinary mathematics; cited Lean ingredients have their own stated scope. Historical novelty and independent expert review are not established.

This paper owns the synthesis exposition and ordinary proofs across the covered problems: capacity and congruence constructions, Lambert subsums, rational-point counts, method limits and their research record.

It is not authority for a solution to any original Erdős target, historical novelty, independent expert review, or a full Lean proof of the analytic capacity criterion or Lambert-chain theorem.

Which dyadic shifts detect irrationality?

Consider a real sequence satisfying

𝑇𝑁+1=2𝑇𝑁−𝑔𝑁+1,𝑔𝑁+1∈ℤ.TN+1=2TN−gN+1,gN+1∈Z.

Write ‖𝑥‖ =dist⁡(𝑥,ℤ)‖x‖=dist⁡(x,Z). Iterating the recurrence shows that 𝑇𝑁 =2𝑁𝑇0 −𝑎𝑁TN=2NT0−aN for some integers 𝑎𝑁aN, and hence

‖𝑇𝑁+ℎ−𝑇𝑁‖=‖2𝑁(2ℎ−1)𝑇0‖.(4)(4)‖TN+h−TN‖=‖2N(2h−1)T0‖.

For 𝐻 ⊆ℤ>0H⊆Z>0 and 𝑐 ∈ℝc∈R, say that 𝐻H detects 𝑇T at threshold 𝑐c when

∀ℎ∈𝐻∀𝑁0∃𝑁≥𝑁0:‖𝑇𝑁+ℎ−𝑇𝑁‖≥𝑐.(5)(5)∀h∈H∀N0∃N≥N0:‖TN+h−TN‖≥c.

The witnessing index may depend on the shift.

The Lean-checked transfer from the #251 tail classifier and the #269 bounded-radix escape theorem gives the implication from irrationality in (5) for every positive shift when 𝑐 ≤1/3c≤1/3; the research record keeps the original 1/311/31 experiment and the later sharp-constant argument distinct. Dubickas’s theorem, in the form stated by Akiyama and Kaneko [4], gives

lim sup𝑁→∞‖2𝑁𝜉‖≥𝜏(𝜉∉ℚ),𝜏=∑𝑛≥0𝑡𝑛2𝑛+1=0.412454…,lim supN→∞‖2Nξ‖≥τ(ξ∉Q),τ=∑n≥0tn2n+1=0.412454…,

where 𝑡𝑛tn is the parity of the binary digit sum of 𝑛n. This cited input extends the implication to every 𝑐 <𝜏c<τ. The endpoint argument below is ordinary mathematics; the sharp bound itself is not formalised here.

For fixed 𝑐c and 𝐻H, call (5) the selected-shift test. The next theorem asks when that test detects irrationality for every integer-digit dyadic recurrence, rather than for one chosen orbit.

Theorem 7.1 (Restricted dyadic shifts). Fix 𝑐 ∈ℝc∈R and 𝐻 ⊆ℤ>0H⊆Z>0. The following conditions are equivalent:

  1. For every integer-digit dyadic recurrence 𝑇T, the initial value 𝑇0T0 is irrational if and only if 𝑇T passes the selected-shift test.

  2. The parameters satisfy 0 <𝑐 <𝜏0<c<τ, and every positive integer 𝑑d divides some shift ℎ ∈𝐻h∈H.

Proof. Suppose first that these two conditions hold. For irrational 𝑇0T0 and fixed ℎ >0h>0, (2ℎ −1)𝑇0(2h−1)T0 is irrational. Equation (4) and Dubickas’s bound give indices as late as desired with distance at least 𝑐c. Conversely, let 𝑇0 =𝑝/𝑞T0=p/q with 𝑞 =2𝑠𝑟q=2sr and 𝑟r odd. Some 𝑑 >0d>0 satisfies 2𝑑 ≡1(mod𝑟)2d≡1(modr); take 𝑑 =1d=1 if 𝑟 =1r=1. Choose ℎ ∈𝐻h∈H divisible by 𝑑d. For every 𝑁 ≥𝑠N≥s, 2𝑁(2ℎ −1)𝑝/𝑞2N(2h−1)p/q is an integer, so (5) fails.

For necessity of the divisibility condition, suppose no member of 𝐻H is divisible by some 𝑑 ≥2d≥2. Put 𝐿 =3𝑑L=3d, 𝑞 =2𝐿 −1q=2L−1, and 𝑇𝑁 ={2𝑁/𝑞}TN={2N/q}. This bounded rational orbit has digits in {0,1}{0,1}. For a tested shift ℎh, let 𝑟 ∈{1,…,𝐿 −1}r∈{1,…,L−1} be its residue modulo 𝐿L. Since 2𝐿 ≡1(mod𝑞)2L≡1(modq), the distance sequence for ℎh is periodic and agrees with that for 𝑟r. The sequences for 𝑟r and 𝐿 −𝑟L−r agree up to a cyclic shift, because 2𝑟(2𝐿−𝑟 −1) ≡ −(2𝑟 −1)(mod𝑞)2r(2L−r−1)≡−(2r−1)(modq). We may therefore use 𝑚 =max(𝑟,𝐿 −𝑟) ≥𝐿/2 ≥3m=max(r,L−r)≥L/2≥3. At the index 𝑁 =𝐿 −𝑚 −1N=L−m−1, one distance is

𝑣=2𝐿−1−2𝐿−𝑚−12𝐿−1=1−2−𝑚2(1−2−𝐿),716≤𝑣<12.v=2L−1−2L−m−12L−1=1−2−m2(1−2−L),716≤v<12.

It recurs every 𝐿L indices. The first six Thue–Morse digits are 011010011010, so 𝜏 <27/64 <7/16τ<27/64<7/16. Thus this rational orbit passes every tested shift at every 0 <𝑐 <𝜏0<c<τ.

If 𝑐 ≤0c≤0, the zero orbit passes. If 𝐻H is empty, every orbit passes. Finally, for 𝑐 ≥𝜏c≥τ and any ℎ0 ∈𝐻h0∈H, take 𝑇0 =𝜏/(2ℎ0 −1)T0=τ/(2h0−1) and 𝑔𝑁 =0gN=0. The cited sharp-bound construction identifies 𝜏τ as irrational [4]. The strict endpoint inequality ‖2𝑁𝜏‖ <𝜏‖2Nτ‖<τ for every 𝑁 ≥1N≥1 follows from the Thue–Morse shift argument just below. Equation (4) makes the test fail at ℎ0h0. ◻

Here is the strict endpoint step used in the proof. Write the Thue–Morse word as 𝑡 =011010…t=011010…, its bitwise complement as ¯𝑡t¯, and let 𝜇μ be the order-preserving substitution 0 ↦010↦01, 1 ↦101↦10. The word 𝑡t is fixed by 𝜇μ. An odd-indexed suffix of 𝑡t starts with 001001 or 010010 when its first bit is 00, both strictly below the prefix 011011 of 𝑡t. When its first bit is 11, it starts with 101101 or 110110, both strictly above the prefix 100100 of ¯𝑡t¯. An even-indexed suffix is the image under 𝜇μ of a shorter suffix, so induction and order preservation give the same strict comparisons at every positive index. The binary value of each suffix is therefore below 𝜏τ or above 1 −𝜏1−τ, according to its first bit. This proves ‖2𝑁𝜏‖ <𝜏‖2Nτ‖<τ for 𝑁 ≥1N≥1. The research record retains the longer historical calculation. Related extremal-word constructions appear in Allouche, Clarke and Sidorov [5], whose published bibliography points to earlier work of Allouche and Cosnard. The linked research record gives the longer nearest-integer calculation; no historical priority for the specific formulation above is asserted.

The factorial family 𝐻 ={𝑗! :𝑗 ≥1}H={j!:j≥1} satisfies the divisibility condition: 𝑑 ∣𝑑!d∣d!. The power-of-two family does not, since none of its members is divisible by 33. An especially small counterexample for the latter is the rational orbit 𝑇𝑁 ={2𝑁/7}TN={2N/7}: for each tested shift its distances cycle through 1/71/7, 2/72/7, and 3/73/7, so it passes at every 𝑐 <𝜏c<τ. No finite shift family suffices. Conversely, excluding all multiples of a large 𝑑d leaves a family of density 1 −1/𝑑1−1/d that fails the test; factorial shifts have density zero and succeed. This criterion does not establish irrationality for the actual prime-gap tail in #251.

Divisor coverage also implies that 𝐻 ∩𝑑ℤ>0H∩dZ>0 is unbounded for every 𝑑 >0d>0: apply coverage to the multiples 𝑘𝑑kd as 𝑘k grows. Consequently deleting finitely many shifts from a working family preserves the criterion.

Polynomially selected shifts

For a polynomial 𝑃 ∈ℤ[𝑥]P∈Z[x] with positive leading coefficient, set

𝐻𝑃={𝑃(𝑛):𝑛≥0, 𝑃(𝑛)>0},𝐻prime𝑃={𝑃(𝑝):𝑝 prime, 𝑃(𝑝)>0}.HP={P(n):n≥0, P(n)>0},HPprime={P(p):p prime, P(p)>0}.

The argument of 𝑃P in the second family is prime; this is different from checking whether 𝑃P has a root modulo every prime.

Corollary 7.2 (Polynomial shift families). Fix 0 <𝑐 <𝜏0<c<τ. The 𝐻𝑃HP-selected test detects irrationality for every integer-digit dyadic recurrence if and only if 𝑃P has a root modulo every positive integer. The 𝐻prime𝑃HPprime-selected test has this property if and only if, for every positive integer 𝑑d, there is a root 𝑟r of 𝑃P modulo 𝑑d with gcd(𝑟,𝑑) =1gcd(r,d)=1.

Proof. By Theorem 7.1, each assertion reduces to whether every 𝑑 >0d>0 divides a member of the selected family. If 𝑃(𝑟) ≡0(mod𝑑)P(r)≡0(modd), all sufficiently large integers 𝑛 ≡𝑟(mod𝑑)n≡r(modd) give positive multiples 𝑃(𝑛)P(n) of 𝑑d. This proves the first assertion in both directions.

For the prime-argument family, a root 𝑟r coprime to 𝑑d gives arbitrarily large primes 𝑝 ≡𝑟(mod𝑑)p≡r(modd) by Dirichlet’s theorem, and hence positive multiples 𝑃(𝑝)P(p) of 𝑑d. Conversely, suppose there is no unit root modulo some 𝑑d. Every prime 𝑝p for which 𝑑 ∣𝑃(𝑝)d∣P(p) then satisfies gcd(𝑝,𝑑) >1gcd(p,d)>1, so 𝑝p is one of the finitely many prime divisors of 𝑑d. Thus 𝐻prime𝑃 ∩𝑑ℤHPprime∩dZ is bounded. Divisor coverage would make this intersection unbounded, since for every 𝑘 >0k>0 it supplies a member divisible by 𝑘𝑑kd. This is a contradiction. ◻

The first condition is the usual intersectivity condition [1]. The unit-root condition for prime arguments is 𝑃P-intersectivity, also called intersectivity of the second kind [2]. For example, 𝑃(𝑛) =𝑛2P(n)=n2 works with integer arguments, while 𝑛2 +1n2+1 fails modulo 33. At prime arguments, 𝑃(𝑝) =𝑝P(p)=p fails already modulo 66, while 𝑝 −1p−1 and 𝑝2 −1p2−1 work: the residue 11 is a unit root modulo every 𝑑d.

Intersectivity need not come from an integer root. Mishra lists 𝐹(𝑥) =(𝑥2 −13)(𝑥2 −17)(𝑥2 −221)F(x)=(x2−13)(x2−17)(x2−221) as a polynomial with a root modulo every positive integer but no rational root [3]. In fact, it also has a unit root modulo every positive integer. For odd primes other than 1313 and 1717, at least one of 13,17,22113,17,221 is a nonzero quadratic residue, since 221 =13 ⋅17221=13⋅17; its root lifts to every prime power. Modulo powers of 1313, use 𝑥2 −17x2−17 with 𝑥 ≡2(mod13)x≡2(mod13); modulo powers of 1717, use 𝑥2 −13x2−13 with 𝑥 ≡8(mod17)x≡8(mod17). At powers of 22, the unit 17 ≡1(mod8)17≡1(mod8) has a square root. The Chinese remainder theorem supplies unit roots modulo arbitrary 𝑑d. Thus both 𝐻𝐹HF and 𝐻prime𝐹HFprime pass the criterion, without relying on a single global root.

Prime moduli alone do not suffice for the first condition. Let 𝑄(𝑥) =(𝑥2 −2)(𝑥2 −3)(𝑥2 −6)Q(x)=(x2−2)(x2−3)(x2−6). It has a root modulo every prime: for odd primes not dividing 66, if neither 22 nor 33 is a square, their product 66 is; the primes 22 and 33 are immediate. But 𝑄(𝑛) ≡4Q(n)≡4 when 𝑛n is even and 𝑄(𝑛) ≡6Q(n)≡6 when 𝑛n is odd, modulo 88. Therefore neither 𝐻𝑄HQ nor its prime-argument subfamily contains a multiple of 88. Lê’s cited arXiv v1 introduction lists this 𝑄Q as intersective [1]; the modulo-88 calculation corrects that example, without affecting the local-root criterion stated there. The failure is visible without the general counterexample construction: take the rational orbit 𝑇𝑁 ={2𝑁/255}TN={2N/255}. Since 28 ≡1(mod255)28≡1(mod255), for every positive shift ℎ =𝑄(𝑛)h=Q(n), indices 𝑁 ≡3(mod8)N≡3(mod8) when ℎ ≡4(mod8)h≡4(mod8) give ‖𝑇𝑁+ℎ −𝑇𝑁‖ =120/255‖TN+h−TN‖=120/255, and indices 𝑁 ≡1(mod8)N≡1(mod8) when ℎ ≡6(mod8)h≡6(mod8) give 126/255126/255. Both distances exceed 7/16 >𝜏7/16>τ, so this rational orbit passes every 𝑄Q-selected test at 0 <𝑐 <𝜏0<c<τ. The modular root and orbit calculations are ordinary proofs; this polynomial extension is not claimed as Lean checked.
