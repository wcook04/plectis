Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 61 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. For rational reciprocal sums under 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, the record also develops sufficient conditions for the eventual recurrence 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1, controlling either increases of an integer tail numerator or a convergent sum at steps setting new maxima. Examples separate numerical growth bounds from the exact recurrences. The cubic proof is distinguished from the theorems formalised in Lean. None of the additional bounds is derived for every rational reciprocal sum in Erdős #243.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #243, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

The direct analytic question

Problem 14.5 (summability from rationality). Let (𝑎𝑛)(an) satisfy the hypotheses of Problem 1.1, and let (𝐷𝑛,𝐶𝑛,𝐸𝑛)(Dn,Cn,En) be its integer tail. Must

∞∑𝑛=0(−𝐸𝑛)+𝐶𝑛<∞?∑n=0∞(−En)+Cn<∞?

An affirmative answer closes Problem #243 by Theorem 13.1. Since the summands tend to zero and log⁡(1 +𝑡) ∼𝑡log⁡(1+t)∼t, convergence is equivalent to bounded partial products ∏𝑛<𝑁(1 +( −𝐸𝑛)+/𝐶𝑛)∏n<N(1+(−En)+/Cn). A negative answer requires the full growth and rationality hypotheses, not merely a locally admissible orbit. By Theorem 6.2, finiteness of (21) for one nonincreasing weight with divergent integral and one fixed 𝐵 ≥0B≥0 also suffices. The equivalent record-only sum in Theorem 6.2 weights the positive excess of each actual upward jump over 𝐵B by 𝑓(𝑈𝑛)f(Un).

Remark. One further question is not left open here, since it is answered in [12]: whether vanishing relative error follows from 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1 and rationality. It does. After deleting a finite prefix, Koizumi’s Corollary 3  [12] makes the sequence the pseudo-greedy expansion of its own reciprocal sum and gives 𝜀𝑛 →0εn→0 under exactly the hypotheses of Problem 1.1; rationality is not needed for that step, only summability of ∑1/𝑎𝑛∑1/an. The matched-index dictionary in Section 3 gives 𝜀𝑛 =𝐸𝑛/𝐶𝑛εn=En/Cn on the restarted tail. For a rational sum, Lemma 4(2)  [12] also gives −𝑐𝑛/2 ≤𝑒𝑛 <𝑐𝑛/2−cn/2≤en<cn/2 at every index of that expansion. Thus |𝐸𝑛| <𝐶𝑛|En|<Cn holds beyond the restart, as required by Theorem 12.1; it need not hold at every original index. The eventual inequality |𝐸𝑛| <𝐶𝑛|En|<Cn also follows directly from vanishing relative error with 𝐾 =1K=1. What remains unsupplied is the eventual lower bound on 𝐸𝑛En in Theorem 12.1. That additional hypothesis is why the theorem is still conditional.
