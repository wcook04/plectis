Long paper

The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

First fix ℎh, then choose 𝑠,𝜂s,η; these must remain fixed as the threshold 𝑋0X0 increases. At each threshold all four bounds must hold for one common 𝑋,𝐿X,L, rather than along unrelated sequences of blocks. Their constants add to 14/25 +1/100 +1/100 +8/25 =9/1014/25+1/100+1/100+8/25=9/10, yielding the real-part criterion. This is sufficient for a certificate and does not assert the stronger 21/2521/25 norm criterion. In particular, no independence of the two phase factors follows from the grouping.

The decomposition is an exact identity, proved in Lean (Erdos249257.TotientTailPeriodKiller.windowFirstExp_sum_eq_pivot_decomposition), as is the implication from the four bounds to the real-part bound (Erdos249257.TotientTailPeriodKiller.first_harmonic_gap_of_pivotBudgetAt). The source proves this membership equality (Erdos249257.TotientTailPeriodKiller.mem_pivotFiber_one_overlap_iff), using 𝜑(𝑚𝑝) =𝜑(𝑚)(𝑝 −1)φ(mp)=φ(m)(p−1) and 𝑝 >𝑚p>m. This special choice has 𝑠 =𝐿 −ℎs=L−h varying with 𝐿L; it illustrates the arithmetic of the groups but does not establish the condition with 𝑠s fixed before 𝑋0X0.

The four inequalities have not been established together. Propositions 12.6 and 12.7 give the unassigned and excluded-cofactor bounds at minimal admissible depth. At 𝑠 =26s=26, 𝜂 =1/1000η=1/1000, the prime-number-theorem argument discussed in Section 3.3 also bounds the mean contribution for all large 𝑋X. With that input, the missing estimate is the real-part bound for the mean-subtracted contribution, at the same parameters and depth, on arbitrarily large blocks. Theorem 3.5 instead asks for one bound on GG, without a group-mean input.

A nonzero determinant alone does not prove the needed correlation estimate. The comparison in the linked source (Erdos249257.ResidualGaugeObstruction.locked_reconstruction_preserves_nonzero_minor) preserves such a determinant while fixing an entire row of weighted phases to 11. A successful application must distinguish that comparison, for example by additional arithmetic information, rather than only by column rescaling. The exact four-term identity supplies no prime-distribution estimate.
