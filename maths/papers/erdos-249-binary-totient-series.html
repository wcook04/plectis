Integral Relations among Totient Sections

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 16 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For integers 𝑘 ≥2k≥2 and 𝑒 ≥1e≥1, the totient sections n↦φ(k^j n+r) with 0 ≤𝑗 ≤𝑒0≤j≤e and 0 ≤𝑟 <𝑘𝑗0≤r<kj have an explicit rational basis of dimension 𝑘𝑒 +1ke+1. Every section is an explicit integer multiple of a retained one, and these reductions generate all integral relations, with unique coefficients. The proof combines the local formula for Euler's totient with a nonsingular evaluation matrix obtained from the Chinese remainder theorem and Dirichlet's theorem. The basis theorem does not decide the irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns the exact finite-level totient-kernel basis, independence proof and integral relations; the long paper develops denominator exclusions and other approaches to the binary series.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Introduction

Splitting the totient sequence into its even and odd subsequences, then splitting once more, gives seven sequences:

𝜑(𝑛);𝜑(2𝑛), 𝜑(2𝑛+1);𝜑(4𝑛), 𝜑(4𝑛+1), 𝜑(4𝑛+2), 𝜑(4𝑛+3).φ(n);φ(2n), φ(2n+1);φ(4n), φ(4n+1), φ(4n+2), φ(4n+3).

The identities

𝜑(4𝑛)=2𝜑(2𝑛),𝜑(4𝑛+2)=𝜑(2𝑛+1)φ(4n)=2φ(2n),φ(4n+2)=φ(2n+1)

leave five independent sequences. Thus every identity among the seven comes from these two substitutions. More precisely, an integral relation is a unique integral combination of the two displayed relations. We will prove the same statement at every depth and in every integer base.

For an integer 𝑘 ≥2k≥2, the totient sections are the sequences 𝑛 ↦𝜑(𝑘𝑗𝑛 +𝑟)n↦φ(kjn+r), where 𝑗 ≥0j≥0 and 0 ≤𝑟 <𝑘𝑗0≤r<kj. Throughout, 𝑛n ranges over ℕ ={0,1,2,…}N={0,1,2,…} and 𝜑(0) =0φ(0)=0. To describe relations we retain a separate index for each pair (𝑗,𝑟)(j,r), even when two pairs give the same sequence. For example, 𝜑(4𝑛 +2)φ(4n+2) and 𝜑(2𝑛 +1)φ(2n+1) are equal sequences but occupy different positions in the list above.

The sections form the 𝑘k-kernel of the totient sequence. A sequence is 𝑘k-regular when the ℤZ-module generated by its 𝑘k-kernel is finitely generated [1]. Coons proved that the totient sequence is not 𝑘k-regular for any 𝑘 ≥2k≥2 [2]. Here we determine those modules at each finite level, including their integral relations. The theorem gives the basis and the substitutions; Corollary 3.1 explains how to recover every relation from them. Level zero contains only 𝜑(𝑛)φ(n) and has dimension one.

Martin’s 2006 theorem on simultaneous totient inequalities already implies independence for every finite family of pairwise nonproportional affine forms with positive integral slopes [4]. Its conclusion is stronger than the independence used here. The point of the present description is the explicit reduction of the sections and the integral relation basis in Corollary 3.1. We also give a direct proof that constructs a nonsingular evaluation matrix. It uses the CRT–Dirichlet separation argument in Yazdani’s proof of Theorem 2, credited there to Shallit [12]. The step that needs care is to keep the progression for the selected affine form reduced while imposing prime divisors on all the others.

The proof in Section 2 separates scalar reduction from independence. Section 3 recovers the integral relations, starting in base six. Section 4 uses prime isolation for bounded functions of totient residues. The series

𝑆=∑𝑛≥1𝜑(𝑛)2𝑛S=∑n≥1φ(n)2n

has unbounded coefficients. Its irrationality, asked in Erdős Problem #249 [10], remains open. The coefficient basis supplies no irrationality conclusion for this value. Appendix A gives further coefficient consequences; Appendices B and C collect the tail identities, comparison sequences and still-unproved cancellation estimates relevant to 𝑆S. Their longer proofs are in The Binary Totient Series.

Proof of the basis theorem

Scalar reductions

The product formula proves spanning. For independence we first separate nonproportional affine arguments, then handle the two proportional arguments 𝑛n and 𝑘𝑛kn separately. Bounded functions of totient residues

The matrix argument separates one value from finitely many others. Now we separate one coefficient from all its neighbours in a long window. Boundedness turns that local separation into an irrationality proof: the right zero block forces a rational tail to vanish exactly; the left makes the isolated nonzero term too small to be rational with the same denominator. Residues in the first sum below are least nonnegative representatives, not necessarily binary digits.

Problem C.1 (Four estimates for the first-harmonic sum). For each ℎ ≥1h≥1, do there exist 𝑠 ≥1s≥1 and 0 <𝜂 <10<η<1 such that, for every 𝑋0X0, there are 𝑋,𝐿X,L with

The ordinary comparison argument gives larger first harmonics at all sufficiently large admissible scales, distinguishing its rational sequences from the cancellation required of 𝜑φ. Its proof is included with the interpolation construction.

Balasubramanian, Giri and Srivastav give correlation estimates applicable to 𝜑(𝑛)/𝑛φ(n)/n, uniformly for |ℎ| ≤𝑥/2|h|≤x/2 [11]. Their correlation is a product of two normalised totient values. Here the modulus grows with 𝐿L, and 𝑤𝑁wN is an exponential of the other, overlapping totient contributions. Uniformity in the shift of that two-point product does not supply the required weighted exponential estimate.
