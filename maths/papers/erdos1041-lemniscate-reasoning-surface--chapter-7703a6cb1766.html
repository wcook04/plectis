Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

Erdős #1041 By Will Cook 29 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 86 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For a monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc, any two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1} by a path of length less than 2. For any squarefree monic polynomial of degree 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, an area-growth argument gives such a path when the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/2513/25; scaling gives length less than (5/2)𝜇1/𝑛(5/2)μ1/n inside {|𝑓| <(25/13)𝜇}{|f|<(25/13)μ}. The record develops this argument, an inverse-ray averaging estimate, and a construction using an isolated simple critical value, together with results for collinear roots and several coefficient families. A weighted Poisson identity gives a sharp mean bound on critical values; examples distinguish that bound from path-length control. The final sections examine inverse-sheet topology and refute an earlier spanning-tree estimate. These results do not prove the unrestricted length-2 assertion.

The question and the degree-seven counterexample

For a monic polynomial 𝑓f, let Ω𝑓 ={|𝑓| <1}Ωf={|f|<1}; the notation 𝐸𝑓Ef used in the segment arguments denotes the same open set. We write 𝐾𝑡(𝑓) ={|𝑓| ≤𝑡}Kt(f)={|f|≤t} for a closed sublevel. Erdős, Herzog and Piranian [4] asked:

Problem 1.1 (Erdős #1041). Let 𝑓(𝑧) =∏𝑛𝑖=1(𝑧 −𝑧𝑖)f(z)=∏i=1n(z−zi) be monic of degree 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, with 𝑧𝑖 ∈𝔻zi∈D, the open unit disc. Must two of the roots be joined by a curve of length less than 22 lying in the open lemniscate {𝑧 ∈ℂ :|𝑓(𝑧)| <1}{z∈C:|f(z)|<1}?

Boundary roots already give an elementary failure of strict containment. For 𝑓(𝑧) =𝑧2 −1f(z)=z2−1, every continuous path from −1−1 to 11 meets the imaginary axis, where |𝑓(𝑖𝑦)| =1 +𝑦2 ≥1|f(iy)|=1+y2≥1. The two endpoints are therefore unjoinable in {|𝑓| <1}{|f|<1}, although the diameter lies in {|𝑓| ≤1}{|f|≤1}. This illustrates the distinction between the open and closed lemniscates.

A repeated zero gives a constant path between two occurrences. Thus the question concerns distinct zeros. The catalogue of Bloom [3] records the problem and its discussion. Pendyala [11] proved the degree-four case by chords or radial segments through the centre of a smallest enclosing disc. The degree-seven construction of ani has a different geometry.

Theorem 1.2 (ani’s counterexample). The monic polynomial in (2) has seven distinct roots in the open unit disc. Every connected subset of its strict unit lemniscate containing two roots has one-dimensional Hausdorff measure greater than 22.

Five components contain one zero each; the remaining component contains two, and the polynomial has degree two on it. A quadratic estimate near its critical point counts two inverse images, with multiplicity, for each value on an outward slit. Since the component has degree two, these are all its inverse images of that value. Hence the entire preimage of the slit in the component lies in the small disc where the local estimate applies.

On each of the two inverse sheets, radial projection gives a lower bound for the Hausdorff measure of a connected set joining the zeros. The sheets are disjoint, so the two bounds can be added. Their sum exceeds 22. Section 2 supplies the estimates and proof. Applied to a continuous path image, this also excludes a rectifiable path of total variation less than 22.

The theorem concerns a fixed polynomial. The small-parameter family reported by ani is not proved here, and independent human review of correspondence with the 1958 wording is not recorded. The supplied formal statements and their scope are listed in Section C.

For trinomials the root identity keeps every root-to-origin segment inside the lemniscate, giving a short broken line between any pair of zeros. The later analytic remarks seek a pair by following sublevel components: one argument uses a small least critical-value modulus, and another uses an isolated simple critical value. Their conclusions have additional formalisation requirements. A bound on critical-value means alone makes no choice of a contained path.
