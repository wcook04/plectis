Irrationality criteria for Lambert subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 26 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. The series ∑𝑎∈𝐴(𝑏𝑎 −1)−1∑a∈A(ba−1)−1 is irrational for every integer 𝑏 ≥2b≥2 and infinite A contained in E∪V when E satisfies a finite-prime weighted summability condition and V has a summable positive divisor cover. The two classes are incomparable, and their union reaches supports in neither class. A common average over dyadic lengths produces one index with both displacements small. Arbitrary infinite supports at base two remain unresolved.

This paper owns the weighted, divisor-cover and mixed support criteria with complete proofs and method limits; the long paper treats finite denominators, the achievement set and rational membership.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, which remains open.

Authorship and AI use. Will Cook built and directed the research infrastructure and maintains the public release. He reviewed claims when he could. AI agents did most of the research and drafting. Cook did not independently verify every claim.

Finite-support denominator periods

For a finite nonempty 𝐹 ⊆ℕ>0F⊆N>0, let 𝐷𝐹DF be the positive reduced denominator of 𝑋𝐹(𝑏) =∑𝑛∈𝐹(𝑏𝑛 −1)−1XF(b)=∑n∈F(bn−1)−1. We use ord1⁡(𝑏) =1ord1⁡(b)=1.

Theorem B.1 (the exact denominator period). Let 𝐹 ⊆ℕ>0F⊆N>0 be finite and nonempty, let 𝑏 ≥2b≥2 be an integer, and let 𝐷𝐹 >0DF>0 be the denominator of 𝑋𝐹(𝑏)XF(b) in lowest terms. Then 𝐷𝐹DF is coprime to 𝑏b, and

ord𝐷𝐹⁡(𝑏)=lcm⁡{𝑛:𝑛∈𝐹}.ordDF⁡(b)=lcm⁡{n:n∈F}.

If moreover lcm⁡(𝐹) ≥2lcm⁡(F)≥2, then lcm⁡(𝐹) <𝐷𝐹lcm⁡(F)<DF. We use ord1⁡(𝑏) =1ord1⁡(b)=1, so the statement includes 𝐹 ={1}F={1} at 𝑏 =2b=2.

Put 𝐿 =lcm⁡(𝐹)L=lcm⁡(F). Clearing denominators gives 𝐷𝐹 ∣𝑏𝐿 −1DF∣bL−1, so the upper divisibility for the order is immediate. For the reverse, choose 𝑛 ≥2n≥2 maximal under divisibility in 𝐹F and a prime ℓ ∣Φ𝑛(𝑏)ℓ∣Φn(b). If 𝑒 =𝑣ℓ(𝑏𝑛 −1)e=vℓ(bn−1), the full prime power ℓ𝑒ℓe has ordℓ𝑒⁡(𝑏) =𝑛ordℓe⁡(b)=n. Every other selected exponent 𝑚m has 𝑛 ∤𝑚n∤m, hence 𝑣ℓ(𝑏𝑚 −1) <𝑒vℓ(bm−1)<e. The 𝑛nth summand has uniquely smallest ℓℓ-adic valuation and cannot cancel. Thus ℓ𝑒 ∣𝐷𝐹ℓe∣DF and 𝑛 ∣ord𝐷𝐹⁡(𝑏)n∣ordDF⁡(b). Taking all maximal selected exponents proves the order statement; the size bound follows from ord𝐷𝐹⁡(𝑏) ∣𝜑(𝐷𝐹) <𝐷𝐹ordDF⁡(b)∣φ(DF)<DF when 𝐿 ≥2L≥2. The case 𝐹 ={1}F={1} has order one directly.

The unique-valuation argument also permits signs ±1±1 on the finite summands. This signed extension is not included in the formal statement. The required cyclotomic prime-power fact is proved in Section E. The distinction between primes and prime powers is visible in

𝑋{2,3}(2)=1021,𝑋{2,6}(2)=2263.X{2,3}(2)=1021,X{2,6}(2)=2263.

In both examples 22 has multiplicative order six modulo the denominator. The first combines orders two and three; the second retains the order-six prime power 99, although no prime divisor of 6363 has order six. The boundary 𝐹 ={1}F={1} at base two has 𝐷𝐹 =𝐿 =1DF=L=1. These lower denominator bounds give no upper height control for infinite partial sums and do not decide an infinite-support value. Van Assche instead constructs approximants for the full Lambert series and proves both nonvanishing and decay of the associated integer linear forms [14]. That approximation mechanism does not follow from denominator survival alone.

In particular, 1/211/21 is not a finite subseries sum. If it were, the order of 22 modulo 2121 would force every selected exponent to divide 66. The exponent 11 is excluded since its weight is 11. Any sum containing 22 or 33 exceeds 1/211/21, whereas the only nonzero remaining sum is 1/631/63, from the exponent 66. This will distinguish finite and infinite representations in the next appendix.
