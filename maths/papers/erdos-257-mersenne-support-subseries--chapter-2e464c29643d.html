Short paper

Irrationality criteria for Lambert subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 27 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The series ∑𝑎∈𝐴(𝑏𝑎 −1)−1∑a∈A(ba−1)−1 is irrational for every integer 𝑏 ≥2b≥2 and infinite A contained in E∪V when E satisfies a finite-prime weighted summability condition and V has a summable positive divisor cover. The two classes are incomparable, and their union reaches supports in neither class. A common average over dyadic lengths produces one index with both displacements small. Arbitrary infinite supports at base two remain unresolved.

This paper owns the weighted, divisor-cover and mixed support criteria with complete proofs and method limits; the long paper treats finite denominators, the achievement set and rational membership.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, which remains open.

Positive divisor majorants

The weighted criterion treats exponents separately. A divisor cover instead uses their joint divisibility. To describe it, write the series in terms of its divisor counts:

𝑐𝐴(𝑛)=#{𝑎∈𝐴:𝑎∣𝑛},𝑋𝐴(𝑏)=∑𝑛≥1𝑐𝐴(𝑛)𝑏−𝑛.(6)(6)cA(n)=#{a∈A:a∣n},XA(b)=∑n≥1cA(n)b−n.

Expand (𝑏𝑎 −1)−1 =∑𝑗≥1𝑏−𝑎𝑗(ba−1)−1=∑j≥1b−aj and interchange the nonnegative sums. The selector 𝟏𝐴1A is zero or one, whereas 0 ≤𝑐𝐴(𝑛) ≤𝜏(𝑛)0≤cA(n)≤τ(n), with 𝜏(𝑛)τ(n) the number of positive divisors of 𝑛n. These are power-series coefficients before any carrying; they need not be base-𝑏b digits.

In the criteria of Kaneko–Suzuki–Tachiya [2], sparsity concerns the positions of nonzero power-series coefficients, not the set of selected Lambert denominators. For nonempty 𝐴A, the coefficient 𝑐𝐴(𝑛)cA(n) is positive on every multiple of min𝐴minA, so its nonzero positions have positive lower density. This violates the support-counting hypotheses of those criteria, which therefore do not apply directly to (6). The corresponding density calculation, and the distinction between their remote-tail average and our displacement, are given in Section 1.2 of the companion paper.
