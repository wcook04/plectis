Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

Erdős #1041 By Will Cook 29 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 86 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For a monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc, any two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1} by a path of length less than 2. For any squarefree monic polynomial of degree 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, an area-growth argument gives such a path when the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/2513/25; scaling gives length less than (5/2)𝜇1/𝑛(5/2)μ1/n inside {|𝑓| <(25/13)𝜇}{|f|<(25/13)μ}. The record develops this argument, an inverse-ray averaging estimate, and a construction using an isolated simple critical value, together with results for collinear roots and several coefficient families. A weighted Poisson identity gives a sharp mean bound on critical values; examples distinguish that bound from path-length control. The final sections examine inverse-sheet topology and refute an earlier spanning-tree estimate. These results do not prove the unrestricted length-2 assertion.

A mean bound for critical values in every degree

The Poisson argument first gives a quadratic mean bound, allowing a disc of any centre and radius and counting all multiplicities. This is the formally supported theorem below. The subsequent fourth-power refinement, and the higher exponents obtained from vanishing complex power sums, are unformalised analytic remarks. A critical-value moment bound supplies no estimate for the lengths of inverse paths ending at those points.

Dubinin [21] proves the sharp unit-disc critical-value product inequality using the resultant identity, the maximum-modulus principle and Schur’s Vandermonde inequality. The positive-moment estimate below implies his product bound by AM–GM. When 𝑛 ≥3n≥3, the product estimate alone cannot control a positive moment: a list 𝑇,𝑇−1,1,…,1T,T−1,1,…,1 has product one, but its sum of 𝑝pth powers is unbounded as 𝑇 →∞T→∞ for every 𝑝 >0p>0. This compares the information in two inequalities for nonnegative lists; it does not claim that these lists occur as critical values of polynomials in the theorem. In degree two there is just one critical value, and the product and positive-moment bounds are equivalent. For a zero prescribed at the origin, his Theorem 3 (p. 1174) gives

(∏𝑗|𝑓(𝑐𝑗)|)1/(𝑛−1)≤(𝑛−1)(|𝑓′(0)|2𝑛𝑛)1/(𝑛−1).(∏j|f(cj)|)1/(n−1)≤(n−1)(|f′(0)|2nn)1/(n−1).

Dubinin explicitly identifies Tischler’s earlier contribution [25]. His Theorem 1 uses dissymmetrisation; that is not the proof mechanism of Theorem 2. Schur’s original paper [27] is the historical antecedent cited there, not a separately re-proved source in this record. A sharp marked-zero positive-moment bound remains a separate question. The addendum [26] reports a reduction to boundary-root configurations, without solving the resulting angular optimisation. That unavailable addendum is not a proof dependency of the critical-value theorem below.
