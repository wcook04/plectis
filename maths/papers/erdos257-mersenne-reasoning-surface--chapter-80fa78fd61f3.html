Long paper

Reciprocal Mersenne Subseries

Erdős #257 By Will Cook 30 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 153 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A weighted summability criterion proves irrationality of reciprocal Mersenne subseries: if h is the part of a supported on a finite nonempty prime set P, every infinite A with ∑ ℎ(𝑎)/(𝑎(𝑏ℎ(𝑎) −1))h(a)/(a(bh(a)−1)) finite has irrational ∑ (𝑏𝑎 −1)−1(ba−1)−1. The base-two condition gives the conclusion at every integer base for every infinite subset, and permits an example with divergent reciprocal sum, while excluding full support and all odd exponents. The proof averages over a finite part of the support and over dyadic observation lengths. Erdős's weaker reciprocal-summable theorem is proved in full, and a common window extends both arguments to unions of weighted and positive-cover supports. Finite-denominator and achievement-set calculations formulate tests for the targets 1/21/2 and 1/211/21; those tests do not decide either target or the universal question. Prime support at base 2 and squarefree support at power-of-two bases are attributed as existing work.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #257: selected checked premises, finite computations, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #257, whose universal and half-value questions remain open.

Observation 9.33 (A sufficient block-certificate condition). irrational_erdosSupportSeries_of_weighted_coeff_certificates (carry variant Lean source). Conclusion. ∀𝑏 ≥2, ∀𝐴 ⊆ℕ∀b≥2, ∀A⊆N (no infinitude, no structure hypothesis at all): if ∀𝑞 >0 ∃𝑁,𝐾,𝐿,𝐶∀q>0 ∃N,K,L,C with 𝐾 ≤𝐿K≤L, 𝑏𝑟 ∣𝑐𝐴(𝑁 +𝑟)br∣cA(N+r) for 𝑟 ∈[1,𝐾]r∈[1,K], ∑𝐿𝑟=𝐾+1𝑐𝐴(𝑁 +𝑟)𝑏𝐿−𝑟 ≤𝐶∑r=K+1LcA(N+r)bL−r≤C, ∃𝑡, 0 <𝑐𝐴(𝑁 +𝐿 +1 +𝑡)∃t, 0<cA(N+L+1+t), and 𝑞(𝐶 +𝑁 +𝐿 +2) <𝑏𝐿q(C+N+L+2)<bL, then 𝑋𝐴(𝑏)XA(b) is irrational. The cited full-support, multiples, periodic, residue-class, pairwise-coprime and fixed-core constructions use this criterion. No such factorisation is asserted for the weighted support theorem in Section 1. Scope of the hypothesis. The criterion is already universally quantified over 𝐴A; the universal quantifier is not the gap. The certificate hypothesis must hold at every precision, and it is supplied only for named classes; certificate existence for an arbitrary infinite 𝐴A remains open. Limit of this sufficient condition. Supplying these certificates for every infinite support would imply the universal statement, but Proposition 11.4 rules out that supply for the two specified certificate schemes. The carry variant replaces digit-wise divisibility by aggregate divisibility 𝑏𝐾 ∣∑𝑟𝑐𝐴(𝑁 +𝑟)𝑏𝐾−𝑟bK∣∑rcA(N+r)bK−r; the squarefree obstruction applies to that variant too. Universal certificate supply is therefore not being posed as an unresolved equivalent formulation of Problem 257.

Observation 9.34 (Rational targets and infinitely many greedy skips). half_mem_mersenneAchievementSet_iff_greedySkippedSupport_infinite (forward Lean source; reverse mem_mersenneAchievementSet_of_greedySkippedSupport_infinite). For every rational 𝑥 ≥0x≥0,

𝑥∈A⟺the greedy algorithm for 𝑥 skips infinitely many ranks.x∈A⟺the greedy algorithm for x skips infinitely many ranks.

The reverse implication holds for every real 𝑥 ≥0x≥0: a fatal remainder 𝑟𝑛(𝑥) >𝑅𝑛rn(x)>Rn forces every subsequent weight to be taken, because 𝑅𝑛 =𝑤𝑛+1 +𝑅𝑛+1Rn=wn+1+Rn+1. Hence infinitely many skips exclude a fatal remainder, and Theorem 1.11 gives membership. For the forward implication, membership identifies the greedy sum with 𝑥x. Finitely many skips would make that sum 𝐸E minus a finite rational sum, which is irrational by Erdős’s theorem [1]. This contradicts rationality of 𝑥x and proves the stated extension as an ordinary argument.

Infinitely many skips do not mean infinitely many selected ranks: 𝑥 =1/3x=1/3 has the single-element support {2}{2} and skips every other rank. Thus the equivalence does not decide which rational targets are represented, or provide an infinite support with rational value. The Lean declarations above concern the half target; the extension to every rational target is an ordinary argument and is not formalised.

For any 𝐴A with a positive element and 𝑋𝐴(2) =𝑝/(2𝑐𝑣)XA(2)=p/(2cv), 𝑣 >0v>0 (any rational, not just dyadic): for every 𝜀 >0ε>0 there is 𝐵 ≥0B≥0 such that, for all 𝑁 ≥1N≥1, every run of ℎh zero divisor counts starting after 𝑐 +𝑁c+N has ℎ ≤𝜀log2⁡𝑁 +𝐵h≤εlog2⁡N+B. More basically, if 𝑎0a0 is any positive element of 𝐴A, then each block of 𝑎0a0 consecutive integers contains a multiple of 𝑎0a0. Every zero window of 𝑐𝐴cA therefore has length at most 𝑎0 −1a0−1, whether or not 𝑋𝐴(2)XA(2) is rational. For a fixed nonempty support, the displayed logarithmic bound thus gives no additional restriction. A proposed alternative between super-logarithmic zero windows and general block certificates cannot split this class: the first branch is already impossible. It would require the certificate branch for every support, which the obstruction in Proposition 11.4 rules out for the specified schemes.

Observation 9.39 (The two hypotheses of the sunflower criterion). irrational_erdosSupportSeries_of_orthogonalPetalBouquet. Conclusion. For 𝐴 =A= (finite exceptional set dividing 𝑄Q) ∪{core𝑖 ⋅petal𝑖}∪{corei⋅petali} with cores ∣𝑄∣Q, petals pairwise coprime and coprime to 𝑄Q, ∑1/petal𝑖 <∞∑1/petali<∞: if 𝐴A satisfies the tail-selection hypothesis below, then its series is irrational.

Different restrictions. Two gaps. (i) On the support: the bouquet class strictly generalises pairwise-coprime supports but still demands globally pairwise-coprime, summable petals; it inherits the exclusion of the primes noted in the preceding observation and adds nothing in the divisor-dense regime. (ii) On the selector: the tail-selection hypothesis must hold at every precision and is unproved.

Missing step. For the selector, ∀𝐾 >0 ∃𝑁∀K>0 ∃N, 2𝐾 ∣2K∣ the carried first block ∧ ∑𝑗≥1𝑐𝐴(𝑁 +𝐾 +𝑗)2−𝑗 ≤16∧ ∑j≥1cA(N+K+j)2−j≤16, from the bouquet’s persistent-reduced-modulus averaging. For the class: the assertion that every infinite 𝐴A admits a bouquet structure is false (take 𝐴 =A= all multiples of 6: there is no coprime petal decomposition). A theorem for every infinite support would therefore require a different structural hypothesis, not merely a proof of that false existence assertion.

Observation 9.40 (What eventual periodicity supplies). irrational_erdosSupportSeries_eventuallyPeriodic. Conclusion. ∀𝑏 ≥2 ∀𝑚 >0 ∀𝑁0∀b≥2 ∀m>0 ∀N0: 𝐴A infinite and 𝑚m-periodic above 𝑁0N0 ⇒⇒ irrational. Plus: the class of supports with irrational series is closed under finite symmetric difference in both directions. The statement follows from the periodic theorem of Luca and Tachiya [3].

Scope of the hypothesis. The theorem allows every positive period and every finite starting threshold. It still requires an eventually periodic support, so it does not include all aperiodic supports covered by the other criteria in this record. Finite symmetric difference preserves eventual periodicity. Thus the finite-perturbation argument alone cannot extend this theorem to an aperiodic tail; a further hypothesis or argument is needed.

Missing step. A “locally periodic at cofinally many scales” version; ∀𝑞 >0 ∃𝑁 ∃𝑚 ≤𝑓(𝑁)∀q>0 ∃N ∃m≤f(N) such that 𝐴 ∩[𝑁,𝑁 +𝐿]A∩[N,N+L] is 𝑚m-periodic for 𝐿L large enough that the periodic sieve manufactures one block certificate. The periodic argument already needs periodicity only across the certificate window, so this is the closest genuine widening; but proving that periodic windows exist at unboundedly many scales for arbitrary 𝐴A is a new statement.

2𝑢(𝐿−𝑐−1)=𝑢(𝐿−𝑐)+𝑣𝑐𝐴(𝐿)≥1+𝑣|𝐹|.2u(L−c−1)=u(L−c)+vcA(L)≥1+v|F|.

Observation 9.44 (A finite-gap criterion for every target in the convex hull). half_mem_mersenneAchievementSet_or_exists_fatal_gap, half_mem_mersenneAchievementSet_iff_no_existsFatalHalfGap; the exclusion for a general support has its own positiveMersenneSupportValue_ne_of_rank_gap. For every real 𝑥 ∈[0,𝐸]x∈[0,E], not just rational targets, one has

𝑥∉A⟺∃𝑚≥1 ∃𝐷⊆{1,…,𝑚−1},𝑋𝐷(2)+𝑅𝑚<𝑥<𝑋𝐷(2)+𝑤𝑚.x∉A⟺∃m≥1 ∃D⊆{1,…,m−1},XD(2)+Rm<x<XD(2)+wm.

Here 𝑅𝑚 =∑𝑗>𝑚𝑤𝑗Rm=∑j>mwj. The implication to the right has a short greedy proof. If 𝑥 ∉Ax∉A, choose the first 𝑚m with 𝑟𝑚(𝑥) >𝑅𝑚rm(x)>Rm using Theorem 1.11. Since 𝑟0(𝑥) =𝑥 ≤𝐸r0(x)=x≤E, we have 𝑚 ≥1m≥1 and 𝑟𝑚−1(𝑥) ≤𝑅𝑚−1 =𝑤𝑚 +𝑅𝑚rm−1(x)≤Rm−1=wm+Rm. A take at 𝑚m would leave a remainder at most 𝑅𝑚Rm, so this step must be a skip. Thus 𝑅𝑚 <𝑟𝑚−1(𝑥) <𝑤𝑚Rm<rm−1(x)<wm, giving the displayed gap with the greedy prefix 𝐷D.

Conversely, the displayed gap contains no achievable value. A support with prefix 𝐷D either skips 𝑚m, giving value at most 𝑋𝐷(2) +𝑅𝑚XD(2)+Rm, or takes 𝑚m, giving value at least 𝑋𝐷(2) +𝑤𝑚XD(2)+wm. A different prefix first differs at some 𝑗 <𝑚j<m; the inequality 𝑤𝑗 >𝑅𝑗wj>Rj orders its entire continuation interval strictly on one side of the interval with prefix 𝐷D. Both gap endpoints themselves are achievable, by the supports 𝐷 ∪{𝑚 +1,𝑚 +2,…}D∪{m+1,m+2,…} and 𝐷 ∪{𝑚}D∪{m}. Consequently no parity argument or irrationality assertion about 𝐸E is needed for this ordinary general-target dichotomy. The restriction to [0,𝐸][0,E] is essential to this formulation: targets outside the convex hull are already excluded and need not lie in an internal gap. The general-target equivalence and both endpoint constructions are Lean-checked in paper_general_target_gap.

Observation 9.45 (Why the signed periodic theorem still requires periodicity). irrational_or_bpow_mul_eq_intCast_intWeightedErdosSeries_periodic, irrational_intWeightedErdosSeries_periodic_of_coeff_nonneg_of_frequently_ne_zero. Conclusion. ∀𝑏 ≥2 ∀𝑚 >0 ∀∀b≥2 ∀m>0 ∀ ℤZ-valued 𝑚m-periodic weight 𝑤w: irrational(∑𝑤(𝑎)/(𝑏𝑎 −1))(∑w(a)/(ba−1)) ∨∨ ∃𝑘,𝑧, 𝑏𝑘𝑥 =𝑧∃k,z, bkx=z; the terminating branch closes unconditionally as soon as 𝑐𝑤cw is one-signed and frequently nonzero. Luca and Tachiya’s theorem already gives irrationality for every nonzero periodic weight [3].

Different restrictions. Two mismatches. (i) Periodicity of the weight is still required; this is the signed version of the periodic-support theorem and keeps its periodicity hypothesis. (ii) The one-sidedness clause is automatic for #257 (a 0/10/1 support indicator gives 𝑐𝐴 ≥0cA≥0), so the sign conditions add nothing here; they were designed for the signed #249 argument. The genuinely transferable content is the dichotomy shape itself: irrational-or-𝑏b-adically- terminating, terminating branch killable by a positive far tail.

Missing step. An aperiodic version of the dichotomy; for arbitrary 𝐴A, either 𝑋𝐴(2)XA(2) is irrational or 2𝑘 ⋅2k⋅it is an integer. For arbitrary infinite 𝐴A, exclusion of the terminating branch is itself an additional unproved assertion; finite-support odd-denominator parity does not pass automatically to an infinite limit. An aperiodic dichotomy together with a valid terminating-branch exclusion would close the obligation. Periodicity is used only to manufacture the full-block certificates, so removing it again requires the missing supply of certificates for arbitrary supports.
