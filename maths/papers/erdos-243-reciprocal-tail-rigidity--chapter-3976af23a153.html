Short paper

Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 21 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For strictly increasing positive integers with 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3), the reciprocal sum is irrational. The argument extracts a cubic polynomial from an integer tail numerator, restricts its coefficients using a square in a cubic field, and excludes the remaining cases modulo seven. The unrestricted Erdős–Graham question remains open.

This paper owns the cubic-rate irrationality theorem and its proof; the long paper develops the separate Sylvester-tail criteria and broader recurrence questions.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

Tail numerators

For the Sylvester sequence 2,3,7,43,…2,3,7,43,…, whose reciprocal sum is 11, the successive tails are 1,1/2,1/6,1/42,…1,1/2,1/6,1/42,…. Multiplication by the products 1,2,6,42,…1,2,6,42,… leaves the constant integer numerator 11. For any rational reciprocal sum, including a denominator of the whole sum in these products again gives integer numerators. We keep these fractions unreduced: the exact multiplicative denominator update will transfer the growth rate to the numerator.

Assume now that 𝑎𝑛an are strictly increasing positive integers, 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, and ∑𝑛≥11/𝑎𝑛 =𝑝/𝑞∑n≥11/an=p/q with positive integers 𝑝,𝑞p,q. All indices in this construction start at 11. Put

𝑥𝑛=∑𝑘≥𝑛1𝑎𝑘,𝑃𝑛=∏1≤𝑗<𝑛𝑎𝑗,𝐷𝑛=𝑞𝑃𝑛,𝐶𝑛=𝐷𝑛𝑥𝑛.xn=∑k≥n1ak,Pn=∏1≤j<naj,Dn=qPn,Cn=Dnxn.

Koizumi constructs these coordinates in Lemma 4 [10]. On a pseudo-greedy tail his 𝑐𝑛,𝑑𝑛,𝑒𝑛cn,dn,en correspond to 𝐶𝑛,𝐷𝑛,𝐷𝑛 −(𝑎𝑛 −1)𝐶𝑛Cn,Dn,Dn−(an−1)Cn. If the tail is restarted with its value in lowest terms, both coordinates are divided by the same fixed integer. Reducing at every step would instead introduce varying cancellation factors into the numerator ratio. We therefore extract the polynomial before dividing by the eventual fixed gcd in Section 3.2.

For large 𝑛n, 𝑎𝑛+1 ≥𝑎2𝑛/2 ≥2𝑎𝑛an+1≥an2/2≥2an. The terms after 1/𝑎𝑛+11/an+1 sum to at most 2/𝑎𝑛+2 ≤4/𝑎2𝑛+12/an+2≤4/an+12, so

𝑥𝑛=1𝑎𝑛+1𝑎𝑛+1+𝑂(𝑎−2𝑛+1),𝐶𝑛+1𝐶𝑛=𝑎2𝑛𝑎𝑛+1+𝑂(1/𝑎𝑛).(2)(2)xn=1an+1an+1+O(an+1−2),Cn+1Cn=an2an+1+O(1/an).

For the second estimate, the first gives 𝑎𝑛𝑥𝑛 =1 +𝑂(1/𝑎𝑛)anxn=1+O(1/an). Use this in

𝐶𝑛+1𝐶𝑛=𝑎2𝑛𝑎𝑛+1𝑎𝑛+1𝑥𝑛+1𝑎𝑛𝑥𝑛.Cn+1Cn=an2an+1an+1xn+1anxn.

The last factor is 1 +𝑂(1/𝑎𝑛)1+O(1/an), and 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 →1an2/an+1→1, giving the stated additive error. Quadratic growth makes 1/𝑎𝑛 =𝑜(𝑛−𝑘)1/an=o(n−k) for every fixed 𝑘k, so this error preserves the precision required in the cubic theorem. We keep the original index 𝑛n throughout that proof. Although an eventual recurrence survives removal of a finite prefix, the expression 1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)1+3/n+o(n−3) changes its lower-order terms under an index shift.
