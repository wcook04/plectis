Long paper

The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Totient sections and tail differences

Put 𝜑(0) =0φ(0)=0 and ℕ ={0,1,2,…}N={0,1,2,…}. At base two, 𝜑(4𝑛) =2𝜑(2𝑛)φ(4n)=2φ(2n) and 𝜑(4𝑛 +2) =𝜑(2𝑛 +1)φ(4n+2)=φ(2n+1) leave five independent sequences among the seven sections through level two. More generally, the sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have the basis 𝜑(𝑛)φ(n), 𝜑(2𝑛)φ(2n) and 𝜑(2𝑗𝑛 +𝑟)φ(2jn+r) with 1 ≤𝑗 ≤𝑒1≤j≤e and odd 0 <𝑟 <2𝑗0<r<2j, of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. The all-base theorem in the short paper gives rank 𝑘𝑒 +1ke+1 and integral coordinates. Its retained positive residues satisfy 𝑘 ∤𝑟k∤r; coprimality with 𝑘k is unnecessary.

The coefficient relations are finite linear identities. The series question asks whether a particular infinite weighted sum can be rational. Put

𝑆:=∑𝑛≥1𝜑(𝑛)2𝑛=12+14+28+216+432+⋯=1.3676308019850223…S:=∑n≥1φ(n)2n=12+14+28+216+432+⋯=1.3676308019850223…

The series converges absolutely because 𝜑(𝑛) ≤𝑛φ(n)≤n. Erdős Problem #249 asks whether 𝑆S is irrational, which remains open. The comparisons below test proposed passages from coefficient structure to the value of this sum. Each retains specified properties while making the corresponding sum rational. It therefore defeats an argument using only those properties, not every argument that might also use the arithmetic of the totient.

Throughout, a cofinal condition means that a witness exists beyond each threshold. It need not hold at every sufficiently large index. Each comparison sequence is used only for the properties it preserves; agreement with a fixed totient prefix, for example, imposes no multiplicativity condition on the continuation.

Remark 1.1 (Earlier coefficient results). Erdős and Graham record the irrationality question for 𝑆S [10]. An integer sequence is 𝑘k-regular when the ℤZ-module generated by its sections 𝑛 ↦𝑐(𝑘𝑗𝑛 +𝑟)n↦c(kjn+r), 𝑗 ≥0j≥0 and 0 ≤𝑟 <𝑘𝑗0≤r<kj, is finitely generated. Coons proved that 𝜑φ is not 𝑘k-regular for any 𝑘 ≥2k≥2 [2]. Martin’s Theorem 1 implies that the functions 𝑛 ↦𝜑(𝑎𝑖𝑛 +𝑏𝑖)n↦φ(ain+bi) are linearly independent for pairwise nonproportional affine forms with positive integer slopes [4]. The exact ranks in §10.8, together with the all-base normal form in the short paper, identify the retained sections and every relation among them. The independence itself is not claimed as new. The CRT–Dirichlet separation method is already present in Yazdani’s Theorem 2 and proof, which explicitly credit Shallit [30]; our evaluation matrix uses one such separation for each column. Yazdani also proves non-automaticity of 𝜑(𝑛)mod𝑚φ(n)modm for every 𝑚 ≥3m≥3 in every base [30]. Allouche, Shallit and Yassawi give a survey account [31].

To test rationality, we remove the finite prefix, which becomes an integer after multiplication by 2𝑁2N. Write 2𝑁𝑆 =Φ𝑁 +𝑅𝑁2NS=ΦN+RN with Φ𝑁 :=∑𝑁𝑛=1𝜑(𝑛)2𝑁−𝑛 ∈ℕΦN:=∑n=1Nφ(n)2N−n∈N and 𝑅𝑁 :=∑𝑗≥0𝜑(𝑁 +1 +𝑗)/2𝑗+1 ∈[0,∞)RN:=∑j≥0φ(N+1+j)/2j+1∈[0,∞) (Erdos249257.TotientTailPeriodKiller.totientTail, two_pow_mul_totient_series_eq). Indeed,

𝑅𝑁+ℎ−𝑅𝑁≡2𝑁(2ℎ−1)𝑆(mod)𝑍,RN+h−RN≡2N(2h−1)S(mod)Z,

and the right side is integral for those choices.

Thus it suffices, for every positive shift ℎh, to find arbitrarily large 𝑁N for which 𝑅𝑁+ℎ −𝑅𝑁RN+h−RN is not an integer. A finite test is obtained by truncating this difference. Throughout, 𝑎mod𝑚amodm denotes the least nonnegative residue, and an unqualified index in a sum is a nonnegative integer. For ℎ,𝑁,𝐿 ∈ℕh,N,L∈N, write

𝐷(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿):=∑𝑗<𝐿(𝜑(𝑁+ℎ+1+𝑗)−𝜑(𝑁+1+𝑗))2𝐿−1−𝑗∈ℤ,D(h,N,L):=∑j<L(φ(N+h+1+j)−φ(N+1+j))2L−1−j∈Z,

the depth-𝐿L truncation of 2𝐿(𝑅𝑁+ℎ −𝑅𝑁)2L(RN+h−RN), and

C(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿):⟺(𝑁+ℎ+𝐿+2)<𝐷(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿)mod2𝐿<2𝐿−(𝑁+ℎ+𝐿+2),C(h,N,L):⟺(N+h+L+2)<D(h,N,L)mod2L<2L−(N+h+L+2),

a decidable condition (Erdos249257.TotientTailPeriodKiller.windowDiscrepancy, Erdos249257.TotientTailPeriodKiller.certifiedKill).

The omitted tails satisfy the exact identity

2𝐿(𝑅𝑁+ℎ−𝑅𝑁)−𝐷(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿)=𝑅𝑁+ℎ+𝐿−𝑅𝑁+𝐿.2L(RN+h−RN)−D(h,N,L)=RN+h+L−RN+L.

Both tails are nonnegative, so their difference is bounded in absolute value by the larger bound 𝐵 =𝑁 +ℎ +𝐿 +2B=N+h+L+2, not the sum of their bounds. The two strict inequalities in C(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿)C(h,N,L) keep the resulting error interval away from every multiple of 2𝐿2L; hence 𝑅𝑁+ℎ −𝑅𝑁 ∉ℤRN+h−RN∉Z. Conversely, a fixed noninteger difference has a fixed positive distance 𝛿δ from ℤZ. Since 2𝐿𝛿 >2𝐵2Lδ>2B eventually, some depth detects it. Completeness concerns detection of an existing difference, not existence of the differences required below (Erdos249257.TotientTailPeriodKiller.exists_certifiedKill_iff_tail_diff_notMem_int). The resulting quantified condition is the following.

Definition 1.2 (The quantified residue condition).

Sep:⟺∀ℎ≥1 ∀𝑁0 ∃𝑁≥𝑁0 ∃𝐿,C(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿).Sep:⟺∀h≥1 ∀N0 ∃N≥N0 ∃L,C(h,N,L).

Sep ⟺𝑆 ∉ℚSep⟺S∉Q (irrational_totient_series_iff_certificate_supply). The equivalence identifies the missing quantified input; it supplies no certificate by itself.

One successful (ℎ,𝑁,𝐿)(h,N,L) excludes reduced denominators dividing 2𝑁(2ℎ −1)2N(2h−1). To exclude an arbitrary rational value, its odd denominator first determines a shift and its power of two determines a threshold; only then may we choose the witness 𝑁,𝐿N,L. This is the order in SepSep. The depth must grow with 𝑁N, since success requires 2(𝑁 +ℎ +𝐿 +2) <2𝐿2(N+h+L+2)<2L. A large enough depth makes the interval test possible, but does not make the actual residue lie inside it. A failed test at one depth likewise does not prove integrality.
