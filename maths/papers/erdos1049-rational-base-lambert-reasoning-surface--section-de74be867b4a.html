Geometric Moments and Rational Lambert Values: Proofs and Further Results

Erdős #1049 By Will Cook September 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 79 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. Specialising Zudilin's 2004 linear forms shows F(a/b) irrational for coprime 𝑎 >𝑏 ≥1a>b≥1 when θ=log b / log a is less than θ^*=0.4056830213840605…, with irrationality-exponent bound (1−θ)/(θ*−θ) for every positive integral power. In particular the bound is less than 301 for every positive integral power of 31/431/4. The constants are Zudilin's; the proof cancels common cyclotomic factors before rational specialisation. For the 2016 Hankel construction with x=z=1, the first nonzero term of the normalised determinant is computed at every rank. A separate positive-measure argument proves positivity and a size estimate for each fixed real 0<q<1. The final sections explain the limits of the stated clearing and congruence arguments at 3/23/2: no small nonzero sequence of divided remainders is constructed there, and the full rational-base conjecture remains open.

Rescaling integer rows

An irrationality form needs integral coefficients and a nonzero remainder tending to zero. We may divide a row (𝑈,𝑉)(U,V) by gcd(|𝑈|,|𝑉|)gcd(|U|,|V|) to reduce its remainder while preserving integrality; a row with gcd 11 is called primitive. This one-form criterion requires no decay of height times error, although an irrationality measure does require height estimates. At a fixed rational base 𝑎/𝑏 >1a/b>1, the following elementary identities describe the effect of integer rescaling. For a real target 𝑆S, put

𝐿𝑆(𝑈,𝑉)=𝑈𝑆−𝑉,Δ((𝑈𝑛,𝑉𝑛),(𝑈𝑚,𝑉𝑚))=𝑈𝑛𝑉𝑚−𝑈𝑚𝑉𝑛.LS(U,V)=US−V,Δ((Un,Vn),(Um,Vm))=UnVm−UmVn.

We call 𝐿𝑆(𝑈,𝑉)LS(U,V) the error of the row at 𝑆S; a good approximation is one that makes it small. The second expression is their 2 ×22×2 determinant. It eliminates 𝑆S exactly:

Δ=𝑈𝑚𝐿𝑆(𝑈𝑛,𝑉𝑛)−𝑈𝑛𝐿𝑆(𝑈𝑚,𝑉𝑚).Δ=UmLS(Un,Vn)−UnLS(Um,Vm).

If this integer determinant does not vanish, then

1≤|Δ|≤|𝑈𝑚|| | |𝐿𝑆(𝑈𝑛,𝑉𝑛)| | |+|𝑈𝑛|| | |𝐿𝑆(𝑈𝑚,𝑉𝑚)| | |,1≤|Δ|≤|Um||LS(Un,Vn)|+|Un||LS(Um,Vm)|,

and the two errors cannot both be smaller than 1/(|𝑈𝑛| +|𝑈𝑚|)1/(|Un|+|Um|). The next theorem compares the divisor introduced by rescaling with the resulting change in the determinant’s absolute value.

Theorem 4.1 (rescaling rows and their determinant). Let 𝑆S be real, let (𝑈𝑛,𝑉𝑛)(Un,Vn) and (𝑈𝑚,𝑉𝑚)(Um,Vm) be pairs of integers, and let 𝑐𝑛,𝑐𝑚cn,cm be integers. Then

𝐿𝑆(𝑐𝑛𝑈𝑛,𝑐𝑛𝑉𝑛)=𝑐𝑛𝐿𝑆(𝑈𝑛,𝑉𝑛),LS(cnUn,cnVn)=cnLS(Un,Vn),
Δ(𝑐𝑛(𝑈𝑛,𝑉𝑛),𝑐𝑚(𝑈𝑚,𝑉𝑚))=𝑐𝑛𝑐𝑚Δ((𝑈𝑛,𝑉𝑛),(𝑈𝑚,𝑉𝑚)),Δ(cn(Un,Vn),cm(Um,Vm))=cncmΔ((Un,Vn),(Um,Vm)),

and consequently

|Δ(𝑐𝑛(𝑈𝑛,𝑉𝑛),𝑐𝑚(𝑈𝑚,𝑉𝑚))|=|𝑐𝑛||𝑐𝑚||Δ((𝑈𝑛,𝑉𝑛),(𝑈𝑚,𝑉𝑚))|.|Δ(cn(Un,Vn),cm(Um,Vm))|=|cn||cm||Δ((Un,Vn),(Um,Vm))|.

In particular 𝑐𝑛𝑐𝑚cncm divides the scaled determinant. Multiplication by these scalars therefore introduces a divisor whose absolute value is exactly the factor multiplying the determinant’s absolute value.

Proof. Expand the linear form and use the bilinearity of the determinant. The original integer determinant is the quotient witnessing divisibility; it need not be primitive. ◻

Example 4.2. Take (𝑈𝑛,𝑉𝑛) =(1,2)(Un,Vn)=(1,2) and (𝑈𝑚,𝑉𝑚) =(3,5)(Um,Vm)=(3,5), so that Δ =1 ⋅5 −3 ⋅2 = −1Δ=1⋅5−3⋅2=−1. Multiplying the first row by 𝑐𝑛 =6cn=6 and the second by 𝑐𝑚 =10cm=10 gives the rows (6,12)(6,12) and (30,50)(30,50), whose determinant is 6 ⋅50 −30 ⋅12 = −606⋅50−30⋅12=−60. That determinant is now divisible by 6060, which looks like a local gain of 6060; and its absolute value has risen from 11 to 6060, which is a cost of exactly the same size.

Lean checks the error identity in error scaling, the determinant identity in content factorisation, the exact absolute-height identity in absolute determinant scaling, the divisor statement in content-product divisibility, and the elimination identity in exterior determinant identity.

The identities allow zero scalars, but cancelling the scalar factors requires them to be nonzero. Multiplication alone gives no improvement in the comparison between a divisor and the determinant’s absolute value. The theorem neither asserts that the determinant is nonzero nor estimates the original approximation error. Cancelling polynomial factors before evaluation, finding factors common to several minors, and adding different rows remain separate operations.
