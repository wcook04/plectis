Paths in Polynomial Lemniscates: A Degree-Seven Counterexample and Radial Connections

Erdős #1041 By Will Cook 29 September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 86 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For a monic trinomial with roots in the open unit disc, any two distinct roots are joined inside {|f|<1} by a path of length less than 2. For any squarefree monic polynomial of degree 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, an area-growth argument gives such a path when the least critical-value modulus μ is at most 13/2513/25; scaling gives length less than (5/2)𝜇1/𝑛(5/2)μ1/n inside {|𝑓| <(25/13)𝜇}{|f|<(25/13)μ}. The record develops this argument, an inverse-ray averaging estimate, and a construction using an isolated simple critical value, together with results for collinear roots and several coefficient families. A weighted Poisson identity gives a sharp mean bound on critical values; examples distinguish that bound from path-length control. The final sections examine inverse-sheet topology and refute an earlier spanning-tree estimate. These results do not prove the unrestricted length-2 assertion.

Collinear roots and Chebyshev comparison

We scale the extreme zeros of 𝑇𝑛Tn to ±1±1 and make the polynomial monic. Its endpoint zeros then agree with those of the polynomial under study, while subtraction lowers the degree. This is the comparison needed for the alternation argument. Put

This theorem is the finite alternation inequality; it does not construct the segment between two roots.

All real-rooted polynomials, and their images under rotations and translations, satisfy the collinearity hypothesis. Three noncollinear roots already fall outside it. The gain is an explicit sharp bound for the modulus along one adjacent-root segment, not a bound obtained by assuming that segment is contained.

Both 𝑞q and 𝑞∗q∗ vanish at ±1±1, and |𝑞∗| ≤𝐶𝑛|q∗|≤Cn on [ −1,1][−1,1]. For each gap [𝑦𝑖,𝑦𝑖+1][yi,yi+1], choose 𝑐𝑖ci at which |𝑞||q| is maximal. Critical sequences and the extremal configuration.

For distinct real zeros, each gap between consecutive zeros contains exactly one zero of 𝑞′q′, and |𝑞||q| attains its maximum over the gap only there. The points 𝑐𝑖ci chosen in the proof of Theorem 9.2 are therefore the critical points of 𝑞q, and the gap maxima are the moduli of its ordered critical sequence (𝑞(𝑐1),…,𝑞(𝑐𝑛−1))(q(c1),…,q(cn−1)), with 𝑐1 <⋯ <𝑐𝑛−1c1<⋯<cn−1. In this distinct-root setting, the ordered sequence determines the real polynomial up to an increasing real affine change of variable [17]. The proof above uses only the alternation count, not this classification theorem. In this language, (9) says that a real polynomial of degree 𝑛n with 𝑛n distinct real zeros, leading coefficient 𝑎a and zero diameter 𝐷D has a critical value of modulus at most 𝐶𝑛|𝑎|(𝐷/2)𝑛Cn|a|(D/2)n.

The comparison polynomial 𝑞∗q∗ is the monic polynomial of least deviation from zero on [ −1/𝑟𝑛,1/𝑟𝑛][−1/rn,1/rn], characterised by the equioscillation conditions recalled in [17]. All 𝑛 −1n−1 of its critical values have modulus 𝐶𝑛Cn, so every critical point of 𝑞∗q∗ lies on the level curve {|𝑞∗| =𝐶𝑛}{|q∗|=Cn}. Extremals with this property also occur in the level-curve length problem, where some extremal polynomial has all its critical points on {|𝑝| =1}{|p|=1} [14], and in the sharp bound for |𝑓′||f′| on a connected sublevel set {|𝑓| ≤1}{|f|≤1} of a monic polynomial, proved by Eremenko and Lempert [18], whose equality cases are 𝑒−𝑖𝑛𝜃𝑇𝑛(21/𝑛−1𝑒𝑖𝜃𝑧 +𝑏)e−inθTn(21/n−1eiθz+b) with 𝜃θ real [19].

Scope.

The sharpness assertion concerns the maximum of |𝑓||f| on the selected adjacent-root segment, and the displayed configurations attain it; no claim is made that they exhaust the equality cases, and no claim is made that the selected segment is a shortest path in {|𝑓| <1}{|f|<1}.
