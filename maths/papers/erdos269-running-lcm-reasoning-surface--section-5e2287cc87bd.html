Running least common multiples: distinct values and multiplicities

Erdős #269 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 5 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 73 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The reciprocal three-prime running-LCM kernel has nonsingular minors of every order: row and column rescaling leaves a two-valued matrix, and density supplies the required threshold pattern. The record computes the rank of every finite restriction and the least uniform approximation error over all matrices of finite separated rank. For the repeated {2,3,5} sum it derives an integer-coefficient tail recurrence and a residue criterion equivalent to irrationality. The unproved step is to find such windows for every positive multiplier coprime to 30 and after every prescribed start. It also gives the full two-prime comparison, following Fan's earlier Hecke–Mahler reduction, and identifies the boundary terms that survive a proposed finite-difference cancellation.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #269, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #269, which remains open.

Why a finite alphabet of bases is not enough.

Kebis, Luca, Ouaknine, Scoones and Worrell [27] work with fixed-base series whose coefficients lie in a finite algebraic alphabet. Their echoing condition requires long near-repetitions, separated intervals containing the mismatches, and nonzero weighted mismatch sums on at least two of those intervals. Claim 7 supplies the non-cancellation step in the proof of the value theorem. Merely having finitely many letters does not give these properties. The four-letter radix sequence is not the numerator sequence: the actual 𝑚𝑎ma is unbounded. A recoding would have to preserve the scalar value, identify its coefficient alphabet and verify the echoing conditions. No fixed-base recoding with verified echoing properties is proved here.

Two natural bases expose the tradeoff. The least integer base whose powers clear every 𝑃𝑛Pn is 3030, while base 88 matches the growth of 𝑃𝑛 =H⁡(2𝑛)Pn=H⁡(2n). The former keeps integrality; the latter keeps polynomial size.

Proposition 12.4 (what direct fixed-base recoding preserves). The following identities converge absolutely:

𝛼=∑𝑎≥0𝑒𝑎30𝑎+1=∑𝑎≥0𝑣𝑎8𝑎+1,𝑒𝑎=𝑚𝑎30𝑎+1𝑃𝑎+1,𝑣𝑎=𝑚𝑎8𝑎+1𝑃𝑎+1.α=∑a≥0ea30a+1=∑a≥0va8a+1,ea=ma30a+1Pa+1,va=ma8a+1Pa+1.

Here 𝑒𝑎 ∈ℤ>0ea∈Z>0 and 𝑒𝑎 ≥(15/4)𝑎+1ea≥(15/4)a+1, whereas 𝑣𝑎 ∈ℤ[1/15]va∈Z[1/15] and 0 <𝑣𝑎 <225(𝑎 +1)20<va<225(a+1)2. For an integer 𝑞 ≥2q≥2, the termwise divisibility 𝑃𝑛 ∣𝑞𝑛Pn∣qn for every 𝑛 ≥1n≥1 holds exactly when 30 ∣𝑞30∣q; that direct recoding then has coefficients at least (𝑞/8)𝑎+1(q/8)a+1.

Proof. Each exponent in 𝑃𝑛Pn is at most 𝑛n, so 𝑃𝑛 ∣30𝑛Pn∣30n. The estimate 8𝑛/15 <𝑃𝑛 ≤8𝑛8n/15<Pn≤8n was proved in Section 8. These facts, 𝑚𝑎 ≥1ma≥1 and 𝑚𝑎 ≤15(𝑎 +1)2ma≤15(a+1)2, give all the coefficient bounds and identities. Necessity of 30 ∣𝑞30∣q follows because each of 2,3,52,3,5 divides some 𝑃𝑛Pn; sufficiency follows from 𝑃𝑛 ∣30𝑛Pn∣30n. The general lower bound follows again from 𝑃𝑛 ≤8𝑛Pn≤8n. ◻

The base-3030 coefficients are integral but grow exponentially, so the cited polynomial-growth criterion does not apply. The base-88 coefficients have polynomial size but are not all integral: already 𝑣1 =4 ⋅82/12 =64/3v1=4⋅82/12=64/3. In fact, their reduced denominators grow exponentially even after cancellation. Since 𝑃𝑎+1/2𝑎+1Pa+1/2a+1 is odd, the expression

𝑣𝑎=𝑚𝑎4𝑎+1𝑃𝑎+1/2𝑎+1va=ma4a+1Pa+1/2a+1

can cancel an odd factor only through 𝑚𝑎ma. Consequently,

den⁡(𝑣𝑎)≥𝑃𝑎+12𝑎+1𝑚𝑎>4𝑎+1225(𝑎+1)2.den⁡(va)≥Pa+12a+1ma>4a+1225(a+1)2.

The last inequality uses 𝑃𝑎+1 >8𝑎+1/15Pa+1>8a+1/15 and 𝑚𝑎 ≤15(𝑎 +1)2ma≤15(a+1)2. Thus the bound on absolute value does not control Diophantine height even for these particular coefficients; fixed denominator-prime support is not enough.

More generally, no fixed integer base gives this termwise recoding both eventually integral coefficients and polynomial growth. If 30 ∣𝑞30∣q, the proposition gives exponential growth. Otherwise choose 𝑝 ∈{2,3,5}p∈{2,3,5} not dividing 𝑞q. Cancellation by 𝑚𝑎ma removes at most a factor 𝑚𝑎ma, so

den(𝑚𝑎𝑞𝑎+1𝑃𝑎+1)≥𝑝⌊(𝑎+1)log𝑝⁡2⌋𝑚𝑎>2𝑎+115𝑝(𝑎+1)2.den(maqa+1Pa+1)≥p⌊(a+1)logp⁡2⌋ma>2a+115p(a+1)2.

This denominator tends to infinity. Thus matching the denominator growth by base 88 keeps the coefficients small but introduces large rational denominators; making the coefficients integral by base 3030 makes them exponentially large. This obstruction concerns termwise rescaling only, not regrouping or carrying.

Carrying resolves a different problem: it bounds the digits but leaves the radices variable. It also requires the integer parts of the actual tails, not just the known block numerators. Set

𝛿𝑎=𝑚𝑎+⌊𝑋𝑎+1⌋−𝑏𝑎⌊𝑋𝑎⌋=⌊𝑏𝑎{𝑋𝑎}⌋.δa=ma+⌊Xa+1⌋−ba⌊Xa⌋=⌊ba{Xa}⌋.

The equality follows from the tail recurrence and 𝑚𝑎 ∈ℤma∈Z. Thus 0 ≤𝛿𝑎 <𝑏𝑎0≤δa<ba and {𝑋𝑎} =(𝛿𝑎 +{𝑋𝑎+1})/𝑏𝑎{Xa}=(δa+{Xa+1})/ba. Iterating gives the finite identity

{𝑋0}=𝑁−1∑𝑎=0𝛿𝑎𝑃𝑎+1+{𝑋𝑁}𝑃𝑁.{X0}=∑a=0N−1δaPa+1+{XN}PN.

The last term is nonnegative, is less than 1/𝑃𝑁 ≤2−𝑁1/PN≤2−N, and tends to zero. This is the greedy mixed-radix expansion of the fractional part of 𝑋0 =𝑆/2X0=S/2, with digits in {0,…,29}{0,…,29}. For the actual series its first eight digits are

(𝛿0,…,𝛿7)=(1,4,8,3,10,1,8,5).(δ0,…,δ7)=(1,4,8,3,10,1,8,5).

To certify them, use the twelve-block enclosure from Section 11, either propagated by the recurrence or evaluated directly as

𝑃𝑎11∑𝑗=𝑎𝑚𝑗𝑃𝑗+1<𝑋𝑎≤𝑃𝑎11∑𝑗=𝑎𝑚𝑗𝑃𝑗+1+𝑃𝑎𝑃12𝑄(𝑛12)(0≤𝑎<12).Pa∑j=a11mjPj+1<Xa≤Pa∑j=a11mjPj+1+PaP12Q(n12)(0≤a<12).

Both descriptions give the same rational endpoints. At indices 00 through 88, each interval lies strictly between consecutive integers, certifying all nine floors needed for the eight digits. Width alone would not suffice: an interval crossing an integer leaves its floor undetermined. For example, ⌊𝑋4⌋ =2⌊X4⌋=2 and ⌊𝑋5⌋ =5⌊X5⌋=5, giving 𝛿4 =65 +5 −30 ⋅2 =10δ4=65+5−30⋅2=10, not the uncarried numerator 𝑚4 =65m4=65. This finite calculation makes no claim about the later digit complexity.

A rational number need not have eventually periodic digits when the radices vary. For example, take radix 55 at the indices 1,2,4,8,…1,2,4,8,… and radix 33 elsewhere. The constant tail fraction 1/21/2 gives digit 22 at those indices and 11 elsewhere. These digits are not eventually periodic, but their mixed-radix expansion sums to 1/21/2. This example uses different radices from our four possible bases; it shows why fixed-base eventual periodicity cannot be transferred to a variable-radix expansion. Our digits also depend on the actual tail fractions, not just on the four-letter radix sequence. The construction supplies neither a fixed-base expansion with verified echoing properties nor a bound on its digit complexity.

Adamczewski–Bugeaud’s complexity theorem [30] concerns the actual digits of an integer-base expansion: for an algebraic irrational their length-𝑛n block complexity divided by 𝑛n tends to infinity. Neither (𝑏𝑎)(ba) nor the uncarried (𝑒𝑎)(ea) is such a digit expansion of 𝛼α.

Problem 12.5 (the repeated three-prime target). Prove irrationality of 𝑆 =R{2,3,5}S=R{2,3,5}. Equivalent forms are 𝖤(𝐾)E(K) and

𝐵𝑋𝑎∉ℤfor every 𝐵≥1 with gcd(𝐵,30)=1 and every 𝑎≥1.(22)(22)BXa∉Zfor every B≥1 with gcd(B,30)=1 and every a≥1.

The residue formulation uses finite integer computations, but asks for a success for every eligible denominator and beyond every prescribed start. The tail formulation requires that no positive integer multiplier coprime to 3030 make any 𝑋𝑎Xa, 𝑎 ≥1a≥1, integral. These are reformulations of the same irrationality assertion, not extra assumptions on the series, and neither follows from the rank obstruction. The next proposition proves the tail reformulation, complementing Theorem 10.2.

Proposition 12.6 (irrationality is equivalent to nonintegrality of every reduced tail). Statement (22), quantified over every 𝐵 ≥1B≥1 coprime to 3030 and every 𝑎 ≥1a≥1, is equivalent to irrationality of 𝑆S.

Proof. If 𝐵𝑋𝑎 ∈ℤBXa∈Z for some such 𝐵B and 𝑎a, then 𝑋𝑎 ∈ℚXa∈Q, and since 𝑆 =∑𝑗<𝑎𝑠𝑗 +𝑋𝑎/ℎ𝑎S=∑j<asj+Xa/ha with ℎ𝑎ha a positive rational and the prefix a finite sum of rationals, 𝑆 ∈ℚS∈Q. Conversely if 𝑆 ∈ℚS∈Q, then Theorem 9.3 produces 𝐵B coprime to 3030 with 𝐵𝑋𝑎 ∈ℤBXa∈Z for every 𝑎 ≥𝑎𝐷a≥aD. ◻

Integrality at one index propagates forwards, but nonintegrality at one early index does not propagate forever: a rational denominator can clear later. Proposition 9.4 describes that onset exactly. The dichotomy in Proposition 8.4 also permits integral tails, so it does not settle this question.
