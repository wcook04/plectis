Factorial Linear Forms and Denominators: Detailed Proofs and Rationality Criteria

Erdős #68 By Will Cook 1 September 2026; revised 7 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 51 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The record proves liminf (log LN)/(N3/2 log N) ≥2√2/3≥22/3 for L_N=lcm(2!−1,…,N!−1), by comparing a terminal block of denominators with pairwise gcds rather than an external multiplicity theorem. Specialising a Louwsma–Martino valuation, it exhibits primes 139 and 2593 absent from all later reduced denominators. Coefficient constructions give integer forms MS+k; at fixed moment the remainder changes only by an integer. Rationality criteria use factorial digits and the next integer above a scaled partial sum; the digit argument also recovers the irrationality of e. Two exact calculations give q∤299999! and 𝑞 ≥239990 >1012038q≥239990>1012038. These exclusions do not prove irrationality.

Growth of the common denominator

A large product of denominators gives a large least common multiple only if their overlap is controlled. We use the last 𝑘k denominators: their logarithmic product contributes about 𝑘𝑁log⁡𝑁kNlog⁡N, while the pairwise-gcd estimate costs about (𝑘3/6)log⁡𝑁(k3/6)log⁡N. A non-polynomial comparison.

For 𝑛! +2𝑛 −1n!+2n−1, Luca and Shparlinski [19] eliminate the exponential term using three indices satisfying the congruence; multiplicative order enters the counting. Their valuation-layer identity [19] records repeated prime-power divisibility. These are related tools, not inputs to Theorem 3.4, and no extension to general non-polynomial perturbations is asserted.

Within the terminal-block estimate just proved, maximising 𝛼 −𝛼3/6α−α3/6 gives 2√2/322/3. This is an optimisation of that one-parameter bound, not an optimality theorem for all block selections or all gcd arguments. The discarded factorial term has size 𝑁3/2N3/2, so a finite normalised ratio need not be close to the limiting lower bound.

Theorem 3.4 concerns 𝐿𝑁LN, not den⁡(𝐻𝑁)den⁡(HN). Theorem 2.1 and the cancellations at 139139 and 25932593 show why these cannot be interchanged: a prime present in the common denominator may be absent after reduction. The two examples establish this possibility, not an asymptotic estimate for cancellation.

Even two consecutive denominators obstruct clearing by the full lcm. For 𝑁 ≥3N≥3, the gcd of (𝑁 −1)! −1(N−1)!−1 and 𝑁! −1N!−1 divides 𝑁 −1N−1, whereas (𝑁 −1)! −1(N−1)!−1 is coprime to 𝑁 −1N−1. Thus those denominators are coprime, and

𝐿𝑁(𝑆−𝐻𝑁)>((𝑁−1)!−1)(𝑁!−1)(𝑁+1)!−1⟶∞.LN(S−HN)>((N−1)!−1)(N!−1)(N+1)!−1⟶∞.

This argument does not use the stronger asymptotic growth theorem.

Reduction could still help, but only in the opposite direction: a clearing argument needs a sufficiently small reduced denominator. Write 𝐻𝑁 =ˆ𝐴𝑁/𝑄𝑁HN=A^N/QN in lowest terms, reserving 𝐴𝑁AN for the numerator over the full lcm. Under 𝑆 =𝑎/𝑞S=a/q, with 𝑎 ∈ℤa∈Z and 𝑞 ≥1q≥1, positivity gives

𝑞𝑄𝑁(𝑆−𝐻𝑁)=𝑎𝑄𝑁−𝑞ˆ𝐴𝑁∈ℤ>0.qQN(S−HN)=aQN−qA^N∈Z>0.

A contradiction requires 𝑞𝑄𝑁(𝑆 −𝐻𝑁) <1qQN(S−HN)<1 at some 𝑁N. A lower bound on 𝑄𝑁QN cannot supply this upper estimate. For comparison, the rational limit 11 and the approximants 1 −1/𝑄𝑁1−1/QN have arbitrarily large reduced denominators without any irrationality. The inequality 𝑑𝑁(𝑆 −𝐻𝑁) <1dN(S−HN)<1 alone would not repair the argument, because 𝑑𝑁dN generally does not clear the earlier summands of 𝐻𝑁HN. The prime-power analysis describes which factors survive reduction, but it does not yet supply the required upper control on 𝑄𝑁QN.

For linear forms in 𝑝p-adic zeta values, with 𝑝 ≥5p≥5 a fixed prime and 𝑛n the index of the form, Lai, Lupu and Sprang quantify a saving of this kind. They bound the denominators of the coefficients by powers of lcm⁡(1,…,𝑛)lcm⁡(1,…,n), show that the bound may be divided by a product Φ𝑛Φn of prime powers, and compute the growth rate of Φ𝑛Φn [15]. That saving enters the inequality [15] under which the rescaled forms, which have integer coefficients, meet the irrationality criterion [15] that they quote from Lai [16]. Along one unbounded subsequence, the forms must be nonzero and their 𝑝p-adic absolute values, multiplied by the largest ordinary absolute value of an integer coefficient, must tend to zero. The coefficient bound is archimedean; the bound on the value is 𝑝p-adic. The conclusion is that at least one of the finitely many 𝑝p-adic numbers in the forms is irrational, not that each is irrational. The corresponding saving for 𝐻𝑁HN is the ratio 𝐿𝑁/𝑄𝑁LN/QN.

This ratio is an integer. Merely naming it supplies neither its growth nor a small linear form; the analogy is about what an eventual estimate would have to accomplish. Theorem 2.1 decides, for each prime 𝑝 ∣𝐿𝑁p∣LN, whether 𝑝p divides this ratio; no asymptotic lower bound for the ratio is established here. This paragraph compares the two methods; no 𝑝p-adic theorem is applied to 𝑆S.
