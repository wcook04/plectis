Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 61 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. For rational reciprocal sums under 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, the record also develops sufficient conditions for the eventual recurrence 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1, controlling either increases of an integer tail numerator or a convergent sum at steps setting new maxima. Examples separate numerical growth bounds from the exact recurrences. The cubic proof is distinguished from the theorems formalised in Lean. None of the additional bounds is derived for every rational reciprocal sum in Erdős #243.

Irrationality at the cubic rate

A sufficiently precise rate constrains an integer tail more strongly than the quadratic limit alone. At rate 1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)1+3/n+o(n−3), its numerator must eventually be cubic. The denominator recurrence will exclude every possible cubic. For the prefix product 𝑃𝑛 =∏1≤𝑘<𝑛𝑎𝑘Pn=∏1≤k<nak, the increments of 𝑃𝑛/𝑎𝑛Pn/an grow like a positive multiple of 𝑛2n2, so the bounded-increase criterion later in the paper does not apply. The eventual Sylvester recurrence gives deviation 𝑂(1/𝑎𝑛)O(1/an) and is also excluded by this rate.

Theorem 2.1 (cubic-rate irrationality). If strictly increasing positive integers satisfy 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3), then ∑𝑛1/𝑎𝑛∑n1/an is irrational.

The same extraction argument rules out nonintegral regular rates above one. These require no number-field calculation.

Theorem 2.2 (nonintegral regular-rate irrationality). Let 𝜆 >1λ>1 be a real number that is not an integer. If strictly increasing positive integers satisfy 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +𝜆/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−𝜆)an2/an+1=1+λ/n+o(n−λ), then ∑𝑛1/𝑎𝑛∑n1/an is irrational.

The zero-indexed Lean theorem also derives convergence of the reciprocal series from the rate.

The rates retain their stated indices throughout. Appendix A gives the finite-prefix construction relating these one-based statements to the zero-based formal statements without changing the rate’s error term.
