Running least common multiples: distinct values and multiplicities

Erdős #269 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 5 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 73 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The reciprocal three-prime running-LCM kernel has nonsingular minors of every order: row and column rescaling leaves a two-valued matrix, and density supplies the required threshold pattern. The record computes the rank of every finite restriction and the least uniform approximation error over all matrices of finite separated rank. For the repeated {2,3,5} sum it derives an integer-coefficient tail recurrence and a residue criterion equivalent to irrationality. The unproved step is to find such windows for every positive multiplier coprime to 30 and after every prescribed start. It also gives the full two-prime comparison, following Fan's earlier Hecke–Mahler reduction, and identifies the boundary terms that survive a proposed finite-difference cancellation.

This paper owns the complete problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #269, including all registered result families and their boundaries.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #269, which remains open.

Certificates for three and four primes

The saved computations apply the interval criterion in exact rational arithmetic to the following finite family. They accompany the ordinary proof of Remark 5.7 and have not been formalised. The exclusions in part (ii) concern these interval calculations; the ordinary torus argument also treats those sets.

Remark 5.8 (three and four primes up to 3131). Let 𝑃P be a set of primes, each at most 3131.

  1. If |𝑃| =3|P|=3, then D𝑃DP is irrational.

  2. If |𝑃| =4|P|=4 and 𝑃P is not one of the 3838 sets listed below, then D𝑃DP is irrational.

Proof. For each set, the program certificate.py of the programs for sums over distinct values builds the automaton, computes intervals 𝐽(𝑠)J(s) by 8080 rounds of the operation above from [0,1/(𝑝 −1)][0,1/(p−1)], with endpoints rounded outwards to multiples of 2−962−96, and tests the disjointness of the sets 𝑈𝜏Uτ for 𝑘 =1,2,…k=1,2,…. Every comparison is made in exact rational arithmetic, and each 𝑈𝜏Uτ is computed as a union of intervals merged where they meet. The test passes for each of the 165165 sets of three primes with some 𝑘 ≤4k≤4, and for each set of four primes outside the list with some 𝑘 ≤5k≤5. Remark 5.7 then gives irrationality. ◻

A second computation replaces the rounded intervals by the exact extremes [𝐿(𝑠),𝑈(𝑠)][L(s),U(s)], found by policy iteration and checked by substitution into their defining equations. It returns the same least depth for all 495495 sets. A third check compares the automaton with the first 400400 actual blocks of every set: every actual word is a path, and every exact enclosure of an actual tail lies in the interval of its state. This comparison is a consistency check only. The whole triple computation takes about three seconds, and the four-prime computation about seventy seconds, on one laptop; the saved logs record every depth and gap.

Hence the sets 𝑈(5,7,3)U(5,7,3) and 𝑈(7)U(7) of Remark 5.7 meet at every depth, for any valid choice of intervals, and the criterion cannot decide {2,3,5,7}{2,3,5,7} with this automaton. Both four-block words occur in the actual block sequence, 1212 and 4040 times among the first 2000020000 blocks. Within this automaton, a tail value therefore need not determine its first block. The identity does not exhibit equal tails at two positions of the actual prime-power sequence: those tails have different later phases. It proves failure of this interval criterion. The proof of Remark 5.1 uses the isolated reversal of two actual jumps instead.
