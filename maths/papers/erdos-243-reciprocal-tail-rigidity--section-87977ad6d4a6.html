Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 21 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. For strictly increasing positive integers with 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3), the reciprocal sum is irrational. The argument extracts a cubic polynomial from an integer tail numerator, restricts its coefficients using a square in a cubic field, and excludes the remaining cases modulo seven. The unrestricted Erdős–Graham question remains open.

This paper owns the cubic-rate irrationality theorem and its proof; the long paper develops the separate Sylvester-tail criteria and broader recurrence questions.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #243, which remains open.

Zero errors and descent

𝐶𝑛+1=𝐶𝑛−𝐸𝑛,(𝑎𝑛+1−𝑎2𝑛+𝑎𝑛−1)𝐶𝑛+1=𝑎2𝑛𝐸𝑛−𝐸𝑛+1.Cn+1=Cn−En,(an+1−an2+an−1)Cn+1=an2En−En+1.

Equation (2) now gives 𝐸𝑛/𝐶𝑛 =1 −𝐶𝑛+1/𝐶𝑛 →0En/Cn=1−Cn+1/Cn→0, so |𝐸𝑛| <𝐶𝑛|En|<Cn for all sufficiently large 𝑛n. Koizumi proves this relative-gap limit for an eventual pseudo-greedy tail [10]. His rounding convention also gives −𝐶𝑛/2 ≤𝐸𝑛 <𝐶𝑛/2−Cn/2≤En<Cn/2, a stronger bound than we need.

Koizumi’s Lemma 3 gives zero absorption for pseudo-greedy tails, and Proposition 1(2) uses descent when their gaps are eventually nonnegative [10]. The following form separates what the two arguments require.

Theorem 4.3 (absorption and descent). Let 𝑎,𝐶,𝐷 :ℕ →ℕa,C,D:N→N satisfy 𝑎𝑛 >1an>1, 𝐶𝑛 >0Cn>0 and (1). If |𝐸𝑛| <𝐶𝑛|En|<Cn for every 𝑛n, then 𝐸𝑛 =0En=0 implies 𝐸𝑛+1 =0En+1=0. For any positive integer sequence 𝐶𝑛Cn with 𝐶𝑛+1 =𝐶𝑛 −𝐸𝑛Cn+1=Cn−En, eventual nonnegativity of 𝐸𝑛En implies its eventual vanishing.

Proof. When 𝐸𝑛 =0En=0, the second identity in Proposition 4.2 shows that 𝐶𝑛+1Cn+1 divides 𝐸𝑛+1En+1. The inequality |𝐸𝑛+1| <𝐶𝑛+1|En+1|<Cn+1 then forces 𝐸𝑛+1 =0En+1=0. For the second assertion, 𝐶𝑛+1 =𝐶𝑛 −𝐸𝑛Cn+1=Cn−En makes the positive integer sequence 𝐶𝑛Cn eventually nonincreasing, hence constant. ◻

Corollary 4.4 (two zero errors). For integer sequences satisfying (1), if 𝐸𝑛 =𝐸𝑛+1 =0En=En+1=0 and 𝐶𝑛+1 ≠0Cn+1≠0, then 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1. In particular, if 𝑎,𝐶,𝐷 :ℕ →ℕa,C,D:N→N also satisfy 𝑎𝑛 >1an>1 and 𝐶𝑛 >0Cn>0, eventual zero error implies the eventual Sylvester recurrence.

Proof. Cancel the nonzero factor 𝐶𝑛+1Cn+1 in the second error identity. ◻
