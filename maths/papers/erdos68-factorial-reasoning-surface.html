Précis. The record proves liminf (log LN)/(N3/2 log N) ≥2√2/3≥22/3 for L_N=lcm(2!−1,…,N!−1), by comparing a terminal block of denominators with pairwise gcds rather than an external multiplicity theorem. Specialising a Louwsma–Martino valuation, it exhibits primes 139 and 2593 absent from all later reduced denominators. Coefficient constructions give integer forms MS+k; at fixed moment the remainder changes only by an integer. Rationality criteria use factorial digits and the next integer above a scaled partial sum; the digit argument also recovers the irrationality of e. Two exact calculations give q∤299999! and 𝑞 ≥239990 >1012038q≥239990>1012038. These exclusions do not prove irrationality.

Problem 0.1 (Erdős #68). Is 𝑆 =∑𝑛≥2(𝑛! −1)−1S=∑n≥2(n!−1)−1 irrational?

Erdős posed this question in 1988, together with the expectation that the corresponding series with denominators 𝑛! +𝑡n!+t should be transcendental for every integer 𝑡t [1]. For negative 𝑡t, the sum begins after all zero or negative denominators. Any further change of starting index adds a rational number. The expectation for all integer shifts remains unproved here. The problem number follows Bloom’s catalogue [2].

For finitely supported integer vectors on 𝑛 ≥2n≥2, cancellation forms 𝑀𝑆 +𝑘MS+k give an explicitly computable ideal of multipliers. Correcting adjacent factorial differences at proper divisors gives an integral basis. Cancellation fixes its lower coordinates; excluding index one leaves a single Diophantine equation. Its gcd, computed below 2𝐷22D2, gives the least positive multiplier and an attaining vector. At depth four, the support restriction strengthens 115 ∣𝑀115∣M to 1380 ∣𝑀1380∣M. This classification is independent of nonintegrality.

The accompanying short paper proves this classification and bounds one resulting remainder between consecutive integers. Here we develop the support and coefficient-norm refinements and a primitive construction on arithmetic progressions. These solve different problems: at fixed 𝑀M, changing the vector changes 𝑀𝑆 +𝑘MS+k only by an integer, so it cannot alter nonintegrality. The progression construction makes 𝑀M divisible by every fixed denominator eventually, but still needs a gap estimate for those same forms.

The later sections study that arithmetic difficulty from two directions. Common-denominator growth measures the cost of clearing terms; prime-power calculations determine what survives reduction. Carries and factorial digits instead characterize rationality itself. The resulting equivalences identify what an irrationality proof must show, without proving that the required departures occur arbitrarily far out.

Thus 𝐻𝑁 =𝐴𝑁/𝐿𝑁HN=AN/LN. We write den⁡(𝑥)den⁡(x) for the positive reduced denominator of a rational number 𝑥x. Under 𝑆 =𝑎/𝑞S=a/q, every form 𝑀𝑆 +𝑘MS+k with 𝑀,𝑘 ∈ℤM,k∈Z and 𝑞 ∣𝑀q∣M is integral. For each positive integer 𝑞q, an irrationality argument of this kind must produce a nonintegral form with 𝑞 ∣𝑀q∣M. Both properties must hold for the same vector; cancellation and growth of 𝑀M alone do not establish this.
