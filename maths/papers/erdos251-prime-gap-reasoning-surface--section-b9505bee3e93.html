Prime-Gap Dyadic Series: Perturbations, Exact Criteria and Certificates

Erdős #251 By Will Cook September 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 47 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. A convergent dyadic series with nonnegative integer coefficients can be made rational by sparse nonnegative corrections of upper Banach density zero, eventually bounded by any f(n)→∞, while preserving every fixed modulus and, for a logarithmic allowance, short-block empirical distributions. For prime gaps the cumulative positions remain asymptotic to n log n; no primality conclusion is asserted. Summation by parts reduces the prime series to the gap series, and the tails 𝑇𝑁 =∑𝑗≥1𝑔𝑁+𝑗2−𝑗TN=∑j≥1gN+j2−j satisfy 𝑇𝑁+1 =2𝑇𝑁 −𝑔𝑁+1TN+1=2TN−gN+1. That recurrence characterises rationality and yields finite tests with explicit remainder bounds. An exact finite certificate shows that a rational value of the prime series would have denominator at least 239998 >1012040239998>1012040. The bound does not establish irrationality.

The finite truncation criterion

For the actual gaps, we enclose each complete weighted difference by a finite sum and a bound on the omitted terms. A nonnegative summable majorant gives such an enclosure, but need not provide a computable remainder. Proposition 6.5 supplies the rational bound used in the finite tests.

Proposition D.3 (finite truncation). Let 𝑀 :ℕ →ℝM:N→R satisfy 𝑀(𝑛) ≥𝑔𝑛M(n)≥gn for every 𝑛n and ∑𝑛≥0𝑀(𝑛)2−𝑛 <∞∑n≥0M(n)2−n<∞, and put

𝑆ℎ,𝑁,𝐿=𝐿∑𝑗=1𝑔𝑁+ℎ+𝑗−𝑔𝑁+𝑗2𝑗,𝑅ℎ,𝑁,𝐿(𝑀)=∑𝑗>𝐿𝑀(𝑁+ℎ+𝑗)+𝑀(𝑁+𝑗)2𝑗.Sh,N,L=∑j=1LgN+h+j−gN+j2j,Rh,N,L(M)=∑j>LM(N+h+j)+M(N+j)2j.

If for every ℎ ≥1h≥1 and every 𝑁0N0 there are 𝑁 ≥𝑁0N≥N0 and 𝐿 ≥1L≥1 with dist⁡(𝑆ℎ,𝑁,𝐿,ℤ) >𝑅ℎ,𝑁,𝐿(𝑀)dist⁡(Sh,N,L,Z)>Rh,N,L(M), then the nonintegrality condition in Problem 10.1 holds.

Proof. The part of ∑𝑗≥1(𝑔𝑁+ℎ+𝑗 −𝑔𝑁+𝑗)2−𝑗∑j≥1(gN+h+j−gN+j)2−j omitted from 𝑆ℎ,𝑁,𝐿Sh,N,L has absolute value at most 𝑅ℎ,𝑁,𝐿(𝑀)Rh,N,L(M), so under the displayed inequality the full sum lies at positive distance from every integer. ◻

With 𝑀(𝑛) =1250(𝑛 +2)4M(n)=1250(n+2)4, the quantities 𝑆ℎ,𝑁,𝐿Sh,N,L and 𝑅ℎ,𝑁,𝐿(𝑀)Rh,N,L(M) are exactly 𝐹ℎ,𝑁,𝐿Fh,N,L and 𝐸ℎ,𝑁,𝐿Eh,N,L from Proposition 6.5. The same enclosure therefore tests separation from the integers, without requiring either of the two consecutive differences to be small. To compute the distance from the truncated sum to ℤZ, write the weighted block sum as

𝐷ℎ,𝑁,𝐿=𝐿∑𝑗=12𝐿−𝑗(𝑔𝑁+ℎ+𝑗−𝑔𝑁+𝑗),𝑆ℎ,𝑁,𝐿=𝐷ℎ,𝑁,𝐿2𝐿.Dh,N,L=∑j=1L2L−j(gN+h+j−gN+j),Sh,N,L=Dh,N,L2L.

Then

dist⁡(𝑆ℎ,𝑁,𝐿,ℤ)=2−𝐿min{𝐷ℎ,𝑁,𝐿mod2𝐿,2𝐿−(𝐷ℎ,𝑁,𝐿mod2𝐿)},dist⁡(Sh,N,L,Z)=2−Lmin{Dh,N,Lmod2L,2L−(Dh,N,Lmod2L)},

where 𝐷ℎ,𝑁,𝐿mod2𝐿Dh,N,Lmod2L is the least nonnegative residue, including when 𝐷ℎ,𝑁,𝐿 <0Dh,N,L<0. The criterion requires this residue to be more than 2𝐿𝑅ℎ,𝑁,𝐿(𝑀)2LRh,N,L(M) from both 00 and 2𝐿2L. One successful comparison certifies nonintegrality at that index; a failed comparison is inconclusive. Irrationality requires such certificates arbitrarily late for every positive ℎh. At prescribed logarithmic depth, the needed residue must be farther from both endpoints than the error bound. With the polynomial majorant in Proposition 6.5, the remainder bound is rational and explicit, so the whole test reduces to integer comparisons. The modular rewriting is an ordinary deduction, not a separately checked Lean declaration.

Proposition D.4 (completeness of finite separation). Suppose 𝐷 ∈ℝD∈R, 𝑆𝐿 ∈ℝSL∈R and 𝑅𝐿 ≥0RL≥0 satisfy |𝐷 −𝑆𝐿| ≤𝑅𝐿|D−SL|≤RL and 𝑅𝐿 →0RL→0. Then

𝐷∉ℤ⟺there exists 𝐿 with dist⁡(𝑆𝐿,ℤ)>𝑅𝐿.D∉Z⟺there exists L with dist⁡(SL,Z)>RL.

Proof. The reverse implication follows from the triangle inequality. For the forward implication put 𝛿 =dist⁡(𝐷,ℤ) >0δ=dist⁡(D,Z)>0. For all sufficiently large 𝐿L, one has 𝑅𝐿 <𝛿/2RL<δ/2, and the 11-Lipschitz property of distance gives dist⁡(𝑆𝐿,ℤ) ≥𝛿 −𝑅𝐿 >𝑅𝐿dist⁡(SL,Z)≥δ−RL>RL. No monotonicity of the errors is required. ◻

For fixed ℎ,𝑁h,N, increasing the depth eventually certifies every nonintegral 𝜎ℎ(𝑁)σh(N). This is not a decision procedure for integrality: finitely many failed tests prove nothing, although a proof of failure at every depth implies integrality. It gives neither a prescribed depth as 𝑁N varies nor arbitrarily late successful indices. For example, [1 −2−𝑁,1 +2−𝑁][1−2−N,1+2−N] contains both the noninteger 1 +2−2𝑁1+2−2N and the integer 11 for every 𝑁 ≥1N≥1. The radii shrink, but the values also change; no enclosure certifies nonintegrality. Completeness keeps the value fixed while refining its enclosure.
