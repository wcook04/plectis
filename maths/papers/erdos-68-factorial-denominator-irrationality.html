Two Incomparable Denominator Exclusions for ∑n≥2(n!−1)−1

Précis

For the factorial-denominator series, Lean gives an exact factorial-successor normal form and the equivalent eventual-unit-carry criterion, together with integral-channel, prime-translator, projection-rigidity, and Cramer-residual consumers. An independently regenerated exact finite certificate reaches index 300000, and the checked consumer excludes every smaller positive rational denominator. Five quantified producer problems isolate the missing cofinal step; none is proved, and Erdős #68 remains open.

This paper owns the problem-specific exposition for Erdős #68: exact factorial normal forms, structural consumers, finite denominator evidence, method boundaries, and five ranked open producers.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #68, which remains open.

The denominator exclusions

The same argument at 𝑚 −1m−1 gives 𝑍𝑚 =𝑚!𝑆 =𝑚𝑍𝑚−1Zm=m!S=mZm−1, proving (1). A non-unit carry at index 𝑚m therefore forces every rational denominator to be at least 𝑚m, since any smaller positive 𝑞q would divide (𝑚 −1)!(m−1)!. The exact value 𝑏300000 ≠1b300000≠1 therefore excludes every divisor of 299999!299999! as a denominator of 𝑆S.

The independent continued-fraction exclusion bounds the denominator’s size. Neither restriction implies the other: a prime between 299999299999 and 599998599998 satisfies the divisibility restriction and fails the size restriction, whereas 299999!299999! does the reverse. Both conclusions are finite; they do not decide the irrationality question posed by Erdős [1][2]. Their computational certificates are specified in §8.

Adjacent factorial differences affect exactly their divisor channels. Integral triangular elimination therefore solves the channel equations. The support restriction determines which factorial moments occur, while at a fixed moment every correction changes the residual by an integer. The argument separates coefficient feasibility from the real separation needed for irrationality.
