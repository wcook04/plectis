Précis

The paper proves that if S=a/q is rational, q cannot divide 299999!299999! and is at least 239990 >1012038239990>1012038. The first exclusion comes from the exact strict-successor carry test and the second from continued fractions. Lean also checks the equivalent carry formulation and several consequences. A large common denominator alone does not control the reduced partial sums. Irrationality is reduced to a cofinal miss of the strict factorial successor, which remains open.

This paper owns the problem-specific exposition for Erdős #68: exact factorial normal forms, structural consumers, finite denominator evidence, method boundaries, and five ranked open producers.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #68, which remains open.

The denominator exclusions

Its proof is the factorial-tail integrality argument that Hančl and Tijdeman use for factorial series with integer coefficients [6]; for the denominators 𝑛! −1n!−1 the tail estimate is proved directly. Indeed, 0 <𝑚!(𝑆 −𝐻𝑚) <10<m!(S−Hm)<1, by comparison with the telescoping series ∑𝑛>𝑚(𝑛 −1)/𝑛! =1/𝑚!∑n>m(n−1)/n!=1/m!. Thus 𝑚!𝑆m!S is the unique integer strictly above 𝑚!𝐻𝑚m!Hm. The same argument at 𝑚 −1m−1 gives 𝑍𝑚 =𝑚!𝑆 =𝑚𝑍𝑚−1Zm=m!S=mZm−1, proving eq:finite-denominator-consumer. A non-unit carry at index 𝑚m therefore forces every rational denominator to be at least 𝑚m, since any smaller positive 𝑞q would divide (𝑚 −1)!(m−1)!. The exact value 𝑏300000 ≠1b300000≠1 therefore excludes every divisor of 299999!299999! as a denominator of 𝑆S. Equivalently, the factorial index 𝜅(𝑞) =min{𝑟 ≥1 :𝑞 ∣𝑟!}κ(q)=min{r≥1:q∣r!} must satisfy 𝜅(𝑞) ≥300000κ(q)≥300000. This index, rather than the largest prime factor, is the relevant measure of factorial divisibility; compare Sondow’s use of it for 𝑒e [15]. His approximation bound for 𝑒e is not being applied to 𝑆S. In particular, the exclusion does not cover every 299999299999-smooth denominator: sufficiently high powers of a small prime need not divide 299999!299999!.

The independent continued-fraction exclusion bounds the denominator’s size. Neither restriction implies the other: a prime between 299999299999 and 599998599998 satisfies the divisibility restriction and fails the size restriction, whereas 299999!299999! does the reverse. Both conclusions are finite; they do not decide the irrationality question posed by Erdős [1] and listed in Bloom’s catalogue [2]. Their computational certificates are specified in §8.

A first reading may follow the two finite certificates in §8, the exact equivalence in Theorem 2.1, and the obstruction in (9). The channel calculations explain why that obstruction persists; they are not premises of either finite exclusion.

Adjacent factorial differences affect exactly their divisor channels. This makes the channel equations solvable by integral triangular elimination. Solving them, however, does not make the residual small: at a fixed factorial moment every correction changes it by an integer. Sections 2–4 identify the exact carry test and this limitation of channel cancellation. Section 5 then shows, at the prime 139139, why a large common denominator need not survive reduction; Section 6 states the additional real comparison needed.
