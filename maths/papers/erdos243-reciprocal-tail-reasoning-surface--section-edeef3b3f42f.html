Reciprocal Sums and the Sylvester Recurrence: Further Results and Proofs

Erdős #243 By Will Cook September 2026
PDFOpen the PDF, 61 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem dossier

Précis. An increasing sequence of positive integers satisfying 𝑎2𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1 =1 +3/𝑛 +𝑜(𝑛−3)an2/an+1=1+3/n+o(n−3) has an irrational reciprocal sum: rationality would force integer tail numerators to agree eventually with a cubic polynomial, which divisibility, a cubic-field calculation, and congruences modulo seven exclude. For rational reciprocal sums under 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1, the record also develops sufficient conditions for the eventual recurrence 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1, controlling either increases of an integer tail numerator or a convergent sum at steps setting new maxima. Examples separate numerical growth bounds from the exact recurrences. The cubic proof is distinguished from the theorems formalised in Lean. None of the additional bounds is derived for every rational reciprocal sum in Erdős #243.

Bounds on the error and on the original sequence

Theorem 7.11 (slow negative part). Let 𝑎,𝐶,𝐷 :ℕ →ℕa,C,D:N→N satisfy (19) with 𝑎𝑛 >1an>1, 𝐶𝑛 >0Cn>0, 𝐷0 ≥1D0≥1, under vanishing relative error. Suppose that for some 𝛿 ∈(0,1)δ∈(0,1) and all large 𝑛n with 𝐸𝑛 <0En<0 one has −𝐸𝑛 ≤(1 −𝛿)ℓ(𝐶𝑛)−En≤(1−δ)ℓ(Cn). Then 𝐸𝑛 =0En=0 for all large 𝑛n, and 𝑎𝑛+1 =𝑎2𝑛 −𝑎𝑛 +1an+1=an2−an+1 for all large 𝑛n.

Theorem 12.1 is the case of a constant bound, since a constant is eventually below (1 −𝛿)ℓ(𝐶𝑛)(1−δ)ℓ(Cn) on a nonzero tail, where 𝐶𝑛 →∞Cn→∞.

Proof. Suppose the error is not eventually zero. The auxiliary lower bound in the proof of Theorem 7.3 applies to this exact integer orbit: it used only 𝑎𝑛 >1an>1, 𝐶𝑛 >0Cn>0, 𝐷0 ≥1D0≥1 and vanishing relative error. It gives lim sup𝑛(𝐻𝑛+1 −𝐻𝑛)/ℓ(𝐻𝑛) ≥1lim supn(Hn+1−Hn)/ℓ(Hn)≥1. On the other hand, the hypothesis and 𝐶𝑛 ≤𝐻𝑛Cn≤Hn give

𝐻𝑛+1−𝐻𝑛≤(−𝐸𝑛)+≤(1−𝛿)ℓ(𝐶𝑛)≤(1−𝛿)ℓ(𝐻𝑛)Hn+1−Hn≤(−En)+≤(1−δ)ℓ(Cn)≤(1−δ)ℓ(Hn)

for all large 𝑛n, a contradiction. Thus 𝐸𝑛 =0En=0 eventually, and Theorem 5.7 gives the Sylvester recurrence. ◻

The corresponding pointwise-rise argument also has formalised arithmetic lemmas: persistence of a common divisor, divisibility of every later error by gcd(𝑎𝑛,𝐷𝑛)gcd(an,Dn), the landing lemma and the resulting exclusion. The count of indices and the simultaneous choice of constants are written out in the earlier proof. Corollary 7.4 includes the boundary coefficient 11, while the preceding theorem on the running maximum also discounts rises that recover an earlier decrease. The present statement is retained for its direct bound on 𝐸𝑛En.

To compare the bounded hypothesis with the classical criteria, number the original sequence from 11 in this subsection and put 𝑃𝑛 =∏1≤𝑗<𝑛𝑎𝑗Pn=∏1≤j<naj. Then

𝑃𝑛𝑎𝑛(𝑎2𝑛𝑎𝑛+1−1)=𝑃𝑛+1𝑎𝑛+1−𝑃𝑛𝑎𝑛.Pnan(an2an+1−1)=Pn+1an+1−Pnan.

Thus the bound concerns upward increments, not boundedness of 𝑃𝑛/𝑎𝑛Pn/an. The quadratic growth limit controls only the ratio of consecutive terms. Sequences satisfying the eventual Sylvester recurrence satisfy the bound, as do exact-square sequences 𝑎𝑛 =𝑏2𝑛−1an=b2n−1 with 𝑏 ≥2b≥2, for which the difference is zero. The latter never satisfies the Sylvester recurrence, so the bounded criterion below recovers irrationality of its reciprocal sum. The rounded examples after Corollary 7.13 show more generally how a term 𝑐/𝑛c/n in the ratio produces increments of order 𝑛𝑐−1nc−1. The theorem allows a finite positive upper limit where the classical product criterion requires a nonpositive one; neither bound is derived from the original problem alone.

Theorem 7.12 (bounded or slowly growing increments of the product ratio). Let 𝑎1 <𝑎2 <⋯a1<a2<⋯ be positive integers with 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1 and ∑𝑛≥11/𝑎𝑛 =𝑝/𝑞∑n≥11/an=p/q, where 𝑝,𝑞p,q are positive integers. Put

If lim sup𝑛𝑄𝑛 <∞lim supnQn<∞, the sequence is eventually Sylvester. The same conclusion holds if, for some 𝛿 >0δ>0 and all large 𝑛n,

𝑄𝑛≤1−𝛿𝑞ℓ(𝑎1⋯𝑎𝑛−1/𝑎𝑛).Qn≤1−δqℓ(a1⋯an−1/an).

For the bounded clause, use the stated denominator 𝑞q, put 𝑃𝑛 =∏𝑗<𝑛𝑎𝑗Pn=∏j<naj, 𝑥𝑛 =∑𝑘≥𝑛1/𝑎𝑘xn=∑k≥n1/ak, and form the integer tail 𝐷𝑛 =𝑞𝑃𝑛Dn=qPn, 𝐶𝑛 =𝐷𝑛𝑥𝑛Cn=Dnxn, 𝐸𝑛 =𝐷𝑛 −(𝑎𝑛 −1)𝐶𝑛En=Dn−(an−1)Cn. Exact cancellation gives

𝐸𝑛+𝑞𝑄𝑛=𝑞𝑃𝑛(1𝑎𝑛+1−(𝑎𝑛−1)∑𝑘≥𝑛+21𝑎𝑘).En+qQn=qPn(1an+1−(an−1)∑k≥n+21ak).

The expression in parentheses is positive eventually, since ∑𝑘≥𝑛+21/𝑎𝑘 ≤4/𝑎2𝑛+1∑k≥n+21/ak≤4/an+12 and 𝑎𝑛+1 >4(𝑎𝑛 −1)an+1>4(an−1) eventually. Thus 𝐸𝑛 ≥ −𝑞𝑄𝑛En≥−qQn, and lim sup𝑄𝑛 <∞lim supQn<∞ supplies the eventual lower bound required by Theorem 12.1. This proves the bounded clause without first estimating the absolute size of the comparison error.

For the slow-growth clause and the later summability comparisons, the same exact formula gives

0<𝐸𝑛+𝑞𝑄𝑛≤𝑞𝑃𝑛/𝑎𝑛+1=𝑂(𝑃𝑛/𝑎2𝑛)0<En+qQn≤qPn/an+1=O(Pn/an2)

eventually. The ratio test in Section 6 shows that these errors have a convergent sum, hence tend to zero. The factor 1/𝑞1/q in the slow-growth clause cancels the factor 𝑞q in 𝐸𝑛 +𝑞𝑄𝑛 =𝑜(1)En+qQn=o(1). Suppose the sequence is not eventually Sylvester. ℓ(𝐶𝑛)ℓ(𝑃𝑛/𝑎𝑛)⟶1.ℓ(Cn)ℓ(Pn/an)⟶1.

The same comparison permits any positive real clearing factor 𝐹𝑛 ≤𝑞𝑃𝑛Fn≤qPn: multiplying 𝐸𝑛 +𝑞𝑄𝑛 =𝑜(1)En+qQn=o(1) by 𝐹𝑛/(𝑞𝑃𝑛)Fn/(qPn) gives

𝐹𝑛−(𝑎𝑛−1)𝐹𝑛𝑥𝑛+𝐹𝑛𝑎𝑛(𝑎2𝑛𝑎𝑛+1−1)=𝑜(1).Fn−(an−1)Fnxn+Fnan(an2an+1−1)=o(1).

Neither 𝐹𝑛𝑥𝑛Fnxn nor 𝐹𝑛 −(𝑎𝑛 −1)𝐹𝑛𝑥𝑛Fn−(an−1)Fnxn need be integral. The LCM choice 𝐹𝑛 =𝐿𝑛Fn=Ln makes both integers, but its update includes the factor 𝜌𝑛ρn of Section 6. The short note uses only this LCM specialisation; no general clearing factor is needed in its bounded-increment proof.

Corollary 7.13 (the 1/𝑛1/n threshold). Let 𝑎1 <𝑎2 <⋯a1<a2<⋯ be positive integers with 𝑎𝑛+1/𝑎2𝑛 →1an+1/an2→1 and ∑𝑛1/𝑎𝑛 ∈ℚ∑n1/an∈Q. If

More generally, if 𝑐 >0c>0, 𝜀 >0ε>0 and

𝑎2𝑛𝑎𝑛+1=1+𝑐𝑛+𝑂(𝑛−1−𝜀),an2an+1=1+cn+O(n−1−ε),
