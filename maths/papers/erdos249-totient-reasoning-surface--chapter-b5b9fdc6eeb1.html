Long paper

The Binary Totient Series

Erdős #249 By Will Cook July 2026; revised 1 October 2026 Authorship, AI use and citation
PDFOpen the PDF, 163 pages TeXLaTeX source Source on GitHub Problem page

Précis. The dyadic totient sections through level 𝑒 ≥1e≥1 have an explicit rational basis of size 2𝑒 +12e+1. Truncating a scaled tail difference produces an integer whose residue certifies nonintegrality when its distances from both endpoints of the residue interval exceed an explicit error bound. The record develops the resulting finite denominator exclusions, equivalent residue criteria, and sufficient exponential-sum estimates. Rational comparison sequences identify the limitations of particular size, parity, rank, and finite-prefix arguments. The short paper supplies the all-base basis theorem; here each conditional result carries the arithmetic hypothesis still needed to apply it. Those hypotheses are not established in the form required to prove irrationality of S=∑ 𝜑(𝑛)2−𝑛φ(n)2−n.

This paper owns a claim-bounded problem-specific reasoning surface for Erdős #249: selected checked premises, finite certificates, closed routes, surviving obligations, and their exact evidence bands.

It is not authority for the validity of claims tagged Lean, which belongs to the cited kernel-checked source, or a solution to Erdős #249, which remains open.

Separation along prime positions

The quantified prime condition.

∀ℎ≥1 ∀𝑁0 ∃𝑝 prime:max(𝑁0+ℎ+1,ℎ+5)≤𝑝 ∧ Re⁡(𝑒(𝑅𝑝−1−𝑅𝑝−ℎ−1))<910.∀h≥1 ∀N0 ∃p prime:max(N0+h+1,h+5)≤p ∧ Re⁡(e(Rp−1−Rp−h−1))<910.

This condition is unproved; it implies irrationality (ErdosProblems.Erdos249.DTWNaturalPrimeTailOrbitStrictGap, ErdosProblems.Erdos249.irrational_totient_series_of_naturalPrimeTailOrbitStrictGap).

Its meaning for the doubling map.

Re⁡𝑒(2𝑝−ℎ−1𝛼ℎ) <9/10Re⁡e(2p−h−1αh)<9/10 says ‖2𝑝−ℎ−1𝛼ℎ‖ℝ/ℤ >arccos⁡(9/10)/2𝜋 =0.0717831…‖2p−h−1αh‖R/Z>arccos⁡(9/10)/2π=0.0717831…, so the requirement is exactly: for each ℎh, the indicated prime-indexed ×2×2 orbit point stays more than 0.0717831…0.0717831… from the nearest integer, for cofinally many primes 𝑝p. A short-run condition on the binary digits can be a sufficient proxy for this circle-distance inequality, but it is not equivalent to it; a run-length description alone does not determine the residual position inside the dyadic cylinder.

What the prime condition adds.

The preceding condition asks for a separated point at a prime-indexed position. The full-block average does not by itself locate such a point on a prescribed sparse set. Conversely, the prime condition does not assert cancellation over the whole interval. They are different sufficient hypotheses; no general comparison of their difficulty is proved here.

Finite comparison.

Among the 501501 primes in [1000,5000)[1000,5000), the strict inequality holds at 432432, 426426 and 430430 primes for ℎ =1,2,5h=1,2,5, respectively. The corresponding proportions are 0.86230.8623, 0.85030.8503 and 0.85830.8583 to four decimal places, and each sample has a cosine value below −0.9999−0.9999. These counts were recomputed using the same interval for 𝑆S as in Table 1, with no interval meeting the threshold 9/109/10. They do not establish separation at arbitrarily large primes.

An average over a prescribed prime sample.

The subset implication, Theorem 9.5, allows a nonempty finite set 𝑇T. If 𝑇T is chosen after a separated point is known, the subset formulation may add no arithmetic content: the equivalence proved using selected pairs, Proposition 2.7(b), illustrates this issue. To avoid that particular choice, fix the sample in advance by

𝑇ℎ,𝑋={𝑁∈[𝑋,2𝑋):𝑁+ℎ+1 is prime}.Th,X={N∈[X,2X):N+h+1 is prime}.

For 𝑋 ≥4X≥4 and 𝐿 ≥ℎL≥h, this is precisely the assigned group with cofactor 𝑚𝑁 =1mN=1 of §3.2, taken with 𝑠 =𝐿 −ℎs=L−h (Erdos249257.TotientTailPeriodKiller.mem_pivotFiber_one_overlap_iff).

A sufficient, unproved condition is

∀ℎ≥1 ∀𝑋0 ∃𝑋≥max(𝑋0,4) ∃𝐿≥ℎ:𝑇ℎ,𝑋≠∅,16(2𝑋+ℎ+𝐿+2)≤2𝐿,∑𝑁∈𝑇ℎ,𝑋Re⁡𝐸(ℎ,𝑁,𝐿)≤910|𝑇ℎ,𝑋|.(5)(5)∀h≥1 ∀X0 ∃X≥max(X0,4) ∃L≥h:Th,X≠∅,16(2X+h+L+2)≤2L,∑N∈Th,XRe⁡E(h,N,L)≤910|Th,X|.

The subset theorem then gives a certificate at some 𝑁 ≥𝑋 ≥𝑋0N≥X≥X0. Nonemptiness matters: for the empty set the displayed sum inequality would hold automatically and would produce no certificate. The bounds 𝑋 ≥4X≥4 and 𝐿 ≥ℎL≥h are also the hypotheses of the identification with that cofactor group.

On this sample the selected cofactor is 11, so its coefficient is the odd integer 2ℎ −12h−1 and its modulus is 2ℎ+12h+1, fixed once ℎh is fixed. The unweighted prime average has zero Ramanujan main term, since 𝑐2ℎ+1(2ℎ −1) =𝜇(2ℎ+1) =0c2h+1(2h−1)=μ(2h+1)=0. That observation does not establish (5): the prime phase is multiplied by a residual phase built from the other totient values. Their correlation is the missing estimate. Restricting the sample removes the complement and unwanted cofactor terms, but not this residual correlation.

Remark 12.8. Fixing the sample rules out the particular argument that selects a pair only after locating separation. It does not prove that (5) is inequivalent to irrationality, or that it is a strictly stronger condition. No such comparison is established here.
