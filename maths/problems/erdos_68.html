Introduction

Erdős asked whether replacing 𝑛!n! by 𝑛! −1n!−1 preserves the irrationality of the reciprocal sum [1]. The question appears as Problem 68 in Bloom’s catalogue [2].

Problem 1.1 (Erdős Problem #68). Is

𝑆=∑𝑛≥21𝑛!−1S=∑n≥21n!−1

irrational?

The elementary proof that 𝑒e is irrational clears the partial sum by a factorial and bounds the remaining positive tail. Here the first step fails: for 𝐻𝑚 =∑𝑚𝑛=2(𝑛! −1)−1Hm=∑n=2m(n!−1)−1, already 3!𝐻3 =36/53!H3=36/5. We instead construct forms 𝑀𝑆 +𝑘MS+k, with 𝑀,𝑘 ∈ℤM,k∈Z, by cancelling initial weighted sums. If 𝑆 =𝑎/𝑞S=a/q and 𝑞 ∣𝑀q∣M, then 𝑀𝑆 +𝑘MS+k is integral; proving it nonintegral excludes that denominator.

At depth four, the weight congruences force 115 ∣𝑀115∣M. Restricting the coefficient support to 𝑛 ≥2n≥2 strengthens this to 1380 ∣𝑀1380∣M, and every multiple of 13801380 occurs. We determine the exact factor at every depth 𝐷 ≥2D≥2 and construct a vector attaining the least positive multiplier.

First allow a coefficient at index one. Theorem 2.1 constructs an integral basis by correcting adjacent factorial differences at proper divisors to isolate each weighted sum. At fixed 𝑀M, cancellation determines the lower basis coefficients; removing index one leaves a single equation in the higher coefficients. Theorem 3.1 reduces its infinite gcd to a calculation below 2𝐷22D2. Bézout coefficients then construct an attaining vector. No assumption on the rationality of 𝑆S enters this classification.

Hančl and Tijdeman’s tail-integrality lemma treats factorial series with integer coefficients [4]. Their factorial-scaled partial sums are integers. Here the scaled partial sum need not be integral. Instead, the weight congruences give R −𝑀𝑆 ∈ℤR−MS∈Z (1); the assumption 𝑆 =𝑎/𝑞S=a/q and 𝑞 ∣𝑀q∣M then makes RR integral. Koepf and Schmersau’s factorial-digit criterion [3] is used in the companion’s digit argument to give an equivalent eventual-pattern condition for 𝑆 −𝑒 +2S−e+2. Failure of that pattern at arbitrarily large indices also remains unproved.

Section 4 tests an attaining form; Section 5 gives the general nonintegrality test.

Fix a vector with 𝑉2 =⋯ =𝑉𝐷 =0V2=⋯=VD=0 and 𝑀 >0M>0, and choose any cutoff 𝑁 ≥𝐷N≥D at or beyond its support. Separate the signed finite sum from the positive tail:

To exclude every denominator, 𝑀M must vary. The companion’s progression construction adds size control: polynomial root conditions give primitive vectors (coefficient gcd one) with 0 <𝑀 <𝑁!0<M<N!, where 𝑁N is their largest supported index. The factorial-divisibility exclusion does not exclude all denominators with only small prime factors, whose exponents may be larger.
