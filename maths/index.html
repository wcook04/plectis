Plectis · Mathematics

Read the mathematics. Find what comes next.

Plectis is a prototype for mathematical exposition and collaborative research. Start with the short papers: the questions, the strongest results and the ideas behind their proofs. Follow the longer records, Lean source and computations in the maths repository, then develop what comes next.

  • Choose a problem. Eight programmes, each with a short expository paper and a longer research record.
  • Explore with your AI. One reading file, no clone: an introduction, a short research instruction and the opening of each paper.
  • Work in the repository. Clone it, name a question, or ask your agent to read the corpus and decide what is worth developing.

For a guided introduction, start with A reader's way in.

See the universe map: every claim drawn with its problem, 2,196 objects on one field.

Will Cook built and directs this AI-assisted research environment and maintains its public release. AI agents did most of the research and drafting. The papers name earlier sources and contributors to particular results, including ani's #1041 construction. For a result, cite its paper and relevant earlier sources; for software, use the release citation and give the commit used. Contact Will with questions or corrections.

You can contribute an insight before writing a formal proof. Will can work with you to develop and formalise it, with credit for the insight and the subsequent work kept distinct. The whole collection is also material for new mathematics: connections, reusable methods and questions across problems.

  • #257Reciprocal sums over infinite exponent supports

    Is the sum of 1/(2𝑛 −1)1/(2n−1) over every infinite set of positive exponents irrational?

    Proved here: if a positive host has finite prime-weighted mass in base 2, every infinite subset has an irrational support series at every integer base. Read the theorem and its hypotheses. The original universal question remains open; novelty is unassessed.

    Short paper · Long paper · Check the 189/388 example · Lean map
  • #269Three-prime running least common multiples

    For a finite set of at least two primes, is the sum of reciprocals of the running least common multiples of the smooth numbers irrational?

    This library treats the three-prime case.

    Short paper · Long paper · Lean map
  • #1041Short connections inside polynomial lemniscates

    Must two roots of a monic polynomial in the open unit disc be joined by a sub-two-length curve inside its unit lemniscate?

    Ani’s degree-seven example refutes the exact Formal Conjectures statement; correspondence with the 1958 wording awaits human review.

    Short paper · Long paper · Lean map
  • #1049Lambert-type series at rational bases

    For which rational bases is the corresponding series irrational?

    The first resistant explicit base is three halves.
