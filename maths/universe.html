    How to read the map

    The rings

    1. At the centre, the Lean universe, with Comparator and Palomar.
    2. On one orbit, the problems, each between its two papers.
    3. Just outside, the claims in each problem’s record.
    4. Outermost, every result its papers state, short paper first, in the order each paper states them.

    A result’s mark

    A result’s mark says how far it is checked: filled in Comparator’s colour when Comparator has replayed it, with a pip when Lean states it exactly and no replay is recorded, ringed when Lean states it under named inputs, and faint when no Lean statement is recorded. Round the outside, a scale has one evenly spaced tick for each result, so the length of a run is its paper’s count.

    A claim’s glyph

    • Proved, formalised or a verified finite instance

    • Conditional reduction
    • Open or cited only

    The evidence in full

    689 results from sixteen papers on eight problems. Lean states 640 of them exactly, in 1,506 declarations, and Comparator has replayed 616.

    How the map is drawn

    The map reads from the centre out, one ring for each layer. The Lean universe sits at the centre, with Comparator and Palomar below it. The problems sit on one orbit, each between its two papers, and just outside each one are the claims in its record; claims two problems share sit on the edge between their sectors. Outermost, a band holds every result its short paper and long record assert, short paper first, each in the order the paper states it. Each problem owns one sector of the circle, as wide as its results need, so the outer ring has the same depth all the way round. Statements that cite the same file-qualified Lean declaration are linked across the two papers. This records shared formal support, not equivalence of the statements. A result is filled in Comparator’s colour when a Comparator replay has checked the Lean declarations that state it, filled in the Lean colour when Lean states it exactly and no replay is recorded, ringed when Lean states it under named inputs, and faint when no Lean statement is recorded. These marks come from the public paper-to-Lean coverage ledger at Lean commit 436f55ebda. A claim’s glyph is its status in the claim registry; a claim and a result that name the same Lean declaration are connected.

    Selecting Comparator or Palomar lights the results each one reaches. The complete universe adds the Lean modules, each placed with the problem whose folder holds it or whose results it states, and the steps of the recorded argument graph. Research and verification files outside the library graph are excluded.
