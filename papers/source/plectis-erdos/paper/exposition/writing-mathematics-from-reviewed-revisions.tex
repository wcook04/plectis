\begin{abstract}
A mathematical sentence can be easy to read and still conceal the inference
that justifies it. This guide explains how to locate that difficulty, study a
relevant passage in the literature and write an explanation warranted by the
manuscript's own proof. Worked cases connect finite choices to an attained sum,
derive a certificate from its target interval and separate convergence of one
summand from passage through an infinite sum. Later examples explain why a
technique recurs and how an ordinary verb can change a claim's scope.
The cases come from recorded revisions of eight Erd\H{o}s-problem papers;
a final section considers their supporting systems paper. Accepted revisions,
proposed improvements and narrower lessons remain distinct. The compact
companion gives instructions; this guide supplies their reasons, sources and
limits. It documents editorial practice, not new mathematical results or a
controlled evaluation of reader understanding.
\end{abstract}
