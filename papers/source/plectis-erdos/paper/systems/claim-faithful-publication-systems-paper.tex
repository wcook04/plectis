\begin{abstract}
% SENTENCE r4.abstract.01
We describe Plectis, a repository-based system for carrying mathematical research from a question to a reviewed publication.
% END SENTENCE r4.abstract.01
% SENTENCE r4.abstract.02
Its public prototype organises eight mathematical programmes around shared source files and structured records.
% END SENTENCE r4.abstract.02
% SENTENCE r4.abstract.03
The design keeps paper statements, registered formal support and explanatory prose in separate records, allowing revisions to be checked against their cited evidence.
% END SENTENCE r4.abstract.03
% SENTENCE r4.abstract.04
Python tools retrieve records, store optional Lean probes, check source bindings and regenerate indexes and summaries. Maintainers decide which changes to adopt, and a journal retains entered contributions and review decisions.
% END SENTENCE r4.abstract.04
% SENTENCE r4.abstract.05
A worked example shows why links to correct formal proofs can survive a paraphrase that drops a stated hypothesis.
% END SENTENCE r4.abstract.05
% SENTENCE r4.abstract.06
A historical author-run test rejected \HistoricalRejected{} of \HistoricalEdits{} false edits and accepted one false completion claim. Reader benefit, discovery rate and adoption by independent laboratories remain unmeasured.
% END SENTENCE r4.abstract.06
\end{abstract}
\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}
\papersectiontarget{systems-lifecycle}
% SENTENCE r4.intro1.01
Research continues across sessions, contributors and drafts. The next researcher needs to recover which question is open, what has been tried, and what supports the claims already published. A formal proof may remain correct while an edited explanation drops a hypothesis, or an unresolved step is later described as complete. Keeping the proof is therefore only part of keeping a usable research record.
% END SENTENCE r4.intro1.01

% SENTENCE r4.intro1.02
Plectis is the public repository prototype studied here. It stores questions, prior sources, computations, proofs and unresolved steps alongside proposed contributions and their review. The implemented workflow is local to this repository; broader autonomous discovery and services for other scientific fields are proposals (Section~\ref{sec:limits}).
% END SENTENCE r4.intro1.02

% SENTENCE r4.intro1.03
The architecture separates authored research and publication records from the tools that inspect them and the indexes and summaries generated from them. Researchers and maintainers decide what to investigate and what a proposed change justifies. Figure~\ref{fig:lifecycle} follows the resulting path from research artifacts to publication and back through contributions.
% END SENTENCE r4.intro1.03

% SENTENCE r4.intro2.01
Erd\H{o}s Problems supplies the questions and their public discussion, while Lean and mathlib supply the formal language and library~\cite{erdosproblems,lean4,mathlib}.
% END SENTENCE r4.intro2.01
% SENTENCE r4.intro2.02
Formalisation blueprints link a written argument to named Lean declarations~\cite{leanblueprint,leanarchitect}, and the Equational Theories Project maintains shared problem records~\cite{etp}. Prove2Me combines human-audited missions, source-linked milestones and separately submitted proofs, with intermediate results available for other agents to reuse~\cite[Sections~3--4]{prove2me}.
% END SENTENCE r4.intro2.02

% SENTENCE r4.intro2.03
Publication review follows a paper assertion at a specified revision. Its coverage record identifies the printed statement, registered Lean supports and their stated relationship. A separate passage record binds the explanation to sources and rationale; contribution records retain entered review decisions. Together these links make the basis of a proposed revision inspectable. Agreement between the records does not establish that the prose is true.
% END SENTENCE r4.intro2.03

% SENTENCE r4.intro2.04
We first describe the stored records, inspection tools and contributor responsibilities. A proposed change to a theorem\textquotesingle s explanation then illustrates the publication checks. The revision and contribution workflows show how accepted work returns to the repository. Recorded observations and limits distinguish these implemented procedures from their unmeasured effects; Appendix~\ref{app:worked-proof} retains the mathematical calculation.
% END SENTENCE r4.intro2.04

\section{A repository organised around problems}\label{sec:world}
\papersectiontarget{systems-scaling}
% SENTENCE r8.architecture.records
\subsection{Stored records and derived views}
Lean and manuscript files contain the arguments. Claim records state selected assertions and what remains open; evidence records identify their registered supports; the journal retains entered returns and decisions. Builders read these authored files to produce indexes, source coordinates and status summaries. The \repolink{docs/ARCHITECTURE.md}{architecture guide} identifies the source of each derived view. A changed summary can therefore be investigated at its input record rather than treated as a new research result.
% END SENTENCE r8.architecture.records

% SENTENCE r12.architecture.correspondence
For publication, the coverage ledger identifies a \emph{paper occurrence}: a labelled assertion in a particular manuscript, together with its source location and a digest of its statement. The row lists its registered Lean supports and their evidence status. Where the formal result is stronger than the printed statement, an authored relation note explains the correspondence and is bound to both statements. The passage ledger used for this systems paper has a different purpose: it records explanatory text, cited source spans, an evidence class and a rationale. Neither ledger derives the prose from a Lean proof.
% END SENTENCE r12.architecture.correspondence

% SENTENCE r4.world1.01
\subsection{Finding the relevant work}
Within each problem collection, a reader may want a proof, a counterexample to a proposed extension, or the argument behind a failed approach. Exact experiments and corrected references remain beside the original question as contributions in their own right.
% END SENTENCE r4.world1.01
% SENTENCE r4.world1.02
The reader can locate these records without first compiling the whole repository. Four views answer different parts of that search (Figure~\ref{fig:world}).
% END SENTENCE r4.world1.02

% SENTENCE r4.world1.03
A declaration is a named Lean definition, theorem or lemma. The declaration index locates its source; the dependency index records which earlier declarations the selected results use.
% END SENTENCE r4.world1.03
% SENTENCE r4.world1.04
A separate graph records authored interpretations of those declarations and their connections, while a claim registry records selected mathematical assertions, their status and the obligations still open.
% END SENTENCE r4.world1.04

\subsection{Operations on the records}
The tools provide the following operations:

\toprule

\midrule

Proof sessions & \repolink{scripts/proof_workbench.py}{proof\_workbench.py} records notes and submitted Lean probes, derives verdicts from the Lean process, and binds a session claim to an accepted probe.\\[3pt]

A separate self-audit, \repolink{scripts/systems_paper_evidence.py}{systems\_paper\_evidence.py}, checks this systems manuscript's text and cited source bindings. These commands provide the operations; the researcher or agent chooses which to invoke. Their presence is not evidence of a successful run or of research quality.
% END SENTENCE r8.architecture.tools

% SENTENCE r9.architecture.state
Control remains with the researcher or agent; persistent state remains in files. An optional proof session stores notes, exact Lean inputs, process-derived probe receipts and a closing outcome. Its claim command requires an accepted probe, then appends a session record; it does not update the reviewed public claim registry. The research journal separately records entered returns and review dispositions, checking their sequence and hash chain. These records allow later work to inspect earlier inputs and decisions without relying on the original conversation.
% END SENTENCE r9.architecture.state

% SENTENCE r8.architecture.roles
\subsection{Researcher, agent and maintainer responsibilities}
A researcher chooses the task and the evidence needed to answer it. A coding agent can inspect files, invoke tools and prepare a proposed change using the public instructions; ordinary written arguments and explanations can also be contributed without a clone or an agent. A maintainer reviews the claim, attribution and remaining uncertainty before adoption. The contributor and reviewer may be the same agent, so this division of responsibilities does not establish independent review. Public setup and review instructions are in the \repolink{docs/agents/README.md}{agent quickstart} and \repolink{CONTRIBUTING.md}{contribution guide}.
% END SENTENCE r8.architecture.roles

% SENTENCE r8.architecture.iteration
A typical iteration starts by retrieving the work relevant to a question. The researcher investigates and submits an argument, experiment, correction or manuscript change with its evidence and limitations. Review may adopt it, request revision, defer it or reject it. Accepted work updates the affected authored records; builders regenerate their dependent views, and release checks test those views for consistency. The journal preserves dispositions for structured returns explicitly entered into it. Ordinary issues, pull requests and ideas can enter review without a journal session. Sections~\ref{sec:paper} and~\ref{sec:contribute} describe these two contribution routes.
% END SENTENCE r8.architecture.iteration

\section{Worked case: reviewing an explanation}\label{sec:example}\label{sec:predigestion}


% SENTENCE r8.case.system-role
The worked case concerns a theorem about infinite sums of reciprocals over a restricted set of positive integer exponents. A base-two weighted convergence condition restricts the allowable sets; under it, every infinite subset gives an irrational sum at every integer base at least two. Removing the condition would assert a solution of a more general problem that remains open. Appendix~\ref{app:worked-proof} gives the exact statement and calculation.
% END SENTENCE r8.case.system-role

% SENTENCE r8.case.proof-role
Suppose a contributor rewrites this theorem\textquotesingle s explanation. Its coverage row, \texttt{res:weighted-support} in \repolink{docs/paper_lean_coverage.json}{paper\_lean\_coverage.json}, identifies the statement in the mathematical paper and two registered Lean supports. An explanatory passage in this systems paper is instead recorded in the \repolink{docs/systems_paper_sentences.json}{passage ledger}, with sources and a rationale. For the appendix\textquotesingle s hereditary conclusion, these sources are the ordinary proof and a formal declaration. If the contributor drops the weighted hypothesis from that explanation, both Lean proofs and both source links can remain unchanged. The reviewer must compare the new wording with the cited statements. Section~\ref{sec:checks} explains which part of this comparison the tools check.
% END SENTENCE r8.case.proof-role


\section{Proof and publication checks}\label{sec:checks}\label{sec:graph}
\papersectiontarget{systems-trust}
% SENTENCE r4.checks1.01
The example has two registered formal supports (Figure~\ref{fig:theorem}). One gives fixed-base irrationality and the all-base conclusion for every infinite subset; the other makes the fixed-base hereditary conclusion explicit. Their roles overlap. Here the \emph{registered support set} means the declarations listed for this paper occurrence, not every declaration on which their proofs depend. The publication check accounts for that full list even when one declaration suffices for the clause under discussion. This is a record-consistency requirement, not an additional hypothesis of the theorem.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks1.01
% SENTENCE r4.checks1.02
The resolver also follows any registered named inputs and checks the identified source revisions. A result dependent on an additional named input retains that dependency; the weighted theorem above illustrates a different issue, a restriction within the proved statement. A changed statement or source must be checked against its records again. Evidence attached to one revision cannot silently support another.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks1.02

% SENTENCE r4.checks1.03
These checks make omissions and changes inspectable. Figure~\ref{fig:checks} separates them from proof checking and from the judgements needed to publish a faithful account.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks1.03

% SENTENCE r4.checks2.01
Lean verifies that a proof establishes the formal statement written in the source; it does not verify whether that statement captures the intended mathematics or whether the paper describes it well.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks2.01
% SENTENCE r4.checks2.02
Comparator adds a separately stated challenge and a check of its selected Lean implementation under the permitted axioms~\cite{leanfrocomparator,nanodalib}.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks2.02

% SENTENCE r4.checks2.03
Each marked passage in this systems paper has a recorded text, specified source lines, an evidence class and a rationale for citing those sources. The record groups prose for checking; it need not correspond to one grammatical sentence.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks2.03

% SENTENCE r4.checks2.04
For the appendix\textquotesingle s hereditary conclusion, the sources explain why restriction to an infinite subset preserves the weighted condition. The passage checker compares cryptographic hashes of the marked text and declared sources and requires a nonempty rationale. It also rejects missing records or unbound passages. It does not assess whether that rationale justifies the text.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks2.04

% SENTENCE r4.checks2.05
Changing the passage alone makes its recorded digest stale. Updating its record as well can restore byte agreement while leaving the new assertion unsupported.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks2.05
% SENTENCE r4.checks2.06
For example, a paraphrase could drop the support hypothesis while retaining both correct Lean links in Figure~\ref{fig:theorem}. A reviewer must compare its hypothesis and quantifiers with the source statement. This is an illustrative editing failure, distinct from historical edit~8, which replaced a limitation by a false completion claim (Table~\ref{tab:mutations}).
% END SENTENCE r4.checks2.06

% SENTENCE r4.checks3.01
An entry in the declaration index with a matching type and a reachable source link identifies the recorded source; it does not establish that any particular continuous-integration run compiled the module.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks3.01
% SENTENCE r4.checks3.02
In the recorded design, the checking interface obtains each verdict from the specified Lean process, so callers cannot supply verdicts through that interface.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks3.02
% SENTENCE r4.checks3.03
This interface rule does not prevent an actor with filesystem access from changing the implementation or stored record.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks3.03
% SENTENCE r4.checks3.04
The protocol distinguishes contributor and reviewer roles but does not technically force a second independent mathematician. No independent human mathematical review of the corpus is recorded.
% END SENTENCE r4.checks3.04


\section{Publication and revision}\label{sec:paper}\label{sec:short}\label{sec:long}\label{sec:production}
% SENTENCE r4.paper1.01
Publication turns a result into an account a reader can use. The short paper states its conditions, explains the decisive step and points to the full argument, so the reader can recover both why the conclusion holds and where it applies.
% END SENTENCE r4.paper1.01
% SENTENCE r4.paper1.02
A dependency graph locates the ingredients. Choosing their order and explaining their role require reading the argument; the manuscript audit supplies no such judgement.
% END SENTENCE r4.paper1.02
% SENTENCE r4.paper1.03
The companion paper retains the complete proof and subsidiary cases. A correspondence link names the passage supporting each linked short-paper claim, taking the reader from explanation to calculation.
% END SENTENCE r4.paper1.03
% SENTENCE r4.paper1.04
The assembler inlines the companion\textquotesingle s authored sections into a flat manuscript, retaining their cross-references. It supplies no missing mathematical step.
% END SENTENCE r4.paper1.04
% SENTENCE reader.guidance
Readers may need a hint, a worked example or the full argument. The reading guide invites them to reconstruct a decisive step or change a hypothesis, and the explanation instructions require an agent to respect the requested depth. These are authoring rules; they do not establish that a reader has understood the proof.
% END SENTENCE reader.guidance

\subsection{Work assignments and review}

% SENTENCE r14.assignment.authority
An integrating agent can alter the live checkout; an advisory agent returns proposed changes against supplied sources. The local names Type A and Type B denote those two permissions.
% END SENTENCE r14.assignment.authority

% SENTENCE r14.assignment.jobs
Repository authority and the assigned task are separate choices. The task may be mathematical research, proof or claim audit, literature and attribution review, or exposition.
% END SENTENCE r14.assignment.jobs


% SENTENCE r14.assignment.products
Research returns candidate arguments, an audit returns source-located findings, a literature review returns attribution proposals, and exposition returns a clearer manuscript; each product has its own review and acceptance criteria.
% END SENTENCE r14.assignment.products


% SENTENCE r14.assignment.exposition
An exposition assignment preserves the established hypotheses, conclusions and evidence status; a suspected mathematical defect is returned for a separate audit or research assignment.
% END SENTENCE r14.assignment.exposition


% SENTENCE r13.paper.07
The reviewer compares hypotheses and implication directions with the whole statement and all its registered declarations. An ordinary argument retains its actual evidence class where formalisation is incomplete.
% END SENTENCE r13.paper.07


% SENTENCE r13.paper.08
After acceptance, the integrating reviewer applies changes to the owning manuscript sources and updates the affected evidence bindings. For an assembled companion, edits belong in its authored sections before rebuilding the flat paper. Integration also requires regenerating evidence and navigation, then inspecting the compiled pages, figures and proof links.
% END SENTENCE r13.paper.08


% SENTENCE r13.paper.09
A writing rule learned from the revision travels with its example and limits as a candidate. Its adoption requires a reviewed change to the owning writing contract.
% END SENTENCE r13.paper.09


\subsection{Proposing and reviewing a revision}
% SENTENCE r13.paper.01
A revision begins by collecting candidate results, antecedents, difficult proof transitions and unresolved questions with their source locations.
% END SENTENCE r13.paper.01
% SENTENCE r13.paper.02
The author decides which argument deserves the short paper; the checker audits source consistency and the specified manuscript rules.
% END SENTENCE r13.paper.02
% SENTENCE r13.paper.03
The exchange freezes selected manuscripts, required inputs, the coverage ledger and audit rules under a manifest of their exact bytes. Writer and integrating reviewer can therefore identify the same starting sources.
% END SENTENCE r13.paper.03

% SENTENCE r13.paper.04
An advisory model can return a proposal against those sources; a tool-enabled agent can prepare it in a checkout. In either case the integrating reviewer decides whether the mathematics and its description remain faithful. The public writer's packet declares which manuscript paths may change and supplies its evidence records and checking tools as read-only inputs.
% END SENTENCE r13.paper.04

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}\label{app:credit}
% SENTENCE r4.related1.01
The closest comparisons concern the objects a system records and the work it lets contributors perform. Lean and mathlib provide formal statements, proofs and a maintained library; blueprints connect declarations to exposition~\cite{lean4,mathlib,leanblueprint}. Prove2Me provides a more direct comparison for agent-assisted collaboration. Appendix~\ref{app:source-map} retains the wider source map.
% END SENTENCE r4.related1.01
% SENTENCE r4.related1.02
\paragraph{Prove2Me.}
We use its published version~2, dated 31 August 2026; the first version appeared on 28 August~2026~\cite{prove2me}. Its basic unit is an immutable theorem statement, stored separately from the proofs submitted for it. A proof is checked against the target type in a pinned verification environment. A \emph{proof-sketch} derives a target using imported theorem statements, some of which may remain open. Contributors can prove those children separately; the target becomes verified when the required children are verified. Immutability makes those local proofs composable without changing their targets~\cite[Sections~3.1--3.2 and~4.2]{prove2me}.
% END SENTENCE r4.related1.02

% SENTENCE r11.related.prove2me-audit
Human review is part of Prove2Me\textquotesingle s design. A mission fixes a human-audited core of goals, definitions and milestones; each milestone pairs a source statement with its attested canonical formalisation. For read-back, an independent agent receives the Lean declaration and dependent definitions without the original source; a human compares its mathematical rendering with that source. Natural-language accounts accompany statements and proofs, statement descriptions support library search, and imports give contributors reuse credit. The discussion record also includes a disproved statement followed by a corrected replacement~\cite[Sections~3.3 and~4.3--4.5]{prove2me}. Explanation, reuse, correction and shared agent work are therefore established parts of this comparison.
% END SENTENCE r11.related.prove2me-audit

% SENTENCE r4.related1.03
For correspondence with a source, the closest analogue is Prove2Me\textquotesingle s milestone: both designs identify the mathematical statement whose formalisation is being reviewed. Our coverage record follows a particular paper occurrence and can list several overlapping formal supports, with a relation note explaining their connection to the printed claim. It is not a proof-sketch: listing those supports does not compose proof terms or discharge open lemmas. A separate passage record binds the explanation to sources and rationale, while entered contribution records retain revision decisions and role credit. The focus here is the consistency and review of these publication records across revisions. This difference of focus establishes neither priority for persistent research records nor a measured advantage over Prove2Me.
% END SENTENCE r4.related1.03

% SENTENCE r4.related1.04
Polymath, BOINC and GIMPS supply precedents for small mathematical contributions and donated compute~\cite{polymath,boinc,gimps}, while the Carleson blueprint shows formalisation organised into publicly claimable tasks~\cite{carleson}.
% END SENTENCE r4.related1.04


% SENTENCE r8.related.systems
Our fixed-order regeneration and freshness checks address the build-system problem of keeping derived views consistent with their inputs~\cite{buildsystems}. The implementation runs an explicit ordered list of builders, then checks the resulting views; it is not a general incremental scheduler. Source bindings and the journal record artifact provenance, including the inputs and revision to which a claim or entered return refers. These records serve inspection and reproducibility, with research efficiency and semantic correctness left to separate evaluation.
% END SENTENCE r8.related.systems

\section{Calculation for the worked theorem}\label{app:worked-proof}

% SENTENCE r4.example1.01
For an infinite set $A$ of positive integers and an integer $b\ge2$, write
\[
 X_A(b)=\sum_{a\in A}\frac1{b^a-1}.
\]
The question is when a condition on the exponents forces this number, and the corresponding sum over every infinite subset of~$A$, to be irrational.
% END SENTENCE r4.example1.01

% SENTENCE r4.example1.02
The condition below rewards exponents divisible by large powers of a fixed collection of primes. For the prime $2$ alone, the relevant part of $a=2^km$, with $m$ odd, is $2^k$. For a finite nonempty set $P$ of primes, this becomes $h_P(a)=\prod_{p\in P}p^{v_p(a)}$, where $v_p(a)$ is the exponent of $p$ in the prime factorisation of~$a$.
\par\Needspace{7\baselineskip}
% END SENTENCE r4.example1.02

% SENTENCE r4.example1.03
The weighted-support theorem of the Problem~257 paper states that, if
\begin{equation}\label{eq:worked-condition}
 W_{2,P}(A):=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{h_P(a)}{a(2^{h_P(a)}-1)}<\infty,
\end{equation}
then $X_B(b)$ is irrational for every integer $b\ge2$ and every infinite $B\subseteq A$~\cite[Theorem~1.2]{paper257}.
% END SENTENCE r4.example1.03

% SENTENCE r4.example1.04
For a fixed base $b$, write $W_{b,P}(A)$ for the sum in~\eqref{eq:worked-condition} with $2$ replaced by~$b$. The fixed-base version assumes $W_{b,P}(A)<\infty$ and gives the same hereditary conclusion at that base. The calculation below gives the ordinary argument; Figure~\ref{fig:theorem} identifies its two registered Lean supports.
% END SENTENCE r4.example1.04

% SENTENCE r4.example1.05
This is a sufficient condition on the support; the question for an arbitrary infinite set of exponents in base two remains open.
% END SENTENCE r4.example1.05

% SENTENCE r4.example2.01
Erd\H{o}s had already stated that $\sum_{a\in A}1/a<\infty$ suffices for this all-base irrationality conclusion~\cite[p.~222]{erdos1968}.
% END SENTENCE r4.example2.01

% END SENTENCE r4.example2.03

% SENTENCE r4.example3.01
The distinction from the reciprocal-summability criterion is visible in the source paper's example
\[
 A_\star=\{2^km:k\ge1,\ m\text{ odd},\ 1\le m\le2^{2^k}\},\qquad P=\{2\}.
\]
Within the layer $v_2(a)=k$, reciprocal summation discounts each odd $m$ by $2^{-k}$; weighted summation discounts it by $(2^{2^k}-1)^{-1}$.
% END SENTENCE r4.example3.01


% SENTENCE r4.example3.02
To compare the layers, put $S_r=\sum_{1\le m\le2^r,\ m\ \mathrm{odd}}1/m$. Grouping the odd integers into intervals $[2^j,2^{j+1})$ gives $r/4\le S_r\le r$ for integers $r\ge2$.
% END SENTENCE r4.example3.02

% SENTENCE r4.example3.03
The reciprocal contribution of each disjoint layer is at least $2^{-k}S_{2^k}\ge1/4$. Its weighted contribution satisfies
\[
 \frac{S_{2^k}}{2^{2^k}-1}\le\frac{2^k}{2^{2^k}-1}\le2^{1-k},
\]
so the layer contributions have a convergent geometric majorant in the weighted sum.
% END SENTENCE r4.example3.03

% SENTENCE r4.example3.04
Thus $A_\star$ lies beyond the reciprocal-summability criterion but satisfies~\eqref{eq:worked-condition}. This is the source paper's ordinary calculation, with no separate entry in the paper-to-Lean ledger~\cite[calculation following the weighted proof]{paper257}.
% END SENTENCE r4.example3.04


% SENTENCE r4.example4.01
\Needspace{9\baselineskip}
\paragraph{A positive displacement.}
Suppose that $X_A(b)=p/q$ with integers $p,q$ and $q>0$. For a positive integer $N$, multiplication by $b^N-1$ removes the complete geometric blocks from each summand, leaving
\begin{equation}\label{eq:worked-displacement}
 \Delta_{b,A}(N)=\sum_{a\in A}\frac{b^{N\bmod a}-1}{b^a-1}
 =(b^N-1)X_A(b)-J_{b,A}(N),\qquad N\ge1,
\end{equation}
where $J_{b,A}(N)=\sum_{a\in A,\,a\le N}\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor N/a\rfloor}b^{N-ja}$ is an integer.
% END SENTENCE r4.example4.01

% SENTENCE r4.example4.02
Every summand is nonnegative, and one is positive because the infinite set~$A$ contains an exponent larger than~$N$. Hence $q\Delta_{b,A}(N)$ is a positive integer, and
\(\Delta_{b,A}(N)\ge1/q\).
% END SENTENCE r4.example4.02


% SENTENCE r4.example4.03
We obtain a contradiction by finding a multiple $N=tQ$ with smaller displacement. Divisibility helps because the summand indexed by $a$ vanishes whenever $a\mid Q$.
% END SENTENCE r4.example4.03

% SENTENCE r4.example4.04
Choose a finite nonempty $F\subset A$ outside which the weighted sum is less than $\varepsilon$, and make $Q$ a multiple of every element of~$F$. Those terms then disappear for every~$t$; we must control the remaining terms on average.
% END SENTENCE r4.example4.04


% SENTENCE r4.example4.05
\paragraph{A finite orbit and its unfinished end.}
With $b=2$, $Q=4$ and $a=6$, the residues $tQ\bmod a$ are $4,2,0$, and their mean displacement is
\[
 \frac13\left(\frac{15}{63}+\frac3{63}+0\right)=\frac2{21}\le\frac19.
\]
For a general exponent $a$, put $g=\gcd(a,Q)$. The residues run through the multiples of~$g$ in a cycle of length $a/g$. Replacing each numerator $b^r-1$ by $b^r$ bounds the sum over one cycle by $1/(b^g-1)$. There are at most $T/(a/g)$ complete cycles and one unfinished cycle among the first $T$ terms. Dividing their total by~$T$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:worked-orbit}
 \frac1T\sum_{t=1}^T\frac{b^{tQ\bmod a}-1}{b^a-1}
 \le \underbrace{\frac{g}{a(b^g-1)}}_{\text{complete cycles}}
    +\underbrace{\frac1{T(b^g-1)}}_{\text{unfinished cycle}}.
\end{equation}
% END SENTENCE r4.example4.05


% SENTENCE r4.example4.06
The first term has the shape of the weighted hypothesis once suitable prime powers have been put into~$Q$. The second has lost the factor $1/a$. Summability of the first therefore gives no licence to sum the unfinished-cycle bounds over all exponents. This is the difficulty that a single progression average leaves unresolved.
% END SENTENCE r4.example4.06


% SENTENCE r4.example4.07
\paragraph{A second average restores the missing factor.}
The source proof averages over the $M$ lengths $T=2^j$, $M\le j<2M$. Thus $M$ counts the lengths being averaged, whereas $T$ counts the terms in one progression average. For nonnegative weights $\alpha_a$ indexed by positive integers, with $\sum_a\alpha_a/a<\infty$, it uses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:worked-dyadic}
 \sum_{j=M}^{2M-1}2^{-j}\sum_{a\le Q2^j}\alpha_a
 \le 2Q\sum_a\frac{\alpha_a}{a}.
\end{equation}
Indeed, a fixed $a$ enters only when $2^j\ge a/Q$, and the sum of the admissible $2^{-j}$ is at most $2Q/a$. This recovers the missing factor before summing over~$a$. Dividing by the number $M$ of lengths then reduces the cost from $2Q$ to $2Q/M$.
% END SENTENCE r4.example4.07


% SENTENCE r6.example.balance
\paragraph{Balancing the two costs.}
The modulus must cancel $F$ while making the remaining exponents amenable to one of two estimates. Fix a positive common multiple $L$ of~$F$, put $p_*=\max P$, and, for $H\ge2p_*$, set
\[
 Q=L\prod_{p\in P}p^{\lfloor\log_p H\rfloor},\qquad
 G=\lfloor H/p_*\rfloor.
\]
If $h_P(a)\le H$, then $h_P(a)\mid Q$. Since $n/(b^n-1)$ decreases for positive integers~$n$, the complete-cycle term in~\eqref{eq:worked-orbit} is bounded by $h_P(a)/[a(b^{h_P(a)}-1)]$. Its sum outside~$F$ is less than $\varepsilon$.

If $h_P(a)>H$, then $\gcd(a,Q)\ge G$: either every $P$-prime-power component of $h_P(a)$ is at most $H$, so $h_P(a)\mid Q$, or one exceeds $H$, and its truncated component in $Q$ is greater than $H/p_*\ge G$. Summing~\eqref{eq:worked-orbit} over these exponents up to $QT$ costs at most
\[
 \frac{G(1+\log(QT))+Q}{b^G-1}:
\]
the harmonic sum supplies $1+\log(QT)$, and at most $QT$ unfinished-cycle terms supply~$Q$.

Increasing $M$ therefore has two effects. It reduces the first group's unfinished-cycle cost $Q/M$, but increases the second group's cost through $\log T<2M\log2$. We need both $Q/M\to0$ and $GM/b^G\to0$, not merely a long average. With $F$ and $L$ fixed, $Q\le LH^{|P|}$ and $G=H/p_*+O(1)$. The choice $M=\lfloor b^{G/2}\rfloor$ meets both requirements.

The source proof combines these estimates into the finite mean
\begin{equation}\label{eq:worked-mean}
\begin{aligned}
 &\frac1M\sum_{j=M}^{2M-1}\frac1{2^j}
       \sum_{t=1}^{2^j}\Delta_{b,A}(tQ)\\
 &\qquad\le \varepsilon+\frac{2QW_{b,P}(A)}M
       +\frac{G(1+\log Q+2M\log2)+Q}{b^G-1}+4\,2^{-M}.
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The terms bound, respectively, the weighted tail over complete cycles, its unfinished cycles, the exponents with large gcd, and the exponents beyond $QT$. The second term uses~\eqref{eq:worked-dyadic} with $\alpha_a=\mathbf1_A(a)/(b^{h_P(a)}-1)$; the last uses the geometric tail bound $4/T$. After $\varepsilon$, $F$ and $L$ have been fixed, every term after $\varepsilon$ tends to zero as $H\to\infty$~\cite[Section~2, weighted-support proof]{paper257}.
% END SENTENCE r6.example.balance


% SENTENCE r4.example4.08
The left side of~\eqref{eq:worked-mean} is an average with nonnegative weights summing to one: each length has weight $1/M$, distributed equally among its $2^j$ terms. For large $H$, at least one displacement is therefore less than $2\varepsilon$. Taking $0<\varepsilon<1/(2q)$ contradicts the lower bound $1/q$. This argument gives no rate of decay in~$N$. Passing to an infinite $B\subseteq A$ only decreases the weighted sum, and $b^{h_P(a)}-1\ge2^{h_P(a)}-1$ gives the all-base conclusion from the binary hypothesis~\cite[Section~2]{paper257}.
% END SENTENCE r4.example4.08
